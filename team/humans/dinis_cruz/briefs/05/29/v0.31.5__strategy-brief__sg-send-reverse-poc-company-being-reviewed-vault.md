# The Reverse PoC: The Company-Being-Reviewed Vault

**version** v0.31.5
**date** 29 May 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Product, Strategy, Architect, @Dev
**type** Strategy / demo brief

## What This Is

A proof of concept that flips the perspective of the VC demo (cross-ref: the v0.27.64 VC confidential-data vault demo): **the vault from the side of the company being reviewed, rather than the VC doing the reviewing.** The VC demo showed a VC handling, creating, managing, and consuming confidential deal data. This PoC shows the other side: a company (a founder, a startup) that needs to present itself to a VC, sharing its data in a controlled, well-organised vault.

The driver is the same VC session that prompted the Google Drive work (cross-ref: the Google Drive connector brief): a signal to **focus on the companies being invested in**, because those companies need better ways to share their data. The project lead: **"focus on the companies investing in, because their companies need better ways to share the data. Let's create a proof of concept that shows the reverse of a particular company being reviewed. We just created one (the VC looking at stuff); let's do the other way around, where this is a company that needs to be reviewed."**

## Why The Company Side Matters

The VC demo addressed the reviewer. But a deal has two sides, and the company being reviewed has its own acute need: presenting itself clearly, controlling what it shares, tracking who saw what, and keeping its materials organised and current across the many parties it talks to. A company raising money shares the same data room with multiple VCs, updates it as the raise progresses, and needs to control and track that exposure. The company-side vault serves that need, and it widens the platform's reach: every company a VC reviews is a potential user, and the VC (cross-ref: the VC demo's channel angle) can bring the vault to its pipeline and portfolio.

## What The Company-Side Vault Shows

The PoC demonstrates the company's side of the workflow:

| Aspect | The Company's Need | The Vault |
|--------|--------------------|-----------|
| **Presenting** | Show the company well (deck, financials, metrics, team) | A customised, well-organised vault (the Vault App views) |
| **Controlling exposure** | Decide what each party sees | Controlled sharing; visibility of what gets shared |
| **Tracking** | Know who saw what | Auditable access (who viewed what) |
| **Organising** | Keep materials structured | The vault structure; sub-vaults per topic |
| **Currency** | Keep it current across parties | Version control; the latest version is clear |

The project lead stressed the visibility and control dimension: **"we need to take into account the visibility of what gets shared, what's the view of the vaults."** A company sharing with multiple VCs needs to know and control its exposure, which is exactly what the vault provides over emailing decks around.

## Vaults Within Vaults For The Company Side

The project lead noted this is where vault-in-vault is interesting: **"this is where vaults within vaults are interesting, because you can have multiple vaults per thing."** A company's materials naturally divide (the pitch, the financials, the product, the team, the legal, the references), and a vault-in-vault structure (cross-ref: the v0.27.64 VIV kernel-model brief) lets the company organise these as sub-vaults, sharing some with some parties and others with others, each controlled and tracked.

A caveat to carry: VIV is design-stage and not fully working yet (cross-ref: the VC demo brief, which deliberately excluded VIV). So the PoC should be designed to show the company-side value on the solid single-vault capabilities first (organisation, controlled sharing, version control, audience views), with the multi-sub-vault structure framed as the coming enhancement rather than the basis of the demo. This keeps the PoC demonstrable now while pointing at the richer VIV future.

## Version Control And The Latest Version

A specific need the project lead raised: **across multiple shares, which is the latest version?** The project lead: **"this is where there's an interesting idea of synchronisation, and where's the latest version, and all the version control stuff we can have."** A company sharing an evolving data room with several VCs faces the version problem: each VC should see the current materials, and the company should not lose track of what is current. The vault's version control (cross-ref: the v0.27.64 commit-queue brief) addresses this: the materials are versioned, the latest is clear, and the history is preserved. This is a concrete advantage over scattered emailed decks (where versions multiply and the latest is ambiguous).

## Parked: Single Source Of Truth Synchronised Across Vaults

The project lead raised, and explicitly deferred, a larger idea: **a single source of truth synchronised across multiple vaults.** The project lead: **"especially keeping single source of truth that then are synchronised across multiple vaults, which is something we can maintain. I think we can add some more thoughts on that a bit later."**

Per the project lead, this is **parked** for now: the idea is that a company maintains one canonical set of materials, and multiple vaults (shared with different parties) stay synchronised to it, so every party sees the current truth without the company maintaining many copies. It connects to the version-control and synchronisation themes, and to the VIV model. It is recorded here as a flagged future direction, not developed, pending the project lead's further thoughts.

## What This Asks For

1. **Build the reverse PoC**: a company-being-reviewed vault, the flip of the VC demo.
2. **Show the company-side workflow**: presenting, controlling exposure, tracking, organising, keeping current.
3. **Foreground visibility and control of sharing** (what gets shared, who saw what).
4. **Use solid single-vault capabilities for the demo** (organisation, controlled sharing, version control, audience views); frame VIV as the coming enhancement (it is not fully working).
5. **Show version control / the latest version** (the data room stays current across multiple shares).
6. **Park the single-source-of-truth-across-vaults idea** (flagged, not developed, pending further thoughts).
7. **Note the channel angle** (every reviewed company is a potential user; the VC is a channel).

Estimated effort: the PoC composes existing capabilities (the vault, Vault App views, controlled sharing, version control), mirroring the VC demo from the other side; the bulk is preparing a realistic simulated company data room and the company-side framing. VIV is excluded from the demonstrable core (not yet working) and framed as coming.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a VIV demo.** VIV is design-stage; the PoC stands on single-vault capabilities and frames VIV as coming.
- **Not the single-source-of-truth design.** That idea is parked, pending further thoughts.
- **Not a real company's data.** Simulated, clearly so.
- **Not a replacement for the VC demo.** It is the complementary other side of the same deal.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Leaning on VIV would block the demo.** VIV is not fully working. Mitigation: build the demonstrable core on single-vault capabilities; frame the multi-sub-vault structure as coming.

**Risk 2: The version/sync story can get complex fast.** Synchronisation across vaults is a deep topic. Mitigation: show version control within a vault now; park the cross-vault single-source-of-truth idea (as the project lead directed).

**Risk 3: Simulated company data must be realistic.** A thin demo will not land. Mitigation: a plausible startup data room (deck, financials, product, team), recognisable to a founder or VC.
