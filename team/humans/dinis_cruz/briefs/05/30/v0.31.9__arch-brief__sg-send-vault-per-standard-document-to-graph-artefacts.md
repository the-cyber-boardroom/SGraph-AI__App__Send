# Vault Per Standard: Turning Compliance Documents Into Shareable Graph Artefacts

**version** v0.31.9
**date** 30 May 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Architect, Developer (lead), Product, Strategy, @Dev

## What This Is

The artefact-building foundation that the assessment and consulting work consumes: **a vault per industry standard or compliance document, each a semantic, graph-based, visualised version of the standard.** Following the GDPR and consulting briefs (cross-ref: the v0.31.5 GDPR assessment and consulting-assessments briefs), the project lead wants to actually build these customised graph-based standards as standalone artefacts: GDPR, ISO 27000, SOC 2, the OWASP standards, each as its own vault. The same universal document-to-graph pipeline produces all of them, and the vault is exactly the right medium.

These are something the project lead always wanted and can now execute, and they are good enough as assets to spread: **a good example of something that could get quite a bit of virality and sharing, because it's a generally good asset.** They are both useful in their own right and the raw material the consulting pack and the assessments draw on.

## A Vault Per Standard

The core: **create a vault per industry standard and per compliance document, as a customised, graph-based version of it.**

The project lead: **"I want to create a vault per industry standard and a vault per compliance document, going to the idea of creating customised versions of them, and now we have the perfect medium for that. I want to create a semantic graph, visualised version of GDPR, ISO 27000, SOC 2, the OWASP standards."**

| Standard | The Artefact |
|----------|--------------|
| **GDPR** | A semantic, graph-based, visualised GDPR vault |
| **ISO 27000** | The same for ISO 27000 |
| **SOC 2** | The same for SOC 2 |
| **OWASP standards** | The same for the OWASP standards |

The framing is **maturity models**: take any compliance document and turn it into a navigable, graph-based maturity model. And the project lead wants to build a batch of them, starting with these complex, high-need documents, then extending the pattern to any document: **"we're going to start with these complex documents because there's a really interesting need, but you can apply this to any document, any article. The pattern is the same."**

## The Universal Document-To-Graph Pipeline

The key reusable idea: **the workflow is the same for every document, so figure out the pipeline once and apply it to all of them.**

The project lead: **"the workflow is the same for all of them, we just need to figure out the workflow and how to leverage the models to do this. The pattern is: you take the document, create a digital Excel version of it, then start creating visualisations, create semantic knowledge graphs, extract ontologies, extract taxonomies."**

The pipeline:

| Stage | What Happens |
|-------|--------------|
| **1. Take the document** | The raw compliance document (GDPR, ISO 27000, etc.) |
| **2. Create a structured version** | A digital, Excel-like structured version of it |
| **3. Create visualisations** | Visual representations (cross-ref: the visualisation-cornerstone brief's three primitives) |
| **4. Extract the semantic knowledge graph** | The graph: ontologies, taxonomies, what connects to what |
| **5. Consume, check, validate** | Ways to consume it, check it, and validate it |
| **6. Feedback loops** | Refine it iteratively (cross-ref: the evidence feedback loop) |

This is the same extract-and-visualise machinery from the assessment series (cross-ref: the visualisation-cornerstone and prompt-context briefs), applied to a compliance document instead of a user's evidence. The models do the extraction (the project lead: **"leverage the models and get them to figure this out"**); the pipeline turns a flat document into a structured, graph-based, visualised, validated artefact. Because the pipeline is universal, building the GDPR vault teaches you how to build all the others, and ultimately any document or article.

## Ontology Of Ontologies: The Fractal Nature

A structural point the project lead raised, the fractal nature made concrete: **you may need an ontology of ontologies, a graph of graphs, where a section has its own ontology connecting up to a top-level one.**

The project lead: **"you see if you need the graph of graphs, which I talk a lot about: do we need an ontology of ontologies? GDPR is an example, because you might need a specific ontology for a specific section that connects to the one at the top. You start to have this fractal nature, which is exactly what we want to see."**

So a standard is not one flat ontology but a fractal of them: a top-level ontology, with specific sections having their own ontologies that connect upward. This is the fractal/graph-of-graphs idea (cross-ref: the VIV kernel brief's fractal nesting and the prompt-context brief's semantic graphs) applied to standards: GDPR's structure (principles, then specific articles, then the detail of each) is naturally a fractal of ontologies, and the artefact should represent it that way. This is what makes a large, complex standard navigable: you zoom from the top-level ontology into a section's own ontology, the same multi-altitude navigation as the visualisation cornerstone (cross-ref).

## GDPR Cannot Be Taken At Face Value: The Standard Is The Graph

A specific, important insight about why the graph matters: **GDPR cannot be taken at face value, because you have to account for the rulings, the guidance, and the per-country relevance, and that is exactly what becomes the graph.**

The project lead: **"GDPR is an example, because you can't just take GDPR at face value. You have to take into account the rulings, the guidance, the relevance for each country, and that becomes the graph."** A compliance standard is not just its text: it is the text plus the case law (rulings), the regulators' guidance, and the country-specific variations. A flat document cannot hold this; a graph can. So the graph artefact is more than a visualisation of the document, it is the document connected to its rulings, guidance, and jurisdictional variations, which is what makes it actually usable for compliance. This is precisely where the value is: the graph captures what the face-value document misses, and it is exactly what an assessment (cross-ref: the consulting brief) needs to map a company against.

## Why The Vault Is The Perfect Medium

The project lead enumerated exactly why the vault is the right medium, and it is a clean argument: **everything the pipeline needs, the vault provides.**

The project lead: **"what do you need to do this? You need a place to store the raw information, a place to store the transformations, a place to store the model's response (so you have provenance), a place to visualise it, a place to build mini tools. Guess what, all of that is our vault."**

| The Pipeline Needs | The Vault Provides |
|--------------------|--------------------|
| Store the raw information | The vault stores the raw document |
| Store the transformations | The vault stores each transformation stage |
| Store the model's responses (provenance) | The vault stores what the models produced, with provenance |
| Visualise it | The vault renders the visualisations (cross-ref: the vault chat and visualisation work) |
| Build mini tools | The vault hosts mini tools (cross-ref: the apps-in-the-vault and Vault App work) |

So the vault is not just where the artefact lives; it is the whole workshop: raw input, transformations, model outputs with provenance, visualisation, and the tools to build and consume, all in one self-contained, version-controlled place (cross-ref: the self-contained-vault and version-control work). The provenance point is especially important for compliance: every element of the graph traces back to its source (the document, a ruling, a piece of guidance) and to the model response that produced it, which is what makes the artefact trustworthy and auditable.

## Intersect And Customise: Feeding The Consulting Pack

Where this connects to the assessments: **once you have a vault per standard, another vault can intersect them and create customised versions per company or person.**

The project lead: **"once you have this, you can start to connect them, look at another vault that intersects them, and create a customised version of each of these standards and compliance requirements for a particular company, for a particular person. This is exactly what feeds our GDPR guidance, our GDPR consulting pack."**

So the per-standard vaults are the reusable foundation, and a company-specific assessment is an intersection vault that draws on them:

| Layer | What It Is |
|-------|------------|
| **The per-standard vaults** | The reusable graph artefacts (GDPR, ISO 27000, SOC 2, OWASP) |
| **The intersection vault** | Connects/intersects the standards relevant to a company |
| **The customised version** | The standard(s) tailored to a particular company or person |

This is the customised-graph-based-standards idea from the consulting brief (cross-ref) given its foundation: the consulting assessment does not rebuild the standard each time; it draws on the per-standard vault and customises it for the company. The per-standard artefact is built once (and shared, even virally) and reused across every assessment. This is the create-once-reuse-many pattern (cross-ref: the evidence-CV and security-report briefs) applied to standards.

## Virality And Sharing

A strategic point worth holding: **these artefacts are good enough to spread on their own.** The project lead: **"these are a good example of something that could get quite a bit of virality and sharing, because it's a generally good asset, something I always wanted, and now we can execute it."** A genuinely good, navigable, graph-based version of GDPR or OWASP is valuable to many people independently of any assessment, so publishing them (cross-ref: the public-vault-previews and Vault App Store briefs) is both a public good and a distribution channel: people discover the vault platform through a useful free artefact, then use it for their own assessments. This is the adoption-by-augmentation strategy (cross-ref: the connector framework brief) in another form: lead with a genuinely useful asset.

## What This Asks For

1. **Build a vault per standard** (GDPR, ISO 27000, SOC 2, OWASP), each a semantic, graph-based, visualised artefact.
2. **Define the universal document-to-graph pipeline** (document, structured version, visualisations, semantic graph/ontologies/taxonomies, consume/check/validate, feedback loops).
3. **Leverage the models for extraction** (get them to figure out the structure, ontologies, and graph).
4. **Represent the fractal ontology-of-ontologies** (section-level ontologies connecting to a top-level one; multi-altitude navigation).
5. **Capture what is beyond the face-value document** (rulings, guidance, per-country relevance, that is the graph).
6. **Use the vault as the whole workshop** (raw, transformations, model responses with provenance, visualisation, mini tools).
7. **Support intersection and customisation** (an intersection vault drawing on the per-standard vaults to customise for a company/person; feeds the consulting pack).
8. **Publish the artefacts** (they are good assets; virality and adoption; cross-ref: public previews and the Vault App Store).
9. **Start with the complex standards, then any document** (the pattern generalises to any document or article).

Estimated effort: the pipeline composes existing machinery (the extraction, the semantic graphs, the visualisations, the vault's storage/provenance/version-control, the mini-tools/apps); the new work is defining the universal pipeline once and building the per-standard vaults. Building GDPR first teaches the pipeline; the others follow more cheaply; the intersection/customisation reuses them.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a one-off per standard.** A universal pipeline produces all of them, and any document.
- **Not the face-value document.** The graph captures the rulings, guidance, and jurisdictional variations the text omits.
- **Not a flat ontology.** A fractal ontology-of-ontologies, navigable by altitude.
- **Not rebuilt per assessment.** Built once, reused and customised via intersection vaults.
- **Not legal advice.** The artefacts support assessments; the not-legal-advice caveat carries (cross-ref: the GDPR brief).

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Standards are complex and the graph must be accurate.** A wrong graph of GDPR misleads. Mitigation: provenance (every element traces to its source and the model response); leverage existing graph versions where they exist (cross-ref: the consulting brief); validate and feedback-loop; the not-legal-advice caveat.

**Risk 2: Rulings, guidance, and per-country variation are a moving target.** The graph must stay current. Mitigation: the vault is version-controlled (the graph evolves with new rulings/guidance); provenance shows what is current; treat the artefact as living (cross-ref: the SG/Sentinel compliance-as-a-living-graph brief).

**Risk 3: The universal pipeline may not fit every document equally.** Some documents resist the pattern. Mitigation: start with the high-need complex standards; refine the pipeline; the pattern generalises but each document may need tuning.

**Risk 4: Model extraction can err.** The models building the graph can make mistakes. Mitigation: provenance and validation; feedback loops; human review for the published artefacts; the confirm-not-assert discipline (cross-ref: the assessment briefs).
