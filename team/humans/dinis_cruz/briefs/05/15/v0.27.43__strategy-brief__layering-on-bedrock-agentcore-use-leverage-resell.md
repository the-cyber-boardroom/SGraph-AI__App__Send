## What This Is

A strategic analysis: assuming AgentCore is fully enabled and working in our AWS account, **where do SG/Compute and SG/Send fit in?** What do we use directly, what do we layer on top of, and where can we resell services that compose AgentCore primitives with our distinctive capabilities?

This is the companion to the AgentCore research brief (today). The research describes what AgentCore is and what it costs. This brief reads that against everything we have shipped (the vault model, vault collections, vaults-of-vaults, agent CLIs, pre-auth payments, credential manager, container hosts, observability work) and identifies the seams where SG/Compute creates value that AgentCore alone does not.

The premise: AgentCore is a substrate, not a product. Substrates compete on commodity dimensions (price, scale, reliability). Products compete on distinctive dimensions. SG/Compute should consume AgentCore as a substrate where it makes us faster and cheaper, and build products on top where the distinctiveness lives in our own architecture.

## The Layering Decision

Three honest categories for how SG/Compute relates to AgentCore:

| Category | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Use directly** | AgentCore capabilities we consume as-is from our agents and workflows; no SG-distinctive wrapper |
| **Leverage with a wrapper** | AgentCore underneath, but a thin SG-native CLI or vault binding makes it more useful in our ecosystem |
| **Resell layered services** | Products built on AgentCore where the distinctiveness is ours and customers pay us, not AWS |

The strategic question is **which AgentCore capabilities sit in which category**. The answer drives both the implementation roadmap and the commercial model.

## Use Directly

Capabilities where we should consume AgentCore as the substrate without trying to layer over them. The distinctiveness is not in our wrapping; it is in what we do with the substrate.

| AgentCore Capability | Why Use Directly |
|---------------------|------------------|
| **Runtime** | Serverless agent hosting with I/O-wait-free pricing is genuinely better than running our own. Our agents (Conductor, Sherpa, etc.) deploy as AgentCore Runtime apps. |
| **Code Interpreter** | Sandboxed Python/JS/TS execution with 8-hour sessions and 5GB S3 file support is a non-trivial build; AgentCore's version is good. |
| **Browser** | Cloud browser runtime for agent web interactions; we have looked at Neko and similar; AgentCore Browser may displace that work. |
| **Memory (short-term)** | In-session memory at $X per event is fine for substrate-style use. |
| **Identity** | OAuth tokens and pre-authorised user consent; reusing AWS-native identity is sensible. |
| **Bidirectional streaming** | Voice agent capability we have not been building; available out of the box. |

For each of these, our role is to use the AgentCore primitive cleanly via our CLI (`sg-compute aws bedrock ...` from today's planning brief) and let the SG-native value emerge from how those primitives compose into our vault-centric workflows.

## Leverage With A Wrapper

Capabilities where AgentCore handles the heavy lifting, but a thin SG-native layer makes them dramatically more useful inside our ecosystem. The wrapper does two things: it speaks our CLI patterns, and it grounds outputs in our vault model.

| AgentCore Capability | What We Layer |
|---------------------|--------------|
| **Memory (long-term)** | Wrap LTM with vault grounding. Every agent memory record also commits to a vault; vault commit history becomes the audit log; agents can read each other's memories through the vault. AgentCore Memory becomes a fast-access cache; the vault is the truth. |
| **Gateway** | Wrap Gateway tool definitions in vault-stored manifests so tools are discoverable, version-controlled, and shareable as vault collections. A vault collection can be a self-contained toolkit. |
| **Observability** | Pull AgentCore traces into the unified observability session (today's brief). The session knows how to query AgentCore traces alongside CloudWatch, CloudTrail, vault histories, billing. AgentCore becomes one source among many. |
| **Evaluations** | Treat evaluation runs as vault commits. A vault tracks the eval history of an agent. Comparable agents can be ranked across evaluations stored as vault data. Connects naturally to the QA stack from 11 May. |
| **Policy** | Cedar policies stored as vault content; vault becomes the policy-management surface. Manager vaults can hold policy bundles applied across many agents. Audit trail for policy changes is the vault's commit history. |
| **Agent Registry** | Treat registered agents as entries in a manager vault. The Agent Registry holds the AWS-side reference; the vault holds the canonical definition, capabilities, contracts, and history. Vault-as-source-of-truth pattern. |
| **Identity (multi-tenant)** | AgentCore custom claims plus our credential manager Simple Tokens; the vault becomes the per-customer identity scope. |

In each of these, the AgentCore capability is consumed; the wrapper makes it 10x more useful for our specific workflows. Not all wrappers are equal: the Memory and Gateway wrappers are the most distinctive; the others are smaller layers.

## Resell Layered Services

The interesting category. Products built on AgentCore that customers pay us for, where the value-add is distinctly SG-shaped. Each one is a candidate for productisation.

### Resell 1: Vault-Grounded Agent Hosting

**The proposition:** "Deploy your agent on our managed AgentCore-backed substrate, with every agent action grounded in a versioned, encrypted vault. Get an audit-trail-for-free, reproducibility, and your work stays portable."

**What customers get that they cannot get from AgentCore alone:**

- Their agent's memory is also their vault; they can export, share, or migrate it.
- Their agent's outputs (drafts, decisions, intermediate artefacts) are all committed to the vault automatically.
- They can publish a "snapshot of how this agent operated" as a sharable vault, useful for partners, auditors, customers.
- Their agent state is portable across substrates (vault stays the same if they move from AgentCore to self-hosted to a different cloud).

**Pricing model:** Pre-auth + micropayments (yesterday's substrate). Per agent-hour, with AgentCore costs visible and a margin on top for the vault-binding and management layer.

**Why customers buy this and not just AgentCore directly:** Because the AgentCore substrate is undifferentiated; the vault-shaped state, audit, and portability is our distinct value.

### Resell 2: Multi-Region Agent Test Fleet

**The proposition:** "Run your agent against thousands of simulated users across regions; get reproducible test runs that commit to a vault and can be replayed."

**What this layers:**

- Our container hosts primitive (today's brief) for the simulated user containers
- AgentCore Runtime hosting the agent under test
- AgentCore Evaluations for quality scoring of agent responses
- Vault for the test scenario definitions, run results, and replay capability
- Pre-auth payments for usage-based billing

**Why customers buy this:** Because spinning up a multi-region test environment ourselves is non-trivial; AgentCore alone provides agent hosting but not the user-simulation fleet; together they form a coherent QA capability that we package and sell.

### Resell 3: Compliance-Bundled Agent Deployments

**The proposition:** "Deploy your customer-facing agent with built-in compliance: full audit trail in a vault, granular Cedar policies controlling every tool call, isolation guarantees, evaluation history. Suitable for regulated industries."

**What this layers:**

- AgentCore Policy for runtime authorisation
- AgentCore Evaluations for ongoing quality checks
- AgentCore Identity with custom claims for multi-tenant auth
- Our vault model for the audit trail
- Our credential manager for scoped third-party access
- Our pre-auth payments for transparent billing

**Why customers buy this:** Compliance is the bottleneck for many enterprise agent deployments. Bundling AgentCore's compliance-relevant primitives with our vault audit story (and our existing OWASP-aligned posture from earlier work) gives a deliverable that they can show to their compliance teams more easily than rolling each piece themselves.

### Resell 4: Agentic Newsroom And Daily Production

**The proposition:** Already partly built. The newsroom layout (12 May) and the daily-band production team (13 May) become customer-facing products. AgentCore makes the substrate cheaper and more capable.

**What this layers:**

- AgentCore Runtime hosts the production agents (Writer, Critic, Editor, Designer, etc.)
- AgentCore Code Interpreter for any code-generation or analysis steps
- AgentCore Memory (long-term, episodic) for agents that learn customer preferences over time
- Our vault model for the canonical record of every story, song, design
- Our CLI-first agent architecture defines the workflows
- Pre-auth payments for end-customer billing

**Why customers buy this:** This is the daily-production-experiment from 13 May, productised. AgentCore lowers the substrate cost; the vault-shaped workflow is the product.

### Resell 5: Multi-Provider Token Brokerage

**The proposition:** Yesterday's credential manager vault, expanded. A customer gets one Simple Token; behind it we route to AgentCore (with our AWS account), to OpenAI, to Anthropic direct, to whatever is cheapest or most appropriate per call. AgentCore Payments enables the per-call micropayments. Customer sees one bill from us.

**What this layers:**

- Our credential manager for token issuance
- AgentCore Payments for micropayment rails to upstream providers
- AgentCore Gateway as one substrate among several
- Pre-auth payments for end-customer billing
- The unified observability session for visibility

**Why customers buy this:** They want LLM access without the operational overhead of managing multiple provider accounts, multiple billing relationships, and routing logic. We are the meter.

### Resell 6: Specialised Manager Vaults

**The proposition:** Pre-packaged manager vaults (yesterday's vaults-of-vaults pattern) for specific operational needs, with AgentCore agents embedded.

Examples:

- **Customer engagement manager**: tracks partner deployments, AgentCore-hosted agents send weekly summary emails, vault is the canonical record.
- **Investor relations manager**: AgentCore agents generate update drafts grounded in real metrics from our observability layer, committed to vault for review and publication.
- **Compliance audit manager**: AgentCore Evaluations runs continuously against compliance-critical agents, results committed to vault as auditable record.

**What this layers:**

- Pre-built vault templates with embedded apps
- AgentCore agents pre-configured per template
- Our vault model as the canonical state
- Pre-auth payments for the manager vault as a product

**Why customers buy this:** Time to value. Buying a "customer engagement manager" with a working setup is faster than building one from primitives.

## The Pricing Layer

Across all resell products, the pricing model is consistent:

1. **AgentCore costs flow through transparently.** Customers see what AWS charges us; we add margin separately.
2. **Pre-auth + micropayments** from yesterday's substrate brief handles all billing.
3. **Simple Tokens** mediate access to anything the customer pays for.
4. **The vault tracks usage** which feeds the observability session and the customer-facing billing reports.

This means **AgentCore's pricing is a wholesale price for us**; our retail price layers our value-add on top. The fact that AgentCore is consumption-priced makes this clean: we know what each customer-agent-hour costs us; we can price our service at a clear margin.

## Strategic Implications

Three implications worth being explicit about:

### Implication 1: AgentCore Reduces Our Build Burden

We had been considering building several of these capabilities ourselves (sandboxed code execution, browser automation, agent memory store). AgentCore being good enough to consume directly **removes those work items** from our roadmap. The team time we save goes into the distinctive layers (vault grounding, policy bundling, multi-region orchestration, manager vault productisation).

This is meaningful. AgentCore is the kind of platform that makes the "build vs buy" calculus tip toward buy for substrate-layer capabilities.

### Implication 2: AgentCore Is Not A Competitor To Us

It would be easy to read AgentCore as a competing platform. It is not, at least not for what we do best. AgentCore competes with self-hosted agent infrastructure. We compete on **distinct product surface area**: zero-knowledge encrypted vaults, vault-grounded workflows, vault collections as portable units, manager vaults, multi-substrate density modes, our CLI-first agent architecture.

The right framing: **AgentCore is one substrate among the spectrum we host on**. Customers who pick us pick us for what we do that AgentCore cannot do. Customers who pick AgentCore alone are not our market.

### Implication 3: The 2026 Entrepreneur Customer Is Better Served

The 2026 entrepreneur strategic capstone (11 May) frames our core customer as solo founders and small teams who want capability without operational burden. AgentCore amplifies what we can offer them:

- They get enterprise-grade substrate without operating it
- They get vault-shaped state portability and audit
- They get one bill, one mental model
- They get the I/O-wait-free pricing without dealing with AWS

This is a strictly better proposition than the substrate alone. And it scales: as a customer grows, they can graduate to direct AWS if they want, with their vault state portable. We do not lock them in.
