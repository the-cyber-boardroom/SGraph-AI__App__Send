## What This Is

A concrete research-and-demonstration programme: **map 33N Ventures' portfolio companies as a set of vault-grounded research briefings**, structured so the workflow itself becomes a publishable artefact demonstrating what the platform enables. The forcing function is a planned meeting with 33N Ventures (the Porto-based cybersecurity and infrastructure software VC), where being able to show "here is what we know about your portfolio, structured as evidence packs you can navigate" makes the demonstration meaningful in a way a generic platform pitch cannot.

The voice memo named a specific structural insight: **a four-vault architecture, with three layers of public deliverables and one private internal vault**. The brief-to-create-the-brief (the meta-vault), the individual company research vaults, the strategic outlook vault, and the private "how SG/Send could collaborate with 33N" vault. The first three are publishable and demonstrate the workflow openly; the fourth is internal preparation for the meeting itself.

This is the MyFeeds evidence-pack repositioning made concrete with a specific real-world target. It is also a working test of the entire stack we have been building this week: vault-as-mini-app pattern, the multi-audience UI capability, the agentic research workflow from the MyFeeds brief, the infographic generator, the JS API for navigability, the public discovery primitive.

## Who 33N Is And Why This Fits

33N Ventures is a Porto-based VC fund founded in 2022, specialising in cybersecurity and infrastructure software. They are backed by Alantra and Mutua Madrileña, target Series A and B rounds with tickets of €4-12M, and have a portfolio of around 27 companies spanning Europe, Israel, and the US. Their stated focus areas are cybersecurity, AI, data infrastructure, DevOps, and cloud-native tools. Notable portfolio companies include Arctic Wolf, Feedzai, OutSystems, Panorays, Exein, Bfore.ai, Apono, Equixly, and Acoru.

The fit is unusually direct. 33N invests in exactly the categories our platform is built around: cybersecurity (the AppSec mini-tools direction), infrastructure software (the substrate spectrum), DevOps (the CI primitives and SG/Compute story), AI (the Nova / AgentCore integration), and cloud-native tools (the serverless-for-agents framing). The kinds of companies 33N funds are the kinds of companies our platform could power. **The conversation is positioning, not selling.**

The meeting opportunity is therefore richer than a typical investor introduction. The natural exchange is: 33N knows the cybersecurity and infrastructure landscape; we know what we are building; the question is whether there are concrete collaboration patterns (portfolio company integrations, follow-on technical partnerships, perhaps investment) that work for both sides.

The research vault programme is the demonstrable artefact that frames the conversation appropriately.

## The Four-Vault Architecture

The voice memo named four distinct vaults, with deliberately different visibilities and audiences. Naming them explicitly:

| Vault | Purpose | Visibility | Audience |
|-------|---------|------------|----------|
| **Vault 1: The Research Brief Vault** | The brief-to-create-the-brief; the template-and-workflow definition | Public | Anyone interested in the methodology |
| **Vault 2: Per-Company Research Vaults (~27)** | One vault per 33N portfolio company; the actual research output | Public | 33N team, portfolio companies, broader audience |
| **Vault 3: Strategic Outlook Vault** | Common-thread analysis across the portfolio; the 33N thesis as visible from outside | Public | 33N, prospective LPs, ecosystem |
| **Vault 4: Collaboration Opportunities Vault** | How SG/Send and our platform could work with 33N and their portfolio | Private | Project lead, then 33N meeting attendees only |

Three public vaults plus one private. This split matters because **the public vaults demonstrate the platform openly while the private vault holds commercially-sensitive thinking that is appropriate for a first meeting but not for public publication**.

The public framing has another property worth being explicit about: **everything published is deliberately positive**. The voice memo flagged this directly. Past attempts at this kind of analysis have sometimes been framed critically; this programme is framed constructively. Not because 33N's choices are above criticism but because **the demonstration value is higher when the framing is collaborative, the research output is more useful to 33N when it surfaces strengths, and the publishable artefact looks more like genuine ecosystem analysis than competitive intelligence**.

## Vault 1: The Research Brief Vault (The Brief-To-Create-The-Brief)

The meta-vault. It contains the methodology, the template, and the workflow that gets applied to each portfolio company. This is the "brief to create the brief" pattern the voice memo named; we have done this kind of meta-level vault before, and it is the right way to make the methodology reusable.

Contents:

```
research-brief-vault/
├── methodology.md              # How we approach the research
├── source-strategy.md          # Which sources we use; perplexity, web search, specialist DBs
├── per-company-template/       # The template applied to each company
│   ├── pack.md                # The investor-pack-shaped output template
│   ├── operating-model.md     # Company business and operations
│   ├── product.md             # What the company builds
│   ├── market-position.md     # Sector positioning and competitors
│   ├── technical-architecture.md # Technical approach (where public info available)
│   ├── relationships.md       # Customers, partners, integrations, investors beyond 33N
│   ├── timeline.md            # Founding, funding rounds, major milestones
│   ├── strengths.md           # Positive framing: what is working well
│   ├── opportunities.md       # Forward-looking: where the company could grow
│   └── platform-leverage.md   # Where our platform's primitives might add value
├── quality-control.md          # Review process and standards
├── ethics-and-framing.md       # Constructive-positive framing principles
├── update-cadence.md           # How and when each per-company vault refreshes
└── readme.md                   # How to use this meta-vault
```

This vault is **the demonstration of methodology**. Anyone reading it can see how the per-company vaults are produced, what we did and did not include, and why. **Transparency at the methodology level makes the per-company output more trustworthy.**

It is also reusable. Once the methodology is captured here, the same pattern applies to other investor groups, other sectors, other research engagements. The 33N programme is the first real instance; subsequent programmes use the same meta-vault as their starting point.

## Vault 2: The Per-Company Research Vaults

Twenty-seven vaults, one per portfolio company. Each follows the template defined in Vault 1.

The output for each company is shaped like an investor-pack but written from the outside-in. It is not the company's own investor pack (we do not have privileged access); it is what a thorough public-source analysis can establish about the company, structured into the pack format for navigability.

Each per-company vault is independent. It has its own URL, its own app, its own audience. A 33N team member interested in Arctic Wolf navigates to that vault; a Panorays customer interested in the company they bought from navigates to a different vault. The company being researched can use it as a third-party perspective on themselves.

The voice memo flagged something important here: **the first vault should be hard, the rest should be much easier**. This is the right expectation. Building the template, validating the methodology, and producing the first complete output takes substantial effort. Once the template is proven, applying it to the remaining 26 companies is largely a matter of running the agentic workflow with quality-control passes, not redesigning each one from scratch.

A reasonable build order:

1. **First vault** (probably one of the better-documented portfolio companies like Arctic Wolf or Feedzai). Substantial effort to validate template.
2. **Second and third vaults** (different sectors within the portfolio). Refine template based on what worked and did not.
3. **Remaining 24 vaults** in batches of 4-6. Each batch is faster than the last as the agentic workflow matures.
4. **Final pass**: cross-vault consistency review; semantic linking between companies that have real relationships.

Estimated effort: 1-2 weeks for vault 1; 1 week for vaults 2-3; 2-3 weeks for the remaining 24. Total: 4-6 weeks for the full set.

## Vault 3: The Strategic Outlook Vault

This is the cross-portfolio analysis. The voice memo framed it as: **what is the common thread across 33N's portfolio choices, viewed from the outside?**

The question is genuinely interesting because it surfaces the implicit thesis of a fund. 33N has made 27 investment decisions over their fund's life; what pattern do those decisions form? Where are the natural clusters (e.g. cybersecurity sub-sectors)? Where are the surprising adjacencies? What is the trajectory across vintages?

Contents:

```
strategic-outlook-vault/
├── thesis-as-visible.md        # The 33N thesis as it appears from outside
├── portfolio-clusters/         # Groupings by sector, stage, geography, theme
├── timeline-analysis.md        # Investment cadence and evolution
├── co-investor-network.md      # Who 33N invests alongside; the network shape
├── exits-and-trajectories.md   # Outcomes to date; what they suggest
├── thematic-bets.md            # Themes 33N appears to be betting on
├── ecosystem-position.md       # Where 33N sits in the European VC landscape
├── infographics/               # Visualisations of the above
└── readme.md
```

This is the most analytical of the three public vaults. It is also where the **constructive-positive framing matters most**. The output should read as ecosystem analysis that 33N themselves would find useful, not as competitive intelligence positioning. The framing principle: **highlight what is interesting, surface what is coherent, acknowledge what is forward-looking; do not snipe at portfolio underperformers or critique investment decisions**.

This is also the vault where the infographic generator earns its keep. The portfolio shape, the co-investor network, the timeline analysis all benefit from visualisation. A cleanly-presented set of infographics summarising the strategic outlook becomes a shareable artefact in its own right.

## Vault 4: The Collaboration Opportunities Vault (Private)

The internal preparation for the actual meeting. Not for publication; not even for sharing with 33N broadly until the project lead has prepared the conversation.

Contents:

```
collaboration-vault/
├── 33n-context.md              # Where 33N is in their fund cycle; what they likely care about
├── meeting-objectives.md       # What we want from the meeting
├── platform-fit-analysis.md    # Which of our primitives map to which 33N priorities
├── per-portfolio-company-fit/  # Where each portfolio company might benefit from our platform
│   ├── arctic-wolf.md
│   ├── feedzai.md
│   ├── panorays.md
│   └── ... (one per company)
├── partnership-models.md       # How a collaboration could be structured
├── investment-angle.md         # Whether 33N might be a relevant investor for us
├── risks-and-sensitivities.md  # What to be careful about; what not to overcommit to
├── presentation-flow.md        # The walkthrough plan for the meeting
└── readme.md
```

This vault is **explicitly commercially-sensitive** and stays private. It captures the kind of preparation thinking that should not be public: where the platform's primitives actually map to specific portfolio company needs, what an investment conversation might look like, where the project lead might want to deliberately under-commit in a first meeting.

The vault exists separately from the public vaults so that **the public artefacts can be genuinely public without bleeding strategic positioning into them**. The split is deliberate and the kind of operational discipline the platform encourages: different audiences, different vaults, different access.

## The Workflow End-To-End

Putting it together, the workflow from start to meeting:

```
1. Build Vault 1 (research brief / methodology)
   - Define the per-company template
   - Document the methodology
   - Validate the framing
   
2. Build first per-company vault (Vault 2.1)
   - Apply the template
   - Validate methodology in practice
   - Refine Vault 1 based on what worked
   
3. Build remaining per-company vaults (Vault 2.2 - 2.27)
   - In batches
   - Cross-link companies that have real relationships
   - Maintain quality-control bar
   
4. Build Vault 3 (strategic outlook)
   - Cross-portfolio analysis
   - Generate infographics
   - Validate framing is constructive
   
5. Publish Vaults 1, 2, and 3 publicly
   - Discovery endpoint surfaces them
   - Public website presents them professionally
   - Distribution: LinkedIn, blog post, possibly direct outreach to 33N before the meeting
   
6. Build Vault 4 (private collaboration vault)
   - Map platform fit per portfolio company
   - Prepare meeting flow
   - Define meeting objectives
   
7. The meeting
   - Walk 33N through the public vaults
   - Demonstrate the platform's primitives in action
   - Discuss collaboration opportunities from Vault 4
   - Establish next steps
```

The public publication step (Step 5) is important. **Publishing the public vaults before the meeting changes the meeting's dynamics meaningfully**. Instead of "let me show you what I built" the conversation becomes "here is the work we have already done in your space; you have probably already seen some of it." Distribution before the meeting positions us as an entity already engaging with their world, not as a vendor approaching cold.

## The Demonstration Value Beyond 33N

Worth being explicit: this programme is valuable beyond the specific 33N meeting. Four reasons:

**First, the methodology vault becomes reusable.** Any future investor research engagement starts from the same meta-vault. We are not redoing work; we are extending a template.

**Second, the per-company vaults are publishable case studies.** Each one is a demonstration of what the platform produces. Anyone evaluating us can see real research output, not pitch deck claims.

**Third, the strategic outlook becomes ecosystem content.** Cybersecurity VC analysis from outside the firm itself is genuinely useful to people in the space. Publishing it builds reputation.

**Fourth, the workflow itself is the platform demonstration.** Anyone interested in our research-and-evidence-pack capability can see a complete end-to-end example, not a contrived demo.

The 33N meeting is the forcing function. The work compounds beyond the meeting.

## Where The Risks Are

Three risks worth flagging explicitly:

**Risk 1: Quality bar must be high.** Publishing research that is sloppy or factually wrong damages credibility disproportionately. Each per-company vault needs careful source-checking; the strategic outlook needs to be defensible. **The agentic workflow does the breadth; human review does the quality control.** This is the same MyFeeds principle from earlier today; the same discipline applies here.

**Risk 2: Constructive framing must be genuine, not sycophantic.** If the public vaults read as flattery, they lose credibility. The framing principle is **honest-but-constructive**: surface what is interesting and coherent; acknowledge what is forward-looking; do not invent praise. **Honest analysis with a positive frame is more credible than uncritical celebration.**

**Risk 3: Public publication has consequences.** Once Vaults 1-3 are public, they are public. If 33N or one of their portfolio companies has a strong negative reaction, retracting cleanly is hard. Mitigation: **review the vaults internally before publication**; consider sending key portfolio companies their own vault privately before public publication, as a courtesy; build in an explicit "corrections welcome" channel so factual issues can be addressed without confrontation.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

Deliberate scope limits:

- **Not a 33N criticism**. Constructive framing throughout; this is ecosystem analysis, not investigative journalism.
- **Not a substitute for 33N's own due diligence**. Our research is from public sources; 33N has privileged access we do not.
- **Not a competitive intelligence product for sale.** This is a free demonstration. Future paid versions of similar work would be commissioned per the MyFeeds model.
- **Not an attempt to recreate 33N's portfolio strategy.** Strategic outlook describes what is visible; it does not prescribe.
- **Not real-time updating in v1.** Vaults are point-in-time as of publication; the update cadence is a v2 question if there is appetite.
