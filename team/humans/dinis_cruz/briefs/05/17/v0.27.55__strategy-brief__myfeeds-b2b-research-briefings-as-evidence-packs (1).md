## What This Is

A strategic repositioning of MyFeeds, the Portuguese / European company the project lead operates. The voice memo names a meaningful shift in the business positioning: **MyFeeds is not primarily a news outlet (though it operates one); it is a B2B research-briefing company that delivers vault-grounded evidence packs to clients on specific topics, with subscription-style ongoing feeds as the recurring revenue model**.

The Portuguese-language journalist work continues; that is the public-facing content layer that creates credibility and visibility. But the business proposition that monetises is different: companies and investors pay MyFeeds to produce structured, evidence-backed research briefings on topics they care about, delivered as vaults that update on a defined cadence. The vault is the deliverable; the evidence pack is the contents; the feed is the recurring delivery mechanism.

This brief names the positioning, the offering, the pricing axis, the technical substrate, the relationship to the broader platform direction, and what needs to be built and decided in the near term.

## The Core Reframe

The voice memo named the structural insight cleanly. **The thing being sold is not "news"; it is the evidence pack as a structured deliverable.** A news article is one expression of an evidence pack. A research briefing is another. An ongoing market intelligence feed is a third. All three are the same shape: a curated set of evidence around a topic, structured semantically, hyperlinked back to source materials, presented through a custom UI.

In the public-facing news context, the evidence pack is published openly and serves the broader information ecosystem. In the B2B research context, the evidence pack is commissioned, customised, and delivered to a paying client. The underlying capability is the same; the commercial model differs.

The reframe matters because:

- **News on its own is a hard business model.** Subscription media is competitive; ad-supported media is exhausting; both are slow to monetise.
- **Research briefings have a known buyer.** Companies, investors, governments, NGOs all pay for high-quality research today, often to traditional firms charging substantial fees.
- **The vault grounding is the differentiator.** Traditional research briefings are PDFs or slide decks; ours are vaults with versioned evidence, semantic linking, and ongoing updates.
- **The two reinforce each other.** The journalism credibility supports the research business; the research revenue funds continued journalism.

## What MyFeeds Actually Sells

The business proposition stated plainly: **MyFeeds creates and maintains data vaults containing semantically-organised evidence packs on topics the client cares about, with optional recurring updates**.

The deliverable in its simplest form:

| Component | What It Contains |
|-----------|------------------|
| **The vault** | The container holding everything; encrypted; versioned; client-owned |
| **The evidence pack** | Curated source materials, organised semantically; all hyperlinked back to originals |
| **The semantic graph** | The structured relationships between entities, claims, sources, time periods |
| **The briefing layer** | Synthesised summaries, key findings, contradiction surfacing |
| **The infographics** | Visualisations generated from the underlying evidence |
| **The custom UI** | Vault app presenting the pack appropriately for the intended audience |
| **The update cadence** | If subscription: daily / weekly / monthly refresh with new evidence and updated synthesis |

The voice memo flagged this point explicitly: the data is captured in a vault with clean folder structures, but it is also **presented in a multi-audience style** through the vault app. The same evidence pack can render as an executive summary for a board, as a detailed analyst view for a research team, as a public-facing article for journalists. **One pack; multiple presentations; same vault.**

## The "How Much Money Do You Want To Spend" Pricing Axis

The voice memo flagged something specific about the pricing model that is worth taking seriously: **research depth scales naturally with budget**. The cost of going from "good research" to "exceptionally thorough research" is largely the cost of running more agentic workflows, more validation passes, more source coverage, more cross-checking.

This means the pricing model has a natural tiered shape:

| Tier | Investment | What It Covers |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| **Starter brief** | £2,000-5,000 one-off | Single-topic evidence pack; one round of research; clean vault; basic UI |
| **Standard brief** | £5,000-15,000 one-off | As above plus multi-source validation, broader coverage, custom UI, structured semantic graph |
| **Deep brief** | £15,000-50,000 one-off | As above plus contradiction surfacing across sources, expert review pass, infographic suite, multi-audience UI |
| **Ongoing feed** | £1,500-10,000/month | Recurring evidence-pack updates on the same topic; cadence configurable; growing semantic graph over time |
| **Enterprise programme** | £50,000+/year | Multiple topics, dedicated support, custom workflows, on-platform deployment, partner-grade SLAs |

Numbers are indicative. The interesting property is that **each tier maps to a real cost shape on our side**. Starter brief: a few hours of agent runtime, a few dozen sources. Deep brief: extensive cross-source validation, multiple LLM passes, human review time. The pricing aligns with the work.

This is the kind of pricing that customers in this market understand. Big-name research firms (Gartner, Forrester, Bloomberg Intelligence) charge in these ranges for comparable work. We can compete on quality (vault-grounded with traceable evidence) and on flexibility (custom topics, custom audiences, custom cadence) rather than on price.

## What Makes The Offering Differentiated

Traditional research deliverables are PDFs, slide decks, sometimes static websites. They are point-in-time artefacts. They go stale. They cannot be easily verified or extended.

The MyFeeds vault-grounded evidence pack offers something structurally different:

| Property | Traditional Research | Vault-Grounded Evidence Pack |
|----------|----------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Source verifiability** | "Sources available on request" | Every claim hyperlinked to source in the vault |
| **Update mechanism** | Buy a new report | Vault updates; commit history shows what changed |
| **Audience adaptation** | One presentation per report | Same vault renders for executive / analyst / public |
| **Semantic structure** | Implicit in prose | Explicit semantic graph; queryable |
| **Cross-reference** | Manual | Vault-native; clicking an entity surfaces all mentions |
| **Ownership** | Vendor-controlled access | Client-owned vault (with appropriate licensing) |
| **Audit trail** | "Trust us" | Vault commit history is the audit trail |
| **Extension** | Commission new work | Client can extend the vault themselves if desired |
| **Privacy** | Often vendor-hosted | Zero-knowledge if desired |
| **Cost shape** | Fixed price per report | Configurable depth = configurable cost |

The combination is genuinely new in the research market. **Traditional research firms produce reports; MyFeeds produces evidence packs that can be reasoned over by humans and machines and updated over time.**

## The Information Overload Frame

A nuance worth being explicit about. The voice memo named something genuinely important: **we are at the point of information overload where agents can do a lot of work, but agent output alone is not trustworthy enough for high-stakes decisions**. Companies cannot just ask GPT-5 to research a market and act on the answer. They need:

- Evidence that can be checked
- Sources that can be verified
- Contradictions that have been surfaced and addressed
- Human judgement in the loop, not as decoration but as quality control
- A structured artefact that holds up to scrutiny

This is the gap MyFeeds occupies. **Agentic capability for breadth and speed; human judgement for quality control; vault structure for trustworthiness; semantic graph for tractability.** The combination is what makes the deliverable usable for actual decision-making, not just for impressing executives.

The "how much money do you want to spend" axis maps cleanly to this trust gradient. More budget means more validation passes, more human review, more cross-source verification, each layer reducing the trust gap further. **The customer is paying for trust, not just for content.**

## The Workflow

Concretely, what MyFeeds does to deliver an evidence pack:

```
Client commissions: topic + scope + budget + audience + cadence
    ↓
Brief is structured: research questions, source-set boundaries, depth tier
    ↓
Agentic team activates:
  - Source discovery (perplexity, web search, specialist databases)
  - Document ingestion (PDFs, articles, transcripts, datasets)
  - Entity extraction (people, organisations, events, claims)
  - Semantic graph construction
  - Cross-source validation
  - Contradiction surfacing
    ↓
Human review layer:
  - Quality control on agent output
  - Editorial decisions
  - Sensitivity / accuracy checks
  - Customisation per audience
    ↓
Synthesis and presentation:
  - Briefing layer (executive summary, key findings)
  - Infographics generated from the semantic graph
  - Custom UI for the vault app
  - Multi-audience rendering
    ↓
Delivery:
  - Vault handed over to client (or accessed via subscription)
  - Briefing call walks through findings
  - Cadence configured if ongoing feed
    ↓
For ongoing feeds:
  - Periodic refresh on the configured cadence
  - Vault commits show what is new
  - Notification of material changes
  - Quarterly review of brief and topic boundaries
```

The agentic team and human review layer is genuinely novel. **Most current "AI research" products skip the human layer entirely; most traditional research firms skip the agentic layer entirely.** MyFeeds combines them, with the vault providing the structured handover between layers.

This is where the broader platform direction connects directly. MyFeeds runs on the platform. Specifically:

| Capability | How MyFeeds Uses It |
|------------|---------------------|
| **Vaults** | Every evidence pack is a vault; every client gets their own |
| **Vault apps** | The custom UI per pack is a vault app |
| **Infographic generator** | Visualisations within packs |

| **Multi-cloud** | Clients in regulated industries can host their packs on their preferred infrastructure |
| **Backup / recovery** | Mission-critical research artefacts get proper backup posture |
| **Vault discovery** | Client packs are private; public outputs (journalism layer) are discoverable |
| **AppSec mini-tools** | The vulnerability-research topic category uses the AppSec direction directly |
| **Agentic workflows** | The research team is genuinely agentic; orchestrated through the vault |

This is the kind of company that the de-commoditising article from earlier today described: **the platform provides the substrate; MyFeeds builds the workflow that absorbs the attrition (research orchestration, quality control, multi-audience presentation, recurring delivery); the customer pays for the absorbed-attrition experience, not the substrate**.

MyFeeds is also a useful **internal proof point** for the platform. Every capability MyFeeds needs gets stress-tested by a real revenue-generating business. Bugs we would never have found in our own dogfooding get surfaced. Improvements we would never have prioritised become urgent.

## The Two-Track Public-Facing Story

The voice memo flagged something nuanced: MyFeeds maintains the journalist website, the public articles, the Portuguese-language news outlet. **These continue.** The new B2B positioning does not replace them; it adds a commercial layer alongside them.

The relationship is symbiotic:

- The journalism layer creates **credibility and visibility**. Investigative articles in the public sphere demonstrate the kind of research capability that B2B clients are buying.
- The B2B layer creates **revenue** that funds continued journalism. The recurring research feeds are higher margin than ad-supported media and pay for the journalist work.
- Public articles can become **case studies** for the B2B offering. "Here is a public investigation we published; here is what a private commissioned version would have included additionally."
- B2B clients sometimes want **public exposure** of certain findings. Carefully managed crossover (with client consent) creates news pegs that drive journalism traffic.

This dual-track structure is similar to how the Economist Intelligence Unit relates to The Economist, or how Bloomberg Terminal relates to Bloomberg News. The public-facing brand creates trust; the B2B product captures the value.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

Deliberate scope limits:

- **Not a replacement for the journalism layer.** That continues; this brief adds a commercial layer alongside.
- **Not a Gartner / Forrester competitor at scale.** Early focus is on niche topics where vault-grounded delivery differentiates.
- **Not an AI-research-as-a-service consumer product.** The B2B offering has human review and custom workflow; this is not a self-serve LLM tool.
- **Not free.** The pricing model is the pricing model; price discovery comes from real client conversations, not desk-thinking.
- **Not exclusive to one platform.** MyFeeds runs on our platform because it is the natural fit, but the business model would work on any sufficiently capable infrastructure. The platform leverage is real but not exclusive.

## Honest Risks

Three risks worth flagging:

**Risk 1: B2B research is sales-heavy.** Even with a strong offering, B2B sales cycles are long. The project lead cannot personally do all the sales motion for very long. Mitigation: start with warm contacts (existing network); validate the offering with 2-3 paying clients before trying to scale; consider a sales-oriented hire as priority once the offering is proven.

**Risk 2: Quality is everything in this category, and quality is hard.** A single inaccurate or sloppy briefing damages reputation disproportionately. Mitigation: the human review layer is not optional; tiered pricing reflects the time required; we do not accept work below the minimum quality threshold even if the customer requests it.

**Risk 3: The dual-track structure (journalism + B2B) requires careful separation of concerns.** Conflicts of interest, source confidentiality, and editorial independence all need formal policies. Mitigation: explicit policies documented and adhered to; no client work that compromises journalism independence; legal review of the structure early.
