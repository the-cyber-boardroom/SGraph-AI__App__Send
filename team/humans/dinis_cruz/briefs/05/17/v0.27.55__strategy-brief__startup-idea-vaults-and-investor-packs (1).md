## What This Is

Three concepts that travel together as a single direction:

1. **The investor pack as a vault product**: a polished, structured, investor-facing workflow for showing companies, built on our vault and app primitives
2. **The startup-idea vault**: a Creative Commons-licensed, vault-seeded business template that someone could actually pick up and build, demonstrating what a vault-powered company would look like
3. **Six to seven seed companies**: concrete startup ideas to seed as vaults: CV/jobs, pitch creation, health score, evidence-research, accountant, lawyer, local-SME Gen AI enablement

The voice memo named the structural insight cleanly: **these are not fake companies; they are "potential companies" or "startup ideas": fully-formed business descriptions that someone could pick up under Creative Commons licensing and actually build, with the explicit framing that the platform makes the build dramatically easier**.

This is a meaningfully new direction. It serves three audiences simultaneously: investors (who get a polished walk-through experience), would-be founders (who get open-source business templates), and us (who get demonstrable proof of what the platform enables). The artefact is the same in each case; the audience reads it differently.

## The Three-Layer Framing

The voice memo named three things explicitly, and the brief preserves the layering:

| Layer | What It Is | Audience |
|-------|------------|----------|
| **The investor pack vault format** | A workflow / template for presenting any company to an investor: business model, market, product, team, financials, ask | Investors reviewing real or potential companies |
| **The startup-idea vault** | A specific vault populated with a Creative Commons business plan for a company that could exist | Would-be founders who could build the company; investors who could fund someone building it |
| **The platform** | The substrate that makes building each startup-idea company tractable | All of the above, plus us (it demonstrates the platform's value) |

Each layer reuses the layer below it. The investor pack is a vault template usable for any company. A startup-idea vault uses the investor pack template to present its business. The platform is what runs it all.

The layering means the work is composable: the investor pack template ships once and serves all companies; each startup-idea vault is content built against the template; the platform substrate just hosts.

## The Startup-Idea Vault: What It Actually Is

The novel concept is worth being explicit about. A startup-idea vault is not a real company. It is also not a fake company. It is something in between:

- **A documented business idea**, complete with market analysis, product description, pricing model, operating model, agent design, technology stack, and investment ask
- **Released under Creative Commons** so anyone can pick it up and build it
- **Designed to be platform-leveraged**: the business model assumes our vault and compute primitives, which is what makes it economically viable at small scale
- **Investor-ready in format**: someone reviewing it could decide to fund a founder to build it
- **Open about its status**: explicitly described as a startup idea, not a real company; readers know what they are looking at

The voice memo struggled with the right framing for this and landed on "startup idea" or "potential company" rather than "fake company." Both terms work; "startup idea" is the cleaner public framing because it carries no implication of being deceptive.

The genuine novelty is the combination of three properties: **fully-formed**, **Creative Commons-licensed**, and **platform-aware**. Each of these exists individually (business plans are written; CC licensing is normal; platform-aware businesses are increasingly common). The combination is what makes this a distinct artefact.

## Why This Works As A Direction

Several reasons make this more interesting than it might first appear:

**It demonstrates the platform without selling it.** A startup-idea vault is a useful artefact in its own right. Anyone reading it gets a business idea they could build; nobody is trying to sell them on our platform. The platform is mentioned as enabling, not as the point. This is more credible than direct marketing.

**It seeds the ecosystem we want.** If even a fraction of the startup ideas we publish get picked up and built, we have a portfolio of platform-leveraged companies validating the architecture from the outside. Each one becomes a case study.

**It costs us little to produce.** A startup-idea vault is one to three weeks of structured writing per company. Compared to building each one as a real demo, the leverage is enormous.

**It generates compounding content.** Each startup-idea vault is a publishable artefact: a vault someone can browse, a blog post derivative, an infographic, a LinkedIn distribution. Six of them is a content programme.

**It clarifies our own thinking.** Writing a viable business model for a vault-powered company forces us to articulate what the platform enables that other platforms do not. The exercise sharpens our positioning.

**It signals confidence.** Publishing open-source business templates says we believe the platform is enabling enough that giving away the business idea is the right move; the moat is the platform, not the idea.

## The Investor Pack As Vault Template

Underneath the startup-idea concept is a more general piece of work: **the investor pack as a vault template**.

An investor pack vault holds:

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Executive summary** | One-page overview |
| **Problem and market** | Who has the problem; how big is it; how is it solved today |
| **Solution and product** | What the company makes; how it works; what is different |
| **Business model** | How the company makes money; unit economics; pricing |
| **Go-to-market** | How customers are acquired; channels; partnerships |
| **Operating model** | How the company is structured; agents, humans, processes |
| **Technology stack** | What is built; what is bought; the platform-leverage story |
| **Traction or proof** | What evidence exists that this works (or could work) |
| **Team** | Who is building (or who would be needed) |
| **Financials** | Revenue projection; cost projection; runway |
| **Ask** | Investment sought; use of funds; terms |
| **Risks** | Honest risks and mitigations |
| **Appendix** | Supporting material, market data, references |

This is industry-standard investor-pack structure. The vault holds each section as a navigable element. The vault's app surface presents it as a clean walk-through experience; the investor can read linearly or jump to any section.

The vault also enables things that PDF investor decks cannot:

- **Interactive scenario modelling** (the financials section lets the reader change assumptions)
- **Linked supporting evidence** (every market claim references a source the reader can drill into)
- **Per-investor commenting** (an investor can leave notes in their own copy without affecting others)
- **Version history** (when the company updates the pack, investors see what changed)
- **Multi-format export** (PDF for those who want it; vault for those who want the interactive experience)

This is the kind of investor experience that gets remembered. It is also a useful product surface independent of the startup-idea framing; real companies can use the same template.

## The Six Seed Companies

The voice memo named six concrete startup ideas plus one extra. Worth treating each briefly so we know what we are committing to seed.

### Seed 1: CV / Job-Search Company

A platform that helps individuals manage their CV, portfolio, and job search through a vault-grounded workflow. Documents flow through the vault; agents assist with tailoring per application; the visible artefact is a polished CV the candidate controls.

Connects to: the existing CV/portfolio market work from earlier in the project; the job search vault work; the "vault becomes invisible app first" milestone.

The startup idea: a paid SaaS targeting active job-seekers, £10-30/month, with a high-touch tier for senior candidates. Platform leverage means the cost structure can support individual customer pricing.

### Seed 2: Pitch Creation Company

A specialist firm that helps founders create investor pitches. Vault holds the pitch artefacts; agents assist with drafting, critique, market analysis; the firm employs human pitch coaches who use the platform as their workflow substrate.

The platform leverage makes the human consultant 3-5x more productive than they would be working in Google Docs and PowerPoint. The firm sells human + AI service to founders preparing for fundraising.

### Seed 3: Health Score Company

The Private Health Score work documented earlier in the project, framed as a startup. Vault holds the individual's health data; the company provides the analysis layer; the user pays for the service and retains full ownership of their data.

This is the most validated seed already; substantial earlier work exists; the model is articulated; it has been explicitly discussed with partner physicians. The startup-idea vault formalises it for someone to actually build.

### Seed 4: Evidence-Research Company

Focused on the "evidence research-driven world" the voice memo mentioned. A service that helps individuals or small organisations gather and present evidence on specific topics, journalists doing investigations, expert witnesses, researchers, advocacy groups.

The platform leverage is in the workflow tooling (vault as evidence locker; agents for source-checking, citation management, document analysis; cross-document linking). The service can support investigations that would not be economically viable at traditional firm rates.

### Seed 5: Accountant Workflow Company

The vertical positioning from yesterday's brief, as a startup. A specialist accountancy that uses platform-grounded workflows to deliver strategic-partner-level service at SME prices. The voice memo flagged this as something the project lead is already dogfooding (has accountants working for them).

The startup-idea vault formalises the model that yesterday's positioning piece described. Builder takes the position and runs with it.

### Seed 6: Lawyer Workflow Company

The same pattern applied to law. Voice memo flagged this is also being dogfooded (lawyers working for the project lead). A small law firm that uses vault-grounded workflows for client communication, document management, case tracking, evidence handling.

The legal industry has many of the same SME-fragmentation properties as accounting. The pitch is structurally identical: empower existing small firms rather than disrupt with new entrants. Vault primitives (encrypted, audit-trail-for-free, client-controlled) map cleanly to legal privilege requirements.

### Seed 7: Local Gen AI Enablement Company

The voice memo's extra. A company that connects local SMEs with local people who can teach them to use AI tools effectively. Micro-freelance shape: short engagements; per-task pricing; focused on document creation, mini-apps, memory-augmented workflows, safe AI use.

This is interesting because it operates at a different scale from the others; community-rooted, not platform-rooted in the same way. But the platform leverage exists: each freelancer uses the platform to deliver their service; the company orchestrates the marketplace.

## Per-Seed Vault Structure

Each seed company gets its own vault with consistent structure:

```
seed-{name}/
├── pack.md                    # The investor pack (using the template)
├── operating-model.md         # How the company runs day-to-day
├── product.md                 # What the company builds
├── financials/
│   ├── projections.xlsx
│   ├── unit-economics.md
│   └── assumptions.md
├── go-to-market/
│   ├── strategy.md
│   ├── pricing.md
│   └── partnerships.md
├── technology/
│   ├── architecture.md
│   ├── platform-leverage.md   # What our platform enables here
│   └── build-vs-buy.md
├── examples/
│   ├── sample-customer-flow.md
│   ├── sample-output.pdf
│   └── (other example artefacts)
├── license.md                 # CC BY-SA 4.0
└── readme.md                  # Front matter; how to use this vault
```

The vault is browsable. The app presents it as a structured walk-through. The financial spreadsheet is interactive (built using our XLSX skill). The whole thing exists at a vault URL someone can share, comment on, or fork.

The voice memo flagged that this could have a dedicated website. The pitch is: **a clean professional site that presents the seed vaults, lets visitors browse them, allows founders to pick up a seed and signal interest in building it, allows investors to express interest in funding someone who builds one**.

Suggested URL: something like `startupideas.sgraph.app` or similar. Each seed gets a landing page; the vault itself is the deep content; the site frames the concept and provides navigation.

This site is itself a vault app. The vaults underneath are the seed-company vaults. Investors get a polished entry experience; founders get full access to the open-source artefacts. The whole thing runs on the platform we are demonstrating, which is intentional dogfooding.

A small but real practice, even just running our own accountant and lawyer interactions through the platform, produces a more credible seed vault than one written from theory.

## Honest Risks

Three risks worth flagging:

**Risk 1: The seed vaults look like marketing if not done with genuine investment-quality content.** A startup-idea vault that is obviously thin or hype-driven will reflect poorly. Mitigation: invest real time in the financial models, market analysis, and unit economics. Treat each seed as if we were genuinely pitching it.

**Risk 2: We seed ideas that get built badly by someone who damages the platform's reputation by association.** Creative Commons licensing means we cannot control how someone builds. Mitigation: the public framing makes clear these are templates, not endorsements; we publish links to high-quality builders if any emerge; we do not officially partner with builders without quality review.

**Risk 3: Seeding ideas competes with our own potential product direction.** If we later want to build the accountant company ourselves, having seeded it as open-source is awkward. Mitigation: be deliberate about which seeds we publish; some directions stay internal until we decide our own plans. The seeds we publish are the ones we are confident we will not build ourselves.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

Deliberate scope limits:

- **Not a startup studio.** We are publishing templates, not building or funding the companies.
- **Not a marketplace.** Founders who want to build a seed pick it up themselves; investors interested in funding find their own builders.
- **Not a guarantee of feasibility.** Each seed is one viable interpretation of an opportunity; the actual founder will iterate.
- **Not exclusive to our platform.** A seed vault under CC BY-SA can technically be built without our platform, though the business model assumes our leverage. We are explicit about this.
- **Not a primary revenue stream.** This is positioning and ecosystem-seeding; revenue, if any, comes indirectly through platform adoption.
