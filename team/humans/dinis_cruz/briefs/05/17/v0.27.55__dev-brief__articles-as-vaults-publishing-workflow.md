## What This Is

A concrete proposal for how MyFeeds publishes articles going forward: **each article is itself a vault containing not just the article text but the full body of evidence, the semantic graph, the source materials, the multilingual versions, the agentic workflow provenance, and every prompt and decision that went into the finished piece**. The website that MyFeeds maintains becomes a presentation layer over this vault collection.

The structural insight from the voice memo is the one worth being explicit about: **the publishing workflow we use for our own articles is the same workflow we sell to B2B research-briefing clients (per yesterday's MyFeeds repositioning brief)**. Our articles become the canonical examples of the evidence-pack-as-vault deliverable. Every article we publish demonstrates the technology to anyone reading it, without any sales pitch attached.

This brief covers the article-as-vault structure, the publishing workflow, the multilingual treatment, the agentic provenance capture, and what gets built first. A complementary brief on the MyFeeds website architecture itself will follow separately (the voice memo flagged this would be its own memo).

## The Core Idea: An Article Is A Vault

Today, an article exists as a markdown document, gets cross-posted to LinkedIn and other platforms, links back to the website. Source materials, supporting data, and provenance live elsewhere (or not at all).

The proposed shift: **each article is a vault**. The vault contains:

| Component | What It Is |
|-----------|------------|
| **The article itself** | Markdown source; rendered HTML; versioned via vault commits |
| **The evidence pack** | Sources cited, with full local copies where licensing permits; otherwise authoritative links |
| **The semantic graph** | Entities, claims, sources, relationships, structured for navigation |
| **The infographics** | Generated visualisations from the semantic graph |
| **Multilingual versions** | English, Portuguese (and others as relevant), each version itself versioned |
| **Author metadata** | Who wrote it, when, what their declared interests are |
| **Agentic provenance** | Every prompt, every model call, every cost, every decision in the production chain |
| **Original source materials** | The voice memo (if applicable), drafts, edits, review notes |
| **Comments and reviews** | From human reviewers and from review agents |
| **Publication metadata** | When published, where syndicated, what licence applies |

Every article becomes a self-contained, browsable artefact. Anyone with the link can read the article in their preferred presentation, follow any claim to its source, see how the article was constructed, view it in multiple languages, and verify that the work was done as claimed.

## Why This Matters Beyond The Article Itself

Four properties make this more than just better article structure:

**First, it dogfoods the MyFeeds B2B offering completely.** The repositioning brief from yesterday said MyFeeds sells vault-delivered evidence packs to B2B research clients. If our own articles are not delivered this way, the value proposition is theoretical. If they are, every article is also a sales asset.

**Second, it makes the article trustable.** A claim with a one-click path to its source is a claim that resists casual dismissal. The vault structure means trust is not an act of faith; it is something the reader can verify.

**Third, it makes the article extensible.** A reader (or a researcher, or another journalist) can fork the vault, add their own analysis, contribute back if appropriate. The article becomes a starting point for further work, not a closed artefact.

**Fourth, it captures the actual work.** The agentic provenance, the prompts, the costs, the model choices, the human review steps: all of this would otherwise be lost. Capturing it means we can learn from our own production process, refine it, and demonstrate it.

## The Article Vault Structure

Concretely, each article gets a vault with a consistent structure:

```
article-{slug}/
├── article/
│   ├── en/
│   │   ├── article.md              # The English article
│   │   ├── article.html            # Rendered HTML
│   │   └── article.pdf             # Exportable PDF
│   ├── pt/
│   │   └── (same structure for Portuguese)
│   └── (other languages as relevant)
├── evidence/
│   ├── sources/                    # Local copies of source materials where permitted
│   │   ├── source-001.pdf
│   │   ├── source-002.html
│   │   └── ...
│   ├── citations.json              # Structured citation data
│   └── claim-source-mapping.json   # Which claim maps to which source
├── semantic-graph/
│   ├── entities.json               # People, organisations, events, concepts
│   ├── relationships.json          # How entities relate
│   └── timeline.json               # Time-anchored events
├── infographics/
│   ├── overview.svg                # Top-level summary visual
│   ├── timeline.svg                # Visual timeline
│   └── (other generated visuals)
├── author/
│   ├── profile.md                  # Author info
│   ├── declarations.json           # Conflicts of interest, funding sources
│   └── methodology.md              # How this piece was produced
├── provenance/
│   ├── source-memo.md              # Original voice memo or notes (if applicable)
│   ├── prompts/                    # Every prompt used in production
│   │   ├── 001-initial-draft.md
│   │   ├── 002-translation-pt.md
│   │   └── ...
│   ├── llm-calls.jsonl             # Every model call with cost and metadata
│   ├── decisions.md                # Human decisions made during production
│   └── cost.json                   # Total cost broken down by stage
├── reviews/
│   ├── agent-reviews/              # Critiques from review agents
│   ├── human-reviews/              # Human reviewer notes
│   └── revision-log.md             # Changes from review
├── publication/
│   ├── timeline.md                 # When published, where syndicated
│   ├── licence.md                  # CC BY 4.0 or whatever
│   └── distribution.json           # Where the article has been shared
└── readme.md                       # Front matter for the vault
```

This structure is intentional. Each top-level folder serves a different reader: `article/` for the casual reader, `evidence/` for the verifying reader, `semantic-graph/` for the machine reader, `provenance/` for the methodology-curious reader. The vault holds the union; the presentation layer (website, syndication, sharing) projects appropriately for the channel.

## The Publishing Workflow

The voice memo named the right pattern: an editorial workflow with editor and publisher roles, similar to the library pattern we already have. The publishing workflow has six stages:

```
1. Source intake
   - Voice memo (if applicable)
   - Initial notes or draft
   - Identified topic and scope
   ↓
2. Article drafting
   - Agentic first-pass draft from voice memo or notes
   - Human revision and shaping
   - Source identification
   ↓
3. Evidence collection
   - Sources gathered (perplexity, web search, specialist databases)
   - Claims mapped to sources
   - Semantic graph constructed
   - Contradictions surfaced
   ↓
4. Review
   - Agent reviewers (multiple models, multiple perspectives)
   - Human review pass
   - Revision based on feedback
   ↓
5. Multilingual rendering
   - Translation to other relevant languages
   - Cultural adaptation per locale
   - Per-language review
   ↓
6. Publication
   - Vault sealed (current version published)
   - Distribution to syndication channels
   - Public URL activated
   - Notification mechanisms triggered
```

Each stage commits to the vault. The commit history is itself the editorial log. Every step is reproducible because the prompts, models, and decisions are captured in the provenance folder.

This is not "use AI to write articles." It is **a structured editorial workflow where AI capabilities are integrated as steps the editor and human reviewer control**. The article still represents the author's judgement; the AI accelerates the work that the author would otherwise do by hand.

## The Agentic Provenance Capture

The voice memo flagged this explicitly: capture every prompt, every request, every model call, every decision, from the original voice memo all the way to the finished article. **Provenance is not a nice-to-have; it is part of the deliverable.**

What gets captured:

| Element | Where It Lives |
|---------|----------------|
| Original voice memo audio | `provenance/source-memo.md` references; audio in separate evidence pack if appropriate |
| Voice memo transcript | `provenance/source-memo.md` |
| First-pass draft prompt and output | `provenance/prompts/001-*.md` and `provenance/llm-calls.jsonl` |
| Each subsequent prompt | `provenance/prompts/00N-*.md` |
| Model choices and rationale | `provenance/decisions.md` |
| Cost per call | `provenance/llm-calls.jsonl` |
| Total cost | `provenance/cost.json` |
| Human revisions | `provenance/decisions.md` plus vault commit history |
| Translation decisions | Per-language prompt files |
| Review agent prompts and outputs | `reviews/agent-reviews/` |
| Human reviewer notes | `reviews/human-reviews/` |
| Time spent at each stage | Inferred from vault commit timestamps |

This makes the article reproducible. Anyone can see exactly how it was made. **For the B2B research-briefing offering, this level of provenance is part of what justifies the price.** For our own articles, it serves as the demonstration.

It also serves as **internal learning data**. We can analyse our own production patterns: which prompts produced good first drafts? Which models did better translation? Where did human revision add the most value? Where did review agents catch issues? Over time, the workflow refines itself based on the captured data.

## The Multilingual Treatment

For MyFeeds specifically, Portuguese and English are the primary languages. The voice memo flagged this as important: the article on next-generation accountants should exist in both, and the publication strategy assumes both languages from day one.

Each language version is a first-class artefact, not an afterthought translation. Specifically:

- **Translation is agentic plus human-reviewed.** First-pass translation by LLM (Nova, Claude, or a translation-specialist model); human review for nuance, terminology, idiom.
- **Cultural adaptation matters.** A Portuguese-speaking accountant has a different regulatory context than a UK accountant; the Portuguese version reflects this where relevant.
- **Per-language semantic graph entries.** Entity names in their local form; localised source citations where local sources exist.
- **Independent commit history per language.** A correction to the English version does not automatically propagate; each language has its own editorial responsibility.

The vault structure supports this cleanly: each language is its own folder under `article/`, with independent files, history, and review trail.

A practical consequence: **publication can be staggered**. The English version might publish on day one; the Portuguese version on day three after the translation review. The vault reflects the staggered timeline; the website shows what is available in each language.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

Deliberate scope limits:

- **Not a CMS replacement for general content.** The vault structure is for our own articles and for the B2B research-briefing deliverable; not a general-purpose publishing tool for arbitrary content.
- **Not real-time collaborative editing.** Editorial workflow is sequential; multiple editors do not work on the same draft simultaneously.
- **Not automated publication.** Every article still goes through a human publication decision; the workflow accelerates production but does not remove the gate.
- **Not a translation service.** Translation is one stage in our workflow, not a product we sell.
- **Not multi-author for v1.** Single-author articles; multi-author becomes interesting later.

## Honest Risks

Three risks worth flagging:

**Risk 1: Provenance capture adds friction that slows publication.** If capturing every prompt becomes a chore, the pattern gets shortcut. Mitigation: tooling that captures provenance automatically as a side effect of the workflow, not as a separate step. The capture should be invisible to the editor in normal use.

**Risk 2: Multilingual review depends on people we may not have.** Portuguese translation review requires native or near-native fluency. Mitigation: identify a small set of reviewers (the project lead is one; recruit one or two others); accept that some articles may be English-only initially.

**Risk 3: The structure becomes elaborate enough to slow article output.** Article output matters; if the vault structure means we publish less, the structure has failed. Mitigation: start with a minimum viable structure (article in two languages; basic evidence pack; light provenance); add layers as the workflow matures.

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Vault format: separate vault per article, or one vault holding all articles? | Separate per article for clean ownership and lifecycle; an index vault links them |
| Provenance schema: bespoke or based on an existing standard (Dublin Core, schema.org)? | Probably a hybrid; use schema.org concepts where they fit; extend where they do not |
| Translation quality target: human-equivalent, or "good enough with disclaimer"? | Human-equivalent for published work; disclaimer for any machine-only drafts |
| Where does the audio voice memo live in the vault? | In `provenance/audio/` if the speaker consents; transcription only if not |
| Should the public website show the provenance layer? | Yes, but as an opt-in drill-down; default presentation is the article and evidence |
| How long do we retain old article vaults? | Indefinitely; vaults are cheap; older articles are part of the body of work |
| What is the licence for the article vaults? | CC BY 4.0 for the article and most contents; original source materials retain their own licences |
| Can readers fork an article vault? | Yes; the licence permits it; we encourage derivative work |
| Cost per article: target budget? | Probably £5-15 in LLM and infrastructure cost for a typical article |
| When does the article actually publish (vault commit vs URL activation)? | Two-step: vault commit is "draft"; explicit URL activation is "publish" |
