## The Shift That Has Just Happened

Three years ago, a doctor who wanted to build a software application around their methodology had three options:

1. Hire a developer (slow, expensive, communication-heavy)
2. Contract a consultancy (expensive, full of misunderstandings)
3. Buy an off-the-shelf SaaS that did 60 percent of what they needed (compromise)

In 2026, the same doctor opens Lovable. They describe what they want. They iterate with the AI. They get a working PoC. They send the developer (us) the JSX files. They create a GitHub repo. They are doing real software engineering, mediated by an LLM.

The doctor we are working with right now did exactly this. They are not the exception. They are the prototype.

## Who This 2026 Entrepreneur Is

The pattern repeats across domains:

| Domain | The Entrepreneur | Their Methodology |
|--------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Healthcare** | A specialist clinician with a scoring framework | Validated assessment criteria for their body of work |
| **Law** | A practising lawyer with a compliance checklist | Jurisdiction-specific rules they have memorised |
| **Investment** | An angel or analyst with an evaluation framework | Their scoring rubric for evaluating startups |
| **Security consulting** | An AppSec specialist with an assessment standard | Threat models they have developed |
| **Education** | A teacher or trainer with a curriculum | Learning paths they have honed |
| **Sports / fitness** | A coach with a performance model | Training plans for specific outcomes |
| **Veterinary** | A vet with breed-specific wellness criteria | Specialised methodology |

In every case: the entrepreneur is the domain expert, has the methodology, owns the customer relationship, and now (because of AI dev tools) can prototype the application themselves.

The constraint that used to gate them (access to engineers) has dissolved. The new constraint is shipping to production responsibly. That is exactly what we solve.

## Why This Is Our Core Opportunity

Three reasons make this segment uniquely well-suited to us:

### 1. Our Tech Stack Is the Right Fit

Their applications share a common shape:

- They are forms or assessments that capture user input
- The input is sensitive (medical, financial, legal, personal)
- The output is analysis or guidance based on the entrepreneur's methodology
- The data should be controlled by the user, not by the entrepreneur
- The methodology should be controlled by the entrepreneur, not by us
- Multiple parties (user, entrepreneur, advisor, regulator) may need scoped access

Every one of these requirements maps directly to a vault primitive. Encrypted storage, version control, selective sharing, audit trail, multi-party access: this is what vaults do. We are not adapting our platform to fit them; their applications fit our platform natively.

### 2. We Solve Massive Problems They Already Have

The 2026 entrepreneur has problems they did not have to think about until recently. They have built a real product. Now they are responsible for:

- **Customer data security.** They now hold (potentially) sensitive customer data. They are not equipped to manage encryption, key rotation, access logs, breach response.
- **Compliance.** HIPAA, GDPR, SOC2: rules they had no exposure to before, now relevant.
- **Customer trust.** Their customers ask "where is my data stored?" and they need an answer better than "in our database."
- **Auditable workflow.** Who saw what when. Real customers will ask.
- **Multi-party workflows.** The doctor needs the patient AND the assistant AND the lab. The investor needs themselves AND the analyst AND the founder.

Every one of these is something a vault solves elegantly. The entrepreneur shipping with us avoids ever having to think about most of these problems.

### 3. Our Open Model Aligns Incentives

The 2026 entrepreneur is not going to lock themselves into a closed platform run by a startup they do not know.

Our open source stance reads as a feature to them:

- They can audit the platform
- They can see the cryptography and the trust boundaries
- They can self-host if they grow large enough to want to
- They can take their data with them at any time
- They can switch to a competitor (or fork us) if we fail them

This is not a vulnerability. It is the property that makes them comfortable building their entire business on our platform.

And it sets up the commercial relationship correctly: they pay us for what is genuinely valuable (the relationship, the customisation, the forward-compat guarantee, the deployment workflow), not for lock-in.

The economic alignment is direct:

```

    |

    |
```

Our incentive is to make every partner massively successful. Their success is our revenue. The platform compounds with each engagement: more templates, more case studies, more documentation, more agent integrations, more deployment patterns.

The platform layer was not ready. Lovable existed; AWS existed; the entrepreneurs existed; but:

- Vaults that handle the encryption, sharing, version control, and rendering: just shipped

- The deployment patterns for the smallest viable architecture: defined today

All the components needed for the 2026 entrepreneur to ship a real product have come together in the last weeks. We are now positioned to be the platform of choice for this segment.

## The Partner List We Should Be Building

Not yet customers, but the right targets to seek out:

- **Domain experts who have shipped a Lovable PoC.** These are the highest signal. They have vibe coded; they understand what they have built; they will hit the deployment wall imminently.
- **Vibe-coding influencers and educators.** The teachers of this generation; they will direct their audience to platforms that work.
- **Investors who have built their own evaluation Lovable apps.** They become both customers (for their own work) and evangelists (to their portfolio).
- **Consultancies repositioning around AI dev tools.** Some traditional consultancies are pivoting; they need a platform their newly vibe-coding clients can adopt.
- **Healthcare, legal, and education professionals running independent practices.** High pain on data control; high autonomy to choose tools.

Each new partner becomes a case study, a template, a domain validation, and a revenue source. The compounding is real.

## How We Sound Different

The pitch to the 2026 entrepreneur is unlike a typical platform pitch:

- We are not selling them code. They have AI for that.
- We are not selling them a SaaS. They want to own their stack.
- We are not selling them a database. The vault subsumes it.
- We are not selling them an auth system. The vault is the auth.
- We are not selling them a hosting plan (only). Many alternatives exist.

We are selling them:

- **The hard infrastructure they cannot vibe code themselves** (vaults, runtime, deployment patterns)
- **The shortcut from PoC to production** (the deployment workflow they will hit imminently)
- **The customer data story they can tell with confidence** (zero-knowledge encryption they did not have to build)
- **The relationship that makes the next twelve months smoother** (forward-compat, customisation, support)

The pitch ends with: "you keep ownership of your customers, your data, your methodology, and your code; we provide the rails."
