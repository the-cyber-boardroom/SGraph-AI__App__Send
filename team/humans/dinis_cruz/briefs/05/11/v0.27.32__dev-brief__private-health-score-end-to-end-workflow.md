## The Target Flow

The complete end-to-end Private Health Score workflow, captured in one paragraph so the rest of this brief has a fixed reference:

A doctor (or their assistant) wants to assess a patient. They use a template vault containing the assessment framework. An LLM agent clones the template, populates the patient-specific scaffolding, and produces a fresh vault. The vault is shared with the patient via a one-time-use share token, delivered as an email link. The patient clicks the link, sees a polished application (not a "vault"), fills in the assessment across several screens, sees their results and explainability, and saves. The doctor sees the populated vault in real time, reviews, optionally adds guidance, and sends a message back. The patient returns to see the doctor's feedback. End of flow.

That is what we are building. The rest of this brief defines the components.

## The Players

| Player | Role | Tools They Use |
|--------|------|---------------|
| **Patient (end user)** | Fills in assessment, sees results, receives doctor guidance | A web browser, an email client. Nothing else. |
| **Doctor (specialist)** | Defines the assessment framework, originates the patient vault, reviews results | LLM agent (Claude, Lovable, etc.), email client, the vault platform |
| **Doctor's Assistant** | Operates the workflow on the doctor's behalf at scale | Same tools as the doctor |
| **The Platform (us)** | Provides vaults, runtime, template cloning, share tokens, hosting | Existing SG/Send infrastructure |
| **LLM Agent** | Clones templates, populates patient vaults, optionally drafts the share email | Claude or equivalent, given access to the template vault and instructions |

The patient is intentionally given the lightest possible setup. A browser and email is the entire toolkit. No accounts to create, no vault keys to manage, no software to install.

## Vault Origination: Doctor Creates, Not Patient

The original model was "patient goes to website, fills in form, creates vault." That model has problems:

- The patient has to learn what a vault is and what a key is before they can do anything
- Patient-side vault creation means the patient holds the key from the start, which complicates onboarding
- Different patients arrive in different states of digital fluency; bootstrapping them all into vault literacy is friction

The revised model: **the doctor (or their assistant) creates the patient's vault.** The patient receives a link to an already-created vault, pre-populated with the assessment scaffolding. The patient never sees the vault creation flow.

Why this works:

- The doctor is the technical sophisticate (often a "vibe coding" entrepreneur using Lovable, Cursor, Claude); the platform empowers them
- The patient is the consumer; they need the simplest possible path in
- One-time-use share tokens (already supported in SG/Send) give patients access without long-lived credentials
- The patient still owns and controls the vault once they engage with it; they just did not create it

## One-Time Share Tokens (Already Supported)

The recent SG/Send feature for one-time-use share passwords solves the security model elegantly. The flow:

```
Doctor's agent creates a vault, pre-populates assessment
    |
    v
Agent generates a one-time-use share token
    |
    v
Token is sent to patient via email (insecure channel is fine)
    |
    v
Patient clicks the link, enters the vault for the first time
    |
    v
Token is consumed; cannot be used again
    |
    v
Patient owns the vault from this point forward
```

Why this is secure enough for the first phase:

- If an attacker intercepts the email and uses the token first, the real patient cannot use it. The breach is detected (patient reports "link does not work")
- If the real patient uses the token, the attacker is locked out
- The token never persists; there is no long-lived secret to steal
- The token represents only access to one patient's pre-populated assessment, not access to anything else

This avoids the need for PKI in the first phase. PKI is still a future capability (for multi-party workflows where multiple parties sign and verify), but the one-time token model ships now without it.

## Template Vault Cloning

The doctor maintains a template vault. The template contains:

- The assessment framework (the methodology, the scoring rubric, the questions)
- The application code (HTML, JS, CSS) that renders the assessment
- A `_template.json` defining what gets cloned and how
- Branding and styling specific to the doctor's practice

For each new patient, an LLM agent:

1. Clones the template vault to a new patient-specific vault
2. Populates patient-specific scaffolding (patient name, scan type, doctor name, any prior context)
3. Generates a one-time-use share token
4. Composes the email to the patient (or hands the link back to the doctor to send manually)

The clone-and-populate step is itself a vibe-codable workflow. The doctor (or their assistant, or their AI agent) runs a single command or clicks a single button to spin up a new patient engagement.

## Real-Time Vault Updates Visible to the Doctor

Because the patient is writing to the vault in real time, and the doctor can subscribe to the same vault, **the doctor sees the assessment fill in live.** As the patient progresses through the screens, the doctor can watch progress without polling, without a separate dashboard, without webhooks.

This is a side effect of the vault commit model: every change is a commit; subscribers to the vault see commits as they happen. The doctor's UI subscribes to the patient vault and renders the latest state.

This enables:

- The doctor can prepare commentary while the patient is still filling in
- If the patient abandons mid-assessment, the doctor sees how far they got
- Multiple patients can run in parallel; the doctor watches a dashboard of in-progress assessments

## The Email Layer (Intentionally Simple)

Email is the messaging layer. Not Signal, not SMS, not in-app notifications. Email.

Why email:

- Email works. Everyone has it. No new app to install
- The content of the email is just a link; the actual data is inside the encrypted vault
- "Insecure email" is fine because the email contains only the link, not the data
- One of our core principles: reduce components. Email is already deployed everywhere

The platform does not run an email server. The doctor's email client sends the email. The patient's email client receives it. We are not in the message path. We just produce the link.

## "Powered by SG/Vault" Branding

The patient-facing experience should feel like the doctor's own application. The vault is invisible by default.

Minor exception: a small "Powered by SG/Vault" badge on the application (likely in a footer or about page) gives credit to the platform without imposing it on the user experience. The badge can link to a page that explains what is happening under the hood, for curious users.

This badge serves three purposes:

1. Brand awareness for the platform without intrusion
2. Discovery for other specialists who see the badge and ask "what is this?"
3. Trust signal: users curious about how their data is protected can click through and see the cryptographic model

## Empowering the Entrepreneur (The Vibe-Coding Doctor)

The doctor in this engagement is technically sophisticated: they use Lovable, Cursor, Claude. They vibe code. They are an entrepreneur with a methodology, not a passive consumer.

This profile is the right one for this commercial layer:

- They understand what an application is
- They can iterate on the assessment UI themselves
- They know the methodology better than we ever will
- They want to ship fast and own the result

What they need from us:

- Documentation and templates on sgraph.ai showing how to set up an assessment vault from scratch
- Worked examples (the Private Health Score POC itself, the investor pack, the security review)
- The GitHub-as-Vault-Projection workflow so they can use their AI dev tools naturally
- The template cloning capability so they can spin up new patient engagements with one command
- The SG/App runtime as the foundation for their interactive surfaces

This is the package manager pattern (SKILL.md, USAGE.md, customisation per environment) applied to the engagement scaffold.

## Zero Lock-In Principle

This must be a core principle of the offering:

- **The vault content is portable.** A doctor can take their template vault, their patient vaults, their assessment framework with them. We do not hold any of it hostage.
- **The application code is theirs.** They wrote it (or their AI wrote it). They own the HTML/JS in the vault.
- **The platform is open source.** Anyone can audit, fork, self-host if they need to.
- **The patient data never reaches us in clear text.** We could not lock it in even if we wanted to.

We make money from:

- **Usage** (hosting, traffic, storage)
- **Maintenance contracts** (relationship-based, as defined in the commercial brief)
- **Customisation work** (when a partner needs help building a new template)
- **Support** (responsive, expert help)
- **Possibly a percentage** of the partner's own revenue, in agreed cases

Not from lock-in. The partner can leave at any time, taking everything. That is the deal.

## PKI: Deferred but Not Forgotten

The original sketch included PKI (signing the vault key with the doctor's public key so only the doctor can decrypt). The one-time token model means we can ship without PKI in Phase 1.

PKI returns in later phases for:

- **Multi-party workflows.** Patient → Primary Doctor → Specialist → Lab → back to Patient. Each party needs verifiable identity and selective access.
- **Audit and compliance.** Regulated industries require signed records of who accessed what when.
- **Cross-organisation trust.** When the doctor and the patient are not in the same trust domain, PKI provides the bridge.

For Phase 1, one-time tokens are sufficient. PKI is a Phase 2 capability with its own brief when needed.
