**date** 11 May 2026
**from** Human (project lead)

## The Story Behind This Brief

A friend asked for help finding his next role. He has done the AI jump (he uses Lovable, he is comfortable with vibe coding). He has a strong background but a typical career-search problem: the right opportunity exists in his network and the people in his network cannot help him because they do not have a clean way to refer him.

The article published today (`v0.27.32__article__2026-job-search-make-it-easy-to-help-you.md`) lays out the principles he should apply. This brief operationalises those principles into a concrete website he will build, using the new vault capabilities (App Mode, file write, fullscreen rendering).

This is also a deliberate case study for the agentic vault collaboration pattern. The friend will build it. The vault will host it. The agentic dev workflow (his Lovable, our agents, the GitHub-as-Vault-Projection workflow) is the engine. If this works for him, it works for any 2026 entrepreneur.

## Objective

Build a personal website that lives inside an SG/Send vault, rendered via App Mode, hosted as a readonly published vault (see the companion brief on published vaults). The site:

- Demonstrates the principles in the job-search article (multiple audiences, hyperlinked work, public output, don't-make-them-think)
- Lets the friend update content weekly without engineering help
- Shows his network exactly what he is looking for and what he will bring
- Doubles as a case study we (and he) can point others to

## The Audience-Pages Architecture

The article identifies six audiences. The site should have a clear page or section for each, plus a landing surface that routes the visitor.

### Landing Page (Above the Fold)

A single sentence that completes "I am looking for [X role] at [Y kind of company] where I can [Z impact]." Plus a row of clear entry points for the different audiences. No scrolling required to understand the basic ask.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|  [Photo]   I am looking for a Head of AI Engineering     |
|            role at a Series A-C company in fintech       |
|            where I can build the AI org from scratch.   |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|                                                          |
|   [For recruiters]  [For friends who might refer me]    |
|   [For hiring managers]  [For potential peers]          |
|                                                          |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
|   Recent work | Writing | Get in touch                  |
+----------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Six Audience Pages

Each follows the same structural pattern but with content tuned to that audience.

| Page | What It Contains |
|------|------------------|
| **For Recruiters** | Compressed: role, industry, location, salary band, stage. One-paragraph case for fit. Direct contact info. Punchy. |
| **For Friends Who Might Refer Me** | A copy-pasteable "Sara is the person who…" sentence. The friend's framing in their words. Easy to forward. |
| **For Hiring Managers** | Depth: the case for hiring him for the specific kind of role. Evidence, projects, references. |
| **For Future Boss** | How he thinks, how he operates, perspective on leadership and craft. Narrative, not bullets. |
| **For Future Peers** | Technical and domain depth. Demonstrates respect-earning competence. |
| **For Future Direct Reports** | What working for him is like, what he stands for, what would change about their day. |

Each page links to the same underlying evidence (projects, talks, writing, references) but compresses and contextualises differently for that audience.

## The Work Graph

The article emphasised: past experience is a graph, not a list. The site should make this visible.

A `/work` section organised as nodes and edges, navigable by:

- **Role timeline.** Linear list of positions, each with a page.
- **Project deep-dives.** Each significant project as a page with narrative, decisions, outcomes.
- **Domain pages.** Cross-cutting themes (e.g. "AI infrastructure," "security at scale") that reference roles and projects.
- **Talks and writing.** Public output linked back to the projects that inspired them.
- **Open source contributions.** Linked to the projects they came from.

Every role page links to the projects from that role. Every project links to the technologies and domains involved. Every domain links back to the roles where it was applied. Every talk or article links to the work that motivated it.

The reader can follow whichever thread interests them. The hyperlinked graph respects their curiosity and reveals the through-line in his work.

## The Update Loop

Critical property: he updates the site every week without engineering help.

The mechanism:

- The vault is the master content store
- His Lovable (or Claude, or whichever AI dev tool) clones the GitHub-projection of the vault
- He edits content via natural language: "update my landing message to mention fintech"
- His agent makes the change, commits to GitHub, the vault auto-syncs
- He refreshes the published URL and sees the update

He never edits HTML or CSS directly. The AI handles the implementation. He directs.

This is the GitHub-as-Vault-Projection workflow (`v0.27.32__dev-brief__github-as-vault-projection-customer-workflow.md`) applied to his use case. He gets a CLAUDE.md in his repo explaining what to do; his agent reads it; the loop works.

## Show His Work Publicly

The article's strongest move: publish what you are working on while you are working on it.

The site should have a `/now` page (inspired by Derek Sivers' /now pattern, but deeper):

- What he is working on this week
- What he is reading, what he is exploring
- Notes from problems he is solving
- Tools he is evaluating, with his take on each
- Links to public code, public docs, public commentary

The /now page is the highest-signal page on the site for someone deciding whether to refer him. It shows current capability, not historical claims.

This page updates weekly. The same agent loop that updates the audience pages updates /now.

## The "Tell People What You Will Give Them" Pages

Each audience page should include a "What you would get" section that translates his past into future value for that specific audience.

For the hiring manager:

- "In six months you would have a working AI infrastructure team."
- "Your CFO would have line-of-sight cost forecasting for the AI workload."
- "Your security team would have a clear story to tell auditors."

For the potential peer:

- "I would unblock you on production incidents within an hour."
- "I would push back on architecture decisions I disagreed with, with reasoning, not authority."

For the potential direct report:

- "You would have a 1:1 every week, and I would never cancel it."
- "You would have explicit goals reviewed quarterly, not vibes."

These are the sentences someone can paste into an email when they want to refer him.

## Powered by SG/Send Branding

A small "Powered by SG/Send" badge in the footer. Optional but encouraged. It links to a page that explains the technology underneath the site, which serves three purposes:

1. Credit for the platform
2. Discovery for other 2026 entrepreneurs who see the badge and ask "what is this?"
3. Trust signal for the visitor curious about how this works

## Vault Structure

The vault structure mirrors the site structure for clarity:

```
job-search-vault/
    |
    +--> _page.json                   (vault navigation, App Mode config)
    +--> app.js                       (entry point; SPA router)
    +--> styles.css                   (site styling)
    |
    +--> content/
    |       +--> landing/
    |       |       +--> headline.md
    |       |       +--> tagline.md
    |       +--> audiences/
    |       |       +--> recruiters.md
    |       |       +--> referrers.md
    |       |       +--> hiring-managers.md
    |       |       +--> future-boss.md
    |       |       +--> peers.md
    |       |       +--> reports.md
    |       +--> work/
    |       |       +--> timeline.json
    |       |       +--> projects/
    |       |       +--> domains/
    |       |       +--> talks/
    |       |       +--> writing/
    |       +--> now/
    |               +--> 2026-05-week-2.md
    |               +--> 2026-05-week-3.md
    |               +--> ...
    |
    +--> images/
    +--> assets/
```

Content lives in markdown and JSON. The `app.js` reads these files via the vault read API and renders them as pages. Updates to content are updates to vault files; the rendered site reflects them immediately.

## Why This Is a Worked Example

This use case shows three things at once:

### 1. The Vault App Mode in Production

Until now, App Mode has been demonstrated on the Private Health Score POC. The job-search site is a second domain, with different content, different audiences, different update patterns. If the pattern works for both, it is general.

### 2. The Agent Collaboration Workflow

His Lovable updates his vault content. The published URL serves the result. The communication vault (from the LLM Agent Communication Protocols brief) is how we coordinate with his agent. The whole loop runs on infrastructure we have just built.

### 3. The 2026 Entrepreneur Pattern Applied to a Non-Commercial Case

The 2026 entrepreneur framing (strategy brief from earlier today) was about commercial deployments: doctors, investors, lawyers building paid products. This use case proves the same pattern works for personal projects: the same infrastructure helps an individual find a job. The platform serves both.

When we write the case study, the angle is: "we used our own platform to help a friend find work. Here is what we built. Here is how anyone can do the same." That is a powerful piece of content for sgraph.ai.

## What the Agent (Lovable / Claude) Should Do First

1. Read this brief
2. Read the companion article (`v0.27.32__article__2026-job-search-make-it-easy-to-help-you.md`)
3. Read the GitHub-as-Vault-Projection brief
4. Ask the friend three questions:
   - What role are you looking for? (one sentence)
   - What industry/sector/stage of company?
   - What is the most important thing you bring to a team?

6. Scaffold the six audience pages with placeholder content tuned to each

8. Commit to the friend's GitHub repo

The first version should ship in under an hour. Iteration happens from there.
