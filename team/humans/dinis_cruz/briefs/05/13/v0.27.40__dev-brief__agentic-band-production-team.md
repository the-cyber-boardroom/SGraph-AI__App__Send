## What This Is

The agentic production team that creates music for the band demo vault. Same pattern as the newsroom layout from 12 May (departments, visible workflow, provenance-as-product), applied to music creation. A simpler version of that brief, because the team is smaller and the artefacts are different, but the same underlying structure.

The team is staffed primarily by agents, with optional human roles in key creative positions. Every step is auditable, every decision documented, every cost tracked.

## The Roles in the Band Production Team

| Role | What They Do | Default Implementation |
|------|--------------|------------------------|
| **Artistic Director** | Sets the creative direction for each song; picks the source material and target style | Claude (or human director) |
| **Composer** | Drafts the arrangement: which classical pieces, which sections, what fusion style | Claude |
| **Writer** | If lyrics or vocal lines are involved, drafts them; provides song titles and concept blurbs | Claude |
| **Producer** | Translates the composition into prompts for the music generation model | Claude |
| **Musician(s)** | Generates the actual audio | Suno, Udio, or current best-in-class generative music model |
| **Visual Director** | Conceives the visual treatment: cover art, atmosphere, video direction | Claude |
| **Visual Artist** | Generates the images | GPT-Image-2, Midjourney, or current best-in-class image model |
| **Video Artist** | Generates clips or full music videos when appropriate | Sora, Veo, or current best-in-class video model |
| **Critic / Reviewer** | Listens to the output, provides feedback, identifies what to iterate | Claude (different instance, different prompt) |
| **Editor** | Decides what is good enough to publish; signs off | Claude (or human, depending on stakes) |
| **Archivist** | Captures prompts, parameters, iterations, decisions into the vault | Automated |
| **Accountant** | Tracks the production budget per song; raises flags when over budget | Automated |
| **Publisher** | Pushes the approved song to the vault, updates the public website | Automated |

This is more roles than the newsroom brief had departments. The reason is that music creation is naturally multi-stage (idea → composition → arrangement → generation → review → publish), and each stage benefits from a distinct agent persona rather than one agent context-switching.

## The Song Creation Pipeline

A new song flows through these stages:

```
Ideation
    The Artistic Director proposes a brief: "Mozart's Symphony No. 40, first movement, 
    rendered in the style of Led Zeppelin's heavier moments. 4 minutes, distorted guitars, 
    a Bonham-style drum interpretation, keep the urgency of the original."
    ↓
Composition
    The Composer maps the source structure to the target style: which themes survive, 
    which get reinterpreted, where the breakdowns happen, where the climaxes land.
    ↓
Production prompts
    The Producer translates the composition into prompts that the music model can act on. 
    For Suno: style descriptors, structure tags, instruction text, reference tracks if 
    supported. Multiple prompt variants for diversity.
    ↓
Music generation
    The Musician(s) produce N candidate tracks from the prompts. 3-5 variants is typical.
    ↓
Review
    The Critic listens (via audio analysis or human review for early iterations) and 
    scores the candidates. Identifies which to iterate further, which to reject.
    ↓
Iteration
    Producer refines the prompts based on review notes. Musicians generate new variants. 
    Loop until one variant is good enough OR budget exhausted.
    ↓
Visual treatment
    Visual Director conceives the cover art and any video. Visual Artist generates. 
    Often parallel with music iteration; sometimes inspired by the chosen take.
    ↓
Final assembly
    Track + cover art + title + description + classical-source attribution + production notes.
    ↓
Editor sign-off
    Editor approves for publication. If the track does not meet quality bar, decision is 
    "publish anyway with notes", "iterate more", or "abandon and try a different brief".
    ↓
Archival
    The full production record commits to the vault: every prompt, every variant, every 
    decision, all costs.
    ↓
Publishing
    Track and assets go live on the band site, with the "How this song was made" section 
    populated automatically from the production record.
```

Every stage commits to the vault. Every iteration is captured. Bounce-backs (review → iteration → review) are normal and visible in the provenance.

## What the Visitor Sees

The band site exposes the production team and the song creation process the same way the newsroom brief exposed the editorial process. Specifically:

- **Per-song production page**: For every published track, a page showing the brief, the composition decisions, the prompt evolution, the candidate variants, the review notes, the final pick, the cost.
- **The studio**: A live page showing what is currently in production. Visitors can see "Mozart's 40th in Zeppelin style is in iteration round 3" or "Bach Cello Suite remix is waiting for cover art."
- **The team**: A page describing each role, who fills it (which model, when human review is involved), recent contributions, agent-to-agent communication examples.
- **Decision logs**: When the Critic and Producer disagree, the reasoning is captured. When the Editor rejects a track that the Producer thought was ready, the why is documented.

This is the same provenance-as-product pattern from the newsroom brief, applied to a creative domain. It does two things at once:

1. Makes the work more interesting to the visitor (process is often as compelling as output)
2. Provides the template for any real band to operate this way (with humans in some roles, agents in others)

## Communication Between Agents

Same email-FS-light pattern from prior briefs. Each agent communicates by writing messages to a structured folder in the vault:

```
band-vault/
    +--> production/
    |       +--> songs/
    |       |       +--> {song-id}/
    |       |               +--> brief.md                  (from Artistic Director)
    |       |               +--> composition.md            (from Composer)
    |       |               +--> prompts/                  (from Producer)
    |       |               +--> generations/              (from Musicians)
    |       |               +--> reviews/                  (from Critic)
    |       |               +--> visuals/                  (from Visual team)
    |       |               +--> decisions.md              (Editor sign-off and reasoning)
    |       |               +--> cost.json                 (from Accountant)
    |       +--> studio/
    |               +--> current-projects.json
    |               +--> queue.json
    +--> team/
    |       +--> roles.md                                  (who does what)
    |       +--> agent-configurations/                     (the prompts and parameters per role)
    |       +--> human-contributions/                      (where humans plug in)
```

Each agent reads from its inputs, writes to its outputs, leaves a trail. Future agents (Critic, Editor, Archivist) read the trail to do their work.

## Where Humans Plug In

For the initial unsupervised mode, agents fill every role. As the project matures, humans can plug in selectively:

| Role | When Humans Are Most Valuable |
|------|------------------------------|
| Artistic Director | When the project needs a specific creative voice or vision |
| Critic | Early iterations, to calibrate what "good" sounds like for this band |
| Editor | High-stakes releases, or when quality is genuinely ambiguous |
| Visual Director | When brand identity matters more than experimentation |

Most of the time, the team runs unsupervised. Humans intervene on edge cases or strategic decisions.

## Connection to the Newsroom Brief

The newsroom brief (12 May) defined 15 departments for a publication. This brief defines 13 roles for a band. The structural similarity is the point: both are creative production teams that produce content, both benefit from visible-process and provenance, both can be staffed by agents with humans in strategic positions.

The newsroom brief's lessons all apply:

- Departments / roles are visible on the website
- Workflow transitions are commits in the vault
- Bounce-backs are normal, captured, valuable
- Cost tracking happens per output unit
- Reader / listener engagement is its own role
- The platform infrastructure is the same

The differences are:

- Music production has more parallel stages (audio and visual happen partly in parallel)
- The output is multi-modal (audio + visual + text) inherently
- Quality is more subjective; the Critic role does more work than the newsroom fact-checker
- Production loops are tighter (a song might iterate 10 times in a day; a news article rarely does)

## Connection to the Music Band Demo Brief

The companion brief (`v0.27.40__dev-brief__music-band-demo-vault.md`) defines the band itself, the visual identity, the website structure. This brief defines **how the band keeps producing new content** after the initial vault is live.

The initial vault has 3-5 songs ready at launch. After launch, the production team described here generates more. Whether at one song per day, one per week, or one when inspiration strikes, the workflow is the same.

## What This Demo Proves

A few things at once:

| Claim | How This Demonstrates It |
|-------|-------------------------|
| Agentic teams can produce creative work | A working track every day demonstrates this concretely |
| Provenance and visibility add value, not friction | The "how this song was made" pages become a feature visitors enjoy |
| The same pattern composes from publications to creative work | Newsroom and band team are structurally similar |
| Humans-in-the-loop is a spectrum, not a binary | The team can run fully autonomous or with selective human input |
| Cost tracking per unit of output is achievable | Every song has its cost recorded |
