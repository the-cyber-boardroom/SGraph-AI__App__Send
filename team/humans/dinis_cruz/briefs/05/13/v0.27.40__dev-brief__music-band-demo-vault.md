## What This Is

A demo vault that presents a fictional, fully Generative-AI-produced music band. Not a real artist with AI-assisted marketing. A band that exists entirely as an AI artefact: the name, the philosophy, the history, the visual identity, the music itself, the website, all generated and presented as a coherent whole.

The demo serves two purposes simultaneously:

1. **A platform showcase**: another worked example of what vaults + SG/Apps + the ephemeral compute substrate can do, alongside the medical POC and the friend's CV site.
2. **A reusable template**: any musician, band, label, or artist could clone this and adapt it. Same pattern, real artist, real content.

This is the music industry equivalent of the CV/portfolio market: a productisable category where the platform fit is excellent.

## The Band

The band is a **fully Gen-AI classical-rock remix project**. Specifically:

- **Genre**: classical compositions reimagined as rock and roll, plus multi-style remixes (could mix Mozart and Beethoven, or weave a Bach fugue into a Led Zeppelin-shaped arrangement)
- **Origin**: fully AI-generated, no pretence of being human musicians
- **Visual identity**: NOT photorealistic AI-generated humans. Instead: illustrations, abstract artwork, symbolic imagery, instrument-focused photography, concert atmosphere shots without identifiable performers
- **Voice**: transparent about being AI. The band's "philosophy" page explains how the music is made, why it exists, what the project explores

The classical-meets-rock remix angle is the artistic concept. It is also a smart constraint: there is no shortage of source material (centuries of classical compositions in the public domain), the remixing direction is a clear creative choice, and the output is recognisably distinct from typical AI-generated music.

## The Naming Workflow

The band needs a name. Rather than picking one, run a naming workflow as part of the demo:

1. Generate a set of candidate names (Claude proposes 15-20)
2. Filter for available domains, social handles, trademark conflicts
3. Generate a brief identity sketch for each surviving candidate
4. Document the naming process in the vault (visible to visitors as part of the "how this was made" story)
5. Pick one

The naming process itself becomes content. The vault has a `/about/naming` section that shows the candidates, the reasoning, the chosen name, and the reasoning for that choice. This is the visible-editorial-process pattern from yesterday's newsroom brief, applied to a creative project.

Domain candidate format: `[bandname].sgraph.app` (using the subdomain pattern from 12 May). Or, if the brand grows, eventually a dedicated domain.

## Content the Demo Should Contain

The vault should not just have a homepage. It should be a complete presence:

| Section | Contents |
|---------|----------|
| **Homepage** | Band identity, latest release, visual hero, call to listen |
| **About / Philosophy** | The artistic vision, the AI-transparency story, the classical-rock concept |
| **Music** | Tracks (audio files), embedded players, lyrics where applicable, source attribution for the classical pieces |
| **Visual gallery** | Illustrations, abstract artwork, concert atmosphere, logo variations |
| **The studio** | "How a track is made" pages: the prompts, the Suno generations, the iterations, the final mix |
| **Show notes** | If applicable, fictional or real performance contexts |
| **News / Updates** | Recent releases, behind-the-scenes notes |
| **Issues board** | Kanban-style: tracks in progress, ideas, retired ideas |
| **Band meetings** | Logged decisions and rationale (real or fictional, presented transparently) |
| **Demo files** | Work-in-progress tracks, alternate versions, source files |
| **Contact / Community** | How fans can engage (vault-based contribution, if relevant) |

The point is to demonstrate that the vault holds **the whole working artefact** of an artist, not just the polished public face. The community-around-transparency angle is genuinely interesting: a band that exposes its inner workings becomes more interesting than one that does not.

## The Multi-Modal Production

Different generative tools produce different parts of the content:

| Content Type | Tool / Approach |
|--------------|----------------|
| **Band concept and philosophy text** | Claude (this session) |
| **Visual artwork (cover art, logos, atmospheric)** | ChatGPT image generation (GPT-Image-2 or current equivalent) |
| **Music tracks** | Suno (or Udio, or current best-in-class generative music) |
| **Music videos / clips** | Video generation models (Sora, Veo, or current equivalent) |
| **Web rendering** | The vault App Mode (HTML/JS) |

Each tool has its own strengths. The vault is the canonical container that brings them all together into a coherent presence.

## Show the Work

Every piece of content should be paired with the **prompts and process** that produced it. The vault contains:

- The prompt that generated each image
- The prompt and parameters that produced each track
- The discussion of why one variant was kept and others were rejected
- The iteration history when something was refined

This makes the demo a learning resource as much as a presence. A real musician visiting the vault sees not just "this exists" but "here is how it was made; you could make your own."

This connects directly to the executable expertise pattern from OWASP Article 06: the methodology is part of the deliverable.

## Multi-Theme Rendering

Use this demo as an exercise of the multi-theme architecture from 02 May. The same content rendered in three or four distinct themes:

- Dark minimalist (modern indie rock look)
- Vintage analog (warm, paper, photographic grain, classic album-cover aesthetic)
- Abstract and modern (heavy use of generated artwork, minimal text)
- Symphonic and ornate (lean into the classical heritage)

Visitors can switch themes via a control on the site. The point: same vault content, dramatically different visual presentations, no underlying content changes.

This is the strongest demonstration we can give of the content-structure separation. Three musicians might want their site to look completely different from each other; the platform supports all three from the same underlying primitives.

## The Audience Pivot Point

The demo is for two audiences:

**Curious visitors and music fans** see a presence they would expect from any modern band, plus the meta-layer of being able to read about how it was made.

**Other musicians, bands, labels** see a template they could adapt. The vault can be cloned. The structure can be reused. The same workflow that produced this band can produce theirs. The conversation becomes "I want my band to be presented like this."

The brief from the friend's job search vault applies the same logic to careers. This brief applies it to music. The pattern composes to any creative or professional domain.

## Where It Lives

This demo runs on the ephemeral vault app substrate (the companion milestone brief). Specifically:

- Hosted at `[bandname].sgraph.app` (subdomain workflow from 12 May)
- The vault holds the whole presence (the static projection from 12 May serves as the public archive)
- The ephemeral SG/Compute instance demonstrates that the same vault works on a per-instance public IP / cert deployment

This makes the demo simultaneously a content showcase and an infrastructure proof. The same band runs on the published subdomain AND on an ephemeral EC2 instance, demonstrating both deployment paths.
