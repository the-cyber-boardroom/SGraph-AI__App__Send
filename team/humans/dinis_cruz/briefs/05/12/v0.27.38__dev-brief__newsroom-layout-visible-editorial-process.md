## What This Is

The structural design for the newsroom that runs the publications (the Portugal GenAI publication being the first instance). A newsroom staffed primarily by agents, with humans in key roles, organised into departments. The departments mirror what a traditional newsroom has, mapped to the vault-driven architecture.

The departments are not just an internal organisational concept. **They are visible on the website itself.** The editorial process is part of the publication. Readers can see how a story moved from research to publication, who decided to cover it, what the discussion was, what was rejected and why. Provenance is the product.

## Why Make the Process Visible

Traditional newsrooms have an editorial process that readers never see. Decisions about what to cover, whose voices to amplify, what to verify, what to spike: all happen privately. Readers consume the output without the context.

This is a missed opportunity. The decisions are interesting. The trade-offs are interesting. The reasoning is interesting. A newsroom that shows its work earns trust that a black-box newsroom cannot.

Three reasons to make this visible:

| Reason | Why It Matters |
|--------|---------------|
| **Trust** | Readers can verify the reasoning behind editorial choices, not just the final article |
| **Accountability** | Disagreements, biases, and errors become traceable rather than hidden |
| **Educational** | Readers (especially aspiring journalists, researchers, students) learn how the work happens |

This is the natural extension of evidence-driven journalism from the previous brief: not just "what is the evidence for this claim" but "what is the evidence for why we chose to publish this claim in this way."

## The Departments

A traditional newsroom contains broadly these departments (synthesised from how real newsrooms operate). Each maps to a vault structure in our model:

| Department | Purpose | Vault Location |
|------------|---------|----------------|
| **Tip line / Inbox** | Where stories enter (reader submissions, alerts, source materials, agent-detected events) | `inbox/` |
| **Assignment desk** | Where editors decide what gets covered and by whom | `assignments/` |
| **Research department** | Background research, fact-gathering, source identification | `research/` |
| **Reporting room** | Where stories are written (article drafts, voice memos, interview transcripts) | `reporting/` |
| **Translation room** | Bilingual rendering (EN ↔ PT for the Portugal publication) | `translation/` |
| **Visualisation / Graphics department** | Infographics, illustrations, data visualisations | `graphics/` |
| **Multimedia room** | Audio, video, interactive components | `multimedia/` |
| **Fact-check / Verification** | Claims checked against sources, expert validation | `verification/` |
| **Copy desk** | Editing, headline writing, polish | `editing/` |
| **Breaking news desk** | Time-sensitive coverage, real-time updates | `breaking/` |
| **Editorial board** | Opinion pieces, editorial positions, disclosed perspectives | `editorial/` |
| **Publishing / Production** | Final assembly, scheduling, release | `published/` |
| **Archive** | Versioned record of everything published | `archive/` |
| **Corrections desk** | Errata, updates to previously-published material | `corrections/` |
| **Reader engagement** | Reader contributions, validations, corrections submitted | `reader-input/` |

This is more than a vault layout. It is the operating model. Each department has agents working in it, possibly humans assigned to it, defined workflows for moving content from one to the next.

## How Content Moves Through the Newsroom

The flow for a typical story:

```
Tip line catches something
    ↓
Assignment desk decides: cover it? Who covers? When?
    ↓
Research department gathers background, sources, context
    ↓
Reporting room drafts the story (may bounce back to research for more)
    ↓
Fact-check verifies claims (may bounce back to reporting for sourcing)
    ↓
Translation room produces the other language (and back for review)
    ↓
Graphics department generates infographics / visualisations
    ↓
Copy desk edits, refines headlines
    ↓
Editorial board reviews if opinion or sensitive
    ↓
Publishing assembles, schedules, releases
    ↓
Archive captures the published version
    ↓
Reader engagement collects contributions, corrections
    ↓
Corrections desk updates the published version if needed
```

Every transition is a commit in the vault. Every transition is timestamped. Every transition has an actor (which agent, which human). The full history of how a story moved through the newsroom is auditable.

Bounce-backs (research → reporting → research → reporting) are normal. They are not failures; they are quality control. The graph captures them.

## What the Reader Sees

The website surfaces the newsroom directly. Some examples of what becomes visible:

### Per-Story Provenance

At the bottom of every published article, a "How this story was made" link expands to show:

- When the tip arrived
- When it was assigned, by whom, to whom
- How long research took
- How many sources were consulted (with names where possible)
- What was fact-checked, by whom, with what result
- What was changed between drafts (a diff view)
- Cost to produce
- Any corrections issued

### Live Newsroom View

A page on the website (live, updating) shows the current state of the newsroom:

- Stories in research
- Stories being drafted
- Stories awaiting fact-check
- Stories scheduled for publication
- Recent corrections

Readers can see the pipeline. They can see what is coming. They can see what has just happened.

### Department Pages

Each department has its own page explaining what it does, who works there (which agents, any humans), what its workflow looks like, what it has produced recently.

The research department page, for example, might show: "47 stories researched this month. Average research time: 3.2 hours. Top sources used: [list]. Recent research notes published: [list]."

The translation room page might show: "EN ↔ PT translation. Powered by [agent] with human review. Currently in the queue: [count]. Recent translations: [list]."

### Reader Engagement

The reader-input department has its own visible surface:

- Reader contributions in queue
- Recent reader-submitted corrections (accepted and rejected, with reasoning)
- Domain expert validations recently received
- Reputation leaderboard for top contributors

This is the validation system from the previous brief made concrete. Readers see how their contributions are processed.

## The Provenance Page Pattern

Every piece of content has a provenance page. The pattern is consistent:

```
Story: "EU AI Act Implementation in Portugal: Where We Are"
Published: 12 May 2026
Languages: EN, PT
Departments touched: [Tip line] → [Assignment] → [Research] → [Reporting] → [Fact-check] → [Translation] → [Copy] → [Published]

Production cost: £8.40 (4,200 tokens main model, 1,100 tokens research, 0 human hours)
Production time: 6 hours 23 minutes (start of research to publication)

Key contributors:
- Research agent: [reference]
- Reporting agent: [reference]
- Fact-check agent: [reference]
- Human editor: [name, if applicable]

Sources consulted: 12
Expert validations: 2 (Dr. X, Prof. Y)
Reader contributions incorporated: 3

Changes log:
- v1: initial draft
- v2: added context on national AI strategy (research bounce-back)
- v3: corrected funding amount (reader correction by [name])
- v4: published

Cost breakdown:
- Research phase: £3.20
- Reporting phase: £2.80
- Fact-checking: £0.90
- Translation: £1.10
- Graphics generation: £0.40
```

This is auditable, browsable, queryable. It is the journalism equivalent of a software project's commit history and CI logs being public.

## How Departments Are Implemented

Each department is structurally:

| Element | What It Is |
|---------|-----------|
| A vault folder | Where the department's work product lives |
| An agent (or set of agents) | The default workers |
| A page on the website | The visible surface |
| A workflow specification | What inputs it accepts, what outputs it produces, what transitions it triggers |
| Optionally, a human reviewer | For quality control, escalation, judgement calls |

The agent communication protocols brief from 11 May covers how these agents talk to each other. The email-FS-light pattern works inside the newsroom too: assignment desk messages research; research messages reporting; reporting messages fact-check.

The vault content collaboration vault model (the one we use for the friend's job search) is reused for each story: the story has its own working vault while in progress, with the relevant departments contributing.

## Humans In the Newsroom

The previous brief was clear: agents enable more humans, not fewer. Specific places humans matter most:

| Role | What They Bring |
|------|----------------|
| **Editor-in-chief** | Editorial judgement, escalation decisions, public face of the publication |
| **Investigative journalist** | Original reporting, source relationships, primary interviews |
| **Domain expert reviewer** | Specialist knowledge that agents cannot reliably reproduce |
| **Translator (native speaker)** | Cultural nuance, voice, idiom |
| **Reader engagement editor** | Curating reader contributions, building the community |
| **Editorial board members** | Stated, named perspectives for opinion pieces |

These are not full-time employees in most cases. The economics work because the platform handles the production grunt-work; humans contribute where they are most valuable.

## What This Implies for the Sgraph.app Subdomain

When the Portugal publication launches on `portugal.sgraph.app` (or whatever slug wins), the URL structure looks something like:

```
portugal.sgraph.app/
    /story/{story-id}                  (the published story)
    /story/{story-id}/provenance       (how it was made)
    /newsroom/                          (live view)
    /newsroom/inbox                     (tip line)
    /newsroom/research                  (research department)
    /newsroom/reporting                 (reporting room)
    /newsroom/translation               (translation room)
    /newsroom/fact-check                (verification)
    /newsroom/corrections               (corrections desk)
    /newsroom/reader-input              (reader engagement)
    /people/                            (humans involved, with their roles)
    /about/how-this-works               (the brief, basically, in reader form)
```

The newsroom is part of the publication. It is not a separate "behind the scenes" section. It is a first-class feature.
