## What This Is

A complete agentic newsroom workflow focused on Portugal: editors, journalists, conductors, researchers, fact-checkers, all as agents collaborating through vaults, producing daily news stories that are evidence-based, semantically structured, and personalisable to specific readers.

This is several things at once:

- A real news operation we can run continuously
- A worked example of agentic team collaboration at scale
- A demonstration of vault-driven content publishing
- A platform for personalised briefings (the Cyber Boardroom pattern applied to general news)
- A stress test for almost every capability we have built

Portugal as the focus is deliberate. It is a manageable market, it is underserved by AI-driven news products, and it lets us map the people, companies, and relationships in a way that compounds with each story.

## Why a Newsroom

The newsroom workflow tests three things that matter for the platform:

1. **Real-time agentic collaboration at scale.** Not two agents on one project. A team of ten or more agents in distinct roles, working through vaults, producing publishable output on deadline. If this works, the platform supports any agentic team.

2. **Evidence-based content pipelines.** Each story has sources, confirmations, contradictions, and a verifiable trail. The vault holds the evidence; the published article links back to it. This is the audit trail every newsroom should have and almost none do.

3. **Personalisation as a service.** One story, dozens of views. Tailored to the reader's role, interests, prior reading, or even identity. The same source material, projected differently for different audiences. This is the Cyber Boardroom briefing pattern at scale.

## The Newsroom as a Modern Editorial Operation

Before defining agent roles, take a step back: what does a modern editorial operation actually look like? The traditional newsroom (editors at the top, reporters chasing stories, copy editors and fact-checkers, designers and producers) has been disrupted by digital but most of the structure is still wrong for AI-era operation.

A modern newsroom built from scratch with AI agents in mind would have:

| Function | Traditional Role | Agentic Equivalent |
|----------|------------------|-------------------|
| **Editorial direction** | Editor-in-Chief, Managing Editor | The Conductor (sets daily priorities, allocates stories) |
| **Story sourcing** | Reporters with beats | Researcher agents (continuous monitoring of named sources) |
| **Investigation** | Investigative reporter | Deep-research agent (multi-day stories, evidence accumulation) |
| **Fact-checking** | Fact-checking desk | Verifier agents (cross-reference sources, flag uncertainty) |
| **Writing** | Reporters and feature writers | Writer agents (multiple voices, tunable per story) |
| **Copy editing** | Sub-editors | Editor agent (style, tone, length, clarity) |
| **Translation / localisation** | Multilingual desk | Translator agents (Portuguese as primary, English as secondary) |
| **Visual production** | Photo desk, graphics | Visual agent (infographics, headers, illustrations) |
| **Personalisation** | (Did not exist traditionally) | Briefing agent (tailored views per reader) |
| **Publication** | Production team | Publishing pipeline (vault to website, social, briefings) |
| **Audience engagement** | Community team | Community agent (responses, follow-ups, surfacing reader signal) |

The humans in this newsroom are the editors-of-editors: setting policy, judging quality, escalating decisions that agents are not equipped to make alone. Humans are the bar; agents are the volume.

## The Vault Structure

The newsroom runs on vaults. Three layers:

### Newsroom Master Vault

The shared workspace for the editorial operation. Contains:

- Editorial policy and standards (what we cover, what we do not, tone, voice)
- The Portugal entity graph (people, companies, government bodies, relationships)
- Source lists (trusted feeds, official statements, primary documents)
- Templates (article structure, briefing format, social post format)
- Daily editorial planning (what the conductor is prioritising today)
- Agent role definitions

### Per-Story Vaults

Every news story gets its own vault. Created when an idea moves from "watched" to "active." Contains:

```
story-{date}-{slug}/
    |
    +--> brief.md                    (what this story is, why it matters)
    +--> sources/
    |       +--> primary/            (original documents, statements, press releases)
    |       +--> coverage/           (how other outlets have reported it)
    |       +--> interviews/         (if any)
    |
    +--> evidence/
    |       +--> facts.json          (verified facts with source attribution)
    |       +--> disputed.json       (claims under dispute, with both sides)
    |       +--> contradictions.md   (where sources disagree)
    |
    +--> drafts/
    |       +--> v1-research-summary.md
    |       +--> v2-writer-draft.md
    |       +--> v3-edited.md
    |       +--> v4-published.md
    |
    +--> personalisation/
    |       +--> general.md          (default audience version)
    |       +--> business-leader.md  (briefing for executives)
    |       +--> sector-banking.md   (banking sector view)
    |       +--> sector-tech.md
    |       +--> regional-{name}.md  (regional perspectives)
    |
    +--> graph/
    |       +--> entities.json       (people, companies, places mentioned)
    |       +--> relationships.json  (what connects to what)
    |       +--> semantic.json       (the knowledge graph for this story)
    |
    +--> _page.json                  (how this story renders on the site)
```

The vault is the audit trail. Anyone can trace from the published article back to the evidence, back to the sources, back to the original documents. This is investigative-journalism-grade transparency, applied to every story by default.

### Daily Briefing Vaults (Personalised)

For each subscriber or each role-based audience, a vault containing today's relevant content, projected from the per-story vaults. A business leader sees the business angle. A tech executive sees the technology angle. A specific named person (with their own briefing subscription) sees content filtered by their declared interests.

This is the Cyber Boardroom briefing model generalised to news. One story, many projections. The personalisation runs in parallel after each story is published.

## The Daily Workflow

```
06:00  Researcher agents run overnight scans of Portuguese news sources, official feeds, social signals
       Surface candidates for stories
       Write summaries to the Newsroom Master Vault

07:00  Conductor reviews overnight surfacing
       Picks today's priority stories
       Allocates each to a writer agent and a verifier agent

08:00  Writer agents and verifier agents begin parallel work
       Verifier checks facts, identifies sources, builds evidence/
       Writer drafts the story, citing the evidence as it goes

10:00  Editor agent reviews drafts
       Returns notes (clarity, tone, length, missing context)
       Writer revises

11:00  Visual agent produces headers and infographics

12:00  Final review (Editor agent, possibly human spot-check)
       Story published to the website
       Personalisation agents fan out: generate sector-specific and audience-specific versions

13:00  Distribution: LinkedIn posts, briefing emails, social shares

14:00+ Ongoing: community agent monitors response, surfaces follow-up signals
       Researcher agents continue scanning for tomorrow's stories
       Investigative agents work on multi-day pieces
```

Every step is auditable through the vault. Every change is a commit. Every decision has reasoning attached. The newsroom literally runs through vault commits as the spine of its workflow.

## The Portuguese Entity Graph

This is what makes the newsroom valuable, not just operational. Over time, we build:

- A map of every significant person in Portuguese business, politics, technology, civil society
- The companies, organisations, and institutions they work for
- The relationships between them (employer, board member, advisor, investor, sibling, ally, opponent)
- Their public statements over time
- The stories they have appeared in

This is a knowledge graph that compounds. Every new story enriches it. Every reference cross-links to it. After three months, we have something no other outlet has: a navigable, evidence-backed, semantically structured view of Portuguese power and influence.

The graph is also the source for personalisation. "Show me stories about people connected to my company" becomes a real query. "What did this person say last year about this topic?" is answerable in seconds.

## Personalisation: One Story, Dozens of Views

The personalisation pattern is the cheeky bit. For any given story:

| Audience | Projection |
|----------|------------|
| **General reader** | Standard published version |
| **Business executive** | What this means for business, sector implications |
| **Technology leader** | The technology angle, implications for digital transformation |
| **Banking sector** | Specific to banking, regulatory implications, market effects |
| **Investor** | Investment implications, affected stocks, deal context |
| **Named individual (subscribed)** | Tailored to their declared interests and prior reading |
| **English-speaking reader** | Translated and contextualised for non-Portuguese audience |

Each projection is generated by a personalisation agent from the same source material. The reader subscribes to one or more projection styles. They get the same news, framed for them.

This is also a "bring your own key" surface (per the package manager commercial model and per the job search collaboration vault brief from yesterday). A reader with their own API key can fork the per-story vault and ask their own LLM to generate a custom projection. The infrastructure supports it.

## Connection to Cyber Boardroom

The Cyber Boardroom project established the personalised-briefing pattern: take security news, project it for specific executives based on their role and concerns, deliver as a daily briefing. This newsroom is the same pattern generalised.

The Cyber Boardroom stack (specifically the briefing generation pipeline) maps directly to the newsroom's personalisation layer. We can use the same agents, the same templates, the same delivery mechanism. The difference is the source material (general Portuguese news vs cybersecurity-specific) and the audience (broader vs CISO-focused).

This is the Cyber Boardroom architecture proving itself in a different domain. If it works for news, it works for any structured-information-projection product.

## The Soft Launch Plan

Three phases:

**Phase 1 (Days 1-7):** One story per day. Manual conductor (human). Test the agent workflow on something low-stakes. Confirm the vault structure works, the personalisation generates sensibly, the publishing pipeline ships.

**Phase 2 (Days 8-30):** Two or three stories per day. Add a community agent for response monitoring. Start tracking which stories and which personalisations get traction.

**Phase 3 (Days 31+):** Full daily operation. Five or more stories per day. Conductor agent runs the daily allocation with human oversight. Personalised briefings to a small set of pilot subscribers.

Each phase has a go/no-go decision. The newsroom is a real product, not a demo. If at any phase the quality is not where it needs to be, we adjust before scaling.

## Open Questions

1. **Editorial voice.** What is the newsroom's house style? Bold/restrained, opinionated/neutral, analytical/narrative? This needs deciding before the agents are tuned.
2. **Portuguese-only or bilingual?** Portuguese as primary clearly, but is English translation a parallel output or a tier 2 feature?
3. **Funding model.** Free with personalised tier subscriptions? Sponsor-funded? Platform demonstration with no revenue model initially?
4. **Distribution.** Own website only, or also LinkedIn, Substack, X, others? How much effort goes into multi-channel publishing?
5. **Editorial accountability.** Who is the editor of record? Legal responsibility for published content in Portugal?
6. **Pace.** Daily publishing is ambitious. Is twice-weekly a sensible start?

These are real questions that need answers before going operational. None are blockers for the infrastructure work.
