## What This Is

A publishing initiative timed to my Portugal trip: a bilingual (Portuguese and English) publication focused on Generative AI in Portugal. The publication maps the companies, the players, the ministers, the policies, the funding, the regulatory landscape, the academic research, the startup ecosystem, the open source contributions, the use cases. Daily articles, infographics, semantic graphs.

The publication itself is the deliverable; the **vault-driven publication infrastructure** is the more interesting outcome. This becomes a flagship case study for what is now possible with vaults, SG/Apps, the sgraph.app subdomain workflow, the agent communication patterns, and the infographic generation.

## Why Portugal, Why Now

The trip provides the catalyst. There is a real reason to focus on Portugal for a defined period: I am there, I will meet people, the content has immediate practical value to those conversations.

A bilingual publication has compounding effects:

- **Portuguese readers** get content in their language about their own ecosystem (which is rare; most coverage of Portugal happens in English from abroad)
- **English readers** get a window into a market that is increasingly relevant but underexposed
- **Portuguese AI players** see themselves named and analysed in a serious publication, which is good for relationship-building
- **The platform** demonstrates bilingual publishing as a first-class capability

The Portuguese GenAI scene is small enough to map comprehensively and large enough to be interesting. Perfect scope.

## The Production Workflow

Same pattern we have been refining for the website content, applied at higher volume and with bilingual output:

```
Research agent runs (Perplexity Sonar, OpenAI deep research, web search)
    |
    v
Facts captured to vault as structured data (entities, relationships, sources)
    |
    v
Ontologist agent updates the semantic graph
    |
    v
Content agent drafts articles (one or more, in both EN and PT)
    |
    v
Infographic agent generates visual summaries (companies map, policy timeline, funding flows)
    |
    v
Editor agent (or me) reviews, requests changes, approves
    |
    v
Vault commits the day's content
    |
    v
Live publication URL updates within minutes
```

Several pieces of this exist already (research agents, infographic generation, semantic graphs from the ontologist brief, content agents writing markdown). What is new is assembling them into a daily publication cadence with bilingual output and consistent quality.

## The Bilingual Property

Both languages are first-class. This is not "English with translations." It is content created with both audiences in mind, sometimes from the same source material, sometimes with different angles per language.

| Aspect | Approach |
|--------|----------|
| Routing | Reader picks language at the top of the site, or the URL reflects it (`pt.publication-url` or `?lang=pt`) |
| Storage | Each article exists as two markdown files in the vault (e.g. `article-id.en.md`, `article-id.pt.md`) |
| Generation | The content agent generates both versions; an agent fluent in Portuguese reviews the PT version specifically |
| Cross-linking | An article in EN links to the PT version and vice versa |
| Search | The semantic graph is language-agnostic; entities have labels in both languages |

The bilingual property is non-trivial in execution but the architecture supports it cleanly. Vaults handle multiple files for the same article. SPA routing handles language switching. The ontologist's graph handles cross-language entity resolution.

## The Knowledge Graph as Backbone

The ontologist work from 02 May becomes essential here. Each article is not standalone; it sits in a graph of entities, relationships, and sources.

The graph captures:

- Every company mentioned
- Every person mentioned
- Every funding round
- Every product launch
- Every policy document
- Every event
- Every open source contribution

Articles reference the graph. The graph powers:

- Per-entity pages (this company, this person, all coverage in one place)
- Network visualisations (who invested in whom, who works with whom)
- Timelines (how funding has evolved, how policy has changed)
- Cross-language navigation (read about a company in EN, click for the PT coverage)
- Search (find every article that mentions a given entity)

This is what makes the publication different from a blog. It is a structured, queryable, continuously-updated knowledge base with an article surface on top.

Each infographic is its own vault object, version-controlled. When the underlying data updates (new funding round, new company), the infographic regenerates from the graph automatically.

## The Domain and Hosting

Following the sgraph.app subdomain workflow:

- The publication lives at a subdomain (working candidates: `pt-ai.sgraph.app`, `portugal.sgraph.app`, `genai-pt.sgraph.app`)
- Later, a custom domain can be registered if the publication has staying power
- The publication serves from a single vault; updates propagate automatically
- Static projections of major articles (per the serverless vault brief) for archival quality and distribution

The Portuguese AI Twitter/LinkedIn crowd can share the URL. The URL works. It looks professional. It does not depend on Substack, Medium, or any other publishing platform.

## The Case Study Value

The publication is also a marketing surface for the platform itself. Every visitor sees:

- A polished bilingual publication
- A subdomain ending in `.sgraph.app`
- A footer mentioning the underlying infrastructure
- Optional "how this site is built" page explaining the agentic publishing workflow

Other publishers (sector-specific newsletters, regional analysts, niche communities) see the publication and realise the same workflow could power their own. The publication becomes the demonstration of the platform's publishing capability.

This is the demonstrate-by-doing pattern from the friend's job search vault and the medical POC, applied to a different domain.

## The Time Constraint

The Portugal trip is the window. The publication should launch with at least a week of content before the trip starts, ramp during the trip, and continue after. This forces concrete decisions:

- Pick the domain by end of this week
- Build the basic vault structure in days, not weeks
- Set up the research-to-publish workflow as an MVP first; refine later
- Soft launch with three to five seed articles before the trip
- During the trip, every meeting becomes potential content (notes, interviews, observations)
- After the trip, the publication continues if it has built an audience

The trip is the deadline, the meetings are the content engine while there, and the platform is the publishing infrastructure throughout.

## What This Requires Beyond Existing Capabilities

Most pieces exist. What is new or needs work:

| Capability | Status |
|-----------|--------|
| Bilingual content management in vaults | New: define the .en.md / .pt.md convention, build a language toggle |
| Research agent tuned for Portuguese sources | New: prompt engineering, source allowlists |
| Portuguese-fluent content review (agent or human) | New: identify the reviewer, define the review pass |
| Infographic templates for ecosystem maps and funding flows | Partly existing: adapt the infographic skill |
| Daily publication cadence and scheduling | New: hook into the QA stack's scheduler |
| Subscriber/follower mechanism | Deferred: not required for launch |

The first launch can be small. Three to five articles, a basic ecosystem infographic, the company list. Quality and depth ramp from there. The point is to ship before the trip starts.

## Future: Other Country Publications

If this works, the pattern generalises. Spain, Brazil, Italy, Germany: each could have its own GenAI publication on the same infrastructure. Same workflow, different domain, different language pair (where applicable).

This is also the natural marketing channel for the platform internationally. A platform that runs the Portuguese AI publication, the Spanish AI publication, the Brazilian AI publication has visibility in each market that other publishing tools cannot match.

This is speculative for now. The Portuguese publication is the first instance. If it works as a publication and as a case study, we revisit the multi-country expansion.
