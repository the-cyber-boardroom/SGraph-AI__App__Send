## The Opportunity

Helping a friend with his CV last week revealed a much bigger market opportunity. The CV/personal-portfolio space has all the right characteristics for a productisable offering on top of vaults and SG/Apps:

- **There is always demand.** People are always looking for jobs, freelance opportunities, or visibility for their work.
- **People do this badly.** Most personal sites are static, dead, generic, or non-existent. The bar is low; the opportunity is high.
- **The customer is not technical but is articulate.** They have content (their experience, their work, their voice). They need help shaping it.
- **The platform fit is excellent.** Vault + SG/App + sgraph.app subdomain + agentic editing = the workflow a non-technical person needs.
- **There is willingness to pay.** Hosting, managing, refreshing a personal site is something people pay for already; this offering is better than the alternatives.

This is also the kind of "deployment as the product" use case that demonstrates our platform without requiring the customer to understand vaults at all. They see a working CV site at `their-name.cv.sgraph.app` (or `portfolio.sgraph.app`). They never need to know what is under the hood.

## Why CVs and Portfolios Specifically

We have other potential market opportunities (medical, investor packs, legal, education). They are all real. But the CV/portfolio market has properties the others do not:

| Property | CV/Portfolio | Medical | Investor Pack | Legal |
|----------|--------------|---------|--------------|-------|
| Time to first paying customer | Days | Months (regulatory) | Weeks (relationship) | Weeks (compliance) |
| Customer technical comfort needed | Low | Medium-high | High | Medium |
| Network effect potential | High (every CV is a marketing surface) | Low | Medium | Low |
| Existing alternatives | Generic (LinkedIn, Wix, weak personal sites) | Specialised (Epic, Cerner, etc.) | Excel-and-PDF | Document management systems |
| Compliance burden | Minimal | High | Medium | High |
| Repeat business | Yes (people update CVs) | Yes | Some | Yes |
| Energy in the market | High (everyone has career anxiety) | Medium | Medium | Low |

CVs and portfolios are the **highest-velocity, lowest-friction market** we can enter. We should not stop pursuing the others; we should let CVs/portfolios be the volume play that funds and informs the rest.

## The Reframe: Not Just a CV

The word "CV" carries a specific connotation (a one-page PDF, dry, formal, dated). The product should sidestep this. The article from 11 May already does this work: it argues for a multi-page, hyperlinked, evidence-rich, audience-tuned personal site that makes it easy for others to help you.

The product name and framing should reflect this:

- **Not a CV.** A living personal site.
- **Not a portfolio in the design-shop sense.** A connected graph of your work.
- **Not a personal blog.** A surface tuned to your goals (job search, freelance pipeline, speaking gigs).
- **Not LinkedIn.** Not optimised for someone else's platform.

Working name options to consider: "Personal pack," "Career hub," "Working portfolio." None of these feel quite right yet; the naming work matters but is not the blocker.

What matters more is the framing: this is your **professional identity surface**, owned by you, hosted on infrastructure that does not lock you in.

## The Domain Strategy

Two natural domains to register:

- `cv.sgraph.app`: for CV-style sites (career history, what you do, what you are looking for)
- `portfolio.sgraph.app`: for work-focused sites (designers, photographers, writers, makers)

Plus the option of dedicated top-level domains we could register if the market validates:

- A short, brandable domain for the CV product specifically
- A short, brandable domain for the portfolio product specifically

The subdomain pattern from the sgraph.app brief gives every customer their own URL automatically: `sara.cv.sgraph.app`, `john.portfolio.sgraph.app`. The same routing infrastructure handles both. The customer picks the slug; we provision the rest.

For higher-tier customers, custom domains: `sara-example.com` or `sara.example.com` pointing at our infrastructure, served from the same vault.

## The Workflow

Same pattern as the other 2026 entrepreneur use cases, applied to the personal site domain:

```
Customer signs up
    |
    v
Onboarding interview (could be agent-driven)
    |
    v
Agent (Claude) builds first version in a vault
    |
    v
Customer reviews at sara.cv.sgraph.app
    |
    v
Customer iterates with their AI tool (Lovable, Claude, etc.)
    |
    v
Updates flow to the live site automatically
    |
    v
Customer optionally publishes a static projection for archival or
custom hosting (per the serverless vault publishing brief)
```

The customer does not learn vaults, SG/Apps, deployment, or anything technical. They have conversations with an agent; the agent builds and updates their site; the URL is the deliverable.

For customers who want to be more hands-on (a developer using this for their own site), the full vault editing experience is available. They use Claude Code or Lovable directly against the vault. Same infrastructure, deeper access.

## The Onboarding Interview

The biggest predictor of a good personal site is whether the customer answered the right questions about themselves. Most customers cannot do this unprompted; they need an agent to interview them.

The interview covers (drawing on the article principles):

| Section | Questions |
|---------|-----------|
| **The Ask** | What are you looking for? What role, industry, location, stage of company? |
| **The Audiences** | Who do you need to talk to? Recruiters, hiring managers, peers, future direct reports? |
| **The Evidence** | What have you actually done? Projects, roles, talks, writing, contributions? |
| **The Voice** | How do you talk about your work? What is your tone? |
| **The What-You-Bring** | What problem do you solve? What changes when someone hires you? |
| **The Now** | What are you working on this week? What are you currently exploring? |

An agent conducts the interview, captures answers into a structured format in the vault, and uses them to scaffold the first version of the site. Subsequent updates use the same structure as the source of truth; the visible site is a projection.

## Recruitment Agency Channel

The voice memo flagged this: recruitment agencies could provide this as a service. They have a client base (jobseekers) who already need help with their professional presence. They could offer "we build and maintain your personal site" as a service tier.

The platform fit:

- The agency manages multiple clients (multi-tenant naturally; one vault per client)
- The agency can customise branding (theming applies; different agencies have different visual identities)
- The agency keeps the relationship with the client; we are infrastructure
- The agency pays for vault usage and seats; the client may or may not see our name

This is the package manager commercial model from yesterday applied to a different domain: we provide platform, the agency provides expertise and relationship, the client pays the agency.

## Pricing Tiers

Following the established platform pattern:

| Tier | What It Includes | Customer |
|------|------------------|----------|
| **Free** | Subdomain on cv.sgraph.app or portfolio.sgraph.app, basic templates, customer self-edits via Claude/Lovable | Casual users, students, early-career |
| **Personal** | Custom domain, advanced templates, analytics, agentic editing support, monthly content review | Active job seekers, freelancers |
| **Pro** | Everything above + dedicated agent for content updates, regular refresh cadence, priority support | Senior professionals, executives, people changing fields |
| **Agency** | White-label, multi-client dashboard, branded sub-platform, partner pricing | Recruitment agencies, career coaches, outplacement firms |

Pricing TBD but in line with adjacent products (Squarespace, Webflow, paid LinkedIn premium tiers).

## The Asset Model

What the voice memo flagged as important: this is an **asset**. The customer's site is a thing they own. It accumulates value over time (more evidence, more links, more polish). It is not a one-off project; it is a long-lived presence.

This matters for retention and pricing:

- A customer paying monthly for site hosting and updates is reasonable
- A customer who has invested months of content into their site will not switch lightly
- The site becomes more valuable the more it has been refined
- The customer is locked in to themselves (their own work, their own voice), not to us

The "locked in to themselves" property is what makes this defensible without being predatory. The customer is not locked in to our infrastructure; they are locked in to the years of refinement they have put into their site. They can take everything with them via the static projection (yesterday's serverless vault brief). But they will not, because what they have built is good and getting better.

## What This Demonstrates

Beyond the direct revenue opportunity, the CV/portfolio market does three things for us strategically:

**Volume.** This produces more deployed sites, more URLs in the wild, more customer references, more case studies, faster than any other market we could enter. Volume is what teaches the platform what is good and what needs work.

**Brand surface.** Every `*.cv.sgraph.app` URL someone sees is a soft pitch for the platform. Career-related content gets shared on LinkedIn, in newsletters, in job application emails. The brand surface is enormous.

**Reference architecture.** A good CV/portfolio site exercises every major capability: app-mode rendering, multi-audience navigation, hyperlinked content, regular updates, theme customisation, custom domain support, static projection. If the platform supports this use case well, it supports almost everything we have in the pipeline.

## Open Questions

1. **Naming.** "CV" vs "portfolio" vs "personal site" vs something new. The category framing matters for marketing. Worth a focused decision soon.
2. **Top-level domain.** Worth registering a dedicated domain (something like `mysite.io`, `livecv.io`, etc.) or stick with `*.cv.sgraph.app`?
3. **Agency partnership model.** Direct outreach to recruitment agencies, or wait for inbound?
4. **First commercial test.** The friend's site is the first instance, but he is not paying. Who is the first paying customer for the productised offering?
