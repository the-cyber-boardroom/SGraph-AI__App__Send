# Pre-Authorisation and Micropayments for Vault-Powered Workflows

**version** v0.27.41
**date** 14 May 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, @Dev, Finance
**type** Strategy brief

## What This Is

A monetisation model for the workflows that have started accumulating real users (the infographic generator is the immediate example, but the pattern applies to any LLM-backed or API-backed feature). The model:

- **Not subscriptions.** Users like that there is no recurring commitment.
- **Not credit packages.** Users dislike pre-paying for a balance that may sit unused, and we dislike custodying a credit ledger.
- **Pre-authorisation + micropayments.** A small hold on the card up front; actual charges accumulate against the hold; periodic settlement; refresh the hold when it drains.

This sits between the two failure modes (subscriptions feel committal; credit balances feel sticky and inflexible) and matches what at least one user has explicitly asked for.

## The User Conversation That Prompted This

The infographic generator already has real users running on my OpenRouter keys. When the conversation about monetisation came up with one of them, the feedback was clear:

- Subscriptions: no.
- Credit packages bought in advance: pushback.
- Visible cost per action ("this image costs 10p"): yes please.
- Paying for what is actually used, not for what might be used: yes.

The user effectively designed the model: **micropayments, visible per-action cost, no large pre-payment, no commitment beyond what is actually used.**

The architectural question becomes: how do we run this without taking unbounded financial risk, and without flooding the user's card statement with 200 line items?

## The Constraints

The model has to respect several boundaries:

| Constraint | Why It Matters |
|------------|---------------|
| **Stripe minimum charge** | Roughly 50p; individual micropayments cannot settle that small |
| **Card-statement noise** | Users do not want 100+ line items on their statement |
| **Our financial risk** | We do not want to ship work and then chase payment; many of our costs (OpenRouter, Suno, image generation APIs) are pay-in-advance for us |
| **User experience** | Whatever model we pick has to feel light and trustworthy |
| **Operational simplicity** | Less custodial ledger work is better; less reconciliation pain is better |

The pre-authorisation approach handles all five.

## How Pre-Authorisation Works In This Model

Stripe (and the underlying card networks) allow placing a **hold** on a card without immediately charging it. The hold reserves an amount; the actual charge can happen later, up to the held amount, with multiple captures over time.

Concretely:

1. User signs up and adds a card
2. We place a £5 pre-authorisation hold on the card
3. User uses the service; each action has a visible micropayment cost (e.g. "0.04 for this infographic", "0.15 for this image set")
4. We accumulate the micropayments against the hold
5. At a settlement point (weekly, or when the hold approaches a threshold), we capture the actual usage from the hold
6. If the user keeps using, we place a fresh hold to cover continued usage
7. If the user goes idle, the hold can be released; when they return, a new hold is placed

The user sees:

- An initial £5 hold on their statement (clearly described as a hold, not a charge)
- Periodic settlement charges showing the actual usage (e.g. £2.40 for the week)
- The pre-authorisation hold release as a credit on their statement
- No surprise charges; everything is bounded by the hold amount

We see:

- Bounded risk: we are never extending more service than the hold covers
- Real cash flow: we collect what was actually used
- Lower custodial overhead: no credit balance ledger to maintain
- Lower reconciliation pain: settlements are weekly, not per-transaction

## Why This Beats Credits

Credits (where the user pre-pays £10 and we draw against the balance) seem similar but have meaningfully worse properties:

| Property | Credits | Pre-Auth |
|----------|---------|----------|
| User feels committed | Yes (the £10 is gone from their account) | No (it is a hold, not a charge) |
| User feels owed something | Yes (unused balance is "their money") | No (unused authorisation just releases) |
| We hold customer funds | Yes (custodial liability) | No (the bank holds the hold) |
| Refunds and reversals | Painful (must process refund) | Trivial (release the hold) |
| Accounting treatment | Deferred revenue (complex) | Revenue at capture (clean) |
| User can drain to zero and forget | Yes (and then complain when service stops) | No (hold provides continuous coverage signal) |
| Trust required | Higher (we hold their money) | Lower (we just have a hold) |

The hold model is friendlier to the user (less commitment, less feeling of "money sitting with them") and friendlier to us (no custodial liability, cleaner accounting).

## Why This Beats Subscriptions

Subscriptions have their place but do not fit this workload:

| Property | Subscription | Pre-Auth |
|----------|-------------|----------|
| User pays for unused time | Yes (most months) | No (only used time charged) |
| Aligns cost with value | Loosely | Tightly |
| Friction to start | Higher (commit to monthly bill) | Lower (just a hold) |
| Friction to leave | Has to cancel; psychological friction | None (just stop using; hold releases) |
| Predictable revenue for us | Yes | No |
| User trust in fairness | Often low (use vs cost mismatch) | High (use is what you pay for) |

The predictable-revenue downside is real but acceptable. The trust upside is more valuable at our stage. We can layer subscriptions on top later for users who want them (e.g. for power users who would actually pay more than their pay-per-use would total).

## How Micropayments Stay Sane

The Stripe minimum charge problem is solved by the accumulation:

- Individual actions cost as little as a few pence
- They accumulate against the pre-authorisation
- Settlement only happens when the accumulated amount crosses a threshold (e.g. £2-5) or when the hold expiry approaches
- A single settlement event might cover dozens of micropayments

Stripe sees: one charge per settlement (above the minimum). The user sees: one charge per settlement on their statement. We see: micropayments per action in our internal ledger, settled in batches.

This is the same shape as utility billing (electricity meter clicks per hour; one bill per month). Familiar, accepted, low-friction.

## Operational Pieces

What the system actually needs:

| Component | What It Does |
|-----------|--------------|
| **Hold placement** | When a user starts (or returns after a long gap), place a Stripe pre-authorisation for the standard hold amount |
| **Per-action cost calculation** | Each feature reports its actual cost in real-time; the cost is visible to the user before they commit |
| **Internal micropayment ledger** | Track per-action charges against the user's current hold |
| **Hold-remaining display** | Show the user how much of the current hold remains |
| **Settlement scheduling** | Periodic (weekly), threshold-driven, or pre-expiry-driven settlement events |
| **Hold renewal** | When the hold drains or approaches expiry, place a fresh hold; release the old one |
| **Idle handling** | If the user is idle for a long period, release the hold cleanly; place a new hold when they return |
| **Refund and dispute flow** | Pre-auth model makes refunds trivial; document the policy |
| **Disclosure to user** | Clear UI explaining the model, current hold, current accumulated usage |

Most of this is straightforward Stripe API work; the novelty is in the user-facing transparency, not the payments plumbing.

## What This Unblocks

Several monetisation paths open up once this is in place:

| Use Case | How The Model Applies |
|----------|----------------------|
| Infographic generator | Per-infographic cost shown; pay-as-you-go |
| Vault publishing | Per-publish or per-vault-hour cost |
| Ephemeral EC2 instances | Per-instance-minute cost (the deploy-container path makes this granular) |
| Daily band song production | Either band-pays-us or per-song-tip-from-listeners model |
| Agent CLI invocations on user-owned vaults | Per-CLI-call cost transparent to the user |
| Encrypted video streaming | Per-minute-watched or per-MB-served cost |
| Pre-paid expert validation | A user could fund a "validation pool" for their content; experts draw against it |

All of these benefit from the same pre-auth + micropayment substrate. Build it once; reuse it across features.

## Risk Considerations

Things to be careful about:

| Risk | Mitigation |
|------|-----------|
| **User exceeds hold mid-action** | Either pre-flight check (refuse to start an action that would exceed the hold) or graceful interruption with explanation |
| **Hold expires mid-use** | Stripe pre-auths expire after some days; renewal logic must be reliable |
| **API costs spike unexpectedly** | Upstream rate limits and budget caps per user per period |
| **Dispute or chargeback** | Settlement creates a single line item; disputes are clean to handle |
| **Fraud / stolen cards** | Stripe Radar handles most; small initial hold reduces blast radius |
| **User feels they were charged for "nothing"** | The settlement event always corresponds to actual usage; per-action cost is visible at the time of use |

These are all standard payment-system risks. None are blockers; all are addressable.

## What Differentiates This From OpenRouter, Anthropic, Etc.

The voice memo noted: OpenRouter does not take the risk; you pay them in advance. Anthropic similarly bills via prepaid credits or invoice. These work for those products because the user is technical and explicitly chose a pay-in-advance vendor.

For consumer-facing or SME-facing workflows on our platform, the pay-in-advance model is friction. The pre-auth + micropayment model is the consumer-friendly translation of the same underlying economics. We absorb a small amount of float risk (the gap between hold-placement and settlement), in exchange for a much friendlier UX.

The float risk is bounded by the hold amount per user. For thousands of users on small holds, the aggregate is manageable.

## Phasing

The model can ship in stages:

| Phase | Capability | Why First |
|-------|-----------|-----------|
| Phase 1 | Hold placement + accumulation + manual settlement | Validates the user experience |
| Phase 2 | Automated weekly settlement | Removes manual operational load |
| Phase 3 | Hold renewal automation | Handles long-term users |
| Phase 4 | Idle-handling and hold release | Improves UX for intermittent users |
| Phase 5 | Multi-feature unified ledger | All features share the same hold |
| Phase 6 | Tiered hold amounts (£5, £20, £100) | Different user profiles, same model |

Phase 1 ships with the first paying use of the infographic generator. Other phases follow as the user base grows.

## What This Asks For

1. Build the pre-auth + micropayment substrate as a shared service (not per-feature)
2. Start with the infographic generator as the first integration
3. Test with the user who provided the feedback (they will be a useful beta participant)
4. Document the model clearly on sgraph.ai as part of the product positioning ("pay for what you use; no subscriptions; transparent pricing")
5. Treat this as a strategic capability, not a feature: most future monetisation routes use the same substrate

| # | Criterion | Verification |
|---|-----------|-------------|

| 3 | Micropayments accumulate against the hold in an internal ledger | Ledger reflects each action; total matches sum of actions |

| 8 | Refund flow tested end-to-end | A user can request a refund; the flow handles it |
