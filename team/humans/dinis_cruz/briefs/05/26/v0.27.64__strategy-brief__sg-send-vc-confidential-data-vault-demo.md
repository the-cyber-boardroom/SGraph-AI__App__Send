## What This Is

A demo brief, the VC equivalent of the security-report vault demo (cross-ref: the v0.27.64 security-report-vault demo brief), focused on the scenarios and workflows a venture capital firm faces in handling, creating, managing, and consuming confidential data and documents. It is written for a live demo with a VC, with the explicit goal of **really showing the vault's read and write capabilities** across the situations a VC deals with every day.

Scope note: this demo deliberately **excludes vault-in-vault (VIV)**, since that is not fully working yet. It stands entirely on the solid, working capabilities: the vault itself (read and write, version-controlled), the Vault App (customised HTML views), the Vault Chat (read documents and write new ones, with tools), public vault previews (shareable confidential links with a real preview), audience-specific views, and simulated exports. Everything below is demonstrable without VIV.

## Why VC Is The Right Audience

A VC firm is, structurally, a confidential-document business. Almost everything a VC does is the handling, creation, management, and consumption of sensitive information: deals, diligence, memos, cap tables, board materials, LP reporting, fund performance. Today that runs on a patchwork of email attachments, generic file-sharing data rooms, PDFs, and spreadsheets, none of which is private by default, version-controlled, audience-aware, or auditable in the way the work actually demands. This is exactly the problem the vault solves, and a VC will feel it immediately, because they live it daily.

The demo meets them where they work: it takes their real workflows and shows each one done in a vault, with the confidentiality, version control, controlled sharing, and customised delivery that their current tools lack.

## The Four Verbs: Handle, Create, Manage, Consume

The demo is organised around the four things a VC does with confidential data, which map cleanly onto the vault's read and write capabilities:

| Verb | What It Means For A VC | Vault Capability |
|------|------------------------|------------------|
| **Consume (read)** | Receive and read confidential inbound: founder decks, data rooms, portfolio reports | Read; controlled access; audience views |
| **Create (write)** | Produce confidential documents: investment memos, LP reports, IC materials | Write; Vault Chat authoring; version control |
| **Manage (read + write + version)** | Maintain evolving confidential artifacts: the deal folder, cap tables, the data room | Read-write; the commit queue; version history |
| **Distribute (controlled read)** | Share confidentially with the right people: LPs, advisors, the IC, founders | Public previews; key sharing; per-audience views |

Read and write run through all four, which is why the demo can show both richly and naturally, without contriving it.

## The VC Scenarios To Demo

The concrete scenarios, each a real VC situation, each demonstrating read and write.

### Scenario 1: The Inbound Data Room (Consume / Read)

A founder sends a pitch deck, financials, and supporting documents during fundraising. Today: email attachments or a generic data-room link, with no real control and no confidentiality guarantee. In the vault: the founder's materials arrive in a vault, the VC reads them with full control, and the access is auditable. The demo shows reading confidential inbound cleanly, and (with the Vault Chat) querying it: "summarise this deck," "what are the key risks in these financials," "what is missing from this data room." This is read plus tool-assisted consumption.

### Scenario 2: The Investment Memo (Create / Write)

The VC writes the internal investment committee memo from the founder's materials. Today: a Word doc or Google Doc, manually assembled, disconnected from the source materials. In the vault: the Vault Chat reads the deck and financials (Scenario 1) and writes the investment memo into the vault, with the analyst editing and the chat generating infographics (the market chart, the cap-table summary, the returns model). This is the headline read-and-write demo: read the confidential inbound, write the confidential memo, all in one vault, version-controlled. The memo is created, not just stored.

### Scenario 3: The Deal Folder (Manage / Read + Write + Version)

A live deal accumulates documents over weeks: the deck, the memo, the term sheet drafts, the diligence findings, the references. Today: a folder that grows messily, with versions in filenames and no real history. In the vault: the deal folder is read-write and version-controlled, every change tracked (the commit queue batches the churn sensibly, cross-ref: the v0.27.64 commit-queue brief), so the VC can see how the term sheet evolved, who changed what, and roll back. This is management as a first-class capability: the confidential artifact evolves, and its full history is preserved and navigable.

### Scenario 4: The IC / Board Materials (Create + Distribute, Audience Views)

The investment committee or a portfolio-company board needs materials, and different people need different views. Today: one PDF for everyone, or several manually-made versions. In the vault: the same underlying materials, with audience-specific views (the Vault App, agent-generated, cross-ref: the security-report demo's personas):

| Audience | Their View |
|----------|------------|
| **The GP / partner** | The decision, the recommendation, the key risks and returns |
| **The investment committee** | The full memo, the diligence, the model |
| **The CFO / finance** | The numbers, the cap table, the returns model, the fund impact |
| **The founder** (where shared) | The terms, the next steps, what is needed from them |
| **The LP** (for portfolio updates) | The performance, the trajectory, the highlights |

One vault, many views, each confidential and controlled. This shows create-plus-distribute with the audience customisation a PDF cannot do.

### Scenario 5: LP Reporting And The Confidential Distribution (Distribute / Controlled Read)

The fund sends quarterly reports, capital calls, and performance data to LPs. Today: emailed PDFs, with no control after sending and no per-LP customisation. In the vault: the LP report is a vault, shared via a controlled link (the public preview gives a professional, branded preview card without exposing the contents, cross-ref: the v0.27.62 public-vault-previews brief), with the decryption key shared separately or via the LP's identity (cross-ref: the v0.27.60 authentication brief). The demo shows confidential distribution that looks professional when shared (the preview), stays controlled (the key), and can carry disclaimers ("confidential, for the named LP only"). This is controlled read at its most valuable.

### Scenario 6: The Fund's Own Raise (Consume + Create + Distribute)

When the fund itself raises from LPs, it is on the other side: creating its own confidential data room (the fund's track record, strategy, team, terms), distributing it to prospective LPs, and consuming their questions and diligence. In the vault: the fund's data room is created and managed in a vault, distributed with controlled, previewable links per prospective LP, and the diligence Q&A flows back in. This shows the full cycle from the fund's perspective, and it is a scenario the VC will personally care about.

## The Side-By-Side: Today Versus The Vault

The contrast to show throughout, the same structural argument as the security-report demo, in VC terms:

| Aspect | Today (Email / Generic Data Room / PDF) | The Vault |
|--------|------------------------------------------|-----------|
| **Confidentiality** | Attachments and links; trust the provider | Encrypted, zero-knowledge; the provider cannot see the contents |
| **Read** | Download and open; no control after | Controlled, auditable read; revocable |
| **Write / create** | Separate tools, disconnected from sources | Created in the vault, from the source materials, with the chat |
| **Version control** | Versions in filenames; no history | Full version history; every change tracked; rollback |
| **Audience** | One artifact for everyone | Audience-specific views (GP, IC, CFO, founder, LP) |
| **Distribution** | Email a PDF; lost control on send | Controlled link; preview card; key-based access; disclaimers |
| **Audit** | Who opened what is unknown | Auditable access (who, when) |
| **Currency** | Stale on send | Live, updatable |

The headline for a VC: **confidentiality by default, control that survives sending, version history on everything, and the right view for each reader, all in one place.**

## The Live Read-And-Write Showcase

For the meeting, the single most compelling moment is the live read-and-write loop, done in front of them. A suggested sequence:

1. **Read**: open a (simulated) founder data room in the vault. Show the deck and financials. Ask the Vault Chat to summarise the deck and flag risks (read plus tool-assisted consumption).
2. **Write**: ask the Vault Chat to draft an investment memo from the materials, and generate an infographic of the market size and the returns model (write, into the vault).
3. **Manage**: edit the memo, show the version history capturing every change, roll one back (read-write plus version control; the commit queue batching the churn).
4. **Distribute**: produce an audience view (the GP one-pager and the IC full memo from the same materials), then share the vault as a controlled link, showing the professional preview card and the key-based access (controlled distribution).
5. **Export** (simulated): show exporting the memo or the cap table to the tools a VC uses (a CRM/deal-tracker, a spreadsheet, the fund's reporting), illustrating "a better way to communicate, not just present."

This sequence shows read, write, manage, and distribute in one continuous flow, on real-feeling VC content, which is far more persuasive than slides.

## Why This Lands With A VC

The demo is persuasive for a VC for specific reasons worth holding in mind during the meeting:

- **It is their actual problem.** Confidential document handling is the substance of their work, and their current tools are visibly inadequate.
- **Confidentiality by default matters to them.** Deal terms, LP data, and fund performance are genuinely sensitive; zero-knowledge is a real benefit, not a feature bullet.
- **Control that survives sending** answers a real fear (a forwarded deck, an LP report in the wrong inbox).
- **Version history on everything** is something they lack and need (how did this term sheet evolve?).
- **Audience views** solve the one-PDF-for-everyone problem they live with.
- **It is live and real**, not slideware: reading a deck and writing a memo in front of them makes it real, exactly as it did for the colleague who finally understood the platform by seeing it.

## The Commercialisation Angle

A VC is both a potential user and a potential channel. As a user: the fund runs its deals, diligence, IC, and LP relations on vaults. As a channel: the fund's portfolio companies all have the same confidential-document needs (fundraising, board materials, data rooms), and a VC that adopts the vault can bring it to its portfolio. The demo is the door to both. Pricing and packaging are follow-on (cross-ref: the v0.27.60 open-evaluation and SG/Sentinel commercial-model briefs), but the VC meeting is a high-value first conversation: a demanding, confidentiality-sensitive user who is also a multiplier.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a VIV demo.** Vault-in-vault is excluded; the demo stands on working capabilities.
- **Not a real fund's data.** Everything is simulated; clearly so.
- **Not a finished product pitch.** It is a demo that shows the capability on the VC's own workflows.
- **Not a replacement for their whole stack.** It feeds their tools (simulated exports); it does not require ripping anything out.
- **Not the commercial offer yet.** It is the conversation-opening demo.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Live demos can fail.** Reading and writing live in front of a VC carries the usual demo risk. Mitigation: rehearse the sequence; have pre-built fallbacks for each step (a pre-generated memo, a pre-made audience view) so a live failure does not stall the story; keep the network-change caution in mind (a known sensitivity).

**Risk 2: Excluding VIV limits some scenarios.** Some richer flows (a deal vault linking to a diligence vault) need VIV. Mitigation: the demo is designed to need only single-vault read-write; frame the multi-vault future as coming, without demoing it.

**Risk 3: Simulated exports could overpromise.** Showing CRM/spreadsheet exports without real integrations. Mitigation: clearly label them simulated; they preview the real interoperability path.

**Risk 4: Confidentiality claims must be precise.** A VC will probe the zero-knowledge claim. Mitigation: be precise (the provider cannot see the contents; the activity may be visible depending on mode, cross-ref: the identity-persistence-budgets and vaults-business-logic briefs' tracked-but-encrypted nuance); do not overstate.
