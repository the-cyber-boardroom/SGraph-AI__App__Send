## What This Is

The first briefing on a significant transformation for SG/Send, sparked by an intersection of ideas that emerged while writing the SG/Sentinel documents. The realisation: **SG/Sentinel's capabilities solve a business problem that has been blocking SG/Send, namely how to let anyone go to the website and upload files and create vaults without enabling exploitation of the service.**

The core insight, and the through-line of this brief: **the lack of security has been preventing a business function.** Until now, SG/Send has not allowed open file uploads and vault creation, because there was not enough control to do so safely. That is a textbook example of why **security is a business enabler**: with the control SG/Sentinel provides, the business function (open evaluation, mass-market trial) becomes possible. Security does not just protect; it unlocks.

This briefing captures the transformation and the proposed model. The project lead flagged that this is a big change needing a whole set of follow-on documents and workflows; this is the foundational briefing those will build on. It also connects two threads that were waiting for it: the product-vs-internal question (the SG/Sentinel path-to-MVP brief left it open) and the maximum-governance free-mode idea (parked in the vault-aware logging brief for exactly this memo).

## The Problem: Lack Of Control Blocked Open Evaluation

The business problem, stated plainly: **SG/Send has not let users just go to the website, upload files, and create vaults, even for evaluation, because there was not enough control to do it safely.**

The voice memo: **"at the moment I don't allow users to just go to the website and send files and create vaults, and the reason is because I don't have enough control. It's a good example of the lack of security actually preventing a business problem, which is why security is a business enabler: it allows business functions to operate securely and safely."**

This is the heart of the transformation. The inability to control who uploads what, from where, has meant SG/Send could not open its doors to evaluation. A prospective user could not simply try it; an attacker could abuse open uploads; and there was no safe way to tell the two apart. The result was a closed evaluation experience, a barrier to adoption, and a missed business opportunity.

The previous approach also leaned on a strong privacy stance (we do not know your IP, we do not know anything about you), which the voice memo noted is a great concept but has limitations: it gives away the visibility needed to operate safely and to serve compliance-oriented customers, and it is not the right fit for every deployment. The transformation repositions privacy as one mode among several, rather than the only stance.

## The Insight: SG/Sentinel Is The Control That Unlocks It

SG/Sentinel provides exactly the control that was missing. With SG/Sentinel in front of SG/Send, open uploads become safe because:

- SG/Sentinel can be ridiculously hostile to bad traffic (cross-ref: the make-the-site-hostile principle, the SG/Sentinel principles brief), blocking abusive IPs, countries, and patterns.
- SG/Sentinel provides maximum visibility into who is using the service and how (cross-ref: the over-index-on-visibility emphasis, the vault-aware logging brief's maximum-governance mode).
- SG/Sentinel's rules can be tuned per deployment model, removing or adding tracking as the model requires (cross-ref: the rule-packs brief, the logging-mode spectrum in the vault-aware brief).

So the thing that was blocking the business (no control) is solved by the thing just designed (SG/Sentinel). The voice memo: **"once we have SGS deployed, we can show the multiple deployment models we have, and the multiple types of visibility."** SG/Sentinel turns open evaluation from a liability into a controlled, observable, tunable business function.

## The Model: Multiple Deployment Modes With Different Visibility

The proposed model positions SG/Send as having **multiple deployment models with different levels of visibility and privacy**, shown transparently. The voice memo: **"I want to position this as a different set of features: multiple deployment models with different levels of transparency, and show them in action once we have SGS deployed."**

The key reframing: privacy is not the only stance. Some deployments are excellent for privacy; others are important for compliance, and compliance cannot use a purely anonymous mode. The question becomes one of providing the right visibility for the right purpose, per mode.

### Mode 1: Free / Non-Private Mode (Maximum Visibility)

The free tier, which doubles as the test bed and the educational on-ramp:

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Access** | Anyone; X transfers per day (five, ten, some daily limit) |
| **Visibility** | Maximum: we find out as much as we can about the user |
| **The educational twist** | We show the user exactly what we can discover about them |
| **Tracking** | Full: track the IP, map the IP |
| **Purpose** | Mass-market trial; the test bed; the place attackers come from |

The voice memo: **"we allow users to do five, ten, X transfers per day, but we call this the free mode, the non-private mode, where we try to find as much as we can about the user, and we show that to the user exactly what we can discover about them."**

This mode is clever on several fronts:

1. **It unblocks mass-market trial.** Anyone can try SG/Send, safely, because SG/Sentinel controls the abuse.
2. **It is educational.** Showing users what can be discovered from their IP (where they are, what their setup reveals) is a striking, memorable demonstration. The voice memo: **"do you realise that from your IP address, this is everything I can know about you, this is where you are."** This is privacy education that doubles as a demonstration of SG/Send's value.
3. **It is the test bed.** Free, open traffic is where attackers come from, so it is the perfect place to exercise SG/Sentinel's hostile-to-bad-traffic capabilities and to test the platform under real adversarial load (cross-ref: the test-driving brief's dogfooding and the over-index-on-visibility emphasis).
4. **It is the maximum-governance mode** parked from the vault-aware logging brief: full capture, full visibility, which here serves the free tier's safety and education.

So the free mode is simultaneously the on-ramp, the educational hook, the test bed, and the adversarial proving ground, all enabled by SG/Sentinel's control and visibility.

### Mode 2: Paid / Non-Tracking Mode

The paid tier inverts the visibility: **pay, and we do not track you.**

| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| **Access** | Paid customers |
| **Visibility** | Minimal: we do not track; we do not store IP addresses |
| **What we know** | Only the payment information stored via Stripe, which stays there |
| **The mechanism** | SG/Sentinel rules remove tracking (back to the privacy-preserving mode) |
| **Transparency** | We can show on the website which rules are enabled at each stage |

The voice memo: **"if you are a paid customer, we don't track anything, we don't store the IP addresses, we go back into the other mode where we use SG/Sentinel with rules to remove stuff. The only thing we know about you is the payment information we store from Stripe, and it stays there."**

This is an elegant value proposition: **paying buys privacy.** The free tier trades visibility for free access (and you are shown exactly what that means); the paid tier restores privacy as the thing you pay for. This aligns incentives cleanly: the people who want privacy pay for it; the people who want free access accept visibility and, importantly, are educated about it.

The transparency feature is a standout: **showing publicly which SG/Sentinel rules are enabled at each tier.** The voice memo: **"we can even share on the website which rules have been enabled for each stage."** This turns SG/Sentinel's rule model (cross-ref: the rules-engine brief, the rule-packs brief) into a trust feature: customers can see exactly what is and is not tracked at each tier, verifiably, because the rules are inspectable. This is the compliance-as-living-graph idea (cross-ref: the SG/Sentinel compliance brief) applied to SG/Send's own tiers: the privacy posture of each tier is a visible, rule-defined fact, not a claim.

### Mode 3: Ephemeral Vaults And Per-Customer EC2

A third capability the model enables, building on the ephemeral-instance work: **paid customers can get ephemeral vaults and even a dedicated ephemeral EC2 instance, where their data exists only in that instance and disappears when it is shut down.**

The voice memo: **"we can have the mode of people creating temporary vaults, ephemeral vaults, vaults that only last for a period of time, and we charge them for it. If you have a paid account, we can start a vault for you, start an ephemeral EC2 instance for you, and your data only exists in that EC2 instance, so once you shut it down, all that data disappears."**

This connects to the bare-EC2 ephemeral-instance reality (cross-ref: the SG/Sentinel test-driving brief, which flagged the bare-EC2 reverse-proxy need, and the day-66 on-demand provisioning work). The ephemeral vault and per-customer instance are a strong privacy and demo capability: data with a guaranteed lifetime, in an isolated instance, gone on shutdown. The voice memo noted it is also a great way to show demos: a clean, isolated, disposable environment per evaluation.

This is where SG/Sentinel's bare-EC2 protection (the reverse-proxy form factor) matters: those ephemeral instances are not behind CloudFront, so SG/Sentinel's reverse proxy is what makes them safe to expose. The SG/Send ephemeral-vault feature and the SG/Sentinel bare-EC2 protection are two sides of the same capability.

## Why This Is A Business Transformation

The voice memo was clear this is big: **"this is a massive transformation, a big change. This is the thing that will unblock a lot of usage and give me visibility and a lot of use cases."** The transformation operates on several levels:

| Dimension | Before | After |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Evaluation** | Closed; could not let anyone try | Open; anyone can try (free mode) safely |
| **Adoption** | Barrier to mass-market trial | Mass-market on-ramp via the free tier |
| **Business model** | Limited | Free (visibility-funded) and paid (privacy) tiers; ephemeral-vault charging |
| **Visibility** | Gave it away (pure-anonymous stance) | Maximum in free mode; the data to operate and improve |
| **Education** | None | Shows users what their IP reveals: memorable, valuable |
| **Test bed** | Separate effort | The free tier is the live adversarial test bed |
| **Privacy** | The only stance | A premium feature you pay for; verifiable via published rules |

The commercial model (cross-ref: the SG/Sentinel commercial-model brief) gains a concrete shape for SG/Send: free tier funded by being the visibility/test bed, paid tier selling privacy and ephemeral vaults, with the SG/Sentinel rule transparency as the trust mechanism. The early free-tier users are likely comfortable with the visibility trade (the voice memo: "those first early batch of users will probably be comfortable with that"), and the education makes the trade explicit and even compelling.

This is the clearest demonstration yet of security as a business enabler: the SG/Sentinel control layer does not just protect SG/Send, it unlocks an entire evaluation funnel, business model, and educational hook that were impossible without it.

## What This Needs Next: The Follow-On Documents

The voice memo was explicit that this is a first briefing and that a whole set of documents and workflows will be needed to implement it. The follow-on work this briefing implies:

1. **The deployment-model specification**: the free/non-private, paid/non-tracking, and ephemeral-vault modes in detail, including the SG/Sentinel rule packs for each.
2. **The free-mode visibility-and-education experience**: how we discover and show what we know about a user (the educational "this is what your IP reveals" experience), designed (cross-ref: the SG/Sentinel UX/designer brief).
3. **The paid-mode privacy workflow**: how SG/Sentinel rules remove tracking on payment; the Stripe-only-data guarantee; the published-rules transparency feature.
4. **The ephemeral-vault and per-customer-EC2 workflow**: provisioning, lifetime, teardown, data-disappears-on-shutdown, SG/Sentinel reverse-proxy protection of the bare instance.
5. **The transfer-limit and abuse-control rules**: the X-transfers-per-day mechanism; the hostile-to-bad-traffic rules for the free tier.
6. **The tier-transition workflow**: moving from free to paid, and the visibility/privacy change that entails.
7. **The commercial and pricing model**: what is charged for (transfers, ephemeral vaults, privacy), connected to the SG/Sentinel commercial-model sketch.

These are the documents to write next, once this transformation is agreed as the direction. This briefing is the foundation they build on.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: The free/non-private mode handles user data and must be handled responsibly.** Showing users what we know is powerful but must be done ethically and legally. Mitigation: full transparency (we show them exactly what we see); clear consent and communication; the education framing makes the visibility explicit, not covert; legal review of the data-handling.

**Risk 2: Open evaluation invites abuse even with SG/Sentinel.** Opening the doors increases the attack surface. Mitigation: this is precisely what SG/Sentinel is for (hostile-to-bad-traffic, detect-before-damage); the free tier is the test bed where this is exercised; transfer limits bound exposure; the bare-EC2 ephemeral instances are isolated and disposable.

**Risk 3: The free-then-pay-for-privacy model must be communicated honestly.** Users must clearly understand the visibility trade in the free tier. Mitigation: the education experience makes it explicit and even compelling; the published-rules transparency lets users verify each tier's posture; the trade is presented as a clear, fair choice.

**Risk 4: It depends on SG/Sentinel being deployed and mature.** The model needs SG/Sentinel's control and rules. Mitigation: the model rolls out as SG/Sentinel matures; passive mode and the easy-win rules (cross-ref: the SG/Sentinel MVP) are enough to start; the tiers deepen as SG/Sentinel does.
