## What Shipped, Recorded Before Critiquing It

The licence-to-operate vault is published with a read key, and the design brief written this morning is visible in it: twelve capabilities in the grant, four in the mandate, an eight-capability delta described on the page as having no policy coverage, the two-store split explained in its own section, and an application granted read access to its own terms and nothing more. The page leads with the delta as the idea worth stealing.

**The design was published before the implementation and the implementation matched it**, which is a pattern this estate has now repeated several times and which is worth continuing to record, because it is the thing that makes a brief worth writing at all.

The criticism that follows is about where the accompanying presentation opens, not about whether it is good. It is good.

## The Diagnosis: A Two-Altitude Jump

The memo says the presentation focuses on the insurance element, that this is important, and that it is not where people start. The useful way to say that is in altitudes, which the graph work already gives us.

| Altitude | The question at it | Who is standing here |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **How do I control the agents already running?** | Almost everybody, on first contact |
| 2 | What can they do, what may they do, and what is the difference? | Somebody who has accepted there is a problem |
| 3 | How is that difference priced, bounded and accepted? | Somebody who has seen the delta and wants a mechanism |
| 4 | How do the objects, schemas and hooks work? | Somebody who is building |

The published presentation opens at altitude three. Its audience is at one. **A presentation may open one altitude above where it lands, because that is a descent an audience can follow; it may not open two above, because the audience has not yet accepted the problem the mechanism solves.** That is the whole diagnosis and it also explains why the deck reads well to anybody already convinced, which is everybody who has reviewed it so far.

So the set is one artefact per altitude, and the rule for whether a new one is needed is whether an existing one is being asked to span two.

| Deck | Opens at | Lands at | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The problem** | 1 | 2 | **Missing, and it is the one to build next** |
| **The map** | 2 | 2 | Missing. Ends with the customer's own inventory, not ours |
| **The delta** | 2 | 3 | Partly exists as the vault page's lead section |
| **The insurance model** | 3 | 4 | **Published today.** Keep it and stop apologising for its depth |
| **The build** | 4 | 4 | Exists as the packs |

**The problem deck must stop before the mechanism.** Its last slide is the delta, not the policy. A deck that opens at the need and closes at the pool has made the same two-step jump in a single sitting.

## The User Need, Frozen

The memo states it and it should not be paraphrased again:

> **How do I manage, contain, monitor, and ensure compliance of the agents that are already running?**

The operative word is **already**. Most material in this market addresses agents an organisation is about to deploy, which lets the vendor assume a greenfield and a place in the path. The need above is about the ones running now, in tools nobody centrally chose, with no broker in front of them. That framing is harder to serve and it is the true state of nearly every estate, and it is the reason passive mode is the on-ramp rather than a lesser tier.

One presentation instruction follows from the memo's observation that the people he talks to arrive at this need by themselves: **do not argue the need.** Name it, show that it is understood in the audience's own words, and move. A deck that spends five slides establishing a problem the audience brought with them is spending its credibility on the one thing it did not need to buy.

## Working With What They Have, And The Dependency Map

The principle is that the solution operates with what an organisation already has. It is not in-line by design, although in-line examples are needed to demonstrate and should be built as demonstrations rather than as the product's shape.

The honest consequence is one the MVPs do not currently state: **an end-to-end MVP describes a complete solution, not a realistic deployment.** Several pieces it assumes will be missing in a real estate. So every pack and every deck should carry a dependency map, and the map's job is to convert an objection into a risk entry.

| Assumed component | If present | If absent |
|---|---|---|
| A record of what agents did | Records are observed, and the derivation is trustworthy | **Risk.** Records are self-reported and low by a measured factor. Passive mode still runs; the tier is marked |
| A broker or proxy the agents pass through | Refusal can be in-line, before the action | **Risk.** Checks are out of band. Escalation is still caught, one action later |
| A registry that measures the grant | The grant is measured and drift is visible | **Risk.** The grant is authored and will be optimistic. The delta is a claim rather than a measurement |
| A meter for the unit being priced | Claims settle without a person | **Risk.** That unit cannot carry a policy, and the pack already refuses one with a null meter |
| A place to accept a draw | A draw has a named acceptor and a date | **Risk.** Draws are recorded with acceptor unknown, and the auditor's question has no answer |

Three properties make this a map rather than a disclaimer. **Every row is a question asked of the customer, not an assumption made about them.** **Every absence is a risk with an owner and a consequence**, which is the form the risk product already speaks. And **every row names which claims survive its absence**, so an engagement can proceed at reduced strength rather than stalling on a missing precondition.

That last property is the commercially important one. A missing broker does not stop anything; it moves the check out of band, and the out-of-band check still catches the escalation that matters. Saying so plainly is more persuasive than pretending the precondition is not needed.

**And the map is itself the first deliverable.** A customer who ends the first conversation holding their own filled-in version of that table has received something real before anything was installed, which is the passive-mode argument from May restated at the level of the engagement.

## The Adoption Ladder, With The Rung Where It Becomes Insurance

The memo is right that the customer should choose how much of the model to implement, and that the whole must work end to end for specific scenarios so that the choice is a choice rather than a compromise. What the memo does not do, and this brief insists on, is name the rungs honestly.

| Rung | What exists | Honest name |
|---|---|---|
| **0** | Observation only. Records of what agents did | Visibility. Not a control |
| **1** | The grant measured, the mandate authored, the delta rendered | An exposure statement |
| **2** | Bands, per-occurrence limits, refusals, an audit trail | **A quota with an audit trail. This is not insurance** |
| **3** | A pool shared across teams, with a reserve | **Insurance begins here**, because pooling is the mechanism |
| **4** | Premium paid in allocation, experience rating on the policyholder | Insurance with an incentive |
| **5** | Claims settling across the organisation, captive economics, repricing | The full model |

**Most customers will stop at rung two, and that is a good outcome that must not be called insurance.** Rungs zero to two are genuinely valuable and genuinely not insurance, and describing them as insurance would spend a vocabulary this corpus has spent months making precise. The word starts at rung three, where a pool is shared, because pooling is what the 26 August work identified as the mechanism rather than the metaphor.

The ladder is also the answer to the memo's point about minimal infrastructure. Rungs zero and one need nothing but a skill and a vault. Rung two needs a place to refuse. Rungs three and above need somewhere for a pool to live and somebody to be the acceptor.

## The Moat, Restated As A Requirement

The memo's moat argument is that being open source and integrating whatever exists means the only true competitor is one occupying the identical position, and that value can always be added in the layers a customer has not implemented. Both halves are right about the market, and both are conditional on something that has to be built.

**A negative moat closes.** "Nobody else is doing this" is an observation about today, and it is the least durable form of advantage. The durable form is that attaching to whatever a customer already has is genuinely cheap, and attaching is cheap only if the seams are real and published. So the moat is a requirement on the architecture:

| Layer | Attaches to what exists by | The seam that must be published |
|---|---|---|
| **Observation** | Consuming records from any source, ours or theirs | `usage-record/v1`, with tier and granularity mandatory |
| **Grant and mandate** | Reading a permissions surface anybody can produce | The grant and mandate schemas, and the measurement that generates a grant |
| **Insurance** | Pricing units any meter already counts | `policy/v1`, whose units name meters in words a reader can re-run |
| **Risk** | Emitting entries into a register they already run | The risk entry shape, and the dependency map's rows as entries |

**If a customer has a mature capability at one layer, we bring the others, and that is only true if each layer can be lifted out.** A product where the insurance layer cannot run against somebody else's observation records has no composability moat, whatever the deck says. So the seams are not a nice property of the design; they are the moat, and they should be built and published before the claim is made in front of a customer.

There is one more competitor the memo does not name, and it is the usual winner: **inertia, and an incumbent adding a checkbox.** The defence against both is the same, which is that the customer gets something real, their own dependency map and their own delta, in the first conversation and before installing anything.

## What To Build Next, In Order

1. **The problem deck.** Opens at the need in the customer's words, lands on the delta, stops there. Nothing about pools.
2. **The dependency map, as a fillable artefact.** Five rows, asked as questions, producing risk entries. It is the first deliverable of any engagement and it should exist as a page before it exists as a slide.
3. **The ladder, published**, with rung two labelled a quota and rung three labelled where insurance starts.
4. **The seams, published**, because the moat claim should not be made in front of a customer before they exist.
5. **The map deck**, which is the dependency map plus the four layers, ending with the customer's inventory rather than ours.

The insurance presentation published today needs no change. It is the altitude-three artefact and it should stay as deep as it is.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a criticism of the published presentation.** It is good at what it is, and the fault is that nothing yet sits above it.
- **Not a sales methodology.** It names the first deliverable and the order of the artefacts, and stops there.
- **Not a claim about what customers want.** The need is stated from the project lead's conversations, and no customer has yet seen either artefact.
- **Not a competitive analysis.** No competitor is named, and the moat section is about our own architecture rather than about anybody else's product.
- **Not a decision about pricing.** The ladder is about what gets implemented, not what gets charged for.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| One deck per altitude | It keeps each honest and it means the first conversation cannot show the thing we are proudest of |
| Refusing to call rung two insurance | It protects the vocabulary and it gives up the strongest word at the rung most customers will actually buy |
| The dependency map shown early | It is disarming and it opens with a list of things we cannot do, which takes nerve in a first meeting |
| Working with what they have | It is the only way to scale and it means we rarely control the quality of our own inputs |
| The composability moat | It is real once the seams exist and it is a claim about work not yet finished |
| Building in-line examples anyway | They demonstrate the mechanism clearly and they model a shape the product deliberately is not |
