## The Correction

The strategy brief written a few hours ago stated that a deck opening at the user need was missing and was the one to build next. The memo reports that one of the decks inside the vault already does this, correctly focusing on agents that are already deployed and already running, and on mapping what the agents can do against what we want them to do.

**That assertion was made from the public vault page, which lists no decks, and the decks are inside the vault where nothing without the key can read them.** The failure was not the missing information; it was stating a conclusion about a corpus of material after reading one page of it. The corpus's own rule applies, that a document written but not reachable is unreachable, and the correct move would have been to say that the presentations could not be read and ask, rather than to infer their absence.

So the position is better than this morning's brief described. The altitude-one artefact exists. What follows treats the problem as sequencing between the decks rather than a hole in the set.

## The Frame: We Have An Endgame Solution

The memo's framing is the most useful thing in it and it should be adopted as the standard way of describing this problem internally.

> **There is no point talking about how to kill the dragon until you reach that level.** We have a great solution, and it is a solution for somebody who has reached a certain point of a problem.

This is a better model than the altitudes offered this morning, and the difference matters. **Altitudes say the audience needs a shallower version of the same explanation. Levels say the audience has a different problem.** Somebody who does not know what their agents are doing is not looking for a simpler account of premium calculation; they are looking for a list of what their agents did. Serving them a simplified insurance model serves nobody.

The implication for every deck, every page and every conversation is the same: **find the level, answer that level's question, and end on the question that opens the next one.** Never answer the next level's question, because it has not been asked, and answering it is what produces the response that this is too much.

## The Level Map

Each level's solution creates the next level's problem. That is what makes the sequence a narrative rather than a menu, and it is why a customer can be moved along it without being sold to at every step.

| Level | The problem the customer actually has | What answers it | The question it creates, which is the next level |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | I do not know what my agents are doing | Observation. Passive mode, records, a status block | Some of this looks wrong, and I cannot say why |
| **2** | I can see it and I cannot say what wrong means | The grant measured, the mandate written, the delta rendered | The delta is enormous. What do I do about it? |
| **3** | The delta is enormous and I cannot close all of it | Bound the parts that matter. Bands, limits, refusals | Refusing stops work. Who says yes when somebody needs more? |
| **4** | A refusal needs a decision and a budget behind it | Draws, a named acceptor, a pool | Whose budget is this, and is the price right? |
| **5** | The pool is shared and mispriced | Premium, experience rating, the captive | This is the endgame and it is where the published deck opens |

**The published insurance deck is a level five artefact and it is correct.** It should not be simplified. It should be reached.

Two properties of this map are worth stating because they are what make it usable. **The customer arrives at each question on their own**, which is the pattern the memo reports from real conversations, so the deck's job is to recognise the question rather than to plant it. And **the value at every level stands alone**: a customer who stops at level one has visibility they did not have, and nothing about that is a partial version of something better.

## The Ladder And The Map Are The Same Ladder

The strategy brief this morning proposed an adoption ladder of rungs zero to five, describing how much of the model a customer implements. This memo describes a progression of problems. Placed side by side, they are one ladder seen from two sides.

| Level, from the customer's side | Rung, from ours |
|---|---|
| I do not know what they are doing | Observation only. Not a control |
| I cannot say what wrong means | An exposure statement |
| I cannot close the delta | A quota with an audit trail |
| Who says yes | A shared pool. Insurance begins |
| Is the price right | Premium and experience rating |

**Having written only our half is why the sequencing looked like a packaging problem.** From our side it reads as a decision about how much to deploy, which invites the question of what to leave out. From the customer's side it reads as a sequence of problems, each one arrived at honestly, which invites the question of what comes next. The second is the conversation we want, and the two columns should be maintained together from now on, because a rung with no matching level is a feature nobody has a reason to turn on.

## Attrition, And The Order Concepts Are Introduced In

The memo observes that some of these concepts are politically charged, carry attrition, and that this is why they have not been done. That is worth making concrete, because it produces the actual sequencing instruction and it is a better ordering principle than logic.

| Concept | Attrition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Observing what agents did | **None** | Nobody argues against visibility, and it changes nothing |
| The grant, measured | **None** | It is a measurement of what is already true |
| The mandate, written down | **Some** | Somebody has to state what they expected, and a written expectation can be wrong in public |
| A band, a limit, a refusal | **Real** | It stops work, and the people it stops have a strong voice |
| **A named acceptor on a draw** | **High** | A person's name goes on a decision about risk. This is the politically charged one |
| A premium that moves budget | **Highest** | It moves money between teams, which is the thing organisations argue about most |

**Introduce in that order, not in the order the model makes sense in.** The logical order would put the policy object early, because everything else refers to it. The attrition order puts it late, because it contains the two hardest things in the list.

The naming of an acceptor deserves separate attention, because it is the hinge. It is what turns a log line into an acceptance, it is what the auditor asks for, and it is the single element that makes the difference between a system that records and a system that decides. It is also the one an organisation will resist hardest, and no amount of good design removes that, because the resistance is to the accountability and not to the mechanism. The corpus already found the same shape in the difference between showback and chargeback, and the lesson there was that the reporting version is adopted and the charging version is fought over.

## What We Are Actually Selling, Stated Plainly

The memo is unambiguous and this should be written down so nobody softens it later.

**We are selling the risk mandate approach: the insurance model and the risk acceptance model.** The destination is not disguised. What is being designed is the route, and the route is a maturity model with deployment phases rather than a single proposition.

**The role is to facilitate, not to supply.** We provide the pieces required to make it happen and we always start with what the organisation already has, operating at the intersection of what they have and what they want to do. That intersection is the whole position, and it is what the dependency map from this morning renders concrete.

**The first conversion is adoption, not purchase.** The project is open source, and the immediate goal is that somebody uses the workflows to reduce the risk of the agents they already have. The early tagline is the right level one framing and should come back: **reduce the probability of catastrophic failure.** User first, customer second, and everything else follows from the first.

What is sold afterwards is named in the memo and is worth listing so the free and paid boundary is deliberate: hosted and web offerings, deployment into their environment, and above all **connectors to their world**, because execution depends on the tooling each organisation already has. The design partner relationship is how we learn what that tooling is and where the sweet spot sits.

And the delivery mechanism is settled: **tools, scripts, small applications, prompts and skills, delivered as vaults.** The licence-to-operate vault is the first instance of the pattern and not a one-off.

## What To Do With The Decks

Not a rewrite. A refactor, so that each deck serves one level and hands over cleanly.

1. **Mark each existing deck with its level.** The insurance deck is level five. The already-running deck is level one and part of two. That single act of labelling is most of the work, because it tells anybody presenting which one to open with.
2. **Make each deck end on the next level's question**, as a single closing slide, with no answer on it.
3. **Refactor around the four things a customer says they want**, which the memo lists: understand what the agents are doing, control what they are doing, understand the risks, and be sure they are operating within bounds. Those are levels one, three, two and four in the customer's own words, and using their words rather than ours is the point.
4. **Introduce our vocabulary one term per level**, always in the same words, so that grant, mandate, delta, draw, acceptor and premium accumulate rather than arrive together. Consistency of voice across every artefact matters more than the elegance of any single explanation.
5. **Keep the end-to-end demonstration**, because a thing that works whole is what makes the early levels credible. The rule is only that nobody is asked to buy the whole thing to stand on the first rung.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a review of the two decks.** They could not be read, and this brief converts the memo's judgement rather than assessing the slides.
- **Not a retreat from the insurance model.** The destination is stated plainly; only the route is being designed.
- **Not a pricing or packaging decision.** The levels describe problems and the rungs describe implementation, and what is charged for is a separate question.
- **Not a claim that customers move up the levels.** Most will stop, and stopping at level one is a success rather than a failed sale.
- **Not a sales methodology.** It names the sequence, the vocabulary rule and the attrition order, and stops.

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Which frame survives, levels or altitudes? | They answer different questions, one about the audience's problem and one about a document's depth, and keeping both requires saying which is which every time |
| Can a customer be at two levels at once? | Almost certainly, in different parts of the estate, and a deck cannot serve a split audience |
| What is the level one deliverable that costs us nothing? | A prompt and a skill, on the evidence of today's work, and it should be published and free |
| Where does the acceptor get introduced without a fight? | Possibly as a report first and a decision later, which is the showback to chargeback path |
| Do design partners get the endgame early? | They probably need it to judge whether to participate, which makes them the exception to the whole sequence |
| Is catastrophic failure the right level one word? | It is vivid and it is a fear appeal, and the corpus already found that high threat without efficacy produces denial |
