## What This Is

A specification for the observe-only mode the memo describes, and a correction to two of its assumptions: **the memo asks for a skill an agent can be given that records its own use of assets and capabilities, reports it in a schema other tools could adopt, and from that recording reverse-engineers a policy, its acceptable-use bands, its claims and its buffers, with the export dropped onto the vault built earlier today for analysis; the name is not new and neither is the mechanism, because passive mode was specified on 24 May for the reverse proxy, with a six-step workflow running from deploy through capture, visualise, derive, simulate and enable incrementally, and it was argued there as the commercial on-ramp precisely because it delivers visibility at zero risk, so this brief continues that work rather than starting it and the eighth instance of the corpus proposing something already specified is recorded as such; what is genuinely new is that the sensor has moved inside the thing being observed, since in May a proxy watched traffic from outside and now an agent watches itself from within, which makes every record self-reported in the evidence sense established this morning and means the schema must carry its tier in every record rather than in a footnote; the derivation was run against this session and it does not produce a policy, it produces a curve, because a band set at the median would draw on half of all actions and a band set near the maximum would never draw at all, and choosing the target draw rate between those is a business decision the tool must present and must not make; three platform artefacts were found in one session that would each have corrupted a naive derivation, namely a meter whose maximum is the platform's ceiling rather than the agent's behaviour, a unit whose granularity is set by how the harness records rather than by the work, and a meter that is bimodal because it is measuring two different activities that need two units rather than one band; the memo drops the fifth of May's six steps, simulation, and it is the one that makes a derived policy safe to enable, and it is nearly free here because the captured record is itself the replay corpus; the memo's own instinct to split this into two skills is right and the split should fall between a recorder that makes no judgements and is small enough to cost almost nothing, and an analyst that runs afterwards outside the session and can therefore be a script rather than a model; and the question of how cheap a model can be has two answers, since the arithmetic needs no model at all while the judgement about what mattered needs a good one, which means the recording runs on the cheapest thing available and the derivation runs on no model whatsoever.** New contributions: **the prior art located and the continuity stated, the sensor moving inside as the one real change, the band as a curve with the choice left to a person, three platform artefacts that corrupt derivation, simulation restored as the step that makes enabling safe, the two-skill split placed at the judgement boundary, and the overhead estimated with the method for measuring it properly.**

## The Name Is Not New, And Neither Is The Mechanism

Searching the corpus before naming anything found that **passive mode was specified on 24 May 2026**, across five briefs of the SG/Sentinel batch, with a debrief two days later and reviews from four roles. It carries a decision reference. Its definition then was logging only, zero side effects. Its argument then was that it is the safest first deployment and the strongest commercial on-ramp, because an operator gets visibility immediately and risks nothing.

And its mechanism then was exactly what the memo describes now, in six steps: deploy in passive mode, capture everything, visualise the surface, reverse-engineer the rules from what was captured, simulate the rules against the capture to see which would have side effects, and enable incrementally as confidence grows.

**This is the eighth instance of the corpus proposing work it has already specified.** The others are the commit queue, the registry, notary countersignatures, the accountant pack pipeline, the persona generator, the underwriting pillar, and the naming and power-of-attorney work. The pattern is not a failure; it is what a large corpus does, and the cost of not searching is that a second design diverges from the first and neither wins. So this brief continues May's design and inherits its vocabulary, including the six steps.

**What actually changed is the sensor.**

| | 24 May | 3 September |
|---|---|---|
| What observes | A reverse proxy in front of an application | A skill inside the agent |
| What is observed | Network traffic | The agent's own tool use, tokens, files and reach |
| Where the sensor sits | **Outside** the thing observed | **Inside** it |
| Evidence tier of the record | Observed. The proxy is not the application | **Self-reported.** The agent is the thing it is describing |
| Deployable to a stranger | Needs infrastructure in the path | **A skill, pasted.** Anybody can give it to their agents today |

The last row is why this is worth doing now and the fourth row is the price. The May design had a sensor that could not be wrong about the traffic because it was the traffic's path; this one has a sensor that is reconstructing its own behaviour from memory, and this morning's measurement put that reconstruction low by roughly four times on tokens. **So every record this mode emits carries its tier, in the record, not in a note**, and where a harness also exposes a transcript the recorder should prefer it and say so.

## The Derivation, Run Once, Against This Session

The memo asks for the policy to be reverse-engineered from usage: the acceptable-use criteria, the claims and the buffer. That was run here, against this session's own transcript, treating the size of each shell command as a stand-in for a metered resource.

```
   n = 112 actions,  total = 1,028,459 bytes

   band at      value    draws   draw rate    drawn total   buffer to max
   p50          1,218       55        49%        932,998          38,735
   p60          3,124       44        39%        839,742          36,829
   p70         16,594       33        29%        317,390          23,359
   p75         21,698       28        25%        158,934          18,255
   p80         24,267       22        20%         90,418          15,686
   p90         27,058       11        10%         37,274          12,895
   p95         28,446        6         5%         25,567          11,507
   p99         32,708        1         1%          7,245           7,245
```

**The output of a derivation is this table, and not a policy.** Every row is a defensible band. The row at the median would have half of all actions requesting a draw, which the pack's own rating rule already condemns, since a pool drawn on most days means the band is wrong. The row at the ninety-ninth percentile draws once in a hundred and therefore teaches nobody anything. Somewhere between them is a target draw rate that suits how much friction the business will accept, and **that number is a business decision the tool must present and must not choose.**

The buffer is the least intuitive column and the most useful. At a band on the ninetieth percentile the buffer to the largest observed action is 12,895 bytes, or about half the band again. A policy written with a per-occurrence limit equal to the band would have refused eleven real actions that were ordinary work.

## Three Platform Artefacts That Corrupt A Naive Derivation

All three were found in this one session, which suggests they are common rather than exotic.

**A meter whose maximum is a ceiling, not a behaviour.** Output tokens in this session run from a median of 1,468 to a maximum of 15,245, and that maximum is the platform's output cap. A per-occurrence limit derived from it is measuring the vendor, not the agent, and it will move when the vendor moves. **The recorder must mark any meter it believes is clipped**, and the analyst must refuse to derive a limit from a clipped meter without saying so.

**A unit whose granularity is the harness's, not the work's.** Tool calls per assistant turn in this transcript have a median of one and a maximum of one, because this harness records each tool call as its own turn. A policy in units of tool calls per turn would be measuring the recording format. **The recorder must state the granularity it is using**, and where a platform's turn boundary is an artefact the analyst should aggregate to something the business recognises, such as a task or a session.

**A meter that is bimodal because it is two activities.** The shell command sizes above jump from 3,124 bytes at the sixtieth percentile to 16,594 at the seventieth, a factor of five across one decile. That is not a long tail; it is two populations, small checks and large writes, sharing one meter. A single band serves neither. **The honest output is two units, not one band**, and the analyst should detect the split and propose the separation rather than average across it.

## The Step The Memo Drops

May's workflow has six steps and the memo describes five of them. The missing one is **simulate the derived rules against the captured record before enabling any of them**, and it is the step that makes the difference between a proposal and a safe proposal.

It is nearly free in this setting, because the captured record is already the replay corpus. A candidate policy is applied to every recorded action in order, and the output is the count of actions that would have been refused, drawn on, or passed, together with the specific actions that would have been refused so a person can look at them. **A derived policy is not offered until it has been replayed**, and the replay result travels with it.

That also gives the vault something to render that no live run can: the same record under three candidate policies, side by side, with the refusals highlighted.

## Two Skills, Split At The Judgement Boundary

The memo suggests this may be two skills, and wants both small. The right seam is not between generic and agent-specific; it is between what requires no judgement and what does.

| | **The recorder** | **The analyst** |
|---|---|---|
| Runs | Inside the session, continuously | Afterwards, outside the session |
| Job | Append one record per action. Nothing else | Read the records, derive the curve, detect artefacts, propose units, simulate |
| Judgement | **None.** It counts and names | Some, at the end, about what mattered |
| Size | As small as it can possibly be. This is a hard requirement | Irrelevant, because it is not in anybody's context |
| Model needed | The cheapest one that follows a format | **None.** It is arithmetic over a log and should be a script |
| Platform-specific | Yes, one thin variant per platform | No |

**The answer to the memo's question about how cheap a model can be is that the derivation needs no model at all.** Percentiles, draw counts, buffers, bimodality detection and replay are ordinary computation. What needs a capable model is the small judgement at the end, naming which assets mattered and whether anything happened outside the mandate, and that judgement is one paragraph over an already-summarised record rather than a running commentary.

So the ladder to test is not which model can run passive mode. It is: can the cheapest available model append a well-formed record for every action without drifting from the format? If yes, passive mode costs almost nothing, and the interesting comparison across ChatGPT, Claude, Cursor, Copilot and the rest becomes a comparison of format adherence and completeness rather than of intelligence.

## The Record, And Why It Is A Standard Worth Publishing

The memo wants a schema other tools can emit, so that in-line control tools already sitting in the path can produce records the same policies consume. That is the right ambition and it needs the schema to be small enough that a proxy can emit it without a parser.

```
usage-record/v1
{ "type": "usage-record/v1",
  "tier": "self-reported" | "observed",
  "source": "skill:passive-recorder" | "proxy:<name>" | "hook:<name>",
  "platform": "<harness and model>",
  "session": "<id or null>",
  "seq": n,
  "at": "<timestamp or turn index>",
  "granularity": "action" | "turn" | "task" | "session",
  "action": { "kind": "tool" | "request" | "write" | "read",
              "name": "<tool or verb>", "capability": "<capability id or null>" },
  "units": { "<unit>": { "amount": n, "clipped": true|false } },
  "assets": [ { "kind": "file"|"record"|"host"|"key", "ref": "<...>" } ],
  "outcome": "ok" | "error",
  "notes": null }
```

Four rules make it interoperable rather than merely shared. **Tier is mandatory**, because a proxy's record and an agent's record are different evidence and a policy derived from a mixture must say so. **Granularity is mandatory**, because of the second artefact above. **`clipped` is per unit**, because of the first. And **unit names are the pack's** wherever a pack unit exists, so that a derived policy is a `policy/v1` the existing evaluator can run without translation.

The output of a session is a directory of these records plus one derivation, and the derivation is a proposed `policy/v1` with its replay result attached and `published: false` on any rate table.

## The Handoff To The Vault

The memo's closing workflow is the right one and it now has somewhere to land, because the vault specified earlier today already holds terms in files and runs in the browser. The handoff:

1. An agent runs with the recorder skill for a day or a task.
2. The records are exported as one JSON file.
3. The person drags that file onto the vault page.
4. The vault reads it into the browser store as a run, exactly as if the viewer had clicked it, and shows the board.
5. The analyst's proposed policy appears beside the vault's own, with the curve, the detected units, and the replay result.
6. The person picks a band from the curve and sees the refusals it would have caused.

**Steps 3 and 4 need nothing new**, because a dropped file is just another way of filling the store that the two-store design already keeps in the browser. That is the second time today that design has absorbed a new requirement without changing, and it is worth noticing.

## The Overhead, Estimated, With The Method To Measure It

The memo asks that the overhead be captured, and it should be measured rather than estimated. An estimate to be checked: a compact record is on the order of a hundred tokens, and this session produced roughly 240 tool actions against about 1.47 million output tokens, which puts a per-action record near two percent. A per-turn status block on every one of 429 turns would be nearer four percent. A single session-end export is negligible.

**The measurement method is the only honest one: run the same task twice, once with the recorder and once without, and compare the totals from the transcript rather than from the agent.** The recorder must not report its own overhead, for the reason established this morning.

## What It Must Not Do

- **Not present a derived policy as a policy.** It is a proposal, with a curve, a replay result and a tier.
- **Not choose the band.** Present the curve; the target draw rate belongs to whoever accepts the friction.
- **Not derive a limit from a clipped meter** without marking it.
- **Not average across a bimodal meter.** Propose two units instead.
- **Not mix tiers silently.** A record set containing both proxy and agent records says so, and the derivation says which rows came from where.
- **Not enable anything.** Passive mode has zero side effects; that was the definition in May and it is the definition now.
- **Not grow.** The recorder's size is a hard requirement, and every field added to the record is paid for in every session that runs it.

## Questions To Settle Before Writing The Skills

| # | Question | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the minimum viable record? | Everything above is defensible and probably too much. Start with tier, granularity, action name, one unit and the assets, and add only what a derivation actually used |
| 2 | Does the recorder write per action or buffer to the end? | Per action survives a crash and costs more. Buffering is cheaper and loses the tail, which is where the interesting actions are |
| 3 | Where does the recorder write on each platform? | A temporary directory where a shell exists; the reply itself where none does, which is the expensive case and the common one |
| 4 | How is a capability id assigned to a tool call? | It is the join to the mandate and therefore to the scope check, and without it the record cannot answer whether an action was in scope |
| 5 | What is the target draw rate the analyst suggests as a default? | Something like one action in twenty, offered as a starting point with the curve beside it and no claim that it is right |
| 6 | Does the analyst detect bimodality automatically? | A jump of more than about three times across one decile is a usable first test, and it should be reported rather than acted on |
| 7 | Which platform is the second one? | The first is the one with a transcript, because only there can the self-report be checked. The second should be one with none, to see what is lost |

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a new mode.** Passive mode exists in this corpus since May; this specifies its agent-side sensor.
- **Not an enforcement point.** Zero side effects is the definition, and the enforcement work lives in hooks and in the pack.
- **Not a measurement of any platform.** One session on one platform produced the shapes above; the ladder is what produces comparisons.
- **Not a replacement for a proxy.** A sensor inside the agent sees intent and capability; a sensor in the path sees truth. The record carries both and marks which is which.
- **Not a finished schema.** It is small on purpose and question one asks for it to be made smaller.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| The sensor is inside the thing observed | It is what makes this deployable to a stranger today, and it is why the record can never be better than self-reported without a transcript beside it |
| A curve rather than a number | It is the honest output and it is worse demo material than a single confident band |
| A recorder that must stay tiny | Every useful field costs tokens in every session forever, and the pressure to add fields will be constant |
| Deriving a policy from observed usage | It encodes what the agent did as what the agent may do, so a period of bad behaviour becomes a generous policy, and a person has to look |
| Reusing May's name | It keeps one design instead of two, and it inherits a vocabulary built for network traffic that may not fit an agent's tool use everywhere |
| Simulation before enabling | It is the step that makes this safe and it is the step everybody skips, including the memo |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Is a period of observation ever long enough? | A policy derived from a quiet week refuses a busy one, and the analyst should report the observation window prominently rather than in a field |
| Should the derivation weight recent actions more? | Probably, and it introduces a parameter nobody can justify yet |
| Can a proxy and a skill record the same action and be reconciled? | The join key is the interesting engineering problem and the strongest possible validation of the self-report |
| What does passive mode do about actions it cannot see? | An agent's record has no row for what it did not notice, which is exactly the class that matters, and only an outside sensor can fill it |
| Does the ladder of models change the record or only its completeness? | Unknown, and the first comparison to run |
| Should the proposed policy be signed? | The pack's policies have a signature field left null; a proposal probably should not be signed at all |
