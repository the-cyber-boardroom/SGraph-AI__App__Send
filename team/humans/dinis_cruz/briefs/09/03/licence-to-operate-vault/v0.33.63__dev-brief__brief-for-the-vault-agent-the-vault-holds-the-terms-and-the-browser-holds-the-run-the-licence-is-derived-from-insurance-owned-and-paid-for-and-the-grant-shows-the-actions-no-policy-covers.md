## Two Data Stores, And Why The Split Is The Architecture

The vault will be published as an example on sgit.ai. Most people who open it will hold a read key and nothing else, and every one of them must experience the full workflow. That settles the storage design before any pane is drawn.

| Store | Holds | Written by | Lifetime |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The vault's JSON files** | The terms: grants, mandates, policies, scenarios, the rate table, the fixtures, the captions | The author, through commits. **Never the app** | Version-controlled. Every change is a commit with a diff, and every past version is in history |
| **The browser's local store** | The run: events, requests, decisions, premium movements, the selected mode and scenario, the licence inputs | The app, as the viewer's agents act | Per viewer, per device. Survives reload. Cleared by a reset button. Exportable as one JSON file |

The bridge provides `sg.state` for exactly this, device-local and never producing a vault commit, with a limit per key that the ledger must respect by chunking per scenario, and the app falls back to the browser's own storage where the bridge is absent so the vault still runs in the preview. **`app.json` requests no write grant at all**, which is the strongest possible statement that the terms are the author's and the run is the viewer's.

Three things follow, and the memo names all three. The vault serves any number of viewers with no calls back to it and no changes to its files. The scenarios and policies are versioned properly, so a scenario can be corrected, a policy can be superseded, and the diff shows what changed. And the app's inability to write is not a limitation to be worked around later; it is the pack's topology in miniature, where the issuer writes the terms and the insured can only append events, and here the insured's events do not even reach the vault.

**A viewer's agents run in that viewer's browser.** Every event on the board was caused by something the viewer clicked, and nothing on the board was caused by anybody else. That is what makes the vault an experience rather than a recording, and it is why the room on pki.sgit.ai, which is read-only and derived from real drains, is a different thing from this.

## Grant, Mandate, Policy, Licence: The Four Objects And Their Order

The memo draws the relationships precisely and the vault must render them precisely.

| Object | Is | Where it lives | Who writes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Grant** | Everything the agent can do. Measured, not authored. Larger than the mandate | `grants/<agent>.json` | The measurement, standing in for the registry |
| **Mandate** | What the user expects the agent to do. An allow-list, a subset of the grant. **This is what the policy insures** | `mandates/<agent>.json` | The user |
| **Policy** | The cover on that mandate: units, bands, limits, pool, exclusions, premium. Pinned to the mandate by its hash | `policies/<agent>/current.json` | The issuer |
| **Licence** | Derived, never stored. See below | Nowhere | Nobody |

**The grant contains actions no policy covers.** That is the whole point of rendering it. An action the agent can take, because it is in the grant, and should not, because it is outside the mandate, is uninsured by scope before any meter is read. The vault shows the grant, the mandate inside it, and the delta between them as a shaded region, exactly as the EXCESS board does, and every action names the capability it uses so that the viewer sees the capability light up either inside the mandate or in the delta.

### The licence, defined once

> **An agent holds a licence to operate when three things are true at the moment of asking: a mandate is in force for it; a policy covering that mandate is in force, with the current interval's premium paid; and its zone is not `outside`.** A licence is computed from the grant, the mandate, the policy and the ledger on every render. It is never written to either store, never cached across turns, and never displayed without the three facts it was derived from beside it.

The memo's stages map onto this with one addition the memo makes explicit, that the licence is directly related to the status of the insurance the agent and its user own and pay for:

| Memo's stage | What is true | Licence | What the agent may do |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal operation | Mandate in force, policy paid, zone `below` | held | Anything inside the mandate. Only the meter is recorded |
| Needs to claim, or claim made | Mandate in force, policy paid, zone `drawing` | held | Continue. A draw is recorded, or requested and waited on |
| No licence, by amount | Zone `outside`: over the limit, or the pool is exhausted | **withdrawn** | **Stop.** The next action is refused until reinstatement or a decision |
| No licence, by scope | The action's capability is in the grant and not in the mandate | **withdrawn** | **Stop.** An escalation is written. This is the unauthorised action, made visible |
| No licence, by lapse | The premium for this interval is unpaid, or the policy interval has ended | **withdrawn** | **Stop.** A full pool does not help, because nobody is covering it |

**The third, fourth and fifth rows all withdraw the licence, and they must look different on screen**, because they teach different things. Amount is the pool argument. Scope is the grant-and-mandate argument. Lapse is the memo's point that the licence is the insurance, owned and paid for.

## What To Build

One vault app, following the sgit.ai authoring contract exactly: a self-contained `index.html`, an `app.json`, a `content.json`, and data folders, with all styles and scripts inlined or loaded through the bridge, no external resources, readiness signalled with `sg-app-ready`, and every vault file read through `sg.vfs`. The Risk Mandate demo vault is the precedent for an application-grade vault app and should be studied before building.

### The panes

Six. The memo asked for five by name, and the grant and mandate delta is the sixth because the memo's last three points require it.

| Pane | Shows | Derived from |
|---|---|---|
| **The chat** | The simulated conversation. Each turn: the user's message, three candidate replies with the capability each uses and its declared cost per unit, the chosen reply, and what it actually cost | `scenarios/` |
| **The grant and the mandate** | Every capability in the grant, the mandate as the allow-list inside it, the delta shaded, and the capability of the current action lit wherever it falls | `grants/`, `mandates/` |
| **The policy** | For each unit: normal band, per-occurrence limit, pool and what remains with the reserve untouchable, exclusions with reasons, interval and reinstatement, the premium for this interval and whether it is paid, and the mandate hash it is pinned to | `policies/`, the browser store |
| **The insurer** | Pool per unit across every policy, reserve, premiums received, payouts, balance, and the line that appears when payouts exceed premiums | `policies/`, `rate_table.json`, the browser store |
| **The checks and the claims** | Every check with its verdict; every draw recorded with its acceptor; every request waiting; every refusal with the zone and the reason, amount or scope or lapse | The browser store |
| **The workflow** | The chain of steps for the current action, animated, one node lit at a time | The engine's step log |

**Zone and licence are shown on every pane**, not on one, and the licence always shows its three inputs.

### The chat, as a choose-your-own-adventure

Each turn offers exactly three replies. Against the current state, one stays inside the mandate and the normal band, one stays inside the mandate and draws, and the third goes outside cover, alternating between the two ways of doing so: by amount, over the limit or into an exhausted pool, and by scope, using a capability in the grant and not in the mandate. The viewer clicks one. **Costs and capabilities are not hidden**: each option shows its capability and its declared cost per unit before it is chosen, and in out-of-band mode shows its actual cost only after.

Two units to start, both made up for the simulation:

| Unit | Meter, in words a reader can re-run | Example normal band | Example per-occurrence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `tokens` | The token count the simulated reply declares, standing in for the four counters the transcript records | 2,000 per turn | 8,000 |
| `customer_records` | The number of customer records the simulated query returns | 5 per turn | 20 |

The memo's own example is the second unit: an agent should not touch more than five records at a time, and a reply that returns ten should light up. A reply that emails the customer list is the scope case: the capability `mail:send` is in the grant because the agent has a mail client, and not in the mandate because the user never expected it, and no meter reading is needed to refuse it.

The replies are hard-coded in `scenarios/*.json`. The plumbing to make them live already exists, since `sg.llm.chat` is available under a grant and `sg.llm.usage` returns counters, and **the second increment replaces the scripted reply with a live one and the declared cost with the measured one**. Design the engine so that swap is one function.

### The two check modes

A toggle, visible, defaulting to in-line.

| Mode | When the check runs | What the verdict decides | What it catches |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In-line** (before) | On the declared capability and cost, before the reply is produced | Whether the action runs at all. `refused` means it does not | The known-cost case, and the pre-commit pattern the pack already enforces |
| **Out-of-band** (after) | On the measured cost, after the reply is produced. The scope check still runs before, because a capability is known before it is used | Whether the licence survives into the next action. A draw is recorded; an overrun withdraws the licence for what comes next | **Escalation.** An agent does not go from zero to the whole database in one step; it goes five, ten, forty. Out-of-band catches the slope |

Both must be built, both visualised, and the workflow pane must show they are different chains. Note the asymmetry, which is real and worth a caption: **scope can always be checked before, amount sometimes only after.**

### The engine

**Two checks in a fixed order, and only one of them is new.** First the scope check: is the action's capability in the mandate? If not, `refused` with reason `outside_mandate`, an escalation written, the licence withdrawn, and the meter never read. Then, and only then, the pack's verdict on the amount. The scope check is the mandate's evaluation, which the 26 August brief on the permissions file placed with an allow-list evaluator, and it is deliberately not part of the pack's verdict function so that the second check stays a port and not a redesign.

**One verdict function, two languages, one set of fixtures.** The pack ships `tools/policy.py` with a verdict function and an acceptance run. Port that function to JavaScript inside the app, and put the pack's acceptance cases into `tests/fixtures/*.json` so that the same inputs produce the same verdicts in both. If the port disagrees with the Python on any fixture, the port is wrong until proven otherwise, and the disagreement is reported rather than smoothed. Add fixtures for the scope check and the lapse check beside them, clearly marked as this vault's and not the pack's.

The verdict on amount is subtraction, as the lexicon says: below the band is `normal`; over the band, inside the limit, pool sufficient is `drawn`; over the limit, or pool insufficient, or a request pending is `refused`, with `requested` when the amount is above the requested-draw threshold and no decision exists yet.

**The unit of composition is the agent engine.** Each instance holds references to its grant, mandate and policy in the vault, its own ledger lane in the browser store, its premium state, and derives its zone and licence. The insurer is a separate object holding the pool, the reserve, the premiums received and the payouts made, also in the browser store. The app instantiates one engine today. It must be able to instantiate a hundred, each with its own scenario and lane, without either object changing shape.

### The premium, and paying it

The memo is explicit that the licence follows the insurance owned and paid for, so paying is a visible act. The policy pane shows the premium for the current interval and whether it has been paid, and the insurer pane shows it received. Two controls sit beside it: **pay**, which the policyholder does at the start of an interval, and **lapse**, which stops paying, and the second exists so that the viewer can see a licence withdrawn with a full pool and every meter green. The premium is paid in the unit of account from the policyholder's allocation, which is the 26 August position that premium is paid in allocation from a finite pool, and the allocation is a number in the browser store that goes down when the premium is paid and down again when a draw is recorded against the policyholder.

### The financial pane, and the rate table

The pack forbids converting between units unless a rate table is published, so this vault publishes one:

```
rate_table.json
{ "published": true, "owner": "the simulation", "unit_of_account": "cr",
  "rates": { "tokens": 0.001, "customer_records": 2.0 },
  "note": "for the simulation only. the pilot policy's rate table is unpublished and stays so" }
```

Name the unit of account whatever reads well; `cr` is a placeholder and it must not be called dollars. With it, the pane shows the premium for this interval, raised by the rating step on every draw recorded; premiums received; payouts, as drawn amounts converted at the table; balance; and **the line**, shown when the balance goes negative: the insurer is losing money on this policy.

The rating step encodes the pack's rule that experience rating lands on the policyholder and that draw frequency is the metric. A multiplicative step per draw is fine for the first increment. The pack's own rule, that a pool drawn on most days has the wrong band and the answer is to raise the band and shrink the pool rather than enlarge it, becomes a **repricing event** the viewer can trigger, and here the two-store split matters: repricing writes a superseding policy, and policies live in the vault, which the app cannot write. So repricing in the browser produces a **proposed** policy in the browser store, rendered beside the current one with its diff, and a caption saying that in the real topology the issuer commits it. That is not a workaround; it is the correct picture of who may change the terms.

**The insurer's pool is shared.** Every policy draws from it, and with one agent the viewer already sees that this agent's draw reduces what every other agent could have used. It becomes the whole point when the engine count goes to a hundred.

### The workflow pane

A chain, drawn once, lit step by step, with the scope check as the first node in both modes:

```
IN-LINE       request -> capability named -> SCOPE CHECK -> [outside mandate: stop, escalate]
                      -> declare cost -> AMOUNT CHECK -> verdict -> [refused: stop]
                      -> [normal | drawn | requested] -> event appended -> pool updated
                      -> premium repriced -> zone -> licence -> reply produced

OUT-OF-BAND   request -> capability named -> SCOPE CHECK -> [outside mandate: stop, escalate]
                      -> reply produced -> meter read -> AMOUNT CHECK -> verdict
                      -> event appended -> pool updated -> premium repriced -> zone -> licence
                      -> [outside: next action refused]
```

Four controls, and the memo asks for all four: **next step**, **run turn**, **run scenario**, **run all**. Every control writes the same events as every other, so a viewer who clicks through by hand and one who presses run all end with the same ledger in their store.

### The files

```
index.html                     the app, self-contained
app.json                       entry, title, hud. permissions: fs read only. no write grant. llm none in v1
content.json                   captions, and the memo's stage names beside the pack's zone names
rate_table.json                published, simulation only

```

A viewer can export the browser store as one JSON file and import it again, which is how a run is shared with somebody else without the vault changing. **The vault's files are the terms; the export is the story.**

## The Acceptance Script

The vault is not done until a viewer with no briefing and a read key can click through this and see each outcome. Write it as `scenarios/acceptance.json` and keep it first in the list.

| Step | Viewer does | Vault shows |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opens the vault with a read key | One agent, grant and mandate drawn with the delta shaded, zone `below`, licence held with its three inputs, pool full, premium paid for the interval, insurer balance at the premium received |
| 2 | Picks the inside-band reply | `normal`. Its capability lights inside the mandate. Nothing appended beyond the meter. Silence, and a caption saying silence is a requirement |
| 3 | Picks the drawing reply | `drawn`. Event appended with the policyholder as acceptor. Pool down. Premium up. Zone `drawing`. Licence held |
| 4 | Picks the drawing reply twice more | Two more draws. Insurer balance moving toward zero. The pane says the agent is getting more expensive |
| 5 | Picks the outside-by-amount reply | `refused`, reason amount. Zone `outside`. **Licence withdrawn.** Chat input disables. Escalation written. Workflow stops at the amount check |
| 6 | Resets, then picks the outside-by-scope reply | `refused`, reason `outside_mandate`. The capability lights in the delta. **Licence withdrawn with every meter green.** Escalation written. Workflow stops at the scope check, and the meter was never read |
| 7 | Resets, presses lapse, picks the inside-band reply | `refused`, reason lapse. **Licence withdrawn with a full pool.** The policy pane shows the premium unpaid |
| 8 | Pays, then switches to out-of-band and reruns 2 to 5 | The same replies produce the same events, but the outside-by-amount reply **runs** and the licence is withdrawn for the action after it, in those words. The scope reply is still stopped before |
| 9 | Triggers reinstatement | Pool refills per the interval. Zone returns to `below`. Premium does not return to its opening value, because experience rating landed |
| 10 | Triggers repricing | A proposed superseding policy appears in the browser store beside the current one, with its diff, and the caption says the issuer commits it |
| 11 | Presses run all | Every scenario plays. The final store equals the one produced by clicking through, event for event |
| 12 | Reloads the page | The run is still there. Presses reset, and it is gone, and the vault's files are unchanged |
| 13 | Opens the fixtures | Every pack case passes in the JavaScript port, every vault case passes, and the pane shows both counts separately |

Steps 11 and 13 prove the engine is deterministic and is the pack's; step 12 proves the two-store split; steps 5, 6 and 7 prove the three ways a licence is withdrawn look different.

## What The Vault Must Not Do

- **Not write to the vault.** No write grant in `app.json`. The terms are the author's.
- **Not store a licence.** Anywhere, in either store. It is derived on every render from the grant, the mandate, the policy, the premium state and the ledger.
- **Not show a silent draw.** If it was not appended as an event in the browser store, it did not happen.
- **Not read a meter before the scope check.** An action outside the mandate is refused on its capability, and the amount is never consulted.
- **Not convert units without the published rate table**, and not let that table's existence leak into any claim about the pilot policy.
- **Not call a live model in the first increment.** `llm` stays ungranted until the second increment.
- **Not model claims that go unpaid.** The memo defers that. In this increment a refusal withdraws the licence and writes an escalation; who bears an uninsured loss is the next brief.
- **Not invent a second amount evaluator.** Port the pack's, test against its fixtures, report disagreement.

## The Second Increment, Named So It Is Not Guessed

Once the acceptance script passes: grant `llm`, replace the scripted reply with `sg.llm.chat`, replace the declared token cost with `sg.llm.usage`, keep `customer_records` scripted because there is no real database behind it, and keep the scope check exactly as it is because a live model's capability use is declared by the tools it is offered. At that point in-line mode has a real problem, which is that the token cost is not known before the call, and the vault will show why out-of-band exists rather than argue it. Then the engine count goes up, the games' rosters become the agents, and the insurer pane becomes the CAPTIVE board with real events under it.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not the pack.** The pack defines the objects; this vault renders and exercises them, and any disagreement is resolved in the pack's favour.
- **Not the room.** The pack's room is read-only and derived from real ledger drains. This vault is interactive and simulated, in the viewer's browser, and it says so in its title.
- **Not a real enforcement point.** The git hooks fail closed and this app fails however a browser fails. It demonstrates; it does not enforce.
- **Not a game.** EXCESS and CAPTIVE have dice. This has terms in a vault and a run in a browser, and nothing happens that a click did not cause.
- **Not multi-agent yet.** The shape is built for it and the render is not.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| The run lives in the browser | It is what makes a read-only example work for everyone, and it means two viewers can never see the same run without exporting it |
| A published rate table | It makes the financial pane possible and it is exactly what the pack refuses for the pilot, so the disclaimer is permanent |

| The grant is made up | It has to be, until the registry measures a real one, and a made-up grant teaches the shape while a measured one would teach the surprise |
