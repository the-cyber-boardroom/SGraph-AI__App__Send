# One Measurement, Five Artefacts: The Audiences Do Not Want Different Assessments, They Want Different Renderings Of One, The Interchange Schemas For Both Ends Already Exist As Standards So A Third Should Not Be Minted, And Every Stage Of The Simple To Complex Progression Must Produce A Finished Thing Rather Than A Partial One

**date** 5 September 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Product, Design, Engineering, Ambassador

**type** Research brief

*Second of 5 September, and a completion of the research brief written on 4 September, which covered the precedents, the pacing and the individual to corporate ladder and under-served three things the memo asked for: the audiences, the progression from a simple first use to a complex one, and the schemas with their import and export, which the memo singled out. Two standards were read for this rather than recalled, and both turn out to be the right answer for one end of the problem each, which makes this the third time in three days that the honest recommendation is to adopt a published vocabulary rather than mint one. Limitation: the two standards were read from their own specifications and neither has been implemented against, so the mapping proposed below is a reading rather than an integration.*

## What This Is

The three parts the earlier research left thin, researched properly: **the audiences are not a ladder and treating them as one is the mistake that would break this product, because a person asking whether they are exposed, an engineer deciding whether a change ships, a risk function deciding what goes in a register, an assessor deciding whether evidence is sufficient and an executive deciding whether to spend are all asking about the same measurement and every one of them wants a different artefact out of it, so the design rule is one measurement and five renderings, and the failure mode is five measurements that disagree; the schemas the memo singled out already exist, one for each end, since the assessment and compliance world has a machine-readable standard whose layers stack as controls, then implementation, then assessment, with a catalog model that defines controls, a profile model that selects and tailors them, an assessment results model carrying when an assessment ran and what evidence was collected, and a plan of action model carrying the findings somebody has to fix, which is almost exactly the object chain this estate has built by hand over three weeks, while the engineering world has an interchange format for tool results that code hosts consume natively so that findings appear where developers already look; the right move is therefore to hold one internal shape and emit both, which makes the raw findings the source of truth and the two standards renderings rather than competitors, and it means neither audience is asked to learn our vocabulary before they can use our output; the progression from simple to complex is a separate axis from individual to corporate and the earlier brief conflated them, since one person can go from a two-minute question to a wired-up pipeline without ever becoming an organisation, and the rule that makes that progression survivable is that every stage produces a finished artefact rather than a partial one, because a tool that says come back when you have done step four loses everybody at step three; the hardest stage is not technical, since writing down what an agent was actually expected to do is an authoring act nobody has done before and no tool can do for them, and it sits in the middle of the progression where most people stop; and import matters as much as export, because a risk function that cannot bring its existing register in will not adopt a tool that produces a second one.** New contributions: **the five audiences with the artefact each actually wants, the finding that the same measurement must render five ways or diverge, the two existing standards mapped onto this estate's own objects, the recommendation to emit both rather than mint a third, the progression as an axis separate from the ladder with a finished artefact at every stage, and the mandate step named as the place people stop.**

## The Audiences: One Measurement, Five Artefacts

The memo names corporate, risk functions, risk agencies, risk teams, internal teams, users, small companies and large ones. Grouped by what they want out, rather than by who they are, that is five.

| Audience | The question | The artefact they want | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The individual** | Am I exposed, and what do I do about it? | **A verdict and a fix**, on one screen | Nowhere. It is read and acted on |
| **The engineer** | Does this change make it worse? | **Findings in a machine format**, and an exit code | A pipeline, and the code host's own view |
| **The risk function** | What goes in the register, owned by whom, accepted by whom? | **Register entries** with an owner, a date and an acceptance | The register they already run |
| **The assessor** | Is this evidence sufficient, and can I trace it? | **Assessment results** with provenance, tier and dates | A report to somebody else |
| **The executive** | Should I care, and what would it cost to change? | **One line in context**, and a cost of the fix | A slide, and a budget conversation |

Two rules fall out and they are the whole section.

**They want the same measurement.** None of these is a different assessment. An engineer's finding and a risk function's register row and an assessor's evidence bundle are the same fact rendered three ways, and if they are produced by three different code paths they will disagree, at which point the tool has created work rather than removed it.

**So the artefact chain has one source.** Raw findings are the truth, and everything else is a projection of them with nothing added that is not derivable:

```
   probes  ->  findings  ->  +-- a verdict            (the individual)
                             +-- a machine format     (the engineer)
                             +-- register entries     (the risk function)
                             +-- assessment results   (the assessor)
                             +-- one line and a cost  (the executive)
```

**Nothing may be authored in a projection.** If a register entry needs an owner and the findings do not carry one, the owner is an input to the chain rather than something the register view invents, or the projections drift the first time somebody edits one.

### The two that are usually served worst

**The risk function is served worst by tools like this**, because most produce a report and a register needs rows: an entry, an owner, a date, a state, and an acceptance by a named person. A tool that emits a PDF has handed the risk function a transcription job, which is why those tools end up used once.

**The executive is served worst by scores.** A number with no scale means nothing to somebody deciding whether to fund something, and what they need is the shape of the decision: this is what we carry, this is what it would cost to reduce, this is what remains if we do. That is derivable from the findings and it is not a score.

### The one that is different in kind

**A risk agency or consultancy is not an audience, it is a multiplier.** They want everything above, for many clients, repeatably, under their own name, without the clients' data crossing. That is a deployment shape rather than a rendering, and it is the same requirement as the enterprise tier of the precedent studied yesterday: the same product, at multiplicity, deployable under somebody else's brand.

## The Schemas Already Exist, One For Each End

The part the memo singled out, and the research answer is that we should mint neither.

### For the assessor and the risk function

The assessment and compliance world already has a machine-readable standard, and its structure is uncomfortably close to what this estate has built by hand.

| Its layer | Its models | What we already have |
|---|---|---|
| **Control** | A **catalog** model, since "Controls used in any other OSCAL model must first be defined in this model", and a **profile** model "for selecting, organizing, and tailoring a specific set of controls" | The AIUC-1 catalogue, and the customisation-by-capability that the maturity brief called a custom standard |
| **Implementation** | A **system security plan**, and a **component definition** "intended to define information about an individual component" | The profiles and per-tool grants of the repository brief |
| **Assessment** | An **assessment plan**, an **assessment results** model representing "information produced from a set of assessment activities, to include when the assessment was performed, the assessment scope, evidence collected", and a **plan of action and milestones** carrying "a set of findings ... that need to be addressed" | The conformance layer, its attestations with tiers and dates, and the unevidenced map |

And the stacking rule is stated in one sentence: **"Each lower layer in the stack provides information structures that are referenced and used by each higher layer."** That is the same layering this estate arrived at independently, with the catalogue underneath, the grant and mandate above it, and conformance above that.

**There is also a control mapping model** for "relationships among controls and control elements, describing how controls and requirements relate to one another across authoritative sources", which is the crosswalk work, standardised.

**So the recommendation is to emit assessment results and a plan of action in that standard**, and to treat its profile model as the shape for a customised standard rather than inventing one. A risk function or assessor asked to consume our output then consumes something their existing tooling may already read, and we are not asking them to learn our vocabulary as the price of using the tool.

### For the engineer

The engineering world has an interchange format for tool results that code hosts consume natively, so uploaded results appear in the developer's existing view rather than in a second place. The relevant property is not the format's expressiveness; it is that **it is already where developers look**.

**So emit that too**, from the same findings, and the engineering audience gets results in the surface they already use with no new dashboard to adopt.

### The rule this makes explicit

Three days, three times: anchor to a published vocabulary rather than minting one. The probe and finding words yesterday, and now the interchange formats at both ends. **The internal shape is ours and every external shape is somebody else's**, which also means our schema decisions stop being load-bearing for adoption.

| Layer | Owner |
|---|---|
| Probes and their runs | Ours, and it is the only thing that must be invented |
| Findings | The published probe-and-finding shape, extended with reversibility and tier |
| Engineering output | The tool results interchange format |
| Assessor and risk output | The assessment results and plan of action models |
| Register rows | The customer's register format, which is neither of the above and is usually a spreadsheet |

That last row is the one nobody standardised and it is the one the risk function actually uses, so **a comma-separated export with a documented column set is not a fallback, it is a first-class output.**

## Import Matters As Much As Export

Under-served everywhere, including in the earlier brief, and it decides adoption for the audience with the most budget.

**A risk function that cannot bring its existing register in will not adopt a tool that produces a second register.** So the imports that matter:

| Import | Why |
|---|---|
| **An existing register** | So findings attach to entries somebody already owns, rather than creating parallel entries |
| **An existing control set or profile** | So an organisation with its own controls sees its own words rather than ours |
| **A previous run** | So the output is a diff rather than a snapshot, which is where the value compounds |
| **Somebody else's findings** | So a tool an organisation already runs feeds this one, which is the composability moat stated as a file format |

**The previous-run import is the one to build first**, because it turns every subsequent use into a comparison, and a comparison is what makes somebody return.

## The Progression Is An Axis, Not The Ladder

The earlier brief conflated two things. Individual to corporate is about **how many** subjects. Simple to complex is about **how deep** one subject goes, and a single person can traverse all of it without ever becoming an organisation.

| Stage | Time | What it costs | The finished artefact it produces |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1 Answer questions** | Two minutes | Nothing. No install | A guess at your grant, and a line you could share |
| **2 Run one probe** | Ten minutes | One command | **A measured fact** about one tool |
| **3 Map your profiles** | Half an hour | Naming what you use | Your grant, measured, across your tools |
| **4 Write the mandate** | An hour, and it is the hard one | **Deciding what you meant** | Your delta |
| **5 Wire it in** | A day | A pipeline change | Findings on every change, and a diff |
| **6 Register and acceptance** | A quarter | **Organisational, not technical** | An owned, accepted risk with a date |

**The rule that makes this survivable: every stage produces a finished artefact.** Not a partial one, not a teaser. Somebody who stops at stage two has a measured fact they did not have, and it is worth having on its own. A tool that says come back when you have done stage four loses everybody at stage three, which is the standard failure of assessment products.

### Stage four is where people stop, and it is not technical

Stages one to three are measurement and a tool can do them. **Stage four asks somebody to write down what they actually expected the agent to do, and nobody has ever written that down.** It is an authoring act, it surfaces disagreement between people who thought they agreed, and no amount of engineering removes it.

Two consequences. **The elicitation is the product's hardest design problem**, harder than any probe, and it should get proportionate attention rather than being a form at the end. And **stage three must be worth having without stage four**, because a large fraction of users will never do it, and a grant with no mandate is still a list of things you did not know you had granted.

### Where the two axes cross

| | One subject | Many subjects |
|---|---|---|
| **Shallow** | An individual with a question. The game and one probe | A team wanting a quick census. Probes across many tools |
| **Deep** | An individual who wired it into their own pipeline | **The corporate case**: register, acceptance, and a watch on the ground moving |

**Most products assume the diagonal and lose the two off-diagonal cells.** The deep individual is who builds the pipeline integration and tells their colleagues, and the shallow census across many subjects is the first thing a large organisation actually does. Both should work.

## What To Build, In This Order

| # | Step | Serves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The findings shape, extended with reversibility and tier | Everything downstream |
| 2 | The verdict projection | The individual, stage one to three |
| 3 | The engineering interchange emitter | The engineer, and results where they already look |
| 4 | The previous-run import and the diff | Everybody, and it is what makes people return |
| 5 | The register export as a documented column set | The risk function, and it is a spreadsheet |
| 6 | The assessment results and plan of action emitters | The assessor, and it is the highest-value paid audience |
| 7 | The register import | The organisation, and it decides whether the tool is adopted or run once |
| 8 | The executive projection: what we carry, what a fix costs, what remains | The budget conversation |

**Steps 1 to 4 are the whole free product.** Five and six are what an organisation needs. Seven is what makes them keep it.

## What This Must Not Do

- **Not produce five measurements.** One source, five projections, and nothing authored in a projection.
- **Not mint an interchange format.** Two exist and both are consumed by tooling our audiences already run.
- **Not hand a risk function a report.** They need rows with owners and acceptances, and a report is a transcription job.
- **Not show an executive a score.** A number with no scale cannot support a spending decision.
- **Not gate a stage behind a later one.** Every stage produces a finished artefact.
- **Not treat the mandate step as a form.** It is an authoring act and the hardest thing in the product.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not an implementation of either standard.** They were read, not built against, and the mapping is a reading.
- **Not a complete audience taxonomy.** Five groupings by wanted artefact, which cuts across job titles deliberately.
- **Not a claim that any audience will adopt this.** The artefacts are what they use today; whether they want ours is untested.
- **Not a replacement for the earlier research.** That covered the precedents, the pacing and the ladder, and this covers the three parts it left thin.
- **Not a schema specification.** It says which schemas to emit and what our internal shape must carry, and stops there.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| One measurement, five renderings | It is the only way they stay consistent and it means every new audience is a projection to build and maintain |
| Emitting two external standards | It removes an adoption barrier and it ties our releases to two other bodies' versioning |
| A spreadsheet as a first-class output | It is what risk functions actually use and it is the least impressive thing we could ship |
| Every stage finished | It is what stops the funnel leaking and it means early stages must be genuinely useful, which is harder than a teaser |
| Naming stage four as the hard one | It is honest and it tells a prospective user the product cannot do the most valuable part for them |
| Import as much as export | It decides corporate adoption and it is work with no demo value |
