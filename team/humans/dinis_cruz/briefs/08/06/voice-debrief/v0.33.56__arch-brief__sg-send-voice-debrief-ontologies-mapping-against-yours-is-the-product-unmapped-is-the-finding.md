# Voice Debrief Ontologies: Extracting One Is Commodity, Mapping Against Yours Is The Product, And What Fails To Map Is The Finding

**date** 6 August 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Engineering, Product

**type** Architecture brief

*Sixteenth of 6 August. Records the project rename. Ontology learning and matching research is grounded and cited, including a finding that determines how the matching step should be built. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

## What This Is

Two related features for the renamed product, which are not equally valuable and should not be built as one thing: **extracting a concept structure from a recording is straightforward and close to commodity, since every assistant does topic extraction and a freely generated structure will name the same idea differently in two recordings, producing a set of mutually incompatible little ontologies that accumulate into nothing; mapping a recording against a scheme the customer supplies is the valuable half, because the output speaks their vocabulary, stays consistent across recordings, indexes a growing corpus against a stable spine, and produces a finding at exactly the point where content fails to map, which is either a genuinely new concept or a misunderstanding and is worth surfacing either way; the format symmetry the memo insists on is the constraint that makes the loop close, since extract, feed back, map, refine and feed back again only works if input and output are the same shape, and the shape was settled in the companion brief written an hour ago as a concept carrying preferred and alternative labels with broader, narrower and related relations; the matching step is where the engineering is, and the literature is clear that language models function best as recommenders rather than as deterministic aligners, with the effective arrangement being a cheap exact matcher first, embedding retrieval for candidates, the model only on the ambiguous remainder, and rule-based filters after, together with the measured finding that presenting five concepts at a time outperforms ten because the model holds context better; the chat surface should emit operations on the scheme rather than replacement schemes, because a sequence of merges and relabelings is inspectable, reversible and attributable while a wholesale rewrite is none of those; and the local storage suggestion is right and the reuse is confirmed, since the virtual file system exists and is documented as working memory for a model, along with a commit queue built for precisely the many small writes that chat-driven editing produces.** It is the sixteenth document of 6 August (cross-ref: the v0.33.56 concepts-not-words brief, the v0.33.56 flow MVP brief, the v0.33.56 payment-to-funded-key brief, the v0.33.55 graph-canvas brief, and the v0.33.53 paragraph-as-bow-tie brief). New contributions: **the two directions separated by value, anchored rather than free extraction, the hybrid matching arrangement with the model as recommender, unmapped content named as the finding, chat emitting operations rather than schemes, and the ontology identified as the artefact that makes the product sticky.**

## The Rename

Recorded because it affects everything downstream. The project lead: **"voice debrief is going to be the new name of the project, and it's going to be voice debrief for WhatsApp, but we're going to support basically multiple voice debriefs."**

So the product is Voice Debrief, the current one is the WhatsApp variant, and the plural is deliberate. Worth propagating through the repository, the interface strings and the concept scheme being built in the companion brief, before more of each accumulates under the old name.

## Two Directions, Unequal Value

The memo is explicit that these are two things. The project lead: **"it's actually two things: is the creation of an ontology and taxonomy from an audio that being processed, but also to be able to consume and provide an ontology and taxonomy."**

They are worth separating further, because one is a feature and the other is a position.

**Extraction alone is close to commodity.** Producing a concept map from a recording is something any capable model does with a prompt, and its weakness is specific: nothing anchors the names. Two recordings about the same project will call the same idea by different names, produce different hierarchies, and merge into nothing. Research on this notes that traditional automated approaches extract an excessive number of concepts and produce output that is hard to use, and free generation inherits that.

**Mapping against a supplied scheme is the valuable half**, and it is rare. The output uses the customer's own vocabulary, stays consistent from one recording to the next, indexes an accumulating corpus against a stable spine, and, most usefully, fails informatively.

```
   EXTRACT ALONE                    MAP AGAINST THEIRS
   fresh names each time            their vocabulary, every time
   two recordings, two ontologies   fifty recordings, one index
   nothing accumulates              the corpus becomes searchable
                                    by concepts they already use
   no failure signal                unmapped content is a FINDING
```

## What Fails To Map Is The Output

Worth stating as the design principle, because it is the fourth instance of the same pattern in this corpus and it keeps being the most valuable part.

When a recording contains something the supplied scheme has no concept for, that is not an error to be smoothed over. It is either a concept the organisation has and has not written down, or a misunderstanding, and both are worth knowing. The other three instances were unanswered stakeholder questions on 31 July, disagreeing transcriptions this morning, and diverging locale graphs an hour ago. **In each case the gap was more informative than the match.**

Practically that means the mapping output has three parts rather than one: what mapped, what mapped only approximately, and what did not map at all. The third is the one to put in front of a person.

## Format Symmetry Is What Closes The Loop

The memo states the constraint correctly and it is load-bearing. The project lead: **"which of course should be in the same format as the one that we can create for a particular voice memo."**

That symmetry is what turns two features into a cycle: extract from the first few recordings, review the result, feed it back as the supplied scheme, map subsequent recordings against it, refine through chat, feed the refinement back. Break the symmetry and each stage becomes a conversion.

The shape is already settled. The companion brief written an hour ago adopts a concept model in which meaning lives in a language-independent concept carrying a preferred label, alternative labels, definitions, scope notes and relations to broader, narrower and related concepts. **The same model serves here**, which means the interface vocabulary scheme and a customer's domain ontology are the same kind of object, and one visualisation, one editor and one storage format cover both.

## Extraction Should Be Anchored, Not Free

Following from the above, the extraction direction should not generate from nothing after the first time.

The right shape is: extract freely only when no scheme exists, and thereafter **extract by merging into the scheme that already exists**. That converts an accumulation problem into a matching problem, which is a solved-ish field rather than an open one.

The literature on collaborative ontology development describes exactly this division, placing language models as a copilot inside an expert-driven iterative workflow, handling the extraction and conceptualisation of structures from source texts and supporting reuse through concept alignment and taxonomy matching, with the human deciding.

## The Matching Step Is Where The Engineering Is

And the literature contains a finding that should shape the build. Work on aligning data to ontologies concludes that language models **function best as recommendation tools rather than as deterministic alignment methods**, and separate work on incremental ontology updates concludes that a hybrid approach combining a model with rule-based validation is necessary for accuracy.

So the model should not be asked to do the matching. It should be asked to adjudicate what cheaper methods could not resolve:

```
   1. EXACT MATCHER          normalised label comparison, lowercase,
                             punctuation stripped. Cheap, high precision.
                             Resolves most of it.

   2. EMBEDDING RETRIEVAL    candidates for what remains

   3. THE MODEL              only the ambiguous candidates, and given
                             the concept's definition rather than its
                             label alone

   4. FILTERS                cardinality and consistency rules over
                             the result

   5. THE PERSON             everything still unresolved, plus
                             everything that mapped to nothing
```

Two further findings worth building in. **Definitions improve matching**: one approach enriches concepts by generating textual definitions before retrieval, combining embedding similarity, lexical precision and model judgement, which is an argument for the scope notes the companion brief already requires. And **explanations are expensive**: generating a justification for every candidate has been reported to produce runtimes measured in days, so explanations should be generated only for the final alignment or on request.

There is also a measured detail worth adopting directly. Work on indexing learning content against an ontology found that **presenting five concepts at a time consistently outperformed ten**, because fewer concepts per batch let the model hold context and reduced the signal-to-noise ratio. So the mapping step should window the scheme into small slices rather than presenting it whole, which also keeps the cost per recording bounded as a customer's ontology grows.

## Chat Should Emit Operations, Not Ontologies

The memo is enthusiastic about this surface and is right to be. The project lead: **"we could use the chat to refine, to improve, to basically sort of manipulate a little bit the ontology, and this is actually going to be a really powerful example of the power of the chat."**

It is a genuinely good fit, because editing a graph through a form is miserable and through language is natural. One constraint decides whether it stays trustworthy.

**The chat must produce operations on the scheme, not a replacement scheme.** Merge these two concepts, add this alternative label, make this narrower than that, attach this definition. A sequence of operations is inspectable before it is applied, reversible afterwards, attributable to whoever asked for it, and diffable. A regenerated ontology is none of those, and after three rounds of regeneration nobody can say why a concept is named what it is named.

That is the same conclusion reached on 2 August about the graph canvas, where the recorded operation sequence turned out to be the artefact worth keeping rather than the resulting picture. The two surfaces should share the operation vocabulary.

## Storage, And The Reuse Is Confirmed

The memo's instinct about the existing capability is right, and the record is more specific than the memo remembers. The project lead: **"we already added this ability to have a virtual file system that is also supported by local storage or local session, so just basically see if we can reuse that capability."**

The project record documents a virtual file system defined explicitly as **working memory for a language model**, reachable through a bridge that intercepts fetches from within an isolated frame and resolves them against a data source. So this is wiring rather than building.

It also documents something the memo has not anticipated but will need. Alongside the file system sits a **commit queue using timer-windowed batching**, built to handle what the record calls the many-files explosion that chat-driven work creates. Ontology editing through chat is precisely that: many small changes in quick succession. **The problem has already been hit once and solved once**, and reusing that solution is worth more than reusing the file system.

On the persistence itself, this is the first rung of the ladder set out this morning. Local storage now, a customer vault next, which matters more here than for transcripts, per the following section.

## The Ontology Is What Makes This Sticky

An observation the memo does not make and that changes how the feature should be valued.

A transcript is disposable. Once read, its job is done, and a customer who loses it loses one file. **A refined ontology is not.** After fifty recordings mapped against a scheme the customer has corrected, extended and argued with, they hold something that took months to produce, that is specific to their organisation, and that makes every future recording immediately useful.

That is the argument made this morning that persistence changes what the product is, made concrete. The transcription tool sells a job. **A tool that holds your vocabulary and indexes everything you have said against it sells a place to keep things**, and the ontology is the thing a customer would actually be unwilling to lose.

Two consequences. It strengthens the case for customer vaults over local storage, because local storage is a poor home for something that took months to build. And it is the strongest argument yet for revisiting the commercial terms before persistence ships, which was flagged this morning and remains open.

## It Should Be A Workflow Variant

Small point with a pricing consequence. Extracting or mapping an ontology is another model call, or several, on top of transcription, summary and infographic.

The workflow work this morning established that a job's steps carry declared budgets and that different declared flows are purchasable at different prices. **Ontology mapping is a workflow variant**, not a feature everyone pays for silently: a quick transcript, or a transcript indexed against your scheme, at a stated difference in price. That also makes the cost visible at the moment of choosing, which is what the billing brief argued the third figure on screen is for.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not one feature.** Extraction and mapping are different in value and should be sequenced accordingly.
- **Not free generation after the first time.** Extraction merges into an existing scheme.
- **Not model-decided matching.** The model recommends on the ambiguous remainder; cheaper methods resolve the rest.
- **Not chat producing ontologies.** Chat produces operations, which are reviewable and reversible.
- **Not silent.** What fails to map is surfaced, because it is the finding.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Mapping is the valuable half and extraction is the demonstrable one | Extraction shows well in a demo and compounds into nothing, which is a temptation to build the wrong half first |
| The customer must supply a scheme | The valuable direction requires something most customers do not have, so the first recordings must bootstrap it |
| Unmapped content as a finding | It is the most useful output and it looks like failure, so the presentation decides whether it reads as insight or as the tool not working |
| A hybrid matcher | It is what the evidence supports and it is more components than a single prompt, which will feel like overengineering until accuracy matters |
| Windowing the scheme | Five concepts at a time is measured and it means many calls per recording as an ontology grows, which is a cost curve to watch |
| The ontology as the sticky asset | It is the strongest retention argument available and it sits in local storage, which is not where months of work should live |
