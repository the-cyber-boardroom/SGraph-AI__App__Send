## What This Is

Three additions to the risk product now that evidence connects through to accepted risks, and one hazard running underneath all of them: **the persona-generated executive view is a compiled artefact produced by lifting technical detail into a statement somebody can act on, which is the same ambiguous operation described this morning for text, so it needs the same mechanism, meaning every element of the executive view resolves to the evidence it was compiled from and the technical view is always one step away; that cannot be designed away, because every message has a frame whether or not one is chosen deliberately, so the question is not whether the system frames but whether it does so honestly, which makes this a governance problem rather than a rendering problem and gives a clean line, that a persona may change emphasis, ordering, vocabulary and format and may never change what is being accepted; the research also supplies mitigations that are cheap to implement, since presenting a visual alongside text eliminated a framing effect that was significant in the text-only condition, and balanced presentation of both directions reduces it; the persona field for feedback from past presentations is the one to treat carefully, because a system that learns what this executive responds to will optimise for acceptance rather than understanding unless the measured outcome is chosen deliberately; the GRC addition is right and its boundary matters, because the team connects dots and chases acceptances and must not become the party that accepts, which is the distinction between validating that an obligation applies and carrying its consequence; and the simulation idea is the most valuable and most dangerous item here, worth building to find the questions a case cannot answer and not to find the version that gets approved, with two rules that should be non-negotiable.** It is the sixth document of 9 August (cross-ref: the v0.33.57 refactoring-meaning brief, the v0.33.49 fractal-registers brief, the v0.33.55 acceptance-flow brief, the v0.33.53 confirmed-validated-accepted material, and the v0.33.54 register-maintains-itself brief). ## The Executive View Is Compiled, Not Written

The memo describes the requirement well. The project lead: **"if this was presented to an executive and I send it to them, what would they see, and how do we communicate the message and provide the answer that they need."** With the layering. The project lead: **"a technical view of the risk, which is basically all the evidence, all the information, all the raw data, probably quite geek, and then we create a persona for each one, and that system prompt is the one that then creates the view."**

That is exactly the fractal register established on 17 July: one register per accepting role, in that role's own language, with a relevance fade. What this adds is that the view is **generated** rather than authored, and that changes what has to be guaranteed.

Generating an executive statement from technical evidence is **lifting**, in the sense set out this morning: concrete to abstract, one-to-many, and therefore ambiguous. The same requirement follows. **Every element of the executive view must resolve to the evidence it came from**, so a reader can descend from a sentence to the finding to the measurement, and the technical view is one step away rather than a separate product.

Without that, the executive is accepting a rendering rather than a risk, and nobody can later establish what they were shown.

## Framing Is Unavoidable, So It Has To Be Governed

The hazard, and it is better documented than almost anything else in decision research.

Logically equivalent information presented differently leads people to divergent choices. The foundational demonstration produced consistent **risk aversion under a gain frame and risk seeking under a loss frame** from identical underlying facts, and the effect has since been extended into attribute and goal framing across health, financial and policy decisions. Reviews describe framing effects as small changes in the presentation of an issue, such as slight modifications of phrasing, producing measurable changes of opinion.

Three specific mechanisms matter here because a persona-driven generator will exercise all of them:

| Mechanism | What it does | Where it appears in this design |
|---|---|---|
| **Selectivity** | Highlighting or hiding parts changes understanding | The persona decides what an executive cares about |
| **Operationalisation** | Different number and statistic formats change understanding | Percentages, counts, currency, intervals |
| **Comparison** | Choosing an unrelated risk to compare against anchors the judgement | Any "this is comparable to" phrasing |

The important conclusion is not that the system should avoid framing. **It cannot.** Practitioner analysis puts it plainly: every message has a frame even if nobody chose one consciously, so the question is not whether you frame but whether you do so honestly and in the interest of the audience.

That reframes this from a rendering problem into a governance one, and gives a line that can be stated and enforced.

## The Line: Emphasis May Change, Substance May Not

**A persona may change ordering, emphasis, vocabulary, length and format. It may never change what is being accepted.**

Concretely, what must be identical across every rendering of one risk:

- The statement of the risk itself
- The level and the acceptable threshold
- The interval being requested
- What happens if nothing is done
- What is not known

And what a persona may legitimately vary: which of those leads, how much supporting detail accompanies each, whether numbers appear as counts or proportions, what vocabulary is used, and how long the whole thing is.

The mechanical check is the source map. **If two personas render the same risk and their elements resolve to different underlying evidence, one of them has changed the substance.** That is testable automatically rather than by review, and it should be tested, because this is exactly the kind of drift that arrives one helpful adjustment at a time.

## Two Mitigations The Research Supplies

Both cheap, both worth building in from the start.

**Show a visual alongside the text.** A study of framing in disaster risk communication found a significant framing effect in a text-only condition which **became insignificant once an icon array displayed the same decision situation**, consistent with earlier findings that visual formats reduce framing effects relative to text alone. For this product that means a risk presented as prose is more susceptible than the same risk presented as prose plus a simple graphic of the position, which is an argument for the visual being standard rather than decorative.

**Present both directions.** Research on reducing framing effects includes having people consider the advantages and disadvantages of both options rather than one, and balanced framing is a standard recommendation. Applied here, an executive view should carry what accepting costs and what not accepting costs, in the same rendering, rather than whichever the persona judges more persuasive.

That second one has a pleasant consequence: it is also the honest version of the acceptance decision the corpus already specifies, since accepting a risk and funding remediation are both choices with consequences and a view showing only one is not a decision aid.

## The Past-Presentation Feedback Field Is The Drift Risk

The memo includes something in the persona that deserves separating out. The project lead: **"each exec has a persona, which is what is focused, how he wants to be communicated, what he cares about, even feedback from I guess past presentations."**

Presentation preferences are legitimate and useful: this person wants one page, prefers numbers to narrative, reads on a phone, wants the ask first. Learning those improves communication.

**What must not be learned is what gets a yes.** A system that optimises against past acceptances will discover that leading with cost, or with peer comparison, or with a particular framing, increases the acceptance rate, and it will then do that regardless of whether cost is the issue. At that point the product has quietly become a persuasion tool aimed at the person it is meant to inform, which is the opposite of its stated purpose and indefensible if it ever has to be explained.

The protection is to choose the measured outcome deliberately:

| Do not optimise for | Optimise for |
|---|---|
| Acceptance rate | Whether the decision was later regretted |
| Speed to yes | Whether the acceptor could restate the risk afterwards |
| Fewer questions | Whether the right questions were asked |

The corpus already has the instrument for the first of those, since the credibility work established that time resolves decisions and the record can then be checked. **Calibration of the acceptor is the honest success metric**, and it is measurable over months rather than in the moment.

## Blocks Are The Right Abstraction, And The Precedent Exists

The memo recalls prior work correctly. The project lead: **"if you see the app vault, and even some of the early app when we had the app JSON, the page JSON, we actually had this idea of blocks that got put together, and I think that's what we need to have here."**

That is the same shape as the declared workflows settled on 6 August: structure as **data rather than code**, composable, renderable, and editable without touching the renderer. The reasoning transfers directly, including the part about the chat.

**The chat should emit operations on the block declaration, not regenerate it.** Move this block up, expand this section, drop the comparison, translate into Portuguese. A sequence of operations is inspectable before it is applied, reversible after, attributable, and diffable. A regenerated view is none of those, and after three rounds nobody can say why a section is missing.

That is the same conclusion reached for the graph canvas on 2 August and for ontology editing on 6 August. **Three surfaces now want the same operation vocabulary**, which is an argument for defining it once.

## The GRC Team Connects, And Must Not Own

A genuinely important addition and its boundary is the part to get right. The memo names both halves. The project lead: **"one is the one that is responsible for some risk, there's definitely some risks that fall into GRC, but more importantly, the GRC is responsible for connecting the dots."** And the work. The project lead: **"it's the one that is responsible for following up, connecting the dots, booking the meetings, following up with the next acceptance, following up with all the stakeholders."**

The corpus has been describing a register that generates its own demand for evidence through accountability, and has not until now named who does the chasing. This fills that gap.

**The boundary is the distinction already established between validating and accepting.** A compliance function can establish that an obligation applies, that a level has been exceeded, or that an interval has expired. It cannot carry the consequence, because it does not hold the authority that created the exposure. A register where the compliance team accepts the risks nobody else would is a register that has been quietly neutralised, and it is the failure mode most likely to occur without anybody deciding on it.

So the pack should be built around chasing rather than owning, and their view is an **inbox at a different altitude** from an executive's: not what should I accept, but what is unaccepted, what is overdue, who has not responded, what expires this week, and which stakeholders have never returned anything.

That last one is worth surfacing explicitly, because the corpus established that a reviewer who has never returned anything is not reviewing.

## Simulation: Build It To Find Gaps, Not To Find The Pitch

The most valuable and most dangerous idea in the memo. The project lead: **"can we simulate the conversations for the risk acceptance, and let's actually add the concept of agents, so we can simulate what will be the response of an executive, especially when they want to challenge back or they want to accept."**

The value is real and it is the same value the corpus found at the field demo, where the primary output of the week turned out to be the questions visitors actually asked. Rehearsing an acceptance conversation surfaces the questions a case cannot answer, before the meeting rather than during it. It also trains people who have never run one.

**The danger is the obvious use.** If the simulation predicts how an executive will respond, and the presentation is then adjusted until the simulation approves, the system has been turned into a machine for getting risks accepted rather than understood. That is the persona-feedback problem with a faster loop.

So the output should be shaped to prevent it:

```
   GOOD OUTPUT     "three questions this case cannot answer"
                   "the evidence for the level is thin here"
                   "no contradiction has been sought"

   BAD OUTPUT      "this version is more likely to be approved"
                   "lead with cost for this persona"
```

The first improves the case. The second improves the pitch, and the difference is the whole thing.

## Two Rules For Simulation

Both should be non-negotiable rather than defaults.

**A simulated acceptance must never be confusable with a real one.** Different storage, different rendering, and an indelible marker that survives export. The corpus has an entire position resting on acceptance being an act by a named person who understood what they accepted; a simulated act that can be mistaken for a real one destroys it. This is the same discipline applied on 6 August to distinguishing machine-asserted from human-confirmed.

**Simulate the role, not the named individual.** Modelling how a chief financial officer generally responds is a training aid. Modelling how a specific named person will respond, and tuning a presentation against that model, is building a tool for routing around a colleague. The corpus already flagged this once, when the field demo work concluded that a compliance agent is persona simulation rather than a reviewer. The line is between preparing for a conversation and pre-empting a person.

## Change Control Per Risk

The memo asks for something the corpus has partly specified. The project lead: **"each risk needs to have change control and actions that were done, kind of like what Jira does."**

Two existing primitives cover most of it. The **level ledger** records movement over time with dates. **Decisions as first-class nodes**, established on 2 August, mean a risk accumulates a series of dated decisions with their own owners and intervals rather than a mutating field.

What this memo adds is that it should be **visible**, which it currently is not. And one rule carries over from 6 August: **supersede rather than delete**, so that what the register said in March remains answerable, which matters for anyone who acted on it.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not an authored view.** The executive rendering is compiled, and must resolve to its evidence.
- **Not frame-free.** Framing is unavoidable, so it is governed rather than avoided.
- **Not a persuasion system.** Personas change emphasis, never substance, and the measured outcome is calibration rather than acceptance.
- **Not a compliance-owned register.** GRC connects and chases; the business accepts.
- **Not a pitch optimiser.** Simulation finds the questions a case cannot answer.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Persona rendering versus informed acceptance | Speaking an executive's language is what makes the register usable and is one adjustment away from telling them what they want to hear |
| Framing as governed rather than avoided | It is the honest position and it means the system is making framing choices on somebody's behalf, continuously |
| Balanced presentation versus brevity | Showing both directions is the documented mitigation and it lengthens a view whose whole value is that it is short |
| Learning from past presentations | Format preferences genuinely help and the same mechanism learns persuasion, and the two are hard to separate in the data |
| GRC chasing without owning | The boundary is right and the pressure to let compliance accept the awkward ones will be constant |
| Simulation | It surfaces gaps nobody would find otherwise and the same capability optimises a pitch, with only the output design separating them |
