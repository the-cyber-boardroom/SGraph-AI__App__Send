# Evidence Packs Attach, They Never Mutate: Abundance Is Only A Feature If The Spine Is Safe, And Confidence Comes From Independence Rather Than Count

## What This Is

The structural definition of evidence packs, completing a unit the corpus has already described from the commercial side: **the term was introduced on 5 July as what a journalist sells, meaning the legwork rather than the prose, the evidence and trails and assurance and graph behind an article, delivered as a versioned vault, and this memo supplies what that thing actually is inside, namely a subgraph that attaches to somebody else's graph, so the two descriptions are one object seen from either end; the architecture the memo describes has a central sequence carrying more weight with an unbounded number of attached contributions around it, and the property that makes abundance a feature rather than a liability is not obvious and must be built for deliberately, because it holds only if packs attach without mutating, so that no contributed pack can alter the spine and a bad one is discarded rather than repaired; what the spine is has already been settled by this morning's positioning, since the graph of author-confirmed meaning is the thing the author vouched for and everything contributed afterwards is commentary on it, which keeps the author as arbiter while allowing anybody to attach anything; contradiction analysis as a named pack type is a stronger move than the guideline offered yesterday, because commissioning disconfirmation as a role rather than hoping enrichment is even-handed can be given to a different party and therefore buys independence by construction; and the confidence figure is the one thing here that could go badly wrong, because more evidence does not mean more confidence unless the evidence is independent, and the corpus has grounded that twice, once in a citation network where hundreds of papers and two hundred thousand paths traced back to nothing and once in ensemble research where the gain comes from independence of errors, so weighting must be by independence rather than by count and any percentage put in front of a reader carries an implied promise of calibration that somebody has to keep.** It is the fifth document of 9 August (cross-ref: the v0.33.44 evidence-packs-as-a-service brief, the v0.33.57 refactoring-meaning brief, the v0.33.54 paying-the-fact-creator brief, the v0.33.42 credibility calibration brief, and the v0.33.56 workflow state machine brief). New contributions: **the structural definition joining the commercial one, attach-never-mutate as the property that makes abundance safe, the spine defined as author-confirmed meaning, contradiction packs as a commissioned role, and weighting by independence rather than count.**

## The Term Already Exists, And This Completes It

Worth establishing first, because it turns two ideas into one rather than creating a collision.

The brief of 5 July defined an evidence pack commercially: what a journalist should sell instead of stories, being **the legwork rather than the prose**, the evidence, the trails, the assurance and the graph of the article, delivered as a versioned vault carrying provenance, explainability and repeatability, and sold on demand in many small payments rather than as rent.

That brief said what a pack is *for* and how it is *sold*. This memo says what it *is*:

> **An evidence pack is a subgraph that attaches to another graph, supplying evidence, analysis or contradiction about nodes in it.**

Those are the same object from either end. A journalist's pack is a subgraph somebody attaches to their own graph, and what makes it sellable is exactly that it composes rather than needing to be integrated.

## The Shape: A Spine And An Unbounded Periphery

The memo describes the structure clearly. The project lead: **"although there might be a central arc and a graph that has more weight, or becomes the central sequence of nodes and edges, we can then connect to it almost an infinite amount of other nodes and edges and evidence."**

```
   THE SPINE                     high weight, curated, small
   claim -- claim -- claim
     |        |        |
     |        |        +---- pack C   contradiction analysis
     |        +------------- pack B   a second model's reading
     +---------------------- pack A   supporting sources
                                      ...and any number more
```

And the claim about abundance. The project lead: **"that becomes not a problem; becomes a feature, which in a way, it's almost like the richer the dataset, the richer the confirmation, the richer the contradictions that we have discovered, the better and more confidence we have."**

That claim is true **conditionally**, and the conditions are the subject of the next two sections.

## Attach, Never Mutate

The property that makes abundance safe, and it has to be designed in rather than assumed.

**A pack may add nodes and edges. It may never alter or delete anything in the graph it attaches to.**

If a contributed pack can change the spine, then every pack is a trust decision before it is a contribution, accepting one is irreversible, and a single bad or hostile pack corrupts everything downstream of it. Under those conditions abundance is a liability and the memo's claim fails.

If packs only attach, four properties follow immediately:

| Property | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Detachable | A pack found to be poor is removed, and the spine is unchanged |
| Composable | Packs from different parties combine without negotiation |
| Attributable | Every added node traces to the pack that supplied it |
| Safe to accept | Attaching costs nothing that cannot be undone |

That last one is what makes an open market in packs possible at all. Nobody accepts contributions from strangers into something they cannot roll back.

This is also the same discipline the corpus has now reached several times: append-only channels between components, supersede rather than delete for corrections, and generated files never sharing a path with contributed ones. **A pack is a contributed path, and the spine is a generated one.**

## The Spine Is What The Author Confirmed

The memo leaves this open and this morning's positioning brief already answers it. The project lead described a central arc with more weight; the question is what earns that weight.

The answer follows from the arbiter position: **the spine is the graph of author-confirmed meaning.** It is what the author looked at and vouched for. Everything attached afterwards, however expert, however well funded, however many models produced it, is commentary on it.

That is a clean division and it preserves something valuable. A third party with a large evidence pack cannot overwrite what somebody said they meant. They can contradict it, at length, with sources, and the contradiction sits beside the claim rather than replacing it. Which is exactly what a reader wants to see.

It also gives packs a natural attachment point: **a pack attaches to specific claims in the spine**, not to the document as a whole, so a reader examining one claim sees what is attached to that claim rather than everything anybody has ever said about the text.

## Contradiction Packs Are Better Than A Guideline

The memo names this as a type and that is stronger than what was recommended yesterday. The project lead: **"more evidence or more analysis, or even contradiction analysis."**

Yesterday's brief argued that disconfirming evidence should be sought by default, on the grounds that confirmation bias is documented and a step that must be requested will not be. That is a **behaviour**, and behaviours drift.

Naming contradiction analysis as a **pack type** is better on three counts. It is a role rather than a habit, so it either exists or does not. It can be **commissioned from a different party or a different model** than produced the supporting pack, which buys independence by construction rather than by hoping. And it makes the absence visible: a claim with a supporting pack and no contradiction pack is in a different, inspectable state from one where contradiction was sought and none was found.

That third property matters more than it sounds, because the difference between *nobody looked* and *somebody looked and found nothing* is the difference between an unexamined claim and a tested one, and only a graph can hold it.

## The Confidence Figure Is Where This Could Go Wrong

The most attractive idea in the memo and the one most likely to cause harm if built naively. The project lead: **"could be something like, oh, we only have 67 percent, or 80 percent, or 20% sure of this, cool, but whereas that all has evidence, that all has weight, and now we can scale that."**

Two problems, both of which the corpus has already grounded.

**More evidence does not mean more confidence unless the evidence is independent.** The citation analysis grounded on 31 July examined a single biomedical belief and found a network of two hundred and forty-two papers carrying more than two hundred thousand supporting citation paths, in which the belief was amplified by papers presenting no data on it at all and a hypothesis became fact through citation alone. That is abundance producing unfounded authority. Counting packs would reproduce it exactly.

**And independence is what makes ensembles work.** The transcription work of 6 August established the same point from the other direction: the gain from combining models comes from exploiting the independence of their errors, and two models from the same family produce correlated mistakes and confident agreement on the same wrong answer.

So the rule is:

> **Weight by independence, not by count.** Ten packs agreeing because they all derive from one source are one piece of evidence, and reporting them as ten is worse than reporting one, because it manufactures confidence.

Which means a pack has to declare enough for independence to be assessable: what sources it used, which model produced it, and whether it had sight of other packs. A pack produced by a model that read the supporting pack first is not independent of it.

**The second problem is the percentage itself.** A number like sixty-seven percent implies calibration: that claims presented at sixty-seven percent turn out correct about sixty-seven percent of the time. If the figure is a heuristic aggregate of weights, it is false precision, and readers will act on it as though it were not.

The corpus has the mechanism for making it honest. The credibility work of 4 July established that what matters is not being right on the day but how often over time, because time resolves predictions and the record can then be checked. **A confidence figure should therefore be measured against outcomes rather than computed and displayed.**

Until that measurement exists, the safer presentation is the evidence structure rather than a scalar:

| Instead of | Show |
|---|---|
| 67% confident | 3 independent sources supporting, 1 contradicting, none from the author's own material |
| 80% confident | Confirmed by the author, plus 2 supporting packs, contradiction sought and none found |
| 20% confident | 1 source, not independently corroborated, contradiction pack outstanding |

Those are more useful and they are honest, and they can be collapsed into a number later once there is a track record to calibrate it against.

## Packs Are Fractal, And Compression Is Lifting

A small observation with a tidy consequence. The memo notes that packs can be compressed and analysed. The project lead: **"that again can be compressed, can be analysed, can contain information."**

A pack that reduces two hundred sources to a structured finding is performing the same operation this morning's positioning brief described: **lifting**, from concrete to abstract, with the ambiguity that entails. So a pack is itself a graph that can have packs attached to it, and a reader who doubts a pack's summary should be able to descend into what it summarised.

Which means the source map requirement applies inside packs too. A finding in a pack points at the evidence it lifted from, exactly as a concept in the spine points at the sentence it came from.

## Packs Are The Natural Unit To Sell

Following from the 5 July framing and worth stating now that the structure is defined.

An individual enriched node is a poor commercial unit because it has no thesis. **A pack has one**: it is a piece of work with a question, a method and a finding, produced by somebody identifiable. That makes it describable, priceable and reviewable in a way a node is not.

And the settlement problem is solved. The payment work of 6 August established a rail with sub-cent settlement and no account required, and yesterday's brief proposed that nodes record their production cost. A pack aggregates both: its cost is the sum of its steps, and attaching it is a single purchase.

The corpus's caution from 31 July still applies. Producing and selling packs is fine; positioning ourselves as the arbiter of which packs are true is the thing that was declined, and a pack marketplace should not quietly become one.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a new term.** The commercial definition exists; this supplies the structure.
- **Not mutable.** Packs attach and never alter what they attach to.
- **Not a replacement for the author.** The spine is confirmed meaning; packs are commentary on it.
- **Not counting.** Confidence weights by independence, and a percentage implies a promise of calibration.
- **Not an arbiter.** Selling packs is fine; ruling on which are true is what the corpus already declined.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Abundance as a feature | It holds only while packs cannot mutate the spine, and one convenience that lets a pack correct something breaks it |
| Independence assessment | It is what makes the confidence figure meaningful and it depends on packs declaring their sources honestly, which is not enforceable |
| The percentage | Readers want one number and one number implies calibration nobody has yet measured |
| Contradiction as a commissioned role | Buying disconfirmation deliberately is right and creates an incentive to produce contradiction whether or not it is warranted |
| Packs attaching to specific claims | It keeps the view focused and means a pack that disputes the framing rather than any single claim has nowhere natural to attach |
| A pack marketplace | The unit is genuinely sellable and the step from marketplace to arbiter is short and was already declined once |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| What does a pack declare about its own independence? | Sources used, model, and whether it saw other packs, since the confidence figure depends on it |
| How is independence actually computed? | Shared sources are detectable; shared training data and shared framing are not |
| Is a confidence number shown at all before calibration exists? | The structure is honest now; the scalar requires a track record first |
| Where does a pack attach when it disputes the framing? | Claim-level attachment does not cover a disagreement about what the text is about |
| How are packs ranked or filtered for a reader? | An unbounded periphery needs an ordering, and the ordering is itself a judgement |
| Who can attach a pack, and does the author see it? | The spine is theirs; whether commentary on it requires notice is a product decision |
| Does a pack carry its production cost? | Yesterday's proposal, aggregated, which makes pricing derivable |
