## What This Is

Enrichment of the extracted graph, the shared anchors that stop it exploding, and the economics that make both worth building: **once a paragraph has been lifted into typed artefacts, each type invites a different follow-up, since a fact invites a search for evidence, a hypothesis invites what would test it, an opinion invites whose it is and what the counter-view is, and a question invites an answer, and the elegant part is that enrichment does not need to relabel anything, because classification is already a path pattern rather than a judgement, so attaching evidence to a claim reclassifies it automatically; the memo's concern about an explosion of graph creation has an answer the corpus already holds, which is that where an external authority publishes an identifier for a thing you link to it and only mint a node where no authority exists, and for entities the largest such authority is the one the memo half-names, with the important correction that of its two candidates only one has language-independent identifiers that stay consistent across languages, the other having language-dependent addresses that have caused exactly the cross-language inconsistency this project is trying to avoid; that same knowledge base turns out to be the concept model settled on 6 August, built at global scale, since an item is a language-independent identifier carrying labels and aliases in many languages, which is precisely one concept with a preferred label per language, already populated with tens of millions of entities and free; the economic idea is the largest thing in the memo, because research is expensive to produce and almost free to read, so a node somebody paid to enrich should be readable by everybody at a fraction of what it cost, and the payment work of 6 August makes that buildable rather than aspirational, since sub-cent settlement with no account is exactly the shape required; and the multilingual observation about gendered forms is a real case that differs from a missing word, because it concerns information one language's grammar carries and another's cannot, which means the concept holds the attribute and each label expresses what it can, with the absence recorded rather than invented.** It is the second document of 9 August (cross-ref: the v0.33.57 refactoring-meaning brief, the v0.33.56 concepts-not-words brief, the v0.33.54 paragraph-as-bow-tie brief, the v0.33.56 402-is-the-protocol brief, and the v0.33.54 registrar-of-evidence brief). New contributions: **enrichment mapped per artefact type with reclassification as a by-product, the correction on which knowledge base to anchor to and why, the identification that it is the concept model at global scale, the economics of research paid once with the rail that makes it real, and grammatical gender as a distinct case from a lexical gap.**

## Enrichment Is Different Per Type

The memo lists the artefact types and the enrichment opportunity together, and the pairing is the useful part. The project lead: **"especially when you have facts, hypotheses, opinions, statements, questions, if the text contains a question about something, we could follow that up."**

Each type invites a different follow-up, which makes enrichment concrete rather than a generic instruction to look things up:

| Artefact | Enrichment | Produces |
|---|---|---|
| Fact | Search for supporting or contradicting sources | Evidence nodes, with edges in both directions |
| Hypothesis | What would test it, and what has been tried | A test, or a prior result |
| Opinion | Whose view, and what the counter-position is | Attribution, and an opposing node |
| Question | Answer it | An answer, with its own type and provenance |
| Statement | Resolve its entities | Links to external identifiers |

Two of these deserve emphasis. **Contradicting evidence matters as much as supporting evidence**, and a system that only looks for confirmation is building a machine for motivated reasoning. The corpus grounded exactly this on 31 July, where the foundational research on biased assimilation found that people accept confirming evidence at face value while subjecting disconfirming evidence to critical scrutiny. An enrichment step that inherits that bias would industrialise it.

And **an opinion's counter-position is often the most useful enrichment available**, because it turns a one-sided assertion into a visible disagreement, which is the shape the corpus has repeatedly found more informative than a resolved answer.

## Enrichment Reclassifies Without Relabelling

A property that falls out of an earlier decision and is worth noticing, because it makes the whole loop cheaper.

The node type formula work established that what a node is should be decided by a required pattern of typed paths rather than by a judgement. So **attaching evidence to a claim does not require anybody to relabel the claim.** The claim now matches a different formula, and its classification follows automatically.

```
   claim, no supporting edge          -> matches the OPINION formula
        |
   enrichment attaches a source
        v
   claim, with supporting edge        -> matches the EVIDENCED formula
```

That means enrichment is purely additive: it adds nodes and edges and never edits a type. Which in turn means it is safe to run repeatedly, cheap to undo by removing what it added, and inspectable, because the reason a node's type changed is a visible edge rather than a model changing its mind.

## Anchoring Is What Stops The Explosion

The memo names the failure mode precisely. The project lead: **"graph databases or graph relationships that start to anchor a lot of this, so you don't have an explosion of graph creation all the time."** And the payoff. The project lead: **"you benefit from the more graphs, the more connections, the more curation you do."**

That is the same conclusion the corpus reached twice already from different directions. The regulatory work of 31 July stated the rule generally: **where an external authority publishes an identifier for a thing, link to it, and only mint a node where no authority exists.** The ontology work of 6 August restated it for extraction: free generation produces a fresh vocabulary each time, so two paragraphs about the same subject yield two graphs that accumulate into nothing.

Anchoring is what converts accumulation from a hope into a property. Every mention that resolves to a shared identifier is a mention that joins everything else resolving to the same one.

## Which Knowledge Base, And Why It Matters

The memo names both candidates and reaches for one of them. The project lead: **"Wikipedia is a great resource because it is full of facts and evidence and is already hyperlinked, and there is DBpedia which already provides a lot of the articles in Wikipedia in triplets."**

Both exist and for this project they are not equivalent. The distinction is the one thing in this brief most worth acting on.

| | Wikidata | DBpedia |
|---|---|---|
| Identifier | Language-independent number, such as Q76 | Derived from an article title, per language |
| Across languages | **One identifier, consistent everywhere** | Separate versions per language edition |
| Labels | Many per item, in many languages, plus aliases | Dedicated editions rather than labels per item |
| Curation | Community-curated directly | Extracted from Wikipedia |
| Freshness | Dumps multiple times a month | Reported delay of about a month |
| Provenance | Better means of capturing it | Weaker |
| Coverage | Smaller share, higher quality | Larger share, partially reduced quality |

**The decisive property for this project is the first two rows.** Analysis of the two notes that the language-dependent addresses used by one of them have caused inconsistencies between property values across language versions, which is precisely the failure this initiative exists to avoid. If a company mentioned in a Portuguese paragraph and the same company in an English paragraph resolve to different identifiers, the graphs do not join and the multilingual work of 6 August is undone at the anchor layer.

So: **anchor entities to Wikidata identifiers.** DBpedia remains useful where broader coverage of article text matters, but it should not be the identity spine.

## It Is Already The Concept Model, At Global Scale

The finding worth pausing on, because it means a large part of the multilingual design is done rather than to be built.

The concept model settled on 6 August is one language-independent concept carrying a preferred label per language, alternative labels, a definition and relations. A Wikidata item is exactly that: a **language-agnostic identifier**, with an obligatory label and description, optional aliases, and statements expressed as property-value pairs, described in the literature as inherently multilingual and as an advantage precisely for multilingual linking.

So for entities, the concept layer this project needs already exists, is free, is community-maintained, is updated several times a month, and holds tens of millions of items. **The work is not to build it but to link into it**, and to mint local concepts only for the domain vocabulary that has no public identifier, which is exactly the rule stated above.

One honest limitation, documented and worth expecting: an item may exist without a label in a given language, so resolution can succeed while the display name is missing. That is the lexical gap case in a different guise, and it is recordable rather than fatal.

## Use It For Identity, Not For Authority

A distinction to hold firmly, because it is easy to slide across.

**Resolving a mention to an identifier is a strong claim** and a well-studied task. Saying that this occurrence of a name refers to that entity is checkable, useful and largely mechanical.

**Treating what that entity's page asserts as true is a much weaker claim.** These are tertiary, editable sources with known and documented quality variation, and one of the two is explicitly characterised as trading quality for coverage.

So an anchor should resolve identity and should not confer factual authority. A fact enriched with a link to an encyclopaedia entry is a fact with a pointer, not a fact that has been verified, and the interface should not blur those. That is the same care the corpus applies to the difference between a claim and evidence for it.

## Research Paid For Once Should Be Readable By Everyone

The largest idea in the memo, and the one with commercial consequences. The project lead: **"if somebody spent quite a lot of money doing a bit of research, why can't that be leveraged?"** With the framing that makes it precise. The project lead: **"the cost of accessing a node that exists."**

That is the produce-once-consume-many principle, and this is its fourth appearance in the corpus: a registrar of evidence on 31 July, shared conversations on 31 July, contextual validation of claims on the same day, and now enriched nodes. **A pattern arriving four times from different directions is a primitive rather than a recurring idea.**

The economics are unusually clean here:

```
   FIRST READER      pays the research cost      model calls, search,
                                                 reasoning: real money

   EVERY READER      pays the access cost        a fraction of a penny
   AFTER                                         to read what exists
```

Two things make it buildable now that were not true in July.

**The rail exists.** The payment work of 6 August established a protocol that settles in around two hundred milliseconds at a fraction of a cent, with no account required, and which one of its schemes handles when the price is not known in advance. Charging a trivial amount to read an enriched node is exactly the case card rails cannot serve and this can.

**And the node knows what it cost.** If enrichment runs through the declared workflows with per-step budgets from 6 August, the production cost of a node is a recorded number rather than an estimate, which means access pricing can be derived rather than guessed.

One design consequence: **nodes should record their production cost and their access count.** Both are cheap to store and neither can be reconstructed later.

## Sources Of Truth We Publish

The memo proposes something adjacent and worth separating. The project lead: **"we could also start to have a certain set of sources of truth that we provide on our website, that allows you to resolve names and companies and news and stories and facts."**

For entities with public identifiers, this is unnecessary and would be worse than linking to the authority. For **domain vocabulary with no public identifier**, it is exactly the concept scheme described on 6 August, published rather than held privately.

There is a caution the corpus has already reasoned through. On 31 July it concluded that a register owned by a participant is not a register, and declined to build one for that reason. Publishing a set of resolvable identifiers is a smaller thing than operating a registry, and the boundary is worth keeping: **publishing identifiers we mint is fine; positioning ourselves as the arbiter of what is true is not.**

## Paragraphs, Not Voice Memos

A positioning statement worth recording because it sets the scope of everything else. The project lead: **"we start with a paragraph, so it doesn't necessarily need to be a voice memo. Voice memos is just what we're using to go to market, but ultimately this is a paragraph of text, a statement, a document. This can work for anything."**

So voice is the route to market and the paragraph is the unit. That is consistent with the positioning brief written earlier today, which identified the transcription tool as a testbed chosen because it is small, real, ours and low-stakes, with the risk work as the destination.

It also means the interfaces should not assume audio anywhere below the ingestion layer, or the generalisation becomes a rewrite.

## Grammatical Gender Is A Different Case From A Missing Word

The memo's multilingual example is sharper than the usual one and deserves separating. The project lead: **"in Portuguese you have a lot of male and female in a lot of verbs, where you don't have in other places, and the definition of verbs in some language might not exist in other languages."**

The concept work of 6 August handled **lexical gaps**, where one language has no word covering another's concept, and the standard supplies vocabulary for partial correspondence. This is different. Here both languages can express the content; one language's **grammar obligatorily encodes something the other's does not.**

The consequence is concrete. A Portuguese sentence may reveal the gender of its subject through agreement, where the English equivalent does not. So lifting from Portuguese produces a node with an attribute that lifting the same content from English does not, and round-tripping either loses it or invents it.

The resolution follows the same principle as the concept model: **the attribute belongs to the concept, and each language's label expresses what its grammar allows.** Where a language cannot express it, that is recorded as not expressible rather than filled with a guess. The shared knowledge base already carries a property for this class of attribute on entities, which helps for people and organisations and not for the general case.

This is also where the memo's point about community lands. The project lead: **"that's where the community can build around this, and can correlate around this stuff."** Cross-language attribute mappings are exactly the kind of curation nobody does alone and everybody benefits from, which is the same shared-anchor argument applied to grammar rather than to entities.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not confirmation-seeking.** Enrichment looks for contradicting evidence as well as supporting.
- **Not a relabelling step.** Enrichment adds edges; the type follows from the formula.
- **Not a new concept layer for entities.** One exists globally, is free and is maintained; link into it.
- **Not an authority on truth.** Anchors resolve identity; they do not verify claims.
- **Not a voice product.** The unit is a paragraph, and voice is the route to market.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Enrichment cost | Every enriched node is at least one model call and often a search, so cost scales with graph size and needs a budget per the workflow design |
| Shared research and trust | If somebody else paid for the enrichment, a reader still has to decide whether to believe it, which makes provenance and confidence load-bearing rather than decorative |
| Anchoring to a community source | It is the right identity spine and it is editable by anybody, so identity resolution is safe and factual reliance is not |
| Publishing sources of truth | Useful for domain vocabulary and adjacent to becoming an arbiter, which the corpus has already declined once |
| Contradicting evidence | Looking for it is correct and produces graphs that are harder to read and less satisfying than confirmation |
| Grammatical attributes | Recording what a language cannot express is honest and adds a dimension most tooling has no place to put |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| What triggers enrichment? | Automatic on extraction, on demand, or by artefact type, and who pays |
| How is an enriched node priced? | Derived from recorded production cost, with a floor, and whether the first reader is charged differently |
| Where is the shared body of evidence held? | The corpus declined to own a registry, and this is smaller, so the boundary needs stating |
| How is confidence in a shared node shown? | Who enriched it, when, with what, and whether anybody has confirmed it since |
| Which domain concepts do we publish? | Only those with no public identifier, per the anchoring rule |
| How are grammatical attributes represented? | On the concept, with per-language expressibility, and a way to record not applicable |
| Does enrichment respect the author-as-oracle position? | An enriched fact is still the author's claim, and the enrichment is a separate assertion with its own provenance |
