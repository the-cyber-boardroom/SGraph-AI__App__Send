# Demo Review, Two Versions On: The Registers Are One Chain Rather Than Three Lists, A Question Is Not A Risk, And The Decision Is Its Own Node

**version** v0.33.55
**date** 2 August 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Engineering, Design, Product

**type** Architecture brief

*Third of 2 August, covering feedback on two successive builds. Regulatory references are checked and one is corrected. Written two days before the field demo opens. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

---

## What This Is

Feedback across two builds, in which the most consequential item is a modelling problem showing through the interface: **the three registers are currently drawn side by side as though they were three parallel lists, and they are not, because the chief information security officer's risk exists because of the operator's risk, which exists because of a fact stating that an agent touches production, so the correct picture is a single chain running from an existence fact up through each altitude to the board, and drawing it as three columns demonstrates nothing a spreadsheet could not already do while drawing it as a chain demonstrates the entire thesis; a second item is a quality gate rather than a feature, because some of the generated entries are questions wearing the clothes of risks, and there is a clean test available since a question cannot be accepted, so if a sentence cannot sensibly carry a named acceptor and an interval it is not a risk and should not be in the register; the twin should visibly update on every answer rather than being reported in a lump at the end, which also fixes the reported defect where every answer appears to have been about existence; paths are the right organising idea and they are better understood as traversals of a graph that already exists rather than as scripts, which makes new paths cheap, makes the tree view and the graph view the same artefact, and makes comparing two paths a genuine experiment measured by the instrumentation specified yesterday; the proposed node types are close to the corpus's established grammar with one real addition, since making the decision a node rather than a property of a risk is what allows one risk to carry many dated decisions, allows a decision to cover several risks, and is the precondition for the calibration record identified three times last week; the regulation should attach where the memo says, at the twin that asserts the thing exists, and the citations should be generated from the canonical Act graph rather than written by hand, which matters immediately because one of the two articles named in the memo is about conformity assessment bodies applying for notification and has nothing to do with an operator running an agent; and with the hall opening in two days the honest split is that the chain, the naming, the risk quality gate and the pre-populated entry are achievable, while the full graph view and the admin tooling are not.** It is the third document of 2 August (cross-ref: the v0.33.55 MVP review, the v0.33.55 monitoring-vault brief, the v0.33.54 no-blank-prompt brief, the v0.33.53 accepted-is-not-acceptable brief, and the v0.33.54 canonical-Act brief). New contributions: **the side-by-side layout diagnosed as a model problem, acceptability proposed as the test of whether something is a risk, paths reframed as graph traversals, decision as a first-class node and its connection to calibration, the regulation attaching at the twin with citations generated rather than hardcoded, and the correction of one article reference.**

## The Homepage Has The Blank Prompt Problem

The first request. The project lead: **"the homepage needs to be a lot more rich, because this is the entry point of the demo."** With the requirement. The project lead: **"even without clicking five clicks, in a couple of clicks, one second, show okay, here is what it would look like, here's the risk register, here's how it flows."**

This is a problem the corpus has already solved once, in a different place. The brief written on 31 July about the conversational layer argued that a blank prompt is the failure mode, because a visitor arrives with a vague sense that something might matter rather than a formed question, and an empty starting point asks them to already know what the tool does. The answer there was to open with worked examples rather than an invitation.

The same answer applies here. The homepage should show what the product produces before asking anybody to produce it: a populated register, a chain, and a set of pre-built scenarios reachable in one tap. That serves both audiences the memo identifies, the partner driving it who needs to find things fast, and the visitor handed the device who needs to see the point before investing eight answers in it.

## The Registers Are One Chain

This is the most important item in either memo, and it is a model problem rather than a layout preference. The project lead: **"at the moment it looks like the cards, they look side by side, and it's actually not that."**

The correction is precise. The project lead: **"the chain is actually from operator all the way to the board, so each of these needs to be connected to each other."** With the derivation stated. The project lead: **"the risk of the CISO actually comes from the risk of the operator, which is the one that connects to the existence, the fact that we have an agent."**

```
   SIDE BY SIDE (now)              A CHAIN (correct)
   [operator] [ciso] [board]        FACT: an agent touches production
   three lists                            |
   nothing a spreadsheet                  v
   could not do                     operator risk: it can change production
                                          |
                                          v
                                     CISO risk: derived from the above
                                          |
                                          v
                                     board risk: derived from that
```

Why this matters more than it appears: **three parallel registers demonstrate a formatting capability; one chain at three altitudes demonstrates the product.** The corpus's central claim, repeated since July, is the path from a fact to the board. If the demo shows three columns, the visitor sees a tool that produces three documents. If it shows a chain, they see the one thing nobody else does, which is that the board's exposure and the engineer's observation are the same object at different heights.

It also makes the altitude flick, which the memo says is working, into something stronger. Flicking between three views of one chain is a translation. Flicking between three separate lists is navigation.

## Name Them

Two naming requests, both small and both worth doing. The project lead: **"it's important to say this is a risk register, we need to name it like that, and we need to write this as risk, and that needs to be accepted."** And for the chain. The project lead: **"they are fundamental risks that are connected to the stakeholders, and I think we should name them."**

Calling the artefact a risk register explicitly is the same argument made yesterday about the spreadsheet view: the visitor recognises the thing before they evaluate it. And naming the three linked risks gives the chain handles, so that a person can refer to the middle one in conversation rather than pointing at a card.

## A Question Is Not A Risk

The sharpest critique in either memo, and it deserves to become a build-time check rather than a review note. The project lead: **"if it goes wrong at 3am, whose call is it? That's an interesting question, but that's a very ambiguous risk. That's not a risk, that's actually a question."** With the correct form. The project lead: **"the question here is, if you had to stop right now, could you?"**

The distinction is real and the corpus already contains the test for it.

**A risk is something that can be accepted.** It states a condition that is true, has a consequence, and can carry a named person, a date and an interval. A question cannot: nobody accepts "whose call is it at three in the morning" for six months.

So the gate is mechanical and can be applied to every generated entry before it reaches the register:

| Candidate text | Can it carry an acceptor and an interval? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| If it goes wrong at 3am, whose call is it? | No | A question. Ask it, do not register it |
| No named person can stop this agent within an hour | Yes | A risk |
| Exposure when owner rolls up to this table by default | No, it is not a statement about anything | Neither. Remove |

The memo flags one entry that is not merely badly phrased but incoherent, and at this event an incoherent register row will be spotted in seconds and will cost more credibility than a thin demo would. Applying the acceptability test to every row before Saturday is cheap and is probably the highest-value hour available.

## The Twin Should Update With Every Answer

A defect and a design principle in the same observation. The project lead: **"there's also a bug, which is that when we map them and see them, it says you said existence to all of them at the beginning, and actually that's not correct."** With the fix. The project lead: **"every time I ask a question, I should be able to see the digital twin being reflected, and I think that's what's missing."**

The defect is a symptom of the twin not being visible. If the model of the estate updates in front of the visitor with each answer, there is no ambiguity about what any given answer contributed, because they watched it land.

This is also the canvas idea from yesterday's review made specific. What fills up is not decoration; it is the twin, which is the corpus's own primitive for the point where the graph meets reality. And it separates cleanly along the line drawn yesterday between the estate, meaning what exists, and the practice, meaning what is done. The reported defect is precisely the two being collapsed into one category called existence.

## Paths Are Traversals, Not Scripts

The second memo proposes the organising idea. The project lead: **"maybe we can think of these as paths, and we can even name them."** With two examples: a path about who can pull the plug, covering who does it, who has the mandate, who authorises, who is available, whether it is documented and whether it has been tested; and a second path about side effects, covering what data is touched and how recoverable things are.

And then the observation that turns this from a content structure into an architecture. The project lead: **"when we are providing the answers, we are just navigating through this path in the graph."**

That is the right reading and it has consequences. If the questionnaire is a traversal of a graph that already exists rather than a script somebody wrote, then a new path is a new route rather than new code, the tree view and the graph view are the same artefact seen at different zoom levels, and the pre-populated demos are saved positions rather than separate fixtures. The memo's own analogy is apt. The project lead: **"it's kind of like the micro attack tree, one leads to the other, leads to the other."**

The proposal to compare paths is then a genuine experiment rather than a preference. The project lead: **"do some kind of A/B testing to see which one of these lands better."** The instrumentation specified yesterday measures exactly this, since the abandonment point per path is the direct comparison, and the change brief of 31 July argued that running several options simultaneously is what makes an experiment informative rather than merely safe.

One recommendation on scope: the second memo also proposes dropping the unbuilt scenarios and going deep on the one that exists. The project lead: **"even the agent with access to production data is already very rich, and I think we should just explore that one."** That is right, and it combines well with the depth-before-breadth restructuring from yesterday. One scenario, two named paths through it, is a better demo than four scenarios with one shallow path each.

## The Node Types, And Decision As Its Own Node

The second memo lists the primitives. The project lead: **"the agent itself, the assets which the agent interacts with, the facts, the vulnerabilities, the risks, which then follow all the way to the multiple levels, the stakeholders, and then even the decisions."**

Most of that is the corpus's established grammar. The grounding ladder already runs Fact, Evidence, Measure, Vulnerability, Risk, with everything grounded to reality through a twin; the agent and the assets are twins; the stakeholder is the accepting role.

**The decision is the addition, and the memo is right about why.** The project lead: **"a decision is actually captured independently from the risk."**

Making the decision a node rather than a property of a risk is a real modelling upgrade, and three things follow that the corpus has separately wanted:

- **One risk carries many decisions over time.** The level ledger, established in July as a dated record of movement, becomes a list of decision nodes attached to one risk rather than a mutating field.
- **One decision can cover several risks.** Accepting a group of related exposures in one act is what actually happens in a meeting, and it cannot be represented if acceptance is a property of each row.
- **It is the precondition for calibration.** The idea that the register eventually shows who was right arrived three times last week, in the downgrading brief, the project manager brief and the pre-approval brief. It requires decisions to be queryable as objects with their own owner, date, stated probability and later outcome. As an edge property that is impossible; as a node it is straightforward.

So the proposed additions are Decision as a first-class node, with the existing `accepted_by` retained as the edge from a risk to it, and the established set exhausted before anything else is minted, per the corpus's own rule that new types are declared in prose rather than introduced quietly.

## The Regulation Attaches At The Twin, And The Citations Come From The Graph

The first memo gets the attachment point right. The project lead: **"the Act should be touched by the place that actually creates that, in this case the digital twin that decides this exists."**

That is correct and it is more than an implementation detail. An obligation applies because of a fact about the estate, so the edge should run from the twin that asserts the fact to the provision that bites on it. Hung anywhere else, the citation floats and the visitor cannot see why it appeared.

On the specific articles, one correction. The memo names two. The project lead: **"what's Article 26, what's Article 29?"**

**Article 26 is right and is the single best anchor available for this scenario.** It sets out the obligations of deployers of high-risk systems, and its paragraphs include a requirement to monitor operation, to inform the provider of serious incidents, and to suspend use where deployment poses an unacceptable risk, together with a requirement to retain automatically generated logs for at least six months. That is, almost word for word, the pull-the-plug obligation and the detection obligation for somebody running an agent they did not build.

**Article 29 is not relevant here.** It concerns the application of a conformity assessment body for notification, which is about bodies applying to become notified bodies rather than about anybody operating a system. The likely intended references are Article 14, on human oversight, which the corpus identified on 28 July as the closest published analogue to the plug profile, and Article 27, on the fundamental rights impact assessment, whose required contents include a description of human oversight measures and of the measures to be taken if the risks materialise.

The process point matters more than the correction. **Article numbers should be generated from the canonical Act graph rather than written into the demo by hand.** The corpus spent last week establishing that no consolidated version of the instrument exists publicly, that the amendment entered into force on 27 July, and that several widely used sources are stale. A demo that hardcodes citations inherits exactly that problem, in front of an audience unusually likely to check. Generating them from the graph is also the only way the demo demonstrates the thing the graph was built for.

## Two Modes, Discover And Explain

The second memo describes what is really a second product mode. The project lead: **"we want to think of the flow, especially this demo, not as you start from scratch all the time; you should also be able to start from a particular predefined scenario where data is already set up, and you just explain the data that exists, and then we map it from there."**

Those are two different interactions with two different audiences:

**Discover** is the visitor answering, watching their own twin and chain assemble. It is the stronger experience and it costs the visitor time, which makes it right for a genuine prospect who has stopped to engage.

**Explain** starts populated, and the partner walks somebody through an existing picture. It is much faster, it is what works in a corridor, and it removes the risk of a stranger stalling halfway.

The homepage is where the choice is made, which is why the first memo's request that pre-populated examples be one tap away is not a convenience but the entry to a whole mode. The memo's own conclusion is the practical one. The project lead: **"which makes the demo easier to do and easier to implement."**

## The Whole Graph In One Go

Requested in both memos, for different reasons: validation and debugging in one, and audience depth in the other. The project lead: **"I want to have a view where I can dynamically expand and see the whole tree and the whole graph, because the graph should be visible and readable in one go."**

Two distinct things are wanted here and they should not be built as one. An **internal view** for checking that the tree is correct is a debugging tool, can be ugly, and is genuinely useful before Saturday. A **visitor-facing view** of the whole space is a design problem, is the more impressive artefact, and is not achievable in two days.

The determinism point from yesterday applies: because the space is a finite tree, the internal view can be generated from the definition rather than drawn, which makes it cheap.

## What Ships By Saturday

The hall opens in two days. On the evidence of both memos, the achievable set is the chain rather than the columns, naming the register and the three risks, the acceptability gate applied to every generated row, the twin updating per answer, one scenario with two named paths, and a homepage that reaches a populated example in one tap.

The full graph view for visitors, the admin section, the path comparison and the separate page architecture are the next version. The dev brief written on 31 July recommended choosing that split deliberately rather than discovering it, and this is the second concrete instance.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not three registers.** One chain, seen at three altitudes.
- **Not a questionnaire that emits questions.** Every row must be acceptable or it is not a risk.
- **Not a script.** Paths are traversals of a graph that already exists.
- **Not hardcoded citations.** Article references generated from the canonical Act graph.
- **Not one mode.** Discover and explain are different interactions for different visitors.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| The chain versus the altitude flick | The chain is the correct model and the flick is what currently works, and the redesign must not lose the thing that is landing |
| The acceptability gate versus content volume | Applying it strictly will delete rows and leave the register looking thin, which is better than looking wrong and will not feel that way on the day |
| Decision as a node versus two days | It is the right model and it touches the data structure, which is the riskiest change to attempt this close to the event |
| Generated citations versus having a graph ready | The principle is right and the canonical Act graph is days old, so the demo may have to hardcode and mark the citations as provisional |
| Two modes versus one build | Discover and explain share most of the code and diverge exactly where the demo is most fragile, which is the opening thirty seconds |
| Showing the whole tree versus showing how small it is | Transparency about the finite space is honest and reveals the demo's scope to a technical audience |
