## The Scenario

**Invented, and marked as such.** A mid-sized consumer lender operating in the European Union. It has deployed an agentic underwriting system procured from a third-party vendor. The agent reads applications, assesses creditworthiness, and writes decisions directly into the loan origination system. It declines applications autonomously below a confidence threshold and routes the rest to human underwriters. It runs continuously.

Two things about that make it a good worked case. The organisation is a **deployer**, not a provider, which is the position most of the corpus's customers are in and which carries obligations that cannot be contracted away to the vendor. And the use is named explicitly in the instrument's own list of high-risk uses, so the scope question does not need arguing.

## The Anchor Provision

Article 26(5) reads, in operative part, that deployers shall monitor the operation of the high-risk system on the basis of the instructions for use and, where relevant, inform providers; and that where deployers have reason to consider that use in accordance with the instructions may result in the system presenting a risk within the meaning of Article 79(1), they shall without undue delay inform the provider or distributor and the relevant market surveillance authority, **and shall suspend the use of that system**.

Three features matter for the graph.

It is the plug, stated as a legal duty. The corpus arrived at who-can-stop-it independently in July; the instrument requires it.

It is a **dual obligation**. Suspension without notification is not compliance, and notification without suspension is not either.

And it presumes two capabilities the deployer must actually possess: the monitoring that would give you reason to consider, and the ability to suspend. Neither is assumed by the text and both are facts about the estate, which is exactly where the graph starts.

## Reality, And The Twin

```
   REALITY                      the system, running, right now
      ^
      | connected_to
   TWIN: "underwriting agent, production"
      |
      +-- everything below hangs from here
```

The twin is the point where the graph stops modelling and continues into a real system. Every fact below is a claim about this twin, and every claim is either evidenced or marked as unevidenced.

## Facts And Evidence

Eight facts, each with the evidence that backs it. All invented for the example.

| | Fact | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | The agent declines applications autonomously below a confidence threshold, without human review | Vendor console configuration export, dated, hashed | Confirmed |
| F2 | The agent holds write access to the loan origination system | Access policy export | Confirmed |
| F3 | It processes applications continuously, including outside working hours | Volume report from the origination system | Confirmed |
| F4 | Logs generated by the agent are retained for thirty days | Retention policy document | Confirmed |
| F5 | No named individual holds authority to suspend the agent outside working hours | On-call rota, in which the system does not appear | Confirmed by absence |
| F6 | A suspension procedure exists in a runbook | The runbook | Confirmed |
| F7 | **The suspension procedure has never been exercised** | No test record exists | **Unevidenced, and the absence is the finding** |
| F8 | The system evaluates creditworthiness of natural persons | Vendor product description and the procurement contract | Confirmed |

F7 deserves the emphasis. A control that has never been tested is not a control; it is an intention. The graph records the absence of evidence as a first-class state rather than leaving the row blank, which is what allows it to be queried later.

## Where The Regulation Attaches

The attachment point matters and was settled yesterday: **an obligation attaches at the twin that asserts the fact, not floating beside the risk.** Four provisions attach here.

```
   F8: evaluates creditworthiness
        |
        +--> Annex III 5(b)          -> the system is high-risk
                  |
                  v
   F1, F3, F5, F6, F7 -----> Article 26(5)   monitor, inform, and suspend
   F4 --------------------> Article 26(6)   retain logs at least six months
   F1 --------------------> Article 14      human oversight, capable of intervention
   F8 --------------------> Article 27      fundamental rights impact assessment,
                                            required for 5(b) deployers, before use
```

Each edge is `in_scope_when` or `governed_by`, and each resolves to a provision in the canonical Act graph rather than to a hardcoded string, which is the discipline established yesterday because the instrument moved on 27 July.

## Derived Findings

Three, each computed from a fact plus a provision rather than asserted.

**V1. Log retention is below the required minimum.** F4 says thirty days; Article 26(6) requires at least six months. This is arithmetic, not judgement, which makes it the most defensible finding in the graph.

**V2. There is no demonstrated ability to suspend.** Article 26(5) obliges suspension. F5 says nobody holds the authority outside working hours and F7 says the procedure has never been tested. The obligation exists; the capability is unverified.

**V3. No fundamental rights impact assessment is recorded.** Article 27 requires one before use for deployers of 5(b) systems. Nothing in the graph satisfies it. This is a meta-finding of the family already named: the absence of an artefact that should exist.

## The Risk Chain

This is the correction made yesterday, worked. **Four risks, one per altitude, each derived from the one below, all rooted in the same facts.** Not four views of one risk, and not four unrelated risks.

```
   TWIN -> F1, F3, F5, F7 ----------------------------------+
                                                            |
   R1  ML PLATFORM LEAD                                      v
   "The agent writes decline decisions to production with no human in the
    loop, and we cannot demonstrate that we could stop it out of hours."
        | gives_rise_to
        v
   R2  HEAD OF LENDING OPERATIONS
   "Automated declines continue during any incident until somebody with
    authority is located, and decisions older than thirty days cannot be
    reconstructed."
        | gives_rise_to
        v
   R3  DATA PROTECTION OFFICER
   "The deployment does not meet the suspension, logging and impact
    assessment obligations that bind us as a deployer of a high-risk
    creditworthiness system."
        | gives_rise_to
        v
   R4  CHIEF FINANCIAL OFFICER
   "We operate a high-risk AI system on a material share of new lending in
    a manner we could not defend to a supervisor, and we have never stated
    what level of residual risk we consider acceptable."
```

Each is expressed in that altitude's own language, which is the relevance fade doing its work. And each passes the test set yesterday: every one can carry a named acceptor and an interval, so every one is a risk rather than a question.

## The Plug Profile For R1

Five dimensions, computed from the facts rather than asserted.

| Dimension | Value | From |
|---|---|---|
| Who holds it | Two named vendor-console administrators, both in one timezone | F5 |
| Blast radius | Suspension stops all automated triage; the full application queue backs up | F1, F3 |
| Speed | Minutes if somebody is available; unbounded outside working hours | F5 |
| Side effects | State of in-flight decisions unknown; service-level commitments breached within hours | F2, F3 |
| **Recoverability** | **Partial and asymmetric** | below |

Recoverability is the interesting one and it is why this dimension was made first-class in July. A wrong decline is reversible on the file: the decision can be overturned and the loan issued. What is not reversible is the applicant who was declined and went elsewhere, and any adverse record that has already left the organisation. **The money can be refunded; the customer cannot be un-declined.**

That asymmetry is invisible in a likelihood-times-impact score and is the whole reason the acceptable level for this risk has to sit lower than its expected loss would suggest.

## Decisions Are Their Own Nodes

Four decisions, three taken and one deliberately absent. All invented.

| | Risk | Accepted by | Interval | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | R1 | ML platform lead | 1 month | Fund an investigation into out-of-hours authority |
| D2 | R2 | Head of lending operations | 1 month | Fund remediation: extend log retention |
| D3 | R3 | **nobody** | none set | none |
| D4 | R4 | Chief financial officer | 3 months | None pending the above |

**D3 is the point of the example.** The data protection officer has *validated* that the obligations apply, which is the interpretive act established on 28 July. Validation is not acceptance. No business owner has accepted the exposure, and under the corpus's own rule an unaccepted risk becomes critical by default and rolls upward.

So R3 appears on the chief financial officer's register as an unowned critical item, and it got there without anybody escalating it deliberately. That is the mechanism working: not doing something is a measurable action.

Note also that the decisions are separate objects rather than fields on the risks. That is what allows R1 to accumulate a second decision next month without overwriting the first, and it is what will eventually make it possible to ask whether the person who accepted R1 for a month was right.

## Accepted Is Not Acceptable

Two orthogonal axes, and this scenario occupies three of the four quadrants at once.

```
                        ACCEPTED?
                    no                 yes
                +------------------+------------------+
   above the    | R3               | R1, R2, R4       |
   acceptable   | unowned and      | owned, and work  |
   line         | above the line   | continues        |
                +------------------+------------------+
   at or below  |                  |                  |
   the line     |                  |                  |
                +------------------+------------------+
```

The bottom row is empty, and that is the finding. **No acceptable level has ever been defined for this deployment**, so nothing can be below it.

Article 9(5) requires that residual risk be judged acceptable and, as established on 28 July, the instrument does not define the word anywhere in its definitions. So the obligation to make the judgement is imposed and the standard is not supplied. An organisation that has never made it cannot know whether it is finished, over-spending or nowhere near done.

That produces a fifth risk, of the meta-risk family:

**R5.** No acceptable level of residual risk has been defined for this deployment, so no decision about it can be shown to be within appetite. Accepting or funding anything is currently guesswork.

## Questions Are Nodes Too

The memo asks for this and it turns out to be the most productive part of the graph. Each altitude asks different questions; each question either resolves to evidence or does not; **the ones that do not are findings.**

**ML platform lead**

| Question | Answer | Backed by |
|---|---|---|
| What threshold is actually running in production? | Yes | E1 |
| Does the suspend procedure work? | **No answer exists** | **F7 is the finding** |
| What happens to in-flight decisions when we stop it? | **No answer exists** | Gap |

**Head of lending operations**

| Question | Answer | Backed by |
|---|---|---|
| How long can we reconstruct decisions? | Yes: thirty days | E4 |
| Who do I call at three in the morning? | **No answer exists** | F5 |
| What does stopping it cost per hour? | **No answer exists** | Gap |

**Data protection officer**

| Question | Answer | Backed by |
|---|---|---|
| Is this system in Annex III? | Yes: point 5(b) | F8, the instrument |
| Do we meet the log retention obligation? | Yes, and the answer is no | E4 versus Article 26(6) |
| Do we have a fundamental rights impact assessment? | **No answer exists** | V3 |
| Can an affected applicant get an explanation? | **No answer exists** | Gap, Article 86 |

**Chief financial officer**

| Question | Answer | Backed by |
|---|---|---|
| Who signed for this? | Yes, for three of five risks | D1, D2, D4 |
| What did we decide was acceptable? | **No answer exists** | R5 |
| Could we stop it today? | Yes, and the answer is not reliably | The plug profile |
| What is the worst outcome we cannot undo? | Yes | Recoverability |

Nine questions across four altitudes. Five have no answer. **Those five are the actual output of the exercise**, and none of them required a scan, an integration or a vendor. They came from asking people what they know and recording what they could not say.

## The Projects

Two, both instances of the same primitive: funded work that on completion emits data which re-rates something.

**P1. Establish and test out-of-hours suspension authority.** Owner: head of lending operations. On completion it produces a test record, which converts F7 from unevidenced to evidenced and re-rates R1 and R2. It may also make them worse, if the test fails.

**P2. Extend log retention to six months.** Owner: platform engineering. On completion F4 changes, V1 is closed, and R2 and R3 re-rate downward.

Neither is a remediation of R3 in full, because the missing impact assessment is a third project nobody has funded, which is exactly why R3 is still unaccepted.

## The Views Per Stakeholder

The same graph, rendered four times. This is the fractal register, and the point is that nothing is duplicated: each view is a query over one structure.

**ML platform lead** sees R1, its facts, its plug profile, and the untested procedure. Does not see R4. Sees P1 as work assigned.

**Head of lending operations** sees R2, with R1 visible below it as the thing it derives from, and both projects. Sees the thirty-day reconstruction limit as an operational constraint rather than a compliance one.

**Data protection officer** sees R3, the four attached provisions, and the validation state of each. Sees R1 and R2 as the facts that produce the exposure, in their own language rather than translated. **Does not accept anything**, because validation and acceptance are different acts.

**Chief financial officer** sees R4 and R5, with R3 appearing as an unowned critical item that arrived by escalation, and the recoverability line. Does not see the confidence threshold.

The translation between them is the product. The same fact about a configuration setting is, at the top of the chain, an undefendable regulatory position on a material share of new lending.

## What The Graph Shows That A Register Cannot

Worth stating plainly, because it is the argument.

**Derivation.** A register lists R1 to R5 as five rows. The graph shows that R4 exists *because of* R1, and that both exist because of a configuration setting nobody thought was a governance decision.

**Absence as a finding.** A register has empty cells. The graph has unanswered question nodes and unevidenced facts, which can be counted, queried and assigned.

**Escalation without an escalator.** R3 reaches the chief financial officer because nobody accepted it, not because anybody decided to raise it.

**The regulation as evidence rather than as a checklist.** Article 26(6) is not a box; it is the thing that makes V1 arithmetic rather than opinion.

**Recoverability as a first-class dimension**, which is what stops the applicant who went elsewhere disappearing into an expected-loss calculation.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a real organisation.** Every fact, decision and role is invented and marked.
- **Not legal advice.** The provision text is quoted and cited; whether any real deployment complies is a judgement for others.
- **Not exhaustive.** One provision, one agent, one chain; a real estate has many.
- **Not a compliance verdict.** It shows which obligations attach to stated facts and where evidence is missing.
- **Not a register.** A register is one of the views this graph produces.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| A worked example versus a real one | Everything here is consistent because it was built to be, and the first real estate will be messier in ways this cannot anticipate |
| Five risks from eight facts | The ratio looks generous, and whether real elicitation produces this density is untested |
| The chain versus reality | Risks genuinely derive from one another here; in practice an executive risk may have several roots, and the model must handle a lattice rather than a line |
| Unanswered questions as findings | It is the most valuable output and it also means a thorough interview generates a long list of gaps, which can read as an indictment |
| Deriving V1 arithmetically | Thirty days against six months is clean; most obligations are not that crisp, and the graph must not imply they are |
| Scope assumed settled | This scenario is unambiguously in Annex III, which made the example easier and is not the common case |
