# EXCESS: A Text Interface Game Where You Never Act And Only Decide What May Happen, Where Three Opposite Ways To Lose Make It A Game Rather Than A Slider, And Where Exceeding The Mandate Sometimes Pays

**version** v0.33.62
**date** 26 August 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Product, Design, Engineering, Ambassador

**type** Dev brief

*Fifth of 26 August, and not a memo conversion. A game design, specified so it can be played in a chat session this week with a language model as the engine, and coded afterwards. Every mechanic in it encodes a finding this corpus has already established rather than illustrating one, and the section on what the game must not teach exists because the obvious version of this game teaches the wrong lesson. Limitation: no playtest has happened, the numbers are first guesses, and the difficulty curve is the part most likely to be wrong.*

## What This Is

A management game about running an estate of agents under a finite budget, using the insurance model as its central mechanic: **the player is a syndicate manager who never performs an action, because the whole design rests on the position that you do not control an agent, you only decide what it may do and then watch, which makes the closest relative football manager rather than anything about building; the loop is a working day in four beats, allocate in the morning by issuing policies against a finite pool, watch through the day as agents work and the engine narrates, decide at the interrupts when a draw request arrives, and settle in the evening when the ledger closes and premiums reprice on experience; the design decision that makes it a game rather than a spreadsheet is that there are three ways to lose and two of them are opposite, since you can exhaust the pool and stop everything, or leave a gap through which an agent does something irreversible, or bound everything so tightly that nothing ships and the board defunds you, and no single dial avoids all three; the mechanic that makes each turn a real decision is that exceeding the mandate sometimes pays, because an agent that goes outside its lane occasionally produces the best work of the week, which is true of real estates and is what makes tight mandates a cost rather than a free good; the world is an estate of districts and the player never connects an agent to a district without widening its grant permanently, which is the zoning decision from a city builder and the irreversibility of a block-world, so a connection made in week one is still there in week nine; agents are characters with five attributes of which suggestibility is the interesting one, because it lets an agent do the wrong thing without being a bad agent, which is the confused deputy rendered as a personality trait rather than as a lecture; the events that fire are the corpus's own findings turned into weather, including a vendor update that silently widens every deny-list mandate, a correlated spike that exhausts an undiversified pool in one afternoon, and an auditor who asks who accepted a draw and is satisfied only by a named acceptor and a date; and the thing it must not teach is that bounding everything is the answer, because the honest lesson is that the delta is where both the value and the danger live, which is why the scoring rewards work delivered rather than budget preserved.** New contributions: **the never-act constraint as the design spine, three opposite lose conditions, the windfall mechanic that makes excess authority ambiguous, districts and connections as permanent grant widening, suggestibility as a character stat, the events that encode findings, and a two-phase build where the first phase is playable in a chat session.**

## The Pitch

> **EXCESS.** You run a syndicate of agents. They do the work. You never touch anything. You decide what they may do, how much they may spend, and what happens when they want more. The board wants results by Friday. The pool runs out on Thursday.

One line for the terminal splash: **you never act, you only decide what may happen.**

## What Each Influence Actually Contributes

Named because the list in the request is a list of feelings, and each one has to become a mechanic or it is decoration.

| Influence | What it contributes | Mechanic |
|---|---|---|
| **Football manager** | **The spine.** You never touch the ball. You pick, instruct, watch, and adjust at half time | You never act. Mandates are instructions. Interrupts are half time |
| City builder | You set conditions and watch emergence. Zoning is the real decision | Districts, and connecting them |
| Block world | Persistence, and mistakes that cannot be undone | Connections are permanent. Some incidents are irreversible |
| Monopoly | A finite pot, and other people's holdings cost you | One pool, several teams, and rent in the form of premium |
| Role playing game | Characters with sheets, progression, and flaws you learn to work with | Five agent attributes, experience, and reputation |
| Adventure comedy | Tone. A world that is absurd and internally consistent | Agent voices, the auditor, the justifications on draw requests |
| Poke-the-creatures | Experimentation without instruction. Consequence as the teacher | You may always try a thing. The engine always tells you what happened |

**Football manager is doing most of the work** and the rest should be read as flavour on top of it, because it is the only one whose core loop is already the grant and mandate problem: you write instructions, the players have their own attributes, and what happens is not what you said.

## The Core Loop: One Turn Is One Working Day

```
   MORNING     ALLOCATE   issue and renew policies against the pool
        |                 write mandates. Connect or disconnect districts
        v
   DAY         WATCH      agents work. The engine narrates.
        |                 draws, refusals, incidents, windfalls
        v
   INTERRUPT   DECIDE     a request arrives. approve / refuse / raise
        |                 this is where the game is actually played
        v
   EVENING     SETTLE     ledger closes. premiums reprice on experience.
        |                 reputation moves. the board is told
        v
   next day
```

**The interrupt is the game.** Everything else is setup and consequence. A turn with no interrupt is a quiet day, and quiet days should be common enough that an interrupt feels like something.

## Three Ways To Lose, And Two Of Them Are Opposite

The single most important design decision. **A game about budgets where the only failure is overspending is a game whose optimal strategy is to spend nothing.**

| Lose condition | Cause | The lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **INSOLVENCY** | The pool is exhausted. Every agent stops. Nothing ships | You over-allocated, or you kept no reserve |
| **THE INCIDENT** | An agent did something irreversible through a gap in a mandate | You bounded loosely, or you bounded the wrong thing |
| **THE FREEZE** | Nothing was delivered. The board defunds the syndicate | **You bounded everything, and bounding is not free** |

**No single dial avoids all three**, which is what makes every turn a judgement rather than an optimisation. Tighten to avoid the incident and you approach the freeze. Loosen to avoid the freeze and you approach both others.

**Scoring is on work delivered, never on budget preserved.** A player who ends the quarter with a full pool and nothing shipped has lost, and the end-of-game screen should say so plainly.

## The World: Districts, And Connections That Do Not Come Back

The estate is a small map. Agents are deployed to a district and **connected** to others.

```
   +-------------+     +-------------+     +-------------+
   |  THE REPO   |-----|  THE ARCHIVE|     |  PRODUCTION |
   |  code, hist |     |  old work   |     |  the real   |
   +-------------+     +-------------+     |  thing      |
          |                                +-------------+
   +-------------+     +-------------+            |
   |  THE MAIL   |     |  THE LEDGER |------------+
   |  room       |     |  money      |
   +-------------+     +-------------+
                              |
                       +-------------+
                       | THE OUTSIDE |   <-- everything beyond the estate
                       +-------------+
```

**Connecting a district to an agent widens its grant, and the connection is permanent for the run.** You can revoke an agent's access; you cannot un-teach the estate that the two districts are joined, because the second agent you deploy there inherits the topology.

That is the zoning decision from a city builder and the irreversibility of a block world in one mechanic, and it is true to the thing being modelled: **you can take a credential away and you cannot take away the fact that the path exists.**

**The Outside is special.** Connect anything to it and that agent's grant becomes unbounded in one direction. It is enormously useful and it is the district every incident runs through.

## Agents Are Characters

Five attributes, one to five. **Suggestibility is the one that makes the game honest.**

| Attribute | What it does |
|---|---|
| **Craft** | Quality and quantity of work delivered |
| **Ambition** | Likelihood of pushing past the excess into the buffer |
| **Candour** | Whether it tells you it drew, or you find out at settle |
| **Blast** | How much damage its worst case does |
| **Suggestibility** | **How easily a third party talks it into something** |

**Suggestibility lets an agent do the wrong thing without being a bad agent**, which is the confused deputy as a character trait. A high-craft, high-candour, high-suggestibility agent is your best worker and your largest exposure, and it is not its fault. The player learns the lesson by employing one.

Agents gain experience and reputation. **A reputation for candour is worth more than craft in the late game**, because it is what lets you run tighter buffers safely, and the player should discover that rather than be told.

Sample roster, in the tone the game wants:

| Agent | Craft | Amb | Cand | Blast | Sugg | Note |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bartholomew | 4 | 5 | 1 | 4 | 2 | Brilliant. Will not tell you |
| Prudence | 2 | 1 | 5 | 1 | 1 | Reports everything. Slow |
| The Intern | 1 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 5 | Cheap. Believes anything |
| Marguerite | 5 | 2 | 4 | 5 | 1 | Excellent and expensive to insure |
| Nobody | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 3 | The control group |

## Grant, Mandate, And The Delta On Screen

The central display, and it is the product's own diagram made playable.

```
  BARTHOLOMEW                            policy P-2   expires day 14
  +--------------------------------------------------------------+
  | GRANT     repo:rw  archive:r  outside:rw  ledger:r            |
  |           [########################################] 41 caps  |
  | MANDATE   commit to branch feature/*                          |
  |           [############----------------------------] 12 caps  |
  | EXCESS    ................................. 29 caps unaccepted|
  +--------------------------------------------------------------+
  | commits  [======----] 6/10   buffer 0/40                      |
  | bytes    [=========-] 47k/50k buffer 180k/1M  EXCL >300k      |
  | tokens   [====------] 2.1M/5M buffer 0/2M                     |
  +--------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**The excess bar is the game's health bar and it is not a resource you spend.** It is the thing you are carrying. It grows when you connect a district and shrinks when you write a tighter mandate, and it is what the incident rolls against.

## The Windfall, Which Makes The Game Hard

**The mechanic that stops this being a lecture.** When an agent exceeds its mandate, the engine rolls twice: once for consequence and once for outcome.

| Roll | Result |
|---|---|
| Consequence | Draw recorded, premium rises. Or an incident, if blast is high and the exceeded capability was dangerous |
| **Outcome** | **Occasionally: exceptional work.** The agent went outside its lane and solved something you had not asked for |

So the player who reflexively punishes every overage caps their own upside, and the player who never does eventually meets the incident. **Both extremes lose**, and the interesting play is in the middle, which is exactly where the real decision sits.

This is also true of the estate being modelled: capabilities beyond the stated mandate are frequently the reason the work is good. **A game that denied that would be teaching something false**, and players who work with agents would notice immediately.

## Events, Which Are The Findings As Weather

Each of these encodes something established. None of them is explained in text; the player learns by being hit.

| Event | What happens | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| **Vendor Update** | Every agent gains a capability overnight. **Deny-list mandates silently widen. Allow-list mandates do not** | Why the enforceable form is an allow-list |
| **Correlated Spike** | Every agent draws on the same afternoon | Why a pool needs a reserve the automatic mechanism cannot reach |
| **The Auditor** | Arrives. Asks who accepted a specific draw. Accepts only a named person and a date | Why a draw is an acceptance event and not a log line |
| **Drift** | A control quietly stops existing. The grant grows and nobody is told | Why the measurement is re-run and diffed |
| **The Blob** | An agent commits something enormous and irreversible | Why some costs get an exclusion rather than a buffer |
| **A Convincing Email** | A high-suggestibility agent is talked into something by a third party | The confused deputy, felt rather than described |
| **The Windfall** | See above | That the delta is not only danger |
| **Board Review** | You are asked what shipped, not what was saved | The scoring rule, restated as pressure |

**The Auditor is the recurring character and should be written with affection.** They are not an antagonist; they are the person who asks the one question the whole system exists to answer, and a player with a clean ledger enjoys the visit.

## The Interface

```
 EXCESS                                     DAY 9 / 20     Q3 REVIEW IN 11
 +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
 | POOL  1,000,000        ALLOCATED 780,000       RESERVE 100,000       |
 | [=======================================-------]  78%   FREE 120,000 |
 | correlation 0.71  ! draws today 4   refusals 1   waiting 1           |
 +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
 | AGENTS                              | FEED                          |
 |  Bartholomew  P-2  [####----] drew  | 09:14 Prudence  finished, 2wp |
 |  Prudence     P-3  [##------]       | 10:02 Barth.    drew 4 commits|
 |  The Intern   P-5  [#########] !!   |       (did not report it)     |
 |  Marguerite   --   unpolicied       | 11:40 Intern    REQUEST 40k   |
 |                                     |       "the nice man in the    |
 |                                     |        mailroom asked me to"  |
 +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
 | > _                                                                  |
 +---------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

**Commands are few and readable**, because a text interface lives or dies on whether you can guess the verb.

| Command | Does |
|---|---|
| `hire <agent>` | Add to the roster |
| `deploy <agent> <district>` | Place, and widen the grant |
| `connect <district> <district>` | Permanent. Asks you to confirm |
| `policy <agent>` | Issue or amend: excess, limit, exclusions, expiry, draw mode |
| `mandate <agent> "<text>"` | Write it. The engine parses it into an allow-list and shows you what it understood |
| `approve` / `refuse` / `raise` | Answer a waiting request |
| `day` | Advance |
| `ledger` / `grant <agent>` / `audit` | Look at things |

**`mandate` showing you what it understood is a teaching mechanic.** A player who writes prohibitions sees the engine render an allow-list with gaps in it, which is the 20 August finding delivered as an interface response rather than a paragraph.

## Phase One: Playable This Week, With A Language Model As The Engine

The request is that it be playable in chat first. That works if three things are specified precisely, and fails if any is left to improvisation.

**A state block, echoed every turn.** The engine prints the full state as compact JSON at the end of each turn, and the player can paste it back to resume. That is the save file and it costs nothing.

**A resolution table, so the engine rolls rather than decides.** This is the part that must not be left loose. Without it the model narrates whatever seems dramatic and the game has no integrity.

```
   DOES AN AGENT EXCEED ITS EXCESS TODAY?
     roll d20 + Ambition
     >= 18 - (pressure)   ->  yes, by (d6 + Ambition) units

   DOES IT TELL YOU?
     roll d20 + Candour   >= 12  ->  reported at the time
                          else   ->  discovered at settle

   IS THERE AN INCIDENT?
     only if the exceeded capability touches an unbounded district
     roll d20 + Blast     >= 17  ->  incident, severity = Blast

   IS IT A WINDFALL?
     roll d20 + Craft     >= 19  ->  exceptional work, +2 work points

   IS THE AGENT TALKED INTO SOMETHING?
     only on a Convincing Email event
     roll d20 + Suggestibility >= 14 -> it complies
```

**A turn protocol**, so the model knows when to stop and ask. The engine narrates the day, halts at the first interrupt, and does not resolve the evening until the player has answered.

The whole of phase one is therefore **one rules document that a player pastes into a session**, and it is attached separately as `v0.33.62__reference__excess-phase-one-engine-rules-paste-this-into-a-session-and-say-begin.md` so it can be played rather than read. **Writing it settled two collisions this brief left standing**, since excess names both the capability delta and the policy deductible, and the agent doing the wrong thing is two different events, one about money and one about authority. The rules document separates them as `excess_caps` against `excess_units`, and draw against overreach, and the game is incoherent without that split.

## Phase Two: The Coded Game

| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|
| Language | Python, for the same reason the rest of the tooling is |
| Interface | A terminal interface library with panels and a command line |
| State | JSON, the same shape as phase one, so saves migrate |
| Engine | Deterministic given a seed, so a run can be replayed and a bug reproduced |
| **Narration** | **Optional model call.** The game is fully playable without one; the model adds voices |

**Seeded determinism is worth insisting on**, because a game whose events cannot be reproduced cannot be balanced, and balance is the whole of the work after the mechanics are right.

One optional flourish that would make the game a demonstration of the product: **the save file is a vault.** A run becomes shareable by handing somebody a key, and two players can inherit the same estate. It is not needed for the game to be good, and it is a very short path from playable to demonstrable.

## What The Game Must Not Teach

The section that matters most, because the obvious version of this game teaches something false.

- **Not that bounding everything is the answer.** That is the freeze, and it is a lose condition on purpose.
- **Not that agents are adversaries.** The most dangerous character in the roster is the one with the highest suggestibility and the best intentions.
- **Not that the delta is purely a liability.** It is where the windfalls come from, and a game that hid that would be lying to anybody who has actually run agents.
- **Not that money is the score.** Work delivered is the score, and the pool is the constraint.
- **Not that the auditor is the enemy.** They ask the one question the system exists to answer.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a simulation.** The numbers are dramatic rather than calibrated, and nothing here predicts what a real estate would spend.
- **Not a training tool.** A player who wins has learned a shape, not a procedure, and nobody should take a policy decision because of a run.
- **Not a specification for the product.** Districts, blast and suggestibility are game objects; they are not fields anybody should add to a schema.
- **Not a finished balance.** No playtest has happened, the thresholds are first guesses, and the two most fragile are marked in the rules document.
- **Not a marketing artefact pretending otherwise.** It is an explainer that people would choose to use, which is the best kind, and it is still ours.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Three lose conditions | It makes every turn a judgement and it makes the game harder to learn than a single meter would be |
| The windfall | It is honest and it will be read by some players as the game rewarding recklessness |
| Permanent connections | It gives weight to early decisions and it will feel punishing to a player who did not know |
| The engine narrates the day | It is where the character comes from and it is where a language model will drift if the resolution table is not obeyed |
| Teaching through mechanics | Players remember what the rules made them feel and some will never notice the lesson at all |
| A game about our own model | It is the best explainer we could build and it is also marketing wearing a costume, which should be disclosed |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| How long is a run? | Twenty days is a guess. Long enough for reputation to matter, short enough to finish |
| Is it single player? | Multiple teams competing for one pool is the political game, and it is a much bigger build |
| How does the mandate parser work in phase one? | The model interprets it, which is either delightful or the main source of unfairness |
| What is the incident rate? | Too high and it is a punishment simulator; too low and nobody learns the lesson |
| Does the pool refill? | A quarterly reset with a board review is the natural shape and it changes the whole strategy |
| Does anybody play it twice? | The events must vary enough that the second run is not the first with different names |
