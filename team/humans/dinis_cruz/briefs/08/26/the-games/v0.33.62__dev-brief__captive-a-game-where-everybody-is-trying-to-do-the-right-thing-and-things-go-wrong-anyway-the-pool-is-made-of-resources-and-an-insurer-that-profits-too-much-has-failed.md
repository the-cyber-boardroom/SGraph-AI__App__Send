# CAPTIVE: A Game Where Everybody Is Trying To Do The Right Thing And Things Go Wrong Anyway, Where The Pool Is Made Of Resources Rather Than Money, And Where An Insurer That Profits Too Much Has Failed As Surely As One That Makes A Loss

**version** v0.33.62
**date** 26 August 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Product, Design, Engineering, Ambassador

**type** Dev brief

*Sixth of 26 August, and the second game. The first, EXCESS, puts the player inside one team as the insured. This one puts the player above every team as the insurer, and supplies the one piece the model was missing, since the corpus has every part of an insurance market except an external insurer and the parent organisation now plays that role. The real industry's name for that structure is a captive. The design has three commitments the request made explicit: nobody in the company is dishonest, so every loss comes from hallucination, from users, or from an outside adversary; the first currency is resources rather than money, because that is what the ledger meters; and the whole world is one graph at several altitudes, which is why a subsidiary here is an estate in EXCESS. One external source was read for the graph vocabulary and is in Sources. Limitation: no playtest, first-guess numbers, and the three difficulty dials are the part most likely to need retuning.*

---

## What This Is

A city-building game about being the insurer inside your own organisation: **the player is the group, running a captive pool that covers its own subsidiaries, each of which employs agents that do work for users under policies the player writes, and three facts shape everything, the first being that when the insurer owns the insured the premium is an internal transfer and not income, so the group's money comes only from products and services sold and from losses that do not happen, and the captive's own result is a calibration signal that should sit in a narrow band, since a pool that makes a large surplus has priced its subsidiaries out of work as surely as a pool that makes a loss has underpriced their risk; the second being that everybody in this company is trying to do the right thing, so there is no deception anywhere in the design, and losses arrive from three sources of weather instead, namely agents that hallucinate with complete confidence, users who do things no product manager imagined, and an external adversary configurable from script kiddies through opportunists to an organised group with a commercial motive that comes back when it succeeds, which makes the gap between what a subsidiary believes about itself and what is true a matter of not knowing rather than of lying, and makes measurement and experience the way the whole company learns together; the third being that the pool is denominated in resources, tokens, bandwidth, hours and records, because that is what the system meters and what a claim can settle in without a person, with money appearing only at the edge as revenue and as an exchange rate the board owns and resets quarterly, so that a pool can be rich in tokens and insolvent in records and the player learns that solvency is the minimum across meters rather than a total; the world is a city, where subsidiaries are districts, users are population that brings revenue and trouble in proportion, conditions are infrastructure that costs something to build and lowers real risk once built, recoverability is a control the player can buy by vaulting a district's records, and the threat dial is the difficulty setting; there are three ways to lose and two are opposite, ruin, starvation and the breach, and the way out is never on the rate dial alone; and the thing the game must not teach is that the most dangerous subsidiary is the bad one, since the district that produces the claims is the district that produces the revenue, and the job is to make it insurable rather than to price it out.** New contributions: **the parent as captive completing the model, the alignment band as the captive's objective, resources as the pool's denomination with money as a named air gap, an honest world where beliefs update rather than lies get caught, three configurable sources of weather including a persistent commercial adversary, vaulting as recoverability purchased, and a subsidiary here being an estate in EXCESS under identical rules.**

## The Pitch

> **CAPTIVE.** You are the group. Five districts work for you and every one of them wants cover. Everybody is doing their best. The agents are confident and sometimes wrong. The users are many and sometimes strange. Somebody outside is watching, and how good they are is up to you. The board wants money. The risk committee wants a pool that is neither rich nor poor. Both are right.

One line for the splash: **the premium is not your income. You own the people paying it.**

## Why The Parent Being The Insurer Changes Everything

The insurance brief of 26 August observed that premium and claim share a denomination, which makes capital allocation a closer analogue than insurance. Once the insurer is the parent, that observation is the whole economy.

| In a commercial insurer | In a captive |
|---|---|
| Premium is revenue | Premium is a transfer between two pockets of the same coat |
| Underwriting profit is the objective | Underwriting profit is a calibration reading |
| A bad risk is declined | A bad risk is a district that still has to exist tomorrow |
| Pricing out a customer loses a sale | Pricing out a district loses its output |

So the only external money is **revenue from products and services** and **losses that leave the group**. Everything else is allocation, and the premium's real job is to change behaviour rather than to fund a pool.

## The Captive's Objective Is A Band, Not A Maximum

The request put it precisely: the insurer should aim to be profitable, but not that much, and not to make losses. Both tails mean the same thing, **the policies and the risks are not aligned**.

| Combined ratio for the quarter | Reading |
|---|---|
| Below 0.85 | Over-collecting. Districts are paying for risk they do not carry, and working less because of it |
| **0.85 to 1.05** | **Aligned.** The premiums match the losses, which is the only thing a captive is for |
| Above 1.05 | Under-collecting. The pool is carrying risk it did not price |

The board review prints the band every quarter, and the end-of-run grade moves one row up for a run mostly in band and one row down for any quarter far outside it. **A captive that ends with a huge surplus has failed at the thing it exists to do**, and the game says so.

## The First Currency Is Resources

The pool holds four meters, because those are what the system counts and what a claim can settle in without a person.

```
   tok    thousands of tokens        what agents think with
   bw     bandwidth                  what users and attackers consume
   hrs    agent hours                what everything costs to recover
   rec    thousands of records       what is irreplaceable
```

Every district has a primary resource. Work costs primary plus hours. Losses land in primary plus hours. Premiums are paid in the same units. **Solvency is checked per meter and reported as the minimum**, so a pool can be rich in tokens and ruined in records, and the player learns that a limit written in one unit needs capital in that unit behind it.

**Money appears only at the edge.** Revenue from products and services is in money. The exchange rate that converts a resource to money is set by the board, reset quarterly, and printed. In the vocabulary of the graph work, that rate is where the game's graph stops modelling and reality begins: a **named air gap with an owner and a frequency**. The owner is the board and the frequency is quarterly. The resource meters are the twins, and money is the thing beyond them.

## Everybody Is Trying To Do The Right Thing

There is no dishonesty anywhere in the design. That has three consequences that make the game truer than a game about liars.

**The gap between belief and truth is not knowing.** Each district fills in a questionnaire sincerely. Some of them are wrong about themselves, because a customer-facing team has never thought about how suggestible its agent is, and a finance team does not know its ledger reaches the outside. **Beliefs update.** A district that is measured learns its true blast. A district that suffers a hallucination adjusts its view of its own ambition. The company converges on the truth together, and the player's choice is whether to pay for measurement now or for experience later.

**Candour becomes observability.** The question is no longer whether a district tells you, but how quickly it notices. A loss unnoticed for a month grows, because a hallucination compounds and an attacker settles in. Audit as a condition improves observability, and nothing is being hidden by anyone.

**Cause is unknown on arrival.** A claim says how much and in what unit. It does not say whether an agent hallucinated, a user did something strange, or somebody outside got in, because the district does not know either. Forensics finds out, and matters, because the causes reprice differently and the risk committee asks.

## The Weather: Three Sources Of Loss

Each is a dial set at the start of a run, which is the difficulty setting of a city builder.

| Source | Dial | What it is | What controls it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hallucination** | 1 to 5 | An agent does the confident wrong thing. Frequency rises with ambition and the dial, falls with craft | Allow-list mandates limit what the wrong thing can touch. Craft. Vaulting makes it recoverable |
| **Users** | 1 to 5 | Population does something unexpected: a surge, odd input, a request outside the product. Frequency rises with users and suggestibility | Guardrails, at the cost of friction, which slows user growth |
| **Threat actors** | 0 to 3 | Somebody outside is trying | Everything, and none of it is free |

**The threat dial has three living tiers**, and they behave differently rather than merely rolling harder.

| Tier | Who | Picks | Wants | Persists |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Script kiddies | Anything reaching the outside, at random | Bandwidth. Noise | No |
| 2 | Opportunists | The exposed district with the fewest controls | Whatever is easy | No |
| 3 | **Organised, commercial** | The district with the most valuable records | **Records, exfiltrated, which cannot be un-exfiltrated** | **Returns next month, better, if it succeeded** |

Tier three is the one that makes vaulting matter. Exfiltrated plaintext is an irreversible loss and the breach condition; exfiltrated ciphertext is a recovery cost in hours. **Recoverability is a control the player can buy**, and the design brief of 26 August said recoverability decides insurability, so buying it is what turns an uninsurable district into an insurable one.

## The World Is A City

The subsidiaries are districts. Users are population. The loop is the city builder's loop.

```
   build       a district ships product and users arrive
      |
   population  users bring revenue, and trouble in proportion
      |
   trouble     surges, strange requests, and attackers following the crowd
      |
   infrastructure   conditions cost something and lower real risk
      |
   friction    some users leave, some output is lost
      |
   build       ...
```

**Users grow when work is delivered and shrink after an incident**, so a district that ships well becomes valuable, and valuable is what the organised tier is looking for. The player who grows The Desk to a hundred thousand users has built the target.

| District | Does | Primary | Users | Reaches | What it does not know about itself |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Foundry** | Builds the product | tok | some | repo, production | Pushes harder than it thinks, and production is further-reaching than it thinks |
| **Archivists** | Records and maintenance | rec | few | archive | Nothing. Its self-assessment is accurate and dull |
| **The Desk** | Customer facing | bw | many | mail, outside | Has never considered how suggestible a customer-facing agent is |
| **Ledgerwise** | Finance operations | rec | few | ledger, outside | Does not know the ledger reaches the outside |
| **Skunkworks** | Research | tok | none | repo, outside | Believes its ambition is ordinary |

## The Market Responds To Belief, Not Truth

Each district holds a view of its own fair rate, computed from **what it believes** about itself. Offered more than that by a margin, it leaves and self-insures, which halves its output because the board will not let an uninsured district touch production. Offered less by a margin, it expands.

**The district that leaves is the one that does not know it needs cover.** Ledgerwise believes it is cheap. At a rate that is right for what it actually is, it walks, and the next loss on the ledger falls on the group directly. That is adverse selection in an honest world, and it is more instructive than the dishonest kind, because the remedy is measurement rather than suspicion: a measured district updates its belief and comes back at a rate it now understands.

## The Tower, And Recoverability As Something You Buy

```
   +-------------------------------------------+
   |            THE BOARD                      |   above the limit: a strike
   +-------------------------------------------+
   |            THE POOL                       |   excess up to limit: the player
   +-------------------------------------------+
   |         THE DISTRICT                      |   the excess: the incentive
   +-------------------------------------------+

   EXCLUDED losses do not enter the tower. The district bears all of it.
   VAULTED districts turn a records loss into a recovery cost in hours.
```

**Every loss lands somewhere.** An exclusion moves it down, a low limit moves it up, and neither makes it smaller. Two things make a loss smaller: a condition that lowers frequency, and vaulting that converts the irreversible into the recoverable.

## Conditions Are Infrastructure

| Condition | The district must | Costs | Does |
|---|---|---|---|
| `measured` | Register and be measured | A little output | Reveals true blast to everyone including the district. Drift becomes visible |
| `allow-list` | Write every mandate as an allow-list | Some output | Fewer hallucinations reach anything that matters |
| `no-outside` | Keep every mandate off the outside | More output. Refused for a district with users | Not targetable. Blast falls sharply |
| `guardrails` | Validate and rate-limit what users can do | User growth slows | Fewer user events, and attacks find less purchase |
| `audit` | Monthly audit of grants against policies | A little output | Notices losses sooner, before they grow |
| `vaulted` | Records in vaults with immutable history | A little output | **Records losses become recoverable.** Exfiltration becomes hours, not records |
| `vendor-move` | Leave the shared vendor | One bad month | Leaves the correlation group |

**Every condition costs output or users, and every condition lowers real risk.** The cheapest premium the player can offer is a condition, not a discount.

## Claims: Three Answers, And The Cause Is Unknown

A claim arrives with an amount, a unit, a district and a story. The district does not know what caused it.

| Answer | Effect |
|---|---|
| **respond** | Spend hours now. If it turns out to be an attack or a user event, the loss halves. If it was a hallucination, the hours are gone |
| **forensics** | Spend fewer hours, wait a month, learn the cause and the true size. Beliefs update. The organised tier's return is warned |
| **accept** | Pay now through the tower. Cause recorded as unknown. **Nobody learns anything**, and the committee will ask |

**Accepting blind is the cheap option and the expensive one**, because a company that does not know why it lost cannot reprice, cannot update a belief, and cannot see the adversary coming back.

## Three Ways To Lose, And Two Are Opposite

| Lose condition | Cause | The lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **RUIN** | Any meter falls below half of what the written limits require. The board recapitalises and removes you | A limit is a promise in a unit, and the unit needs capital behind it |
| **STARVATION** | Revenue collapses for two quarters | **Terms or conditions so heavy that nothing ships. Bounding is not free** |
| **THE BREACH** | The pool paid any part of an irreversible loss, or one loss went through the tower to the board | Something uninsurable was insured, and vaulting would have made it insurable |

**Scoring is group money over twelve months**, with the captive's alignment band and the board's goodwill moving the grade a row either way. The end screen prints group profit first, the pool's meters second, and beside the meters the words "this is not profit".

## Events, Which Are The Findings As Weather

| Event | What happens | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| **Vendor Update** | Every district on the shared vendor hallucinates more this month | Correlation through a dependency nobody chose |
| **Model Regression** | Everybody hallucinates more for two months | The dial is not yours |
| **Correlated Spike** | Every district on one vendor takes a loss in the same month | Why capital exceeds the average month |
| **Traffic Surge** | Users double for a month, and so do their surprises | Population is revenue and trouble in one number |
| **Campaign** | The threat tier rises by one for two months | The adversary has plans of their own |
| **The Risk Committee** | Solvency per meter, and three claims: who accepted, and what caused it | A draw is an acceptance event, and a cause is a fact |
| **Consolidation** | The board moves a district onto the shared vendor | Diversification undone by somebody else |
| **The Windfall** | A district overreaches and ships something extraordinary, with a loss attached | Reprice up for the overreach, or down for the result |
| **Drift** | A district's real blast grows, and it does not know | Why measurement is repeated |
| **Exchange Move** | The board reprices one resource | The air gap has an owner, and it is not you |
| **Appetite** | The board wants a dividend, or wants more capital | Two masters, both you |

**The Risk Committee is the recurring character**, two decent people with a spreadsheet, and a player with clean answers enjoys the meeting.

## Phase One, Phase Two, And The Fractal

Phase one is a rules document a player pastes into a chat session, attached as `v0.33.62__reference__captive-phase-one-engine-rules-paste-this-into-a-session-and-say-begin.md`, with a state block, resolution tables and a turn protocol. **One thing is on the honour system**: the truths the districts do not yet know about themselves sit in a marked section of the state block the player is asked not to read. Nobody is lying; the player is simply asked not to know what the company does not know yet. Phase two seals it properly.

Phase two is the same engine as EXCESS with a different player position: Python, a terminal interface, JSON state in the phase one shape, deterministic under a seed, narration optional.

**The two games are one graph at two altitudes.** The graph work publishes a test for whether a system is fractal rather than merely hierarchical: zoom into any node and it must expand into a graph obeying identical rules, with no new format and no special case. A district in CAPTIVE is an estate in EXCESS. The five attributes are the same five, the districts inside are the same districts, and the state blocks share a shape on purpose. A subsidiary row can be expanded into a playable estate, a month of CAPTIVE is twenty days of EXCESS, and nothing changes format on the way down. That is the requirement, and it is also the reason a run of one game could eventually be saved as a vault and opened by the other.

## What The Game Must Not Teach

- **Not that the premium is income.** It is a transfer, and a captive that behaves like a commercial insurer starves its own group.
- **Not that a large surplus is success.** It is the sign that the policies and the risks are misaligned, and the band exists to say so.
- **Not that the dangerous district is the bad one.** Foundry produces the claims and the revenue, and the job is to make it insurable.
- **Not that anybody is at fault.** The agent was confident, the user was curious, and the adversary was doing their job. The design has no villain inside the company.
- **Not that exclusion removes risk.** It moves the loss to a district you still own. Vaulting is the thing that changes what a loss is.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not an actuarial model.** Frequency and severity are dice with modifiers, and nothing here estimates a real distribution.
- **Not a threat model.** The three tiers are caricatures with different habits, useful for a game and useless for a security assessment.
- **Not a description of how captives are regulated.** Real captives sit under regimes this reduces to four meters and one committee.
- **Not a specification for the product.** Districts, dials and towers are game objects, and the shipped schema has none of them.
- **Not a balanced game.** The dials and the demand table are the guesses most likely to be wrong, and they are marked in the rules.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Money as the score, resources as the currency | It is true to the ledger and it asks the player to hold two denominations in their head, which some will find fussy |
| An honest world | It removes the cheap drama of catching a liar and replaces it with the slower drama of not knowing, which is truer and less fun in the first hour |
| Cause unknown on arrival | It makes forensics a real decision and it makes every claim feel like an interrogation of a colleague who did nothing wrong |
| The organised tier returning | It gives the adversary a memory and it can make a run feel persecuted if the dial is high |
| The alignment band | It is the right objective for a captive and it will read to some players as the game punishing them for winning |
| A game about being the parent | It is the best explainer of the captive position we could build, and it is also an argument for our product wearing a costume, which should be disclosed |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Are four meters too many for a chat engine? | Two might teach the same lesson; four is truer to the ledger |
| Should users be a fifth meter? | Population as a resource the pool could insure is the natural extension and a much bigger build |
| What does the organised tier do with what it took? | Nothing, in this design. An extortion mechanic is the obvious sequel and the obvious way to make it unpleasant |
| Should beliefs update on their own? | A district that suffers a loss learns something now; whether it learns without one is the calibration question |
| Can EXCESS runs feed CAPTIVE? | Designed for and not built, and the most interesting thing either game could become |
| Who owns the exchange rate in phase two? | The board in the design; a live rate from a real meter is the demonstration |
