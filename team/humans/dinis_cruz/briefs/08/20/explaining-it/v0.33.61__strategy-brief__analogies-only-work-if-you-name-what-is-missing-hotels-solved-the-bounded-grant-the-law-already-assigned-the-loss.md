## What This Is

An analogy set for the grant and mandate distinction, and the argument that most analogies for it fail in a specific and predictable way: **the memo offers two, a contractor given the keys to a whole bank branch to fix one lamp and a person given house keys to fetch one item, and they are good, they teach different things, and both share a flaw that makes them dangerous on their own, which is that a human is holding the keys, so a listener's intuition silently supplies a name, an employer, a licence, insurance, a reputation and a legal consequence, and concludes that the arrangement is fine because in the real world it usually is; every one of those is a control that lives outside the key and none of them exists for a rented agent, so an analogy of this family has to be delivered with what is missing attached to it or it reassures rather than warns; a second and stronger test follows, which is that an analogy must survive an honest holder, because ambient authority means the danger is a deputy persuaded by somebody else rather than a thief, and any framing that only lands when the listener imagines a burglar teaches the wrong controls, which is why the best of the new analogies is a locksmith talked into letting the removal men in; the most useful analogies turn out not to be the frightening ones but the ones where somebody already solved the problem, since a hotel issues a card that opens one room until Thursday and a car maker ships a valet key that starts the engine and locks the boot, which reframes the whole conversation from please be careful into why does a code host do less than a budget hotel; the business set has an instrument that is exactly this object rather than a metaphor for it, since a limited power of attorney carries a principal, an attorney, an enumerated scope, a duration and a revocation path, which is the five-field mandate this corpus specified this morning, written down by lawyers centuries before anybody had an API key; and the sharpest finding is that the excess is not merely unaccepted, because under the doctrine of apparent authority a principal is bound by an act a reasonable third party would take to be authorised even where actual authority was absent, the doctrine turns on an act or knowing omission by the principal that created the appearance, and issuing a visibly broad credential is exactly such an act, so the gap this corpus has been calling unaccepted has in fact already been assigned, to the party that issued the grant, by default and by long precedent.** It is the eighth document of 20 August (cross-ref: the v0.33.61 grant and mandate brief, the v0.33.56 plugins brief, the v0.33.61 register interface brief, the v0.33.52 outbound maturity model, and the v0.33.60 service twin brief). New contributions: **the reassurance failure named as the family flaw, the honest-holder test, the solved-elsewhere analogies as the strongest register, the third overlap region where the mandate exceeds the grant, the limited power of attorney identified as the instrument rather than a metaphor, and apparent authority shown to have already allocated the loss.**

## The Two In The Memo Do Different Jobs

Both are good and they are not interchangeable, which matters because whichever one gets used first sets what the listener thinks the problem is.

| Analogy | What it teaches | What it does not |
|---|---|---|
| **The bank branch contractor**, given keys to a building of unlocked terminals and an open vault to fix one lamp | **Scale.** The gap is not a margin, it is orders of magnitude | Says nothing about why it usually turns out fine |
| **The house keys**, given so somebody can fetch one thing | **Irreversibility, and depth.** You cannot un-give a key, and everything inside is reachable | Feels small, because one house is not a bank |

The memo's own sharpest observation belongs to the second, and it deserves lifting out because it kills an entire family of proposed mitigations. The project lead: **"even if the documents are in a safe, because maybe the person can then hack into the safe."**

> **A safe inside a house you handed the keys to is a delay, not a boundary.** The holder has time, privacy and physical access, which is what safes are designed to be given only briefly.

The software form is exact and it is common: **encrypting a file on a disk the agent can read, using a key the agent can reach, is a safe in a house you gave the keys to.** So is a configuration flag the agent can flip, a confirmation prompt the agent answers, and a policy file the agent can edit. Any control living inside the granted perimeter is a speed bump, and the corpus has held the general form of this since 6 August: enforcement belongs where it cannot be bypassed.

## And Both Reassure, Because A Human Is Holding The Keys

This is the flaw in the family and it is worth naming before adding any more analogies.

The reason the bank branch story does not frighten anybody is that in the real world it works. It works because the contractor is surrounded by controls that have nothing to do with the key.

| Control that makes the human case tolerable | Present for a rented agent? |
|---|---|
| A named individual | **No.** A session |
| An employer who can be sued | Sometimes, and the terms usually disclaim it |
| A licence or registration that can be withdrawn | **No** |
| Insurance and a bond | **No** |
| A reputation that persists to the next job | **No. The session ends and nothing carries** |
| Deterrence, because consequences land on a person | **No.** This is the load-bearing one |
| One body, in one place, at one time | **No.** Arbitrarily parallel |
| A working day's worth of time to act in | **No.** Milliseconds |
| Traces somebody else keeps: entry logs, cameras | **Partial, and self-reported by the agent** |

So the analogy must be delivered with its correction attached. The honest form:

> It is the bank branch contractor, except that nobody knows their name, they cannot be sued, struck off, or insured against, they will never work here again so reputation buys nothing, there are four thousand of them on the premises at once, and they have finished before you reached the end of this sentence.

**That is the version that does work.** The short version reassures; the long version is the argument. A site or a deck that uses the first without the second is arguing against itself.

## The Test: Does It Survive An Honest Holder

A second filter, and it removes more bad analogies than the first.

**An analogy is only useful here if it still bites when the holder is honest.** The corpus established the reason on 6 August: ambient authority is why prompt injection works, because the attacker does not need to acquire authority, only to persuade the party that already holds it. So a framing that only lands when the listener imagines a thief teaches people to look for thieves, and the thing that will actually happen is that a helpful agent does what a convincing instruction told it to do.

The analogy that passes this test cleanly is new and it is the one to lead with for a security audience:

> **The locksmith and the removal men.** You give a locksmith your house key so he can fix the front door. While he is there, someone calls and says the owner has asked him to let the removal men in as well. He is not dishonest. He has the key, the request is plausible, and letting them in is entirely within what he can do. Nothing about his honesty prevented it, and nothing about vetting him more carefully would have.

That is prompt injection told as a story, and it points at the right control, which is that the locksmith should not have held a key that opens anything but the front door.

## The Strongest Analogies Are The Ones Where Somebody Already Solved It

The instinct is to reach for frightening comparisons. **The more effective register is embarrassment**, because the bounded grant is not an unsolved research problem, it is ordinary in every industry that thought about it for an afternoon.

**The hotel key card.** You arrive at a hotel and are handed a card that opens one room and the pool, works from today until Thursday, and stops working when you check out. Nobody negotiated it, nobody wrote a policy, and nobody hoped you would only enter your own room. **A budget hotel chain solved the bounded grant decades ago, at a cost of pennies per guest.**

**The valet key.** Some cars ship a second key that starts the engine, limits the speed, and will not open the boot or the glovebox. The manufacturer anticipated that owners hand their car to a stranger for a narrow purpose, and shaped the grant at the point of issue rather than asking the stranger to behave.

The rhetorical move is the pairing, and it is more effective than any threat:

> The same engineer who cannot issue a token scoped to one repository for one afternoon was, this year, handed a key that opens one hotel room and expires on Thursday. **The question is not whether bounded grants are possible. It is why the tools we give agents do less than a Travelodge.**

That reframes the ask from a warning into a missing product feature, which is where it belongs, and it matches what today's testing found: the surface question fails not because the fact is unknowable but because nobody offers to distribute it.

## The Everyday Set

Ordered by how fast they land.

| Analogy | The grant | The mandate | Teaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hotel key card** | Would be: every room, every floor, forever | One room, until Thursday | **The solved case.** Bounded at issue, invisibly |
| **Valet key** | Would be: the whole car, boot and all | Drive it to the space, slowly | **Shaping the grant is a product decision** |
| **House keys** | Every room, every drawer, every document, and the ability to burn it down | Fetch the passport from the hall table | **Irreversibility and depth** |
| **Bank branch contractor** | Unlocked terminals, signed-in users, an open vault | Replace the lamp | **Scale** |
| **The babysitter** | Your home, your children, your medicine cabinet, all night | Keep them alive and asleep | **How much of the real answer is social scaffolding rather than access control** |
| **The locksmith and the removal men** | Whatever the key opens | Fix the front door | **The confused deputy: honesty is not the control** |

## The Business Set

Where the analogy stops being an analogy.

**The cleaning contract and the master key.** The contract says empty the bins and vacuum the floor. The key opens the chief executive's office and the filing room. The mandate is written down, in a document, that the door has never read. **That gap is the whole subject, and it exists in every office building on earth**, which is why it is a comfortable place to start with a non-technical audience.

**The purchasing card.** A card issued with a five thousand pound limit and a merchant category restriction. Nobody writes a policy asking the holder not to spend more, because the bound is in the instrument. **This one is worth using with anybody who owns a budget**, because it shows that the organisation already knows how to do this when the resource is money, and simply has not done it when the resource is access. The corpus has said the same thing since 27 July, naming spend and time ceilings as among the cheapest containment controls available and observing that almost nobody implements them.

**The locum clinician.** Arrives, is registered, has a defined scope of practice, is insured, is supervised, and can be struck off. **Use this one to enumerate what is absent**, because it is the densest example of the missing-controls table above.

**The limited power of attorney.** And this one is not a metaphor.

| Field this corpus specified this morning | The instrument's name for it |
|---|---|
| Issuer | The principal, or donor |
| Subject | The attorney, or donee |
| Scope | The enumerated powers |
| Interval | The term, or the triggering and ending conditions |
| Revocation path | Revocation, and the formalities for it |

**The five-field mandate is a limited power of attorney**, and the distinction lawyers draw between a general and a limited power is exactly the distinction between a grant and a mandate. It is worth saying plainly to a professional audience: **the profession that has thought hardest about one party acting on another's behalf writes the narrow instrument by default, and considers the broad one a thing you do only when you must and with your eyes open.** Software inverted that, and issues the general power as the default because it is the only one the interface offers.

The word itself carries the same lineage. A mandate in Roman law was a contract in which one party undertook, at another's request, to do something in good faith, and a mandate to do an unlawful act created no obligation at all. That last point has a direct register consequence and it agrees with a conclusion this corpus reached independently earlier today: **a mandate cannot authorise what its issuer had no authority to authorise**, which is the same rule as a mandate's trust ceiling being its issuer's rather than its subject's.

## The Law Already Assigned The Loss

The finding that changes what this brief is for, and it corrects an emphasis in this morning's strategy brief rather than a claim.

That brief established that excess authority is unaccepted by construction, because nobody wrote it down and a risk needs a named acceptor. **That is true as an internal statement about the organisation's own records and it is not true as a statement about the world.**

Under the doctrine of apparent authority, a principal is bound by an agent's act where a reasonable third party would understand the agent to have had authority, **even where the agent had no actual authority, express or implied.** The doctrine rests on estoppel: the third party was given an assurance, relied on it, and it would be inequitable for the principal to deny it. It requires some act or knowing omission on the part of the principal that created the appearance, and a principal who stands silently by can be bound by acquiescence.

Mapped across:

| The doctrine | The agent case |
|---|---|
| The principal | The organisation that issued the credential |
| The act creating the appearance | **Issuing a credential whose grant is visibly broad** |
| Actual authority | The mandate, which nobody outside can see |
| **Apparent authority** | **The grant, which everybody outside can see** |
| Who is bound by an act in the gap | **The principal** |

So the correct sentence for an executive audience is not that the exposure is unowned. It is:

> **The gap between grant and mandate is not an exposure nobody accepted. It is an exposure already assigned to you, by a rule older than the technology, on the basis that you are the one who made it look permitted.**

Two honest qualifications, and they belong in any deck that uses this.

**Whether these doctrines reach software agents is unsettled**, varies by jurisdiction, and is being argued rather than decided. This brief is not legal advice and the site should say so wherever it uses the argument.

**But the shape of the answer is the point**, and the shape is not in doubt: when the legal system has been asked what happens where what somebody could do exceeded what they were told to do, its answer has consistently been that the party who created the appearance carries it. Anybody hoping software will be treated differently is hoping for an exception rather than relying on a rule.

## The Overlap Has Three Regions, Not Two

The memo asks how the two overlap, and the answer has a part that gets forgotten.

```
   +-------------------------------------------------------+
   |  THE GRANT                                            |
   |  everything the credential permits                    |
   |                                                       |
   |     +-----------------------+                         |
   |     |  THE WORKING SET      |    EXCESS AUTHORITY     |
   |     |  authorised and used  |    held, unauthorised,  |
   |     |                       |    unaccepted, and      |
   |     +-----------------------+    already assigned     |
   |          |                                            |
   +----------|--------------------------------------------+
              |
        +-----+---------------+
        |  THE SHORTFALL      |     <-- the region nobody draws
        |  mandated and not   |
        |  permitted          |
        +---------------------+
```

**The third region is where the mandate asks for something the grant does not cover**, and it matters because of what happens next. An agent told to do a job it lacks a capability for does not usually stop and report; it improvises. It looks for another route, another credential, another tool, a wider token that somebody left lying about.

So the shortfall is not a harmless inefficiency. **It is the pressure that produces the next over-broad credential**, and it is the mechanism behind the pattern the corpus recorded on 19 August, where every workaround for a missing capability hands over a larger identity than the one that was needed. A narrow grant with a mandate it cannot satisfy is not safe; it is unstable, and it resolves in the wrong direction.

Which gives a rule worth stating alongside the arithmetic: **shrinking a grant without checking the mandate against it moves the problem rather than solving it**, and the register should be able to show the shortfall as readily as the excess.

## The Analogies To Avoid

Named, because each of these is in circulation and each teaches something wrong.

- **The burglar with your keys.** Invites vetting, monitoring and trust scores, and misses the confused deputy entirely. The agent that causes the loss will have been honest.
- **The insider threat.** Same defect, and it makes the response a personnel matter, which is a department that cannot fix a token's scope.
- **It is like sharing your password.** Too small. It makes an architectural asymmetry sound like a hygiene lapse and invites a training module as the remedy.
- **Zero trust.** A phrase that has been absorbed until it means a posture rather than an arithmetic, and it lets an audience nod without any number changing.
- **Nuclear launch codes and the two-man rule, used first.** True and useful later, and used first it makes the problem sound exotic, when the entire point is that it is ordinary and that hotels solved it.
- **Any of the good ones, unaccompanied.** The bank branch and the house keys both reassure without the missing-controls table. Never ship one alone.

| Press and general audience | **The house keys**, followed immediately by the correction | Fastest to land, and useless without what is missing |

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not legal advice.** The doctrines are described as they apply to human agents and their reach into software agents is unsettled.
- **Not a claim that agents are dishonest.** The best analogy here has an honest locksmith in it, and that is the point.
- **Not a replacement for the arithmetic.** An analogy explains why the number matters; it does not produce the number.
- **Not a set to use whole.** Each one teaches one property, and using the wrong one for the wrong property is how this messaging goes wrong.
- **Not new vocabulary.** Grant, mandate and excess authority were settled this morning; this makes them explicable.
