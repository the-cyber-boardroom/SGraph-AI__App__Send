## What This Is

The domain ontology and taxonomy for the agent-mandate work, the shared backbone everything else hangs on: **a small set of core entities, a principal who grants a mandate to an agent, a mandate that authorises capabilities delivered through tools, the actions those enable, the assets they affect, the parties those assets belong to, the harms that result, the risk carried and its acceptance, the evidence that supports it, the environment and integration mode it runs in, and the controls and standards it maps to, joined by the relationships that turn them into a graph; alongside the taxonomies, the hierarchies of party, asset, action and harm, capability, integration mode, certainty, and risk state; and the ontology-of-ontologies structure, a stable core with per-company and per-audience ontologies layered on top; it is the formal vocabulary the assessment, the blast-radius graph, the compliance subset graph, the third-party mapping, the PBOM, and the supply-chain model all share.** It develops the ontology and taxonomy started in the corpus-vault and graphs-of-graphs work (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 graphs-of-graphs brief, the v0.33.28 corpus-vault-architecture, assessment-template, and compliance briefs, and the v0.33.30 third-party-impact and compromise-mapping briefs). New contributions: **the core entities, the relationships, the taxonomies, and the ontology-of-ontologies layering.**

## The Framing

What this is: **the formal vocabulary for the domain.**

The earlier briefs began to look at an ontology and taxonomy for this material. This develops it into a usable model, so that the assessment, the graphs, and the mappings all speak the same language and connect.

## The Core Entities

The classes the model is built from:

| Entity | What it is |
|--------|-----------|
| Principal | The human or organisation that owns assets and grants the mandate |
| Agent | The non-human identity that acts under the mandate |
| Mandate | The bundle of authority granted to the agent |
| Capability | A power the mandate confers (read, write, send, run) |
| Tool | The mechanism that delivers a capability (shell, files, web, email, MCP) |
| Action | A concrete thing the agent can do with a capability |
| Asset | A thing of value an action can affect (data, credentials, systems, communications, identity, reputation) |
| Party | Anyone an asset belongs to or who is impacted |
| Harm | An adverse outcome of an action |
| Risk | The potential for harm carried by a mandate |
| RiskAcceptance | A party's recorded acceptance of a risk, for a duration |
| Evidence | A fact that supports a risk, harm, or capability |
| Environment | Where the agent runs (local, cloud, region) |
| IntegrationMode | How it connects (inline or out-of-bounds, linked or business, local or cloud) |
| Control | A standard's requirement the model maps to |
| Provenance | The recorded history of who did what, when |

## The Relationships

The edges that turn the entities into a graph:

```
  Principal --grants--> Mandate --authorises--> Capability
                                                   |
                                          delivered-through
                                                   v
                                                 Tool
  Capability --enables--> Action --affects--> Asset --belongs-to--> Party
                              |                                         ^
                          causes                                       |
                              v                                   impacts
                            Harm --------------------------------------+
  Mandate --carries--> Risk --accepted-by--> Party (for a duration)
  Evidence --supports--> Risk and Harm and Capability
  Capability --gates--> Exploitability        (the PBOM relationship)
  Control --maps-to--> Capability and Asset   (the compliance relationship)
  Provenance --records--> Action
```

Read in plain terms: a principal grants a mandate, which authorises capabilities delivered through tools, which enable actions, which affect assets that belong to parties and cause harms that impact parties; the mandate carries risk that a party accepts for a duration; evidence supports the claims; capabilities gate exploitability; and controls map to capabilities and assets.

## The Taxonomies

The hierarchies that classify the entities:

- **Party.** Principal, individual, company, peer executive, department, government agency, customer, vendor.
- **Asset.** Data, credentials and secrets, systems, communications, identity, reputation.
- **Action and harm.** Access, delete, corrupt, corrupt irreparably, act on behalf of, exfiltrate, contact others, impact other parties (from the compromise mapping).
- **Capability and tool.** Read, write, send, run; shell, files, web, email, memory, MCP, skills, autonomy.
- **Integration mode.** Inline or out-of-bounds; linked, dedicated, or business; local or cloud.
- **Certainty.** Fact, hypothesis, opinion (with a certainty level), and status potential, accepted, mitigated.
- **Risk state.** Exists, accepted, narrowed, removed.
- **Evolution.** A Wardley stage attribute, genesis to commodity, on any component.

## The Ontology Of Ontologies

How it scales without becoming rigid: **a stable core, with layers on top.**

The core ontology above stays small and stable. On top of it sit per-company ontologies, an organisation's own departments, systems, roles, and standards, and per-audience ontologies, the investor view, the customer view, that present the same instances in their own terms. The master vault holds the core; the layered ontologies live in their own vaults and reference it. This is the ontology-of-ontologies the graphs-of-graphs brief described, made concrete (cross-ref: the graphs-of-graphs and corpus-vault-architecture briefs).

## How It Underpins Everything

The payoff: **one model, many uses.**

- The **assessment** captures instances of these entities for a given integration.
- The **blast-radius graph** is the Capability-Action-Asset-Party-Harm subgraph.
- The **compliance subset graph** is the Control-maps-to-Capability-and-Asset edges.
- The **third-party mapping** is the Asset-belongs-to-Party and Harm-impacts-Party edges.
- The **PBOM** is the Capability-gates-Exploitability relationship.
- The **supply-chain model** is Party-to-Party across organisations.
- **Risk acceptance** is the Risk-accepted-by-Party edge, with duration and provenance.

Because they share the model, they connect: a change to one mandate propagates through the graph to the compliance score, the third parties, and the risk register.

## What This Asks For

1. **Adopt the core entities** as the shared classes.
2. **Adopt the relationships** as the shared edges.
3. **Adopt the taxonomies** as the shared hierarchies.
4. **Keep the core small and stable** and layer specifics on top.
5. **Hold the core in the master vault** and reference it from the others.
6. **Wire the assessment, graphs, and mappings to it** so they connect.

Estimated effort: moderate; the model is clear; the work is formalising it and wiring the existing artefacts to it.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a single flat taxonomy.** It is a core with layered ontologies.
- **Not exhaustive.** It is the smallest model that connects the artefacts.
- **Not static.** It is a living graph that updates with reality.
- **Not company-specific.** The core is general; specifics are layered.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: ontologies can over-engineer.** A heavy model slows everything. Mitigation: keep the core small; layer only what a use needs.

**Tension 2: layered ontologies must reconcile.** Per-company terms differ. Mitigation: they reference the stable core, which is the reconciliation point.

**Tension 3: the model must stay close to reality.** A model that drifts is worse than none. Mitigation: it is a living graph, updated as mandates and evidence change.
