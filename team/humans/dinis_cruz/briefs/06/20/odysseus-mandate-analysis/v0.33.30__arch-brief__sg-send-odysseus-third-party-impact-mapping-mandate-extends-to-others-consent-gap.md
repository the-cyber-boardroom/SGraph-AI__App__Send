## What This Is

The Odysseus compromise mapped from the point of view of the third parties it would impact: **granting a mandate over one's own assets, in effect, extends that mandate over assets that are not the user's to grant, because the agent's blast radius reaches individuals, companies, peer executives, departments, government agencies, customers, and vendors, none of whom granted a mandate or accepted the risk, yet all of whom would bear the consequence; for each party type the mapping states, factually, the assets reached, how they are impacted, and the gap between the user's grant and the party's consent; the actions are attributable to the user, because the user holds the mandate, but the harm lands on parties who had no say; these are potential risks that have not occurred.** It extends the Odysseus mapping outward to the parties the first mapping only named (cross-ref: the v0.33.30 Odysseus case study and compromise-mapping briefs, the v0.33.40 risk-acceptance cross-domain and side-effects briefs, and the v0.33.28 supply-chain and compliance briefs). New contributions: **the mandate-the-user-cannot-grant point, the third-party taxonomy, and the per-party consent gap.**

## The Framing

What was asked: **map it from the third parties' side.**

The mapping should take the point of view of the third parties impacted by the compromise, because the logic of granting a mandate over an asset, in a way, applies to them too. Those third parties could be individuals, companies, peer executives, departments, or government agencies, among others.

## The Core Point: A Mandate The User Cannot Rightfully Grant

The principle, stated plainly: **the user grants authority over assets that are not theirs.**

A user can only rightfully grant a mandate over their own assets. But the agent's blast radius reaches assets that belong to others, so in granting the agent its mandate, the user, in effect, extends authority over things that were never theirs to grant. The third party granted no mandate and accepted no risk, yet bears the consequence. This is a statement of structure, not of fault: the user did not intend to reach those assets, but the mandate does.

## The Third Parties, Mapped

Each party type, with the assets reached, the impact, and the consent gap. The reach scales with the integrations the user enables; with email and web on, most of these become live.

| Third party | Assets reached | How impacted | The consent gap |
|-------------|----------------|--------------|------------------|
| Individuals (contacts, family) | Their messages and shared files in the user's mailbox and drives | Their conversations exposed; messages sent to them as the user | They never agreed to an agent reading or writing in the user's name |
| Companies (employer, partners) | Work data, credentials, and systems if a work account or connector is attached | Company data exposed or altered; the company's own compliance implicated | The company never authorised the agent on its assets |
| Peer executives and colleagues | Shared documents, threads, and decisions | Shared content exposed; the agent acting in shared spaces as the user | Peers never accepted the risk to their shared work |
| Departments | Cross-department data the user can reach | Other teams' data exposed or changed; ripple effects across teams | Other departments had no say |
| Government agencies and regulators | Regulated data the user can reach | The regulatory regime implicated; obligations triggered | The user cannot accept obligations on the regulator's behalf |
| Customers and counterparties | Their data in the user's files and mailbox | Their data exposed; contact made in the user's name | They never consented to agent handling |
| Vendors and supply chain | Connected systems reached via MCP or stored credentials | Their systems acted on through the user's access | The vendor never authorised the agent |

## The Consent And Acceptance Gap

The accountability point: **none of these parties accepted the risk.**

In every row, the third party neither granted a mandate nor accepted the risk, yet the consequence reaches them. By the risk-acceptance model, acceptance should flow to whoever owns each party's risk, which the user cannot provide alone, because it is not theirs to give. The compromise therefore creates exposure that no accountable owner of the affected assets ever underwrote (cross-ref: the risk-acceptance cross-domain point and the supply-chain brief).

## Why This Matters For Representation

The product point: **show the parties, not just the account.**

For the picture to be accurate and decidable, the assessment must show not only the user's own blast radius but the third parties it would reach, so that each affected party, the employer, the colleague, the regulator, can see what a mandate granted on one machine would mean for them, and accept or refuse it. The same facts, stated in each party's terms (cross-ref: the side-effects recipient-language brief and the assessment-template multi-stakeholder views).

## Honest Notes

- **These are potential risks.** They follow from the mandate's reach; they have not occurred.
- **Attribution, not blame.** The user did not intend to reach others' assets; the mandate does, which is why it must be examined before it is granted.
- **The reach scales with integrations.** Chat-only reaches almost no third parties; email, web, and connectors are what extend the mandate outward.
- **Not unique to Odysseus.** Any broadly privileged agent extends the user's mandate over others' assets; Odysseus is the worked example.
