## What This Is

The scan of OpenRouter's wider platform and what SGraph could leverage in its agentic workflows: **we currently use only a small subset, model execution and key management, while OpenRouter, fresh off a large Series B and growing fast, exposes much more, OAuth so users can connect their own OpenRouter account, programmatic provisioning of scoped and spend-limited keys, per-request usage accounting that gives the billable-units metering for free, normalized tool calling and strict structured outputs for the agents and the mapping engine, web-search and PDF plugins, model fallbacks and cost-aware provider routing that serve the no-new-cost-base constraint, server-side presets for versioned per-agent configs, prompt caching, transcription, rerank, embeddings, image and video models, and zero-data-retention and sovereign routing that support the privacy and sovereignty positioning; the recommendation is to adopt the key, billing, and structured-output features first, then the agentic and knowledge features, while noting that OpenRouter is a managed cloud service that fits the hosted path rather than the run-everywhere self-hosted one.** It answers the request to map what we are not yet using (cross-ref: the v0.33.26 openrouter-key-acquisition and central-key-management briefs, the v0.33.40 commercial-model brief, the agentic-team and need-to-ship briefs, and the v0.33.28 run-everywhere brief). Sources are paraphrased and listed. New contributions: **the capability map, the feature-to-workflow mapping, and the adoption priorities.**

## The Framing

What was asked: **map the OpenRouter features we could leverage.**

OpenRouter keeps innovating and has just taken on significant investment, and SGraph uses only a small slice, model execution and API-key management. The task is to scan the wider platform and map what could strengthen the agentic workflows.

## OpenRouter Today

The context, briefly: **a fast-growing, well-funded gateway.**

OpenRouter raised a $113M Series B led by CapitalG, Alphabet's growth fund, in late May 2026, at a roughly $1.3B valuation, more than double a year earlier, with participation from NVIDIA's, ServiceNow's, Snowflake's, MongoDB's, and Databricks' venture arms, plus Andreessen Horowitz and Menlo, bringing total funding to around $173M. The platform routes across more than 400 models from over 60 providers through an OpenAI-compatible API, serves around 8 million users, and processes roughly 100 trillion tokens monthly. The point for us is that the platform surface is wide and expanding, and we are using a fraction of it.

## What We Use Versus What Exists

The gap: **we use two features; the platform offers dozens.**

We use model execution, routing a request to a model, and key management, provisioning and tracking keys. Beyond that sit identity, billing, agent-capability, knowledge, routing, and privacy features we have not adopted.

## Keys And Identity

The most directly useful: **let users bring their own account, and mint scoped keys.**

- **OAuth (PKCE).** OpenRouter has an OAuth flow that sends a user to OpenRouter and returns a user-controlled API key to the app. This is the cleanest answer to the key-acquisition journeys: a user connects their own OpenRouter account, and SGraph never holds their credentials (cross-ref: the openrouter-key-acquisition brief).
- **Provisioning and management keys.** Keys can be created, limited, rotated, and deleted programmatically, which lets SGraph mint a scoped, spend-limited key per vault, per recipient, or per ephemeral task, and revoke it after (cross-ref: the central-key-management and ephemeral-compute briefs).
- **BYOK.** Users can bring their own provider keys, with fallback ordering, for direct cost and rate-limit control.

## Billing And Metering

The commercial unlock: **usage accounting we do not have to build.**

OpenRouter returns per-request cost and token accounting, a bring-your-own-key flag, and user tracking, which is exactly the metering the billable-units and markup model needs, so SGraph can meter and mark up usage without building its own accounting layer. A crypto billing API also exists for alternative payment (cross-ref: the commercial-model brief).

## Agent Capability

For the agentic team and the mapping engine: **tools, schemas, and presets.**

- **Tool calling**, normalized across models, for the vault agents to call tools consistently regardless of the underlying model.
- **Structured outputs**, including strict JSON-schema mode, for the assessment output, the permission and blast-radius map, the risk-factorisation matrix, and the compliance subset graph, all of which want reliable structured JSON.
- **Presets**, server-side saved prompt, model, and routing configurations, to version each agent role's config centrally, matching the librarian, architect, and ambassador roles and the version-everything ethos.
- **Reasoning controls and guardrails** for effort and safety on agent steps.

## Knowledge And Input-Output

For research, documents, and retrieval: **plugins and extra modalities.**

- **Web search plugin**, real-time search, for the deep-research and proactive-evidence agents without building a search integration.
- **File-parser plugin**, PDF processing, for document assessment.
- **Transcriptions**, for the audio-transcribe tool to route through OpenRouter rather than a separate Whisper integration.
- **Rerank and embeddings**, for the librarian's retrieval and the semantic indexes and lenses.
- **Image and video models**, for the image-transformation and url-to-image services.
- **MCP server support**, aligning with the tools-in-vault and MCP work.

## Cost, Resilience, And Routing

For the no-new-cost-base constraint: **route to the cheapest model that clears the bar.**

- **Model fallbacks** for automatic failover when a provider fails.
- **Provider routing**, sorting by price, latency, or throughput, and an auto router that picks a model per request.
- **Prompt caching** to cut the cost of the large repeated context the agents use.

Together these let SGraph hold a policy, matching each task to the cheapest model that meets the quality bar, which is exactly the discipline the cost constraint demands (cross-ref: the need-to-ship cost-constraint point).

## Privacy And Sovereignty

For the positioning, and the parked no-tracking item: **route to providers that do not retain data.**

OpenRouter exposes zero-data-retention routing, a sovereign-region routing option, and provider data-policy filtering, so SGraph can route to providers that do not retain or train on the data and to specific regions, which supports the privacy and sovereignty positioning and helps reconcile the no-tracking claim (cross-ref: the sovereignty thesis and the Odysseus privacy-versus-safety case study).

## Recommended Priorities

| Priority | Features | Why |
|----------|----------|-----|
| First | OAuth, provisioning with limits, usage accounting | Directly serve the key journeys and the billable-units model |
| Second | Structured outputs, tool calling, web search | Power the agents and the mapping engine |
| Third | ZDR, sovereign routing, data-policy filtering | Back the privacy and sovereignty claims |
| Fourth | Presets, prompt caching, fallbacks, auto router | Cost discipline and versioned configs |
| Fifth | Transcriptions, rerank, embeddings, image and video | Replace or simplify separate integrations |

## Honest Notes

- **OpenRouter is a managed cloud service.** Every request takes a data hop through its infrastructure, so it fits the SGraph hosted path, but it conflicts with the run-everywhere, run-in-the-customer-environment, and no-new-cost-base principles for the self-hosted case, where BYO-key or a self-hostable gateway is better (cross-ref: the run-everywhere brief).
- **There is a markup.** OpenRouter charges roughly 5% on inference, and 5% on BYOK above a free monthly allowance, which stacks under any SGraph markup.
- **Dependency risk.** Leaning on one gateway is a dependency; the OpenAI-compatible API keeps switching cost low.
- **Verify against live docs.** The feature set changes weekly; confirm specifics before building.
