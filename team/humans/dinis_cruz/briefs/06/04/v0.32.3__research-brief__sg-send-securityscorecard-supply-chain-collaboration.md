The company facts below are from SecurityScorecard's public material; the collaboration mapping is the project lead's.

## What SecurityScorecard Does (And The Gap It Names)

SecurityScorecard began as a security-ratings leader (continuously scanning organisations' external attack surface and rating their cybersecurity posture across risk factors) and has since repositioned into a category it calls **Supply Chain Detection and Response (SCDR)**: not just rating vendors, but detecting, prioritising, and remediating risk across an organisation's vendor ecosystem, and driving action and remediation rather than producing static assessments. Its platform leans on AI and threat intelligence, it goes to market substantially through a partner and channel network (service providers managing supply-chain risk for their clients), and it makes basic attack-surface management freely available so organisations can work with suppliers on resolving issues.

The gap SecurityScorecard itself names is the important part for this mapping. In its own words (paraphrased), legacy ratings vendors dump mountains of data on customers without the context and tooling needed to **collaborate with their business partners**, so security teams struggle to actually act on supply-chain risk. SecurityScorecard's stated aim is to remove friction from vendor collaboration and reduce the time from identifying an issue to resolving it.

| SecurityScorecard | Detail |
|-------------------|--------|
| **Origin** | Security ratings: scan the external attack surface, rate posture |
| **Now** | Supply Chain Detection and Response (SCDR): detect, prioritise, remediate vendor risk |
| **Go-to-market** | Partner and channel network; service providers managing supply-chain risk for clients |
| **The named gap** | Data without the context and tooling to collaborate with business partners; friction in vendor collaboration |
| **The goal** | Remove collaboration friction; reduce time from issue to resolution |

That named gap, collaboration with business partners across a supply chain, is precisely where the vault fits.

## Why The Vault Fits: The Collaboration Layer SCDR Needs

The core of the mapping: **SecurityScorecard detects and rates supply-chain risk; the vault is the collaboration layer that lets the affected parties act on it together, securely and selectively.** SCDR is, by SecurityScorecard's own framing, about driving action with business partners, and the corpus has built exactly the multi-player, supply-chain, evidence, and messaging machinery that action requires.

| What SCDR Needs | What The Vault Provides |
|-----------------|--------------------------|
| **Collaboration with business partners** | The vault as a shared, controlled collaboration surface (cross-ref: the v0.31.12 three-agent-demo and FAQ briefs) |
| **A supply chain of multiple players** | The vault travelling a chain of parties, each accessing the parts they should (cross-ref: the v0.31.12 PKI-not-secrets brief's key-gated supply chain) |
| **Selective sharing (not data-dumping)** | Selectively share only some parts of a vault with others; messaging beats full access (cross-ref: the v0.31.12 vault-to-vault-comms and three-agent-demo briefs) |
| **Evidence and remediation tracking** | Evidence held with provenance and version control (cross-ref: the v0.31.5 evidence briefs and the v0.31.9 blast-radius briefs) |
| **Trust between parties that do not fully trust each other** | Zero-knowledge and PKI: only the right party reads the right part (cross-ref: the PKI-not-secrets brief) |

So where SecurityScorecard identifies and prioritises supply-chain risk, the vault is how the organisation, its vendors, and the channel partners actually collaborate on resolving it, sharing exactly the right evidence with exactly the right party, no more.

## The Supply-Chain And Multi-Player Fit

The project lead points first at the supply-chain work: **the supply-chain primitives, and the model of multiple players each accessing the parts of the supply chain they should, fit SecurityScorecard's world directly.**

The project lead: **"a lot of the supply chain stuff we have talked about works really well. The whole delivery, the whole vault, the multiple players that have access to the multiple parts of the supply chain, that is very relevant."** This is the key-gated supply chain from the PKI brief (cross-ref: the v0.31.12 PKI-not-secrets brief): a vault travels the chain (the organisation, the vendor, the fourth and nth parties, the channel partner), and at each hop the right party accesses the part they should and no more. For SCDR, where the whole point is acting across a multi-party vendor ecosystem, this is the collaboration substrate: the supply chain of vaults mirrors the supply chain of vendors, with access gated per party.

## Selective Sharing, Messaging, And PKI

The project lead emphasises the about-to-be-added messaging capabilities as the powerful part: **parties can hold vaults and selectively share only some parts with others, especially with PKI in the mix.**

The project lead: **"especially taking into account some of the messaging capabilities we are about to add, you can have a really powerful situation where people can have vaults and only selectively share some parts of it with others, especially when you add our PKI to the mix, which is super powerful and super important."**

This addresses SecurityScorecard's named gap (collaboration without dumping data) precisely:

- **Selective sharing** (cross-ref: the three-agent-demo brief's messaging-beats-access): a vendor shares only the specific evidence or remediation status the organisation needs, not blanket access, and the organisation shares only the relevant finding with the vendor.
- **The messaging capabilities** (cross-ref: the v0.31.12 vault-to-vault-comms append-token brief): the organisation, vendor, and partner communicate vault-to-vault about issues and remediation, encrypted, with no broker.
- **PKI** (cross-ref: the PKI-not-secrets brief): only the right party reads the right part, gated by keys, so the collaboration is secure even across parties that do not fully trust each other, which is the defining condition of supply-chain security.

So the vault turns SecurityScorecard's "data with no collaboration tooling" into "selective, secure, PKI-gated collaboration," which is exactly the friction it says it wants to remove.

## Evidence

The project lead also flags the evidence angle: **the evidence machinery fits SCDR's need to track findings and remediation with proof.** SCDR is about moving from static assessment to tracked remediation, and the vault holds evidence with provenance and version control (cross-ref: the v0.31.5 evidence-discovery and GDPR-assessment briefs, and the v0.31.9 blast-radius brief's evidence handling). A finding, its supporting evidence, the remediation steps, and the sign-offs can live in a vault, shared selectively with the parties involved, with the version history as the audit trail of who did what and when. This gives SCDR the evidenced, trackable, collaborative remediation record it is reaching for.

## Open Source And Productivity

Two framing points the project lead makes for the collaboration:

- **Open source means they can use it.** The vault is open source (cross-ref: the v0.27.60 open-evaluation brief), so SecurityScorecard and its partners can adopt and run it without lock-in, which lowers the barrier to collaboration and fits a partner-and-channel go-to-market.
- **Make them more productive and effective.** The aim is not to compete with SecurityScorecard but to make them (and their partners and customers) more productive and effective at the collaboration and remediation that SCDR is about, the vault as the value-adding collaboration layer on top of their detection and ratings.

## The Industry, Not Just One Company

The project lead suggests mapping the industry, not only SecurityScorecard: **find a couple of companies in the security-ratings / supply-chain-risk industry and ask how the vault adds value across the category.** SecurityScorecard is the lead example (and the meeting), but the same collaboration-layer value applies to others in third-party-risk and security-ratings (the category includes several players). The mapping is therefore reusable: any company whose product identifies supply-chain or vendor risk needs a way for the affected parties to collaborate on remediation securely and selectively, and the vault is that layer. This makes the collaboration a category play, not a single-partner one.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a competitor to SecurityScorecard.** The vault is the collaboration layer on top of their detection and ratings.
- **Not data-dumping.** Selective, PKI-gated sharing, the opposite of mountains of data without context.
- **Not a single-partner play only.** The collaboration-layer value applies across the security-ratings / supply-chain-risk category.
- **Not a finalised partnership.** A research mapping and proposal ahead of the meeting; terms to be agreed.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: SecurityScorecard may see overlap, not complementarity.** They are expanding into collaboration tooling themselves. Mitigation: position the vault as the layer they can adopt (open source, no lock-in) to do collaboration better, not a competitor; lead with their named gap.

**Risk 2: The messaging capabilities are still being added.** Some of the value rests on the about-to-be-added messaging. Mitigation: be clear about what exists versus what is coming (cross-ref: the vault-to-vault-comms and three-agent-demo briefs, which are at the build/demo stage); show the demo path.

**Risk 3: Security data is highly sensitive.** Supply-chain findings are sensitive. Mitigation: zero-knowledge and PKI (the company is not the vulnerability; cross-ref: the vaults-for-the-blast-radius-company brief); selective sharing; the vault is built for exactly this.

**Risk 4: Company facts may have moved.** SecurityScorecard's offering evolves. Mitigation: confirm current details before the meeting; this brief reflects public material as researched.
