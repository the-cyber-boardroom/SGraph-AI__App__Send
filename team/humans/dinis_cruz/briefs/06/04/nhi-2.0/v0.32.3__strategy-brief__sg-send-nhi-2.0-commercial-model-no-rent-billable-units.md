## What This Is

It also builds on the investment-strategy thesis (cross-ref: the v0.31.9 investment-strategy brief's consumption and value-tied-to-users). New contributions: **the no-rent model, billable units as the central pricing primitive, the two kinds of markup (on models and on other platforms), customised versions as a licence of open-source plus a guarantee plus a reduction of software, value tied to users and adoption, the multilingual worldwide market, and the laser-focus-start-simple discipline.** The project lead flags a separate memo on selling by specific solutions, workflows, and deliverables; this brief touches it but does not develop it.

## The No-Rent Model

The first principle: **because it is open source, you cannot charge licence rent or lock the customer in, so you play sovereignty from the start and charge for value instead.**

The project lead: **"one of the key principles is the no-rent model. You have open source, so you cannot do licences, you cannot lock the customer in, and that is amazing, because it means we play the sovereignty card right from the beginning. What you can do is charge for value."** So the model deliberately forgoes the rent-seeking that proprietary software relies on (per-seat licences, lock-in) and instead leans into sovereignty as a selling point (the customer owns and controls it; cross-ref: the blast-radius and open-source-not-free briefs) and charges for value delivered. No rent does not mean no revenue; it means revenue comes from value (the billable units, markups, and customised versions below), not from renting access to code the customer could otherwise run.

## Billable Units: The Central Pricing Primitive

The core mechanic, and the heart of the model: **there should be lots and lots of billable units, small things that can be glued together into a solution, paid for on demand as the customer uses the platform.**

The project lead: **"one of the most important things is that we should have lots and lots and lots of billable units. Billable units are literally small things we can do that can be glued together to create a solution, and they are fundamentally how everything fits together. These billable units are what customers pay for when they use the platform, and they should be paying on demand, so as they use, they pay for it."**

| Property | Detail |
|----------|--------|
| **Many and small** | Lots of billable units, each a small thing the platform can do |
| **Gluable** | They combine to create a solution |
| **The connective tissue** | They are how everything fits together |
| **On demand** | The customer pays as they use, consumption-based |

So pricing is granular and consumption-based: the platform is composed of many small billable units (an action, a visualisation, a mapping, a customisation step), and a solution is units glued together. The customer pays on demand for what they use, which aligns price with value and fits the platform-of-primitives architecture (cross-ref: the platform-of-primitives brief): the same primitives that build solutions are the billable units. This is the no-rent model made concrete: revenue accrues from usage of valuable units, not from a licence.

## Two Kinds Of Markup

A revenue mechanic the project lead highlights: **you can mark up on the models themselves, and on other platforms, making it easy for the customer to buy a third-party product and taking a cut.**

The project lead: **"there are interesting markups you can do. You can mark up on top of the models themselves, but you can also mark up on top of other platforms. If somebody says, I need an identity solution for this, we say, we integrate with this one and that one, and we make it easy for the company to buy product X, and we take a cut, we mark it up, whatever they charge, we charge a bit more on top."**

| Markup | How It Works |
|--------|--------------|
| **On the models** | A margin on the LLM usage the platform brokers (cross-ref: the OpenRouter-broker brief) |
| **On other platforms** | Integrate a needed third-party product, make it easy to buy, take a cut on top |

So there are two markup streams: the model-access margin (brokering LLM usage; cross-ref: the OpenRouter-broker and investment-strategy briefs) and a platform markup, where the startup integrates the third-party products a customer needs (for example an identity solution the customer must buy), makes them easy to adopt, and takes a margin on top. The second is an aggregator move: be the easy path to buy what the customer needs, and earn the cut for making it easy and integrated.

## Selling By Solutions, Workflows, And Deliverables

A powerful stream the project lead flags for a separate memo, while noting it here: **selling by specific solutions, workflows, and deliverables that add value, the blast-radius mapping, the risk understanding, the action understanding, which are all billable.**

The project lead: **"a very powerful workflow, which I will come back to in a separate memo, is when you sell by specific solutions, specific workflows, specific deliverables that add value to the user. That is why mapping the blast radius, understanding the risks, understanding the actions, all those things are valuable, and they are billable."** So beyond per-unit usage, the startup sells whole valuable deliverables (a blast-radius map, a risk assessment, an action analysis; cross-ref: the visualisation and risk-management briefs), each a billable outcome. This brief notes the stream; the deeper treatment (selling by deliverables) is the flagged separate memo. The point here: the valuable outputs of the earlier phases are themselves billable products, not just steps.

## Customised Versions: The Big Commercial Opportunity

The opportunity the project lead calls the big one, and it reframes licensing: **providing customised versions of the platform is, in effect, selling a licence of open-source technology plus a guarantee that it works plus a reduction of software.**

The project lead: **"the big commercial opportunity is when you provide customised versions of the platform, or whatever tech stack we are using, to the customer. In a way you are selling a licence, but a licence of open-source technology, and a licence of the guarantee that the software is going to work, and also a reduction of software. You are reducing the amount of code, the amount of agents, the amount of things that happen in there, because we customise it to their needs, visualise it to their needs."**

| What The Customer Buys | What It Means |
|------------------------|---------------|
| **A licence of open-source technology** | They can run it themselves (no lock-in), but they buy the supported, customised version |
| **A guarantee that it works** | The creator stands behind it; it is supported |
| **A reduction of software** | Less code, fewer agents, less surface, customised to their needs |

So the customised version is the flagship product, and it reframes what a licence is: not rent for access to code, but a guarantee (the creator stands behind a version that works) and, crucially, a reduction (the customer gets less software, fewer agents, less attack surface, tailored to exactly their needs; cross-ref: the open-source-not-free brief's 10% subset version and the visualisation brief's customised mini-apps). The value is not more software but the right, smaller, guaranteed software. This is the no-rent model's premium product: you sell the guarantee and the reduction, on top of technology the customer is free to own.

## Value Tied To Users And Adoption

The valuation thesis: **the more users, the more value; the company's value should be connected to users and adoption.**

The project lead: **"the more users we have, the more value of the company, and the value of the company should be connected to the amount of users and the amount of adoption that exists."** So the north-star is users and adoption, not only revenue (cross-ref: the investment-strategy brief's reachable-users test and value-tied-to-users). The no-rent, billable-unit, sovereignty model is designed to maximise adoption (low friction, no lock-in, run-it-yourself), and adoption is what the company's value tracks. This aligns the commercial model with growth: make it easy and sovereign to adopt, grow users, and value follows.

## The Multilingual Worldwide Market

A reach point the project lead emphasises: **identity is a global problem, LLMs open the multilingual worldwide market fast, and the further from the UK and US, the worse the problem and the bigger the opportunity.**

The project lead: **"we can have a multilingual worldwide market quite fast, because especially with LLMs, the identity solution is not limited to England, America, or English-speaking markets. I would argue that the further you go from the UK and America, the worse it gets."** So the market is not English-speaking only: identity and non-human-identity problems are universal, LLMs make multilingual delivery fast (cross-ref: the skill-as-projection brief's multilingual projections and the finance-team brief), and the problem is often worse further from the UK and US, which is more opportunity, not less. The commercial model is global from early, which feeds the users-and-adoption valuation thesis.

## Valuation, Exits, And Laser Focus

The framing on ambition, and the discipline: **for certain valuations you do not need crazy revenues, so the question is how much you want and what exit, but the discipline is laser focus on delivering simple value first.**

The project lead: **"for certain types of valuations you do not need crazy amounts of revenue to be valued at a certain amount, so the question becomes how much do you want, what kind of exits do you want. But it is very important to have a laser-sharp focus on delivering value to customers, solving the identity problem they have. In the future, after the first couple of phases and some more custom primitives, there are interesting opportunities and adjacent businesses to get into, but in the beginning let us focus on delivering simple value and a simple service."**

So two things: ambition is a choice (the model can reach meaningful valuations without crazy revenue, given users and adoption; cross-ref: the investment-strategy brief), and the discipline is to start simple, deliver value, solve the customer's actual identity problem, and only later (after the first phases and more custom primitives) expand into adjacent businesses. Laser focus on simple value now; adjacencies later.

## What This Asks For

1. **Run the no-rent model** (no licence rent, no lock-in; sovereignty from the start; charge for value).
2. **Build lots of billable units** (small, gluable, consumption-based; how solutions are composed and paid for on demand).
3. **Use both markups** (on the models; on other platforms, make it easy to buy and take a cut).
4. **Sell valuable deliverables** (blast-radius maps, risk and action analyses; the flagged separate memo deepens this).
5. **Make customised versions the flagship** (a licence of open-source plus a guarantee plus a reduction of software).
6. **Tie value to users and adoption** (maximise low-friction, sovereign adoption; value follows).
7. **Go global early** (multilingual via LLMs; the problem is worse further from the UK and US).
8. **Choose the ambition, keep the focus** (valuations without crazy revenue; start simple; adjacencies later).
9. **Instrument the billable units** (metering and on-demand billing as core infrastructure).

Estimated effort: this is the commercial-model design on top of the existing open stance, broker, and customisation machinery (cross-ref: the open-source-not-free, OpenRouter-broker, and visualisation briefs); the contribution is the no-rent, billable-unit, markup, and customised-version model. The proof is a customer paying on demand for billable units, a marked-up integration, and a guaranteed, reduced, customised version, with value tracked by users and adoption.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a licence-rent or lock-in model.** No rent; sovereignty; charge for value.
- **Not coarse pricing.** Many small, gluable, consumption-based billable units.
- **Not more software.** Customised versions are a reduction; less code, fewer agents, less surface.
- **Not revenue-only valuation.** Value tied to users and adoption.
- **Not the sell-by-deliverables memo.** That is flagged separately; only touched here.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: No-rent revenue must still add up.** Forgoing licence rent needs the billable units and customised versions to generate enough. Mitigation: many billable units plus markups plus the flagship customised version; the value-margin logic (cross-ref: the open-source-not-free brief).

**Risk 2: Billable-unit metering is real infrastructure.** Consumption billing at granularity is non-trivial. Mitigation: build metering as core (cross-ref: the OpenRouter-broker brief); start with a few high-value units.

**Risk 3: Platform markup depends on integrations and partners.** Taking a cut needs real integrations and agreements. Mitigation: integrate the products customers actually need; partner agreements (cross-ref: the SecurityScorecard and Tavon briefs); be the easy path to buy.

**Risk 4: Global and multilingual adds complexity.** Many markets and languages. Mitigation: LLMs make multilingual delivery feasible (cross-ref: the skill-as-projection brief); start where the value is clearest; expand.

**Risk 5: Focus can drift toward adjacencies too early.** The temptation to expand. Mitigation: laser focus on simple value first; adjacencies only after the first phases and custom primitives.
