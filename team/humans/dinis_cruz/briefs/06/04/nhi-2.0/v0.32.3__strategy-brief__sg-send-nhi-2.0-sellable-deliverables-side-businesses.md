## Side Businesses As Side Effects Of The Core Primitives

The framing the project lead challenges companies with: **there are many side businesses and much visibility a company should have, not because it set out to, but as a side effect of doing its core primitives right.**

The project lead: **"I always challenge companies operating on this kind of cybersecurity solution: there are a lot of side businesses, a lot of visibility, they should have, not because they want to, but as a side effect of what they need to do for their business. The more SG vault becomes better, the more it becomes an application hosting environment, but that is a side effect. The more it becomes a secret manager, the more it becomes all these other tools, which is not the primary objective, just the thing that happens when you do your core primitives right."**

| Core Primitive Done Right | The Side-Effect Business |
|---------------------------|--------------------------|
| The vault gets better | It becomes an application hosting environment |
| Keys and secrets are managed | It becomes a secret manager |
| Identity is mapped and governed | The visibility deliverables below |

So the side businesses are not distractions to chase; they fall out of doing the core well (cross-ref: the blast-radius brief's side-effect taxonomy and the platform-of-primitives brief). The vault, by being a good vault, becomes app-hosting and a secret manager as side effects, and the identity work, by being done properly, produces a catalogue of sellable visibility. The discipline is to do the core primitives right; the side businesses then present themselves.

## Visibility First, Then The Side Effects

The precondition (cross-ref: the visualisation and risk-management briefs): **before you touch any identity or change anything running, you need visibility to model the side effects, or you are doing hope-driven development; and once you have that visibility, you can see what it lets you sell.**

The project lead: **"before you touch one identity, before you make any change to prod or anything running, you need visibility, because you need to model it and understand the side effects, or else you are doing hope-driven development, hope change control, which is very dangerous. The interesting thing is you can now think: what can you do as a side effect of having that visibility?"** So visibility comes first (the start-with-visualisation thesis; cross-ref: the visualisation brief), both because change without it is hope-driven and dangerous (cross-ref: the living-off-the-land brief's hope-driven development), and because the visibility itself is what generates the sellable deliverables below. The question becomes: given the visibility, what can you sell?

## The Catalogue Of Sellable Deliverables

The meat of the memo, the concrete things a company will pay for, all side effects of the identity visibility:

| Deliverable | What It Is |
|-------------|-----------|
| **Org charts** | The real organisation, mapped from the identities (cross-ref: the visualisation brief) |
| **Identity mappings** | What identities exist and how they relate |
| **Licence management mappings** | Which licences exist, who is using them, who has access |
| **Identity health scores** | How many accounts have passwords, how many are expiring, how many have MFA, how many human identities are being used by agents |
| **Identity access management (IAM) graphs** | The graph of an organisation's identities, their permissions, and what each can do |
| **Cost mappings** | Licence cost, cloud cost, and local cost tied to identities |

The project lead on the IAM graph: **"giving somebody the graph of their identities and their permissions, and what each identity can do, is gold. People will pay for that because they do not have it, and once they know they can have it, they will demand it."** So the IAM graph in particular is a high-value deliverable: organisations do not have a clear picture of who and what can do what, and once shown it is possible, they demand it. The health-score deliverable is similarly concrete and immediately valuable: counts of password-only accounts, expiring credentials, missing MFA, and (the NHI 2.0 point) human identities being used by agents, exactly the findings the visualisation surfaces (cross-ref: the visualisation brief's low-touch findings). Each of these is a billable deliverable (cross-ref: the commercial-model brief's billable units and sell-by-deliverables).

## The Chain: Identities To Assets To Risk To Cost

The unifying thread that makes all of these sellable: **identities are connected to assets, which are connected to risk, which are also connected to cost.**

The project lead: **"identities are connected to assets, which are connected to risk, which also are connected to cost."** So the identity graph is not only a security artefact; following it reaches assets (what the identity touches), risk (what could go wrong; cross-ref: the risk-management brief), and cost (what it all costs; licences, cloud, local). This chain is why one body of work (mapping identities) yields deliverables across security, governance, and finance: the same graph, followed to different endpoints, produces the org chart, the IAM graph, the risk map, and the cost map. It is the visualisation brief's identities-connect-to-assets-and-business-function (cross-ref: the visualisation brief) extended through risk to cost.

## Finance Data Plus Identities: Guaranteed Waste

A specific, high-value play the project lead is emphatic about: **connect the organisation's financial data (its source of truth) to its identities, and you will find waste and over-provisioning.**

The project lead: **"even something as simple as going to the finance team, consuming or mapping the finance data, which is one of the best in an organisation because financial data is the source of truth: if they are paying for something, they are the ones paying, and somebody is chasing if it is not paid. Connecting financial data with identities, I guarantee you will find waste, over-provisioning, under-provisioning, all sorts of challenges."** So finance data is the source of truth (cross-ref: the finance-team-skills brief), because payment is real and tracked, and joining it to the identity graph (the identities-to-cost end of the chain) reliably surfaces waste: things paid for but unused, over-provisioned, or under-provisioned. This is a concrete, almost guaranteed value finding, and a strong wedge: it speaks to finance and security at once, and it is hard to argue with money.

## Creating Markets, And Consulting Companies As Power Users

Two go-to-market points:

- **You create markets, or go where the need already exists.** The project lead: **"you are creating markets here, or going to places where people already need this. People will pay because they do not have it, and once they know they can have it, they will demand it."** So some deliverables meet existing demand (IAM, licence management), and some create new demand once shown to be possible.
- **Consulting companies are power users and a channel.** The project lead: **"there is a play to work a lot with consulting companies, which are not software houses, not in the business of developing software, who will be power users of this."** So non-software-house consultancies (who advise but do not build software) are natural power users and a channel: they use the platform to deliver these visibility deliverables to their clients (cross-ref: the consulting-firms-to-product brief, here the consultancy using the platform rather than productising its own knowledge, and the Tavon channel brief).

## The Side-Business Areas, And The Game Of Identity

The project lead lists the areas and frames them as definitional: **visualisation, risk management, performance, disaster recovery, and incident simulation or tabletop exercises are all side businesses, and if you cannot do these, you are not playing the game of identity.**

The project lead: **"the side businesses are visualisation, risk management, performance, disaster recovery, even incident simulation and tabletop exercises. My argument is, if you cannot do this, you are not playing the game of identity. There should be lots of areas where you can help and sell to businesses."** So these are not optional add-ons; being able to deliver them is what it means to be in identity at all. The digital twin and the identity graph make visualisation, risk, performance, disaster recovery, and incident-simulation tabletops all achievable from the same core work (cross-ref: the visualisation and risk-management briefs), and each is a place to help and sell.

## Serverless, Low Marginal Cost, Profitable Fast

The economics that make the catalogue viable: **everything is serverless with very low marginal cost, so charging a little for any of these deliverables is profitable, and the company should be profitable fast.**

The project lead: **"a lot of these things use scale, because the key principle is that everything we do is serverless, with very low marginal cost. So as long as you can charge a little for one of these, you are profitable. The whole company should be profitable very fast, apart from development costs. In terms of execution it should be highly profitable, because the cost is minimal, and that is something SG/Send provides."** So the serverless, low-marginal-cost architecture (cross-ref: the blast-radius brief's serverless and the commercial-model brief) means each billable deliverable carries almost no incremental cost, so even small charges are profitable, and the company can be highly profitable on execution very quickly (development cost aside). The catalogue of deliverables plus near-zero marginal cost is the path to fast profitability.

## What This Asks For

1. **Treat side businesses as side effects** (do the core primitives right; the vault becomes app-hosting and a secret manager, and the visibility deliverables present themselves).
2. **Lead with visibility** (before any change; both because change without it is hope-driven, and because it generates the deliverables).
3. **Build and sell the catalogue** (org charts, identity mappings, licence mappings, identity health scores, IAM graphs, cost mappings).
4. **Follow the chain** (identities to assets to risk to cost; one graph, many deliverables).
5. **Run the finance-plus-identities play** (connect the financial source of truth to identities; find waste and over-provisioning).
6. **Create markets and meet existing demand** (some deliverables are demanded once shown possible; some meet existing need).
7. **Partner with consulting companies** (non-software-house consultancies as power users and a channel).
8. **Cover the side-business areas** (visualisation, risk, performance, disaster recovery, incident-simulation tabletops; the game of identity).
9. **Exploit the serverless economics** (low marginal cost; charge a little per deliverable; profitable fast).

Estimated effort: low-to-moderate per deliverable, because each is a side effect of the core identity work and the serverless architecture keeps marginal cost near zero; the contribution is recognising and packaging the catalogue. The proof is delivering several of these (an IAM graph, a health score, a finance-plus-identities waste report) profitably from one body of identity-mapping work.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a distraction from the core.** The side businesses are side effects of doing the core primitives right.
- **Not change without visibility.** Visibility first; no hope-driven change.
- **Not a single product.** A catalogue of deliverables from one identity graph.
- **Not high marginal cost.** Serverless; charging a little is profitable.
- **Not optional.** If you cannot do these, you are not playing the game of identity.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: The catalogue could dilute focus.** Many deliverables risk spreading thin. Mitigation: they are side effects of one core body of work, not separate builds; lead with the highest-value (IAM graph, finance-plus-identities); the laser-focus discipline (cross-ref: the commercial-model brief).

**Risk 2: The deliverables touch sensitive data.** Identity, licence, finance, and cost data are sensitive. Mitigation: the vault's secure, access-controlled handling (cross-ref: the visualisation and foundations briefs); the company is not the vulnerability (cross-ref: the vaults-for-the-blast-radius brief).

**Risk 3: Findings must be accurate and defensible.** Claiming waste or risk is sensitive. Mitigation: ground in the data (finance is the source of truth); conservative, evidenced findings (cross-ref: the evidence briefs).

**Risk 4: Consulting-channel quality must be maintained.** Partners delivering the deliverables affect the brand. Mitigation: the platform standardises the deliverables; the trust moat depends on quality (cross-ref: the moat brief); enable, support, and quality-gate partners.
