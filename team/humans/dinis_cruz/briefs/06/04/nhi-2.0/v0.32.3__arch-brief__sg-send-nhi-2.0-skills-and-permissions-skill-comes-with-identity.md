## The Intersection: A Skill Comes With Its Identity And Permission Set

The central principle, and the synthesis of the two series: **a skill must come with the identity and permission set needed to execute it.**

The project lead: **"the intersection here is identities, policies, and mappings with skills. When we talk about skills, a skill has to come with an identity, with the identities you need in order to execute that skill. For every skill we have, we create this model where the skill itself needs to come with an attached permission set, with the ability to understand what is actually happening, the capabilities and the resources needed to do it."**

| The Skill | The Attached Identity And Permission Set |
|-----------|-------------------------------------------|
| Describes what you want to do | Describes what is needed to do it |
| The how-to and the intent (cross-ref: the skills series) | The identities, capabilities, and resources required |
| Useless without the means | The means to actually execute |

So a skill is not just instructions; it carries the identity and permission set required to run it. This unites the two threads of the day: the skills series established what a skill is (a graph, a projection, an evolving package; cross-ref: the skills-as-graph and skill-as-projection briefs), and the NHI 2.0 series established granular identities and permission mappings (cross-ref: the permission-granularity and cloud-permissions briefs); here they meet, the skill comes bundled with the identity and permissions it needs, so it can actually be executed safely.

## Skills Capture How A Business Operates, But Need Identities And Permissions

Why this matters, reinforcing the skills thesis: **skills are how a business captures how it operates, which is gold, but that value only executes with the right identities and permissions.**

The project lead: **"one reason skills are going to be so big is that skills are a way for the business to capture how it operates, and that is gold, businesses and functions need that. But you need the identities, you need the permissions."** So the business-knowledge value of skills (cross-ref: the skills-as-graph brief's capturing-how-a-business-works) is only realised when the skill can run, and running requires the identity and permission set. A skill that captures how the business operates but cannot execute (because it lacks the identities and permissions) is inert; bundling them is what turns captured knowledge into executable capability. This is why the skills work and the identity work need each other.

## The Joiner-Mover-Leaver Use Case

A concrete, relatable operational example: **even the simple case of someone joining or changing roles, making sure they have the right accounts and privileges, deprovisioning the old and provisioning the new, and doing it temporally, is exactly this.**

The project lead: **"even something as simple as somebody just joined a company or got a new role: how do we make sure that person has the right accounts, the right privileges, the right resources? And if somebody changes role, we need to make sure the privileges and access they had in the previous role are removed, and the privileges required for the current role are added. And it is temporal, because the privileges on day one should be different from day ten, day a hundred, and even the other way around, day five hundred should be less. You want a situation where the fewer privileges you have, the better, and they all map together in this flow."**

| Event | What Must Happen |
|-------|------------------|
| **Joining** | The right accounts, privileges, and resources for the role |
| **Changing role** | Remove the previous role's access; provision the new role's privileges |
| **Over time** | Day 1 differs from day 10, day 100; day 500 should be less; trend toward fewer privileges |

So the everyday joiner-mover-leaver problem (right access on joining, clean handover on a role change, evolution over time) is precisely the skill-plus-identity-plus-permission model: the role's skill defines what the person does, and the attached identities and permissions define their access, deprovisioned and provisioned as the role changes, and evolving over time toward least privilege (cross-ref: the time and trust-and-identity briefs' earn-and-evolve-privilege-over-time). This is a familiar, high-value problem that the model solves directly.

## The Business Case: The Waste Of Under-Provisioning

Why companies will invest, and the flip side of over-provisioning: **the money, time, and inefficiency wasted when people do not have the resources to do their job is enormous, and that justifies the investment.**

The project lead: **"the reason companies would invest in this is that the amount of money, inefficiencies, and time wasted when a person does not have the resources they need to do their job is just crazy. It makes business sense to invest in this, which is why a lot of what I am talking about will be supported once we can actually do it."** So alongside the security case for least privilege (not too much access) there is a productivity case for sufficient privilege (not too little): people who lack the accounts, privileges, and resources to do their job waste enormous time and money. The skill-plus-identity-plus-permission model addresses both, the right access, neither too much (security) nor too little (productivity), which is a business case finance and operations feel directly (cross-ref: the sellable-deliverables brief's finance-plus-identities waste finding). This is why the work will be funded: it saves real money.

## The Synthesis: A Role's Needed Resources Are The Skill

The closing synthesis of the day: **mapping the business resources a role actually needs to operate is the skill, the skill describes what to do, and the identity and permissions describe what is needed, customised and mapped.**

The project lead: **"imagine being able to map, in a company, the business resources actually needed for a role to operate, which is the skill. The skill describes what you want to do; the identity and the privileges describe what is needed to do that, at that moment in time. Again, customised and mapped."**

| Component | What It Is |
|-----------|-----------|
| **The skill** | What you want to do; how the role operates (cross-ref: the skills series) |
| **The identity and privileges** | What is needed to do it, at that moment in time (cross-ref: the NHI 2.0 series) |
| **Customised and mapped** | Tailored to the role, the context, and the moment |

So the day closes on a unifying idea: a role is described by its skill (what it does, how it operates, the captured business knowledge) together with the identities and permissions it needs (what is required to do it, at that moment, evolving over time). Mapping the resources a role actually needs is the skill, and the skill carries the identity and permissions that make it executable. This is where the two series of the day converge: skills capture how the business works, identities and permissions make that executable safely, and customised-and-mapped is how each role gets exactly what it needs, no more and no less.

1. **Bundle the identity and permission set with the skill** (the skill describes what to do; the attached identity and permissions describe what is needed).
2. **Make captured business knowledge executable** (skills capture how the business operates; identities and permissions let it run).
3. **Solve the joiner-mover-leaver case** (right access on joining; deprovision old and provision new on role change; evolve over time toward least privilege).
4. **Make the under-provisioning business case** (the waste when people lack resources; the productivity case alongside the security case).
5. **Map a role as skill plus identity plus permissions** (what to do, plus what is needed, customised and mapped, at that moment in time).
6. **Connect to the time and trust models** (privileges evolve and are earned over time; cross-ref: the time and trust-and-identity briefs).
7. **Connect to the permission-mapping work** (the attached permission set draws on the per-application and per-API mappings; cross-ref: the permission-granularity and cloud-permissions briefs).
8. **Produce the skill-plus-permissions MVP** (one role mapped as a skill with its attached identities and permission set, provisioned and evolving).

Estimated effort: moderate, sitting on the skills and identity machinery built across the day; the contribution is the bundling principle and the role-as-skill-plus-permissions synthesis. The MVP is one role expressed as a skill with its attached identity and permission set, handling joining and a role change, with privileges evolving over time.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not skills without permissions.** A skill comes with the identity and permission set needed to execute it.
- **Not captured-but-inert knowledge.** Skills capture how the business works and are made executable.
- **Not only a security case.** The productivity case (under-provisioning waste) matters too.
- **Not static.** Privileges are temporal, evolving and trending toward least privilege.
- **Not one-size-fits-all.** Customised and mapped per role, context, and moment.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Bundling permissions with skills adds coupling.** Skills and permissions must stay in sync. Mitigation: the graph and version control keep them connected (cross-ref: the skills-as-graph and FAQ briefs); the permission set is derived from the mappings.

**Risk 2: Joiner-mover-leaver is sensitive and high-stakes.** Wrong provisioning breaks people's work or over-grants. Mitigation: the digital twin and simulation (cross-ref: the visualisation and risk-management briefs); deprovision-and-provision verified; evolve toward least privilege.

**Risk 3: The under-provisioning and over-provisioning balance is delicate.** Too little hurts productivity, too much hurts security. Mitigation: customised and mapped per role; observed usage over time (cross-ref: the time brief); the right access, neither too much nor too little.

**Risk 4: The permission set depends on the mapping work.** It needs the per-application and per-API mappings. Mitigation: those are the permission-granularity and cloud-permissions projects (cross-ref: those briefs); start with mapped applications; the middle layer where needed.
