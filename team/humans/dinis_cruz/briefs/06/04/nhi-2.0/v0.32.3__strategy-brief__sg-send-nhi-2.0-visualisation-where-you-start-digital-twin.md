## Visualisation Is Where You Start

The central claim: **for non-human identity, visualisation is not something you add in future; it is where you start, and likely the first revenue stream.**

The project lead: **"visualisation in this project of non-human identities is not a small problem, it is where you start. The first revenue streams should be all about visualising, understanding what is happening in the organisation, the identity flows, how much of a problem you already have, the current blast radius, and how much you are rolling the dice. There is a massive revenue model here, and the problem is most people do not start there. You cannot propose a solution unless you can visualise it; you cannot make change, you cannot compare before and after, and you cannot simulate."**

| Without Visualisation | With Visualisation |
|-----------------------|--------------------|
| You cannot see the identity flows or the blast radius | You see what is happening and how much you are rolling the dice |
| You cannot propose a solution credibly | You can show the problem, then the fix |
| You cannot compare before and after | You can demonstrate the change |
| You cannot simulate | You can run blast-radius scenarios |

So visualisation is the entry point, not a later addition. You cannot sell or deliver a solution to a problem nobody can see, and you cannot prove change without a before and after. The project lead's judgement is that visualising the identity problem (the flows, the blast radius, the dice-rolling) is itself a massive revenue model, and the reason it is open is that most people do not start there.

## The Digital Twin Built From Identities

The thing visualisation builds toward: **a digital twin of the organisation, built by following the identities, because identities connect to assets, capabilities, and the business function.**

The project lead: **"one of the things we need to build is a digital twin of the organisation. What is so interesting about identities is that they are always connected to assets, capabilities, and the business function. So by following the identities you are following the org chart, you are following who has access to what, and you should be able to create spectacular blast-radius simulations that make the business case to solve the identity problem."** So identities are the thread that, when followed, reconstructs the organisation: each identity links to the assets it can reach, the capabilities it has, and the business function it serves, so the identity graph is, in effect, the org chart and the access map (cross-ref: the blast-radius brief's identity blast radius and the SG/Sentinel compliance-as-living-graph). From that digital twin you can run blast-radius simulations that are vivid enough to make the business case, showing what an identity can reach, and what happens if it is compromised.

## Risk Is Contextual: Is It Really A Disaster?

A sharp and important insight: **the same privilege is high or low risk depending on the surrounding controls, so you must visualise and measure the actual impact, not assume it.**

The project lead: **"another thing that happens with a lot of solutions is they do not take risk into account. It is easy to say developers should not run with admin, and there will be a massive disaster if they are compromised. But is it a disaster? Are you sure? Even if a developer's machine gets compromised, if the company has version control and checks and balances so that injected code does not reach production, then it is much less risky. But if it has access to an email, or a database with no versioning, no integrity, no ability to recover from an incident, then that is way more dangerous."**

| The Same Privilege | Low Risk When | High Risk When |
|--------------------|---------------|----------------|
| Developer with broad access | Version control and checks stop injected code reaching production | Direct access to systems with no recovery |
| Access to a data store | The store has versioning, integrity, recoverability | The store has none of these |

So risk is not a property of the privilege alone; it is a property of the privilege in its context of controls. A scary-sounding access (a developer with admin) may be low risk if the surrounding controls (version control, review, integrity, recoverability) contain the consequences, while an innocuous-sounding access (to a database with no versioning or recovery) may be far more dangerous. This is why visualisation matters: you cannot judge real risk without seeing the identity, its reach, and the controls around what it can reach. It also connects to the positive-risk-score-of-change idea, you map which identities, if changed, actually reduce risk, so you address real risk rather than theatre (cross-ref: the blast-radius brief's side-effect taxonomy, where the real effect of a change is what matters). Knowing and measuring the actual impact is where you start.

## Where The Vault Shines: Customised Mini-Visualisations And Secure Ingest

The vault's role, and a strong fit: **the vault is where customised mini-apps and mini-visualisations are created and distributed, and a secure place to ingest the most sensitive identity data.**

The project lead: **"this is where the vaults really shine, because the vaults allow the creation and distribution of mini-apps and mini-visualisations that are 100% customised to the problem we are solving. The vault becomes a great place to securely ingest data, for example from Active Directory or a Google or identity store, and a nice place to make sure that data is only accessed by some people, because an identity solution deals with some of the most sensitive data in an organisation, so it has to be done securely."**

| The Vault Provides | For Visualisation |
|--------------------|-------------------|
| **Customised mini-apps and mini-visualisations** | 100% tailored to the specific identity problem (cross-ref: the mini-apps and webify briefs) |
| **Secure ingest** | Dump data from Active Directory, Google, or an identity store into the vault |
| **Access control** | The data, some of the most sensitive an organisation has, is seen only by those who should |

So visualisation is exactly the kind of customised, distributable mini-app the vault is built to host (cross-ref: the v0.31.9 webify-spreadsheets and mini-apps briefs), and because identity data is some of the most sensitive in an organisation, the vault's secure, access-controlled, zero-knowledge handling is not incidental but essential. The project lead notes that modern LLMs are very good at creating custom apps and visualisations, and the vault is where you run them, so the customised visualisation can be produced quickly and hosted securely.

## Low-Touch Go-To-Market: Just Give Us A Data Dump

The commercial mechanics, and an attractive one: **gaining visibility is low-touch, you only need some data, and a single dump (for example AWS CloudTrail) maps vast numbers of identities and surfaces vast numbers of problems.**

The project lead: **"the good news is it is very low-touch, you just need some data. If you get a dump from AWS CloudTrail, you can already map vast numbers of identities, and you will probably already find vast numbers of problems, security vulnerabilities, risks, things that should be rotated as soon as possible, even hard-coded credentials that have been used. You start to map and understand all that."** So the entry engagement is light: ingest an existing data source (a CloudTrail export, a directory dump), and from it the digital twin maps the identities and immediately surfaces findings, over-privileged and orphaned identities, things needing rotation, hard-coded credentials, risks. This is a low-friction wedge: companies will pay for visibility, and the cost to deliver the first value is just getting some data, leveraging what already exists (cross-ref: the living-off-the-land brief).

## What Comes Next: The Org Chart From The Identities

The project lead flags the next memo, while touching it here: **from the identities you can reconstruct the organisation, its real org chart, its true owners, and its orphans, often revealing that the recorded picture is wrong.**

The project lead: **"the key element I am going to cover next, also important for visualisation, is that we consume or recreate and tell the business how it is operating and what exists. You should be able to map the org chart of the company from the identities you see, and in most cases you realise the data they have about the org chart is wrong, or you find who really owns what, who has the power, who is actually responsible, and the orphans, applications with no owners, identities with no rotation."** This brief captures the visualisation-relevant part (the digital twin reveals the real organisation and its orphans); the deeper treatment of recreating-the-business-from-identities is the next memo and is noted here as the through-line. The point for visualisation: the digital twin does not just show risk, it often corrects the organisation's own understanding of who owns and controls what.

## What This Asks For

1. **Start with visualisation** (the first revenue stream; you cannot propose, change, compare, or simulate without it).
2. **Build the digital twin from identities** (follow identities to the org chart and the access map; run blast-radius simulations).
3. **Make risk contextual** (judge a privilege by its surrounding controls; measure actual impact; the positive-risk-score-of-change).
4. **Host customised visualisations in the vault** (100% tailored mini-apps; LLM-built; run securely).
5. **Ingest sensitive identity data securely** (Active Directory, Google, identity stores into the vault; access-controlled).
6. **Go to market low-touch** (just a data dump, for example CloudTrail; map vast identities; surface rotation, orphans, hard-coded credentials).
7. **Reveal the real organisation** (the digital twin corrects the org chart, finds true owners and orphans; the next memo deepens this).
8. **Leverage what exists** (use the organisation's data and tools where present; recreate where not).
9. **Produce the visualisation MVP** (ingest a CloudTrail or directory dump, map the identities, render the blast radius, surface findings).

Estimated effort: moderate and high-leverage; the vault hosts the mini-visualisations, the data ingest is light, and LLMs build the custom apps quickly; the contribution is making visualisation the entry point and first revenue stream. The MVP is a CloudTrail-or-directory ingest that maps the identities, renders the blast radius and digital twin, and surfaces the immediate findings.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a later add-on.** Visualisation is where you start and the first revenue stream.
- **Not context-free risk scoring.** Risk is judged in the context of the surrounding controls.
- **Not insecure handling of identity data.** The most sensitive data; the vault handles it securely and access-controlled.
- **Not high-touch.** A data dump is enough to deliver the first value.
- **Not the org-chart-from-identities memo.** That is next; only the visualisation-relevant part is here.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Identity data is extremely sensitive.** Ingesting directory and CloudTrail data concentrates sensitive information. Mitigation: the vault's zero-knowledge, access-controlled handling (cross-ref: the foundations and blast-radius briefs); the company is not the vulnerability (cross-ref: the vaults-for-the-blast-radius-company brief).

**Risk 2: Contextual risk is harder to compute than raw privilege.** Judging real impact needs knowledge of the surrounding controls. Mitigation: the digital twin captures the controls; start with the clear findings (rotation, orphans, hard-coded credentials) and deepen.

**Risk 3: A data dump may be incomplete or messy.** CloudTrail and directory exports vary. Mitigation: low-touch by design; surface what the data supports; iterate as more data is ingested.

**Risk 4: Visualisation must lead to action, not just dashboards.** Visibility alone is a start, not the whole. Mitigation: the visualisation makes the business case for the fixes the rest of the series delivers; before-and-after and simulation tie visibility to change.

**Risk 5: Claims of risk must be defensible.** Telling a company its risk picture is sensitive. Mitigation: ground in the data; contextual, measured impact; conservative, evidenced findings (cross-ref: the evidence briefs).
