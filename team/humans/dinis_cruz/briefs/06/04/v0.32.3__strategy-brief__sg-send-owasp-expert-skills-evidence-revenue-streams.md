## What This Is

The skills selling-and-customisation model applied to a specific archetype and its variation: **an OWASP leader or member (an open-source application-security expert) who wants to turn their knowledge into a revenue stream, and, as a variation, a tool developer who is best placed to provide the skill for their own tool.** It is a close sibling of the named-individual case in the Ivan Ristic brief (cross-ref: the v0.31.9 expert-brand-vaults-Ivan-Ristic brief) and the opinionated-expertise and ownership theses (cross-ref: the v0.31.7 skills-as-opinionated-expertise and v0.31.9 ownership briefs); this brief does not re-derive those. It adds what is specific here: **the open-source-community and contributor-revenue angle, evidence as business-value proof, the multi-audience buy-side deliverables, the expert-as-quality-gate operating model, and the tool-developer variation.**

This is back in the 4 June skills series, after the Snowflake comparison.

## The Archetype: An OWASP Expert With No Easy Revenue Stream

The subject: **an OWASP leader or member with a strong reputation in a set of application-security skills, who today has no easy revenue stream beyond employment or consulting.**

The project lead: **"say you are an OWASP leader or member with a good reputation for a certain set of application-security skills, threat modelling, code review, secure architecture, cryptography, an area where you have published materials and are a known expert. One of the challenges today is that you do not have good revenue streams easily, apart from working for companies or doing consulting. That is where skills come into play, because you should be selling those skills."** So the archetype is a recognised open-source security expert (the OWASP community is the example) whose knowledge is real and published but monetised only through their time (a job or consulting). Skills are the missing revenue stream: they should be selling their customised skills.

The open-source-as-credibility point applies as in the Ivan brief (cross-ref: the Ivan brief): ideally the skills are open source, because openness is what gives the expert credibility and distinguishes them from the several others claiming to offer the same thing, what you sell is the customisation, the adaptability, and your opinion, with the openness as the proof. This brief takes that as established and focuses on the new layers below.

## Evidence As Business-Value Proof

A genuinely emphasised new contribution: **the creator should provide evidence, referrals, and data points showing the business value of the skill, that it saves money, reduces risk, accelerates delivery, or improves productivity.**

The project lead: **"the creator should be able to provide evidence, referrals, and data points to show why their skill will save somebody money, make the code more productive, accelerate deliveries, align things, or reduce risk, especially in appsec. It can move into a much more focused business value proposition, where the business case is straightforward: you can spend the money reading the materials of this expert, versus you should hire the person."**

| Evidence Of Value | The Business Case It Supports |
|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| **Saves money** | The skill costs less than the alternative |
| **Reduces risk** | Especially in appsec, the core value |
| **Accelerates delivery** | Faster, with the expert's method |
| **Improves productivity / aligns things** | Better, more consistent output |
| **Referrals and data points** | Proof, not just claims |

So a skill should not be sold on reputation alone; it should carry evidence of its business value (referrals, data points), so the buyer has a straightforward, quantified business case. This is the evidence-and-provenance thesis (cross-ref: the v0.31.5 evidence-CV and evidence-discovery briefs) applied to selling a skill: the skill is backed by proof that it delivers value, which in appsec means a focused value proposition (risk reduced, money saved). It makes the buy decision a business decision, not a leap of faith.

## The Buy-Versus-Hire Business Case

The sharp framing of the value (sharpening the Ivan brief's current-painful-path): **buying the skill is an effective way of hiring the expert for a small, effective slice of time, because you start from how the expert thinks and apply it to your project.**

The project lead: **"this allows a very effective way of hiring somebody for a very small slice of time, because you are starting from the person's way of thinking. Here is how the person thinks, now how do you apply that to this particular project, this particular environment? That is fundamentally what we are doing: customising that."** So the business case is buy-the-skill-versus-hire-the-person, and the skill is, in effect, hiring the expert for the small slice of time that matters: you get their way of thinking, and the customisation applies it to your project and environment. This is the cheaper, faster alternative to months of the expert's time (cross-ref: the Ivan brief's current-painful-path and the consulting-firms-to-product brief), framed as a clean buy-versus-hire decision.

## The Multi-Audience Buy-Side Deliverables

A strong new contribution: **the skill should include the materials that make the business case to everyone involved in buying it, the executive briefing, the implementation plan, the action plan, and the next steps.**

The project lead: **"you should be able to deliver messages and materials that make the business case to everybody who needs to be involved, including the purchasing side. You can have an executive briefing on why you should hire this person or buy this skill, the implementation plan, the action plan, the next steps. These become powerful customisation elements and deliverables. With experience, you know who needs to be involved, the sequence of events, what needs to happen at each moment."**

| Deliverable | Audience / Purpose |
|-------------|--------------------|
| **Executive briefing** | The decision-makers: why buy this skill / hire this expert |
| **Implementation plan** | How it will be applied |
| **Action plan and next steps** | What happens, in what order |
| **Stakeholder and sequence map** | Who needs to be involved, and when |

So the skill is not just the technical knowledge; it includes the whole package needed to get it bought and applied, the executive briefing for the purchasers, the implementation and action plans, and the knowledge (which comes from experience) of who must be involved and in what sequence. This is itself expert knowledge: a seasoned professional knows the stakeholder map and the order of events, and encoding that into the skill's deliverables is part of the value. These deliverables are both customisation elements (tailored per buyer) and outputs the buyer receives.

## The Expert-As-Quality-Gate Operating Model

The operating model, and a sharp one: **the end result is a set of version-controlled artefacts, produced with agents but reviewed and quality-gated by the professional in a couple of hours, on their own schedule.**

The project lead: **"the end result can be 5, 10, 20 different artefacts, customised to the thing, version-controlled, managed and quality-gated by the professional. The professional is still doing this, but now they spend a couple of hours reviewing, making memos, talking to agents, and collaborating, on their time, on their schedule. They can be highly productive, you have a revenue stream, and you are leveraging the person's knowledge into a company. It is also a great way to train and learn."**

| Old (Consulting) | New (Expert-As-Quality-Gate) |
|------------------|-------------------------------|
| The expert does the work, on the clock | Agents produce the artefacts; the expert reviews and quality-gates |
| Months of the expert's time | A couple of hours of the expert's review, on their schedule |
| One project at a time | A set of artefacts (5, 10, 20), version-controlled |
| The expert is the bottleneck | The expert is the quality gate; productivity multiplies |

So the model inverts the consulting bottleneck: agents produce the customised artefacts (the executive briefing, the plans, the technical guidance), and the professional spends a couple of hours reviewing, annotating, talking to the agents, and quality-gating, on their own schedule. The professional is still doing the expert work (the judgement and the quality gate), but their knowledge now scales into many artefacts and many engagements, producing a revenue stream and leveraging their knowledge into companies. The project lead notes the side benefits: it is a great way to train and to learn, and it lets the expert be highly productive without being the bottleneck.

## The Variation: The Tool Developer

A second archetype the project lead raises: **the developer (or team) of a tool is best placed to provide the skill for using it, customised to a particular environment.**

The project lead: **"another variation is somebody who developed a tool. Who better to provide knowledge on how to use that tool? A skill to use the tool, but customised to a particular environment. The people and teams who develop tools could do this."** So a tool's creator is the authoritative source for the skill of using it, and that skill, customised to the buyer's environment, is a natural product (cross-ref: the v0.31.7 skill-comparison-CLI-wrapping brief, wrapping a tool in a skill). This extends the revenue-stream model from knowledge-experts to tool-builders: the same selling, customisation, evidence, and quality-gate model applies to the authoritative skill for a tool.

## The Mission: Revenue Streams For Open-Source Contributors

The why, and the through-line: **this is a way to create revenue streams for the actual contributors and creators of open source, who largely have none today.**

The project lead: **"it is very important to start to have revenue streams for the actual contributors and creators of open source, and this is a way to do that."** So the deeper purpose is to give open-source contributors (the OWASP members, the tool developers, the experts whose published work the industry relies on) a revenue stream based on their knowledge, which today they monetise only through employment or consulting. The skills model (selling customisation, maintenance, evidence, and the buy-side deliverables, with the expert as quality gate) is how their open contribution becomes a sustainable income, which strengthens the open-source ecosystem rather than closing it (cross-ref: the Ivan brief's open-source-as-credibility and the v0.27.60 open-evaluation brief).

## What This Asks For

1. **Target the OWASP / open-source-expert archetype** (and contributors generally) who lack revenue streams beyond employment and consulting.
2. **Sell customised, ideally open-source skills** (the customisation, adaptability, and opinion are the product; openness is the credibility; cross-ref: the Ivan and opinionated-expertise briefs).
3. **Attach evidence of business value** (referrals, data points; saves money, reduces risk, accelerates delivery; a quantified business case).
4. **Frame buy-versus-hire** (buying the skill is hiring the expert for the small, effective slice of time; their way of thinking, applied to the project).
5. **Include the buy-side deliverables** (executive briefing, implementation plan, action plan, next steps, stakeholder and sequence maps).
6. **Use the expert-as-quality-gate model** (agents produce 5 to 20 version-controlled artefacts; the professional reviews and quality-gates in a couple of hours, on their schedule).
7. **Support the tool-developer variation** (the tool's creator provides the authoritative, customisable usage skill).
8. **Serve the open-source-contributor-revenue mission** (a sustainable income from open contribution; strengthening, not closing, the ecosystem).
9. **Produce the library page and example** (the page explains the expert-revenue model; an example OWASP-expert skill with evidence, buy-side deliverables, and a quality-gate flow is the MVP).

Estimated effort: this is a use-case framing on documented machinery (base vaults, customisation, ownership, evidence, the marketplace); the contribution is the OWASP/contributor archetype, the evidence-as-business-value, the buy-side deliverables, the quality-gate operating model, and the tool-developer variation. The example is an OWASP expert's skill with evidence, an executive briefing and plans, and an agent-produced, expert-quality-gated artefact set.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a re-derivation of the Ivan brief.** That covered the named-individual expert, anonymisation, and open-source-as-credibility; this adds the community, the evidence, the deliverables, the quality-gate model, and the tool variation.
- **Not reputation-only selling.** Evidence and data points back the business case.
- **Not the expert doing all the work.** Agents produce; the expert reviews and quality-gates.
- **Not closing open source.** The aim is revenue for open contributors, strengthening the ecosystem.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Evidence of value can be hard to substantiate.** Claims that a skill saves money or reduces risk need real proof. Mitigation: referrals and data points (cross-ref: the evidence-CV brief); provenance; conservative, defensible claims.

**Risk 2: Quality-gating must be real, not rubber-stamping.** The expert's couple of hours must be genuine review. Mitigation: the professional owns the quality gate (cross-ref: the ownership brief); version control records their review; their reputation depends on it.

**Risk 3: The buy-side deliverables are themselves skilled work.** Executive briefings and stakeholder maps need expertise. Mitigation: that expertise is part of what is sold; agents draft, the expert refines; it is a customisation element.

**Risk 4: Appsec is high-stakes.** Security guidance errors matter. Mitigation: the expert quality-gates; evidence and provenance; open-source scrutiny; not a substitute for the buyer's own security responsibility.

**Risk 5: Tool-developer skills and licensing.** A tool's usage skill touches the tool's licensing. Mitigation: the creator is the authoritative source; customisation to the environment is the product; respect the tool's licence.
