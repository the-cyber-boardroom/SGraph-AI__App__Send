## What This Is

A comparison of SG/Vault with Snowflake data clean rooms, prompted by reviewing Snowflake's own explainer of the concept. The two overlap on the goal (privacy-preserving multi-party data collaboration) but sit at almost opposite ends architecturally, which makes them more contrasting-and-complementary than head-to-head competitors. This brief sets out what a data clean room is, where the two converge, where they diverge, a feature-by-feature table, and an honest assessment of where each wins, with a positioning framing for SG/Vault. The Snowflake material is paraphrased from its public description; the SG/Vault characterisation draws on the existing corpus.

## What A Snowflake Data Clean Room Is

In Snowflake's description, a data clean room is a secure, controlled environment that lets multiple companies, or divisions of one company, bring data together for joint analysis, with internal guidelines to keep handling aligned with privacy regulations such as GDPR, HIPAA, and CCPA, and where personally identifiable information can be anonymised. The dominant use case is linking anonymised marketing and advertising data from multiple parties for attribution, motivated in large part by advertisers and media responding to the loss of signals such as cookies and device IDs.

Mechanically, the clean room controls what data comes in, how it can be joined with other data, what analytics each party may run, and what data can leave. The distributed (cloud) variant removes the need to move data: each partner keeps control of their own data while governed analytics run across one or more partners at once, and participants make data visible only to authorised parties without necessarily moving it. Snowflake's positioning is that it provides the platform that companies configure so data can be securely accessed and analysed by selected partners while preserving privacy, with named customers such as Merkle (Merkury Clean Rooms) and Roku.

## Where The Two Converge

Both are fundamentally about privacy-preserving collaboration among parties that do not want to hand over raw data:

| Convergence | Both Do This |
|-------------|--------------|
| **Privacy-preserving multi-party collaboration** | Multiple parties collaborate on data without exposing the raw underlying data |
| **Regulatory alignment and anonymisation** | Both target GDPR/HIPAA/CCPA-style compliance and lean on anonymisation (cross-ref: the v0.31.9 expert-brand-vaults-Ivan-Ristic brief's anonymisation path) |
| **Data stays where it is** | Snowflake's distributed clean room and the vault's connector/external-vault model both avoid centralising and moving data (cross-ref: the v0.31.5 connector briefs) |
| **Controlled sharing among low-trust parties** | Parties that do not fully trust each other collaborate under controlled rules (cross-ref: the v0.31.12 three-agent-demo brief's messaging-beats-access) |

So at the level of intent, they are solving a similar problem: let parties collaborate on sensitive data under controls, without raw-data hand-over.

## Where The Two Diverge

This is where they are near-opposite, and where the substance of the comparison lies.

**The trust model.** A Snowflake clean room is a trusted central platform that both parties bring data into; the operator is inside the trust boundary, granting data owners control while approved partners receive anonymised feeds. SG/Vault is zero-knowledge: the operator cannot read the data at all. Snowflake protects data within a platform that can technically see it (governed by configuration and policy); the vault protects it cryptographically, so there is nothing for the host to see. This is the deepest divide: governance-based privacy versus cryptographic privacy.

**The architecture.** Snowflake is a backend, a managed cloud data platform (compute, storage, SQL), and a clean room is a configured environment running queries on that platform. SG/Vault is the no-backend, client-side, file-as-substrate model (cross-ref: the v0.31.12 vault-as-platform-of-primitives brief): the vault is an encrypted file that travels, with no server-side logic able to read it. Snowflake sends queries to the data on a platform; the vault is the data, encrypted, moving to where it is used.

**What is collaborated on.** Snowflake clean rooms are overwhelmingly about structured tabular data and analytical joins; the canonical task is joining first-party datasets to compute an audience or attribution without exposing rows. SG/Vault carries arbitrary content: documents, graphs, evidence, tools, mini-apps, skills, and messages, and the collaboration is workflow and agentic, not only analytical joins (cross-ref: the v0.31.12 FAQ and three-agent-demo briefs). A clean room answers "what does the joined data tell us"; a vault carries "here is the work, its provenance, and who can do what with it."

**The unit of control.** Snowflake controls through configuration and SQL grants on a platform (what enters, what joins, what leaves). The vault controls through PKI and keys (cross-ref: the v0.31.12 PKI-not-secrets and vault-to-vault-comms briefs): per-hop key-gated reads, append-only tokens, encrypt-on-write. Snowflake's "only authorised parties see the listing" is a permissions feature; the vault's equivalent is "only the holder of the private key can decrypt," which holds even against the host.

**Lock-in versus sovereignty.** Snowflake is a proprietary platform, and collaboration is easiest when both parties are on it (its own Merkle example notes partners adopting Snowflake opening new collaboration). SG/Vault's open-source, no-lock-in, runs-inside-the-company stance (cross-ref: the v0.31.9 blast-radius and v0.27.60 open-evaluation briefs) is the deliberate opposite: sovereignty over platform gravity.

## Feature-By-Feature Comparison

| Dimension | Snowflake Data Clean Room | SG/Vault |
|-----------|---------------------------|----------|
| **Primary goal** | Privacy-safe multi-party data collaboration | Privacy-safe multi-party collaboration on content and workflows |
| **Dominant use case** | Adtech: audience, attribution, joins on tabular data | Broad: documents, evidence, skills, agentic workflows, messaging |
| **Privacy mechanism** | Governance and configuration; anonymisation; PII secured on a platform that can see it | Zero-knowledge encryption; the host cannot read the data |
| **Architecture** | Managed cloud backend (compute, storage, SQL) | No backend for the vault; client-side; the vault is an encrypted file |
| **Where data lives** | On the platform (distributed: each partner's own Snowflake) | In the vault (an encrypted, portable artifact); connectors reach data in place |
| **What is shared** | Structured/tabular data; analytical results | Arbitrary content: docs, graphs, tools, apps, skills, messages |
| **Unit of collaboration** | Governed query / join / analytics | Messages, shared/sub-vaults, workflows, agent handoffs |
| **Access control** | Configuration and SQL grants; listings to authorised parties | PKI and keys; per-hop key-gated reads; append-only tokens; encrypt-on-write |
| **Who must be trusted** | The platform operator (inside the trust boundary) | No one, by construction (zero-knowledge) |
| **Provenance / audit** | Platform audit and governance | Version control as the audit trail (cross-ref: the commit-queue and FAQ briefs) |
| **Agent-native** | Not the focus | Yes: agent-to-agent messaging and coordination (cross-ref: the FAQ and three-agent-demo briefs) |
| **Lock-in** | Proprietary platform; easiest when both parties on Snowflake | Open-source; no lock-in; runs inside the company; sovereignty |
| **Maturity / scale** | Very mature; battle-tested at large scale; named customers | Emerging; the substrate and primitives are the play |

## The Honest Assessment

Snowflake data clean rooms are far more mature, battle-tested, and optimised for one job: large-scale, privacy-safe analytical joins, especially adtech and attribution, with real customers and heavy tooling. If the problem is two large enterprises computing an audience overlap on billions of rows without exposing PII, that is Snowflake's home turf, not the vault's, and it would be a mistake to claim otherwise.

SG/Vault is not really competing for that job. Its angle is different and broader: collaboration over arbitrary content and workflows rather than only tabular joins, with cryptographic rather than governance-based privacy, no backend and no lock-in, and agent-native messaging. The clean room is a destination you query; the vault is an envelope that travels. They converge on "privacy-preserving collaboration" and diverge on who has to be trusted, and "no one, by construction" is the vault's distinctive claim.

The sharpest positioning line: **a data clean room is a governed room inside a trusted platform; a vault is a sealed envelope that needs no trusted platform.** Where Snowflake's privacy rests on configuring and trusting a platform that can technically see the data, the vault's rests on the host being unable to read it at all. For the adtech analytical-join use case, the clean room is the stronger tool; for content, workflow, agentic, and sovereignty-sensitive collaboration, the vault occupies ground the clean room does not target.

There is also a complementarity worth noting: the two are not mutually exclusive. A vault could carry the governed, provenanced, agentic workflow around a collaboration while a platform like Snowflake does the heavy tabular join inside it, the vault as the envelope and audit trail, the clean room as the analytical engine called from within the workflow (cross-ref: the v0.31.9 blast-radius brief's live-off-the-land and the connector briefs).

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a claim that the vault replaces a data clean room.** For large-scale adtech analytical joins, the clean room is the stronger, more mature tool.
- **Not a strawman of Snowflake.** Its clean rooms are mature, proven, and well-suited to their use case.
- **Not a full Snowflake comparison.** Snowflake also has marketplace, native-apps, graph, and Cortex pieces; this compares the clean-room concept only.
- **Not the same privacy model.** Governance-based versus cryptographic privacy is the core distinction, not a marketing nuance.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Overclaiming against a mature incumbent.** Snowflake clean rooms are proven at scale. Mitigation: be precise that the vault targets different ground (content, workflow, agentic, sovereignty), not adtech joins; acknowledge Snowflake's strength on its use case.

**Risk 2: The comparison rests on one Snowflake page.** A full picture needs more of Snowflake's stack. Mitigation: this brief is scoped to the clean-room concept; a fuller competitive analysis would compare the broader platform.

**Risk 3: "No backend" must stay scoped.** The vault is backend-less, but workflows call out to backends (cross-ref: the platform-of-primitives brief's scoped claim). Mitigation: state it precisely; the contrast is the vault's own data needs no readable backend, not that no backend is ever involved.

**Risk 4: Maturity gap.** Snowflake is battle-tested; the vault is emerging. Mitigation: lead with the distinctive model (zero-knowledge, agent-native, sovereignty), not with scale claims; build the example vaults that demonstrate it.
