## What This Is

The skills selling-and-customisation model (cross-ref: the v0.31.7 skill-base-vaults and skills-as-opinionated-expertise briefs, and the v0.31.9 ownership-of-delegated-processes and finance-team briefs) seen from the point of view of a specific archetype: **a specialised, quality-focused development or web consulting firm, the kind of partner brought in to do work, that today sells time and materials and could instead sell skills.** The general principles (you-touch-it-you-own-it, customisation and maintainability as the product, skills as software artefacts, buying an expert's view) are documented; this brief grounds them in the consulting-firm case and adds what is specific to it: **why time-and-materials is structurally inefficient, and how skills let such a firm move from a service company to a product company.**

This is the first in a series of skills memos.

## The Archetype: A Quality Consulting Firm Selling Time And Materials

The subject is a familiar partner type: **a specialised, quality-focused consulting firm (development, web, from small to very large) whose people and consultants have deep experience, and who sell time and materials, that is, knowledge.**

The project lead: **"a specialised, quality-focused development or web consulting company, a typical partner you bring in to do development, from smaller companies to massive companies. They fundamentally have the same pattern: they have people and consultants with lots of experience in their world, and what they do is sell time and materials. They sell knowledge."** This is the standard professional-services model, and the project lead is clear it works, he has used such firms successfully, but it is structurally inefficient.

## Why Time-And-Materials Is Structurally Inefficient

The project lead set out, from experience, the inefficiencies built into the time-and-materials model, and naming them is what motivates the shift.

| Inefficiency | Why It Happens |
|--------------|----------------|
| **Expensive onboarding** | The only way it works is to bring consultants on board for days, weeks, months, sometimes years, which is costly and limits where you can use them |
| **The experts disengage** | The most expert consultants do not want to be on many projects (not interesting or challenging enough), so you end up with more junior people |
| **Effort goes to the wrong things** | A lot of effort goes into collecting information and maximising billable time, rather than into value |
| **The mythical man-month** | Throwing more resources at a project can slow it down; adding people creates an exponential communication problem, so people form small teams |

The project lead: **"it was always very inefficient, because the only way to make it work is to bring them on board for months, which is expensive and limits where you can do it. It creates a weird situation where the most expert consultants do not want to be on a lot of projects, because it is not interesting, so you end up with more junior people. There is a lot of effort in collecting information and maximising billable time, and you fall into the mythical man-month, where throwing more resources at a project can slow it down, because of the exponential communication problem."** So the model wastes the experts (who disengage), leans on juniors, spends effort on information-gathering and billing rather than value, and hits the communication ceiling of adding people. These are not failures of execution; they are structural to selling time.

## The Premise: Skills Make The Knowledge Scalable

The shift: **skills, mapping, facilitated communication, and agentic information-gathering make the firm's knowledge scalable, and make a junior consultant far more valuable.**

The project lead: **"with the advent of skills, the ability to map things together, to facilitate communication, and especially through agentic workflows to do a lot of the information gathering and customisation, a lot of this knowledge becomes a lot more scalable, and the value a junior consultant can provide is much bigger. We can optimise the workflows and get a much better value system for both the customer and the service provider: the customer gets a lot more value for money, and the consultants can maximise their billing time and what they get involved in, and have a much better experience."**

So skills directly attack the inefficiencies above:

| The Inefficiency | What Skills Change |
|------------------|--------------------|
| Expensive onboarding | The skill carries the firm's knowledge; less onboarding to transfer it |
| Experts disengage | Experts encode their knowledge into skills once, rather than repeating it on many projects |
| Effort on info-gathering and billing | Agentic workflows do much of the information gathering quasi-automatically |
| Juniors are limited | A junior wielding the firm's skills delivers far more; the value gap narrows |
| The man-month communication problem | The skill, not a growing team, carries the knowledge, reducing the communication explosion |

The result is a better value system for both sides: the customer gets more value for money, and the consultants maximise their billable time, choose more interesting work, and have a better experience. The expert's knowledge scales without the expert being on every project.

## What You Are Buying: The Firm's Methodology And View

A point the brief makes precise (cross-ref: the opinionated-expertise brief): **what the customer buys is the firm's (or individual's) methodology, framework, and view, the years of experience encoded.**

The project lead: **"imagine buying a customised pack created by a company with a particular ideology and framework, and this is important, because every company has its methodology, its approach, and that is what you are buying. Including when you have individuals, you are buying that individual's view. That is what you are buying: the years and years of experience and knowledge that go into it."** So the skill is not generic capability; it is *this firm's* (or *this consultant's*) opinionated way of working, their methodology and framework, the thing that distinguishes them. This is the opinionated-expertise thesis (cross-ref: the opinionated-expertise brief) applied to a firm: the firm's brand and methodology are what is encoded and sold, and customising that methodology to a project is the value.

And, tying to the ownership model (cross-ref: the ownership and finance-team briefs): skills are treated like software artefacts, you-touch-it-you-own-it, somebody has to maintain them, so the firm sells not a static pack but a customised, maintained one.

## The Shift: From Service Company To Product Company

The strategic payoff, and the heart of the brief: **skills let a consulting firm move from being a service company to being a product (or SaaS) company.**

The project lead: **"fundamentally this is where these companies move from being a service to being a product company, or a SaaS company, which is very powerful, because now they can sell skills in marketplaces, they can scale, but more importantly, they can sell the customisation and the maintainability of skills, and that is the key piece of the puzzle. These consulting companies can then compete by maximising their knowledge, and more importantly, customising their knowledge to particular projects, and now we can do this at scale."**

| Service Company (Today) | Product Company (With Skills) |
|-------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Sells time and materials | Sells skills (products) |
| Revenue capped by consultant hours | Revenue scales beyond hours |
| One project at a time, per consultant | Skills sold many times; in marketplaces |
| Value is the consultant's presence | Value is the customised, maintained skill |
| Hard to reach small or emerging markets | Many smaller projects become viable |

So the firm's knowledge becomes a product: sold in marketplaces (cross-ref: the skills-marketplace brief), scaled beyond billable hours, with the real value (per the recurring thesis) in the **customisation and maintainability** of the skills (cross-ref: the ownership and base-vault briefs). The firm competes by maximising and customising its knowledge to particular projects, at scale.

This also opens markets the time-and-materials model could not serve. The project lead: **"when these companies want to provide services to emerging markets, or markets that are not good at paying for large projects, they can have a lot of smaller projects, which lets them scale a lot more, be a lot more engaged, and have more interesting revenue streams."** So smaller projects, and emerging or budget-constrained markets, become viable, because a customised skill is far cheaper to deliver than months of consultant time, the firm scales down-market and out, with new revenue streams.

## The Underestimated Win: Automating The Information Gathering

A specific point the project lead stresses: **do not underestimate the value of doing the information gathering, processing, and collecting quasi-automatically, because that is where much of the early project time goes.**

The project lead: **"do not underestimate the time and effectiveness of having a lot of information gathering, processing, and collecting done in a quasi-automatic way, which is what a lot of the time is spent on initially in these projects."** So a large share of a project's early cost is the consultant gathering and processing information, and agentic workflows (cross-ref: the GDPR-assessment and discovery briefs) can do much of this automatically. This is both an efficiency (less expensive consultant time on low-value gathering) and a value shift (the consultant's time moves to the high-value methodology and customisation). It is a concrete reason the junior-with-skills becomes far more valuable: the skill plus agentic gathering does the groundwork that used to consume the early weeks.

## What This Asks For

1. **Target the consulting-firm archetype** (specialised, quality-focused dev/web firms selling time and materials).
2. **Name the time-and-materials inefficiencies** (onboarding cost, expert disengagement, effort on gathering and billing, the man-month) as the problem skills solve.
3. **Make the firm's knowledge scalable via skills** (encode the methodology once; agentic workflows do the gathering; juniors deliver more).
4. **Sell the firm's methodology and view** (the opinionated framework is the product; customisation is the value; cross-ref: the opinionated-expertise brief).
5. **Treat skills as maintained software artefacts** (you-touch-it-you-own-it; sell customisation and maintainability; cross-ref: the ownership brief).
6. **Enable the service-to-product shift** (skills in marketplaces; revenue beyond hours; the firm becomes a product/SaaS company).
7. **Open smaller and emerging markets** (many smaller projects; new revenue streams; scale down-market and out).
8. **Automate the information gathering** (quasi-automatic gathering and processing; the underestimated early-project win).
9. **Produce the library page and example** (the page explains the consulting-firm-to-product model; an example customised, maintained firm-methodology skill is the MVP).

Estimated effort: this is positioning and a use-case framing built on documented machinery (base vaults, customisation, ownership, the marketplace, agentic discovery); the contribution is the consulting-firm archetype, the time-and-materials critique, and the service-to-product shift. The example is a firm's methodology encoded as a customised, maintained skill, with agentic information-gathering.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a defence of time-and-materials.** It names the model's structural inefficiencies.
- **Not generic skills.** The firm's specific methodology and view are the product.
- **Not a static pack.** Customised and maintained (software artefacts; you-touch-it-you-own-it).
- **Not replacing consultants.** The experts encode and customise their knowledge; juniors deliver more; the experience improves.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Encoding a firm's methodology into skills is real work.** A firm's knowledge is hard to capture. Mitigation: the base-vault and knowledge-capture machinery (cross-ref: the base-vault and knowledge-capture briefs); agentic gathering; start with the firm's highest-value methodology.

**Risk 2: Firms may resist productising (it changes their model).** Service firms are built around billable hours. Mitigation: the value system improves for both sides (more value for money; better consultant experience and billing); the product model is additive, not a replacement, at first.

**Risk 3: Quality and maintenance of sold skills.** A firm's reputation rides on its skills. Mitigation: maintainability is the product (cross-ref: the ownership brief); provenance and the marketplace scoring (cross-ref: the marketplace brief); the firm owns and maintains its methodology skills.

**Risk 4: Smaller/emerging-market economics.** Many small projects must be cheap to deliver. Mitigation: the customised skill plus agentic gathering makes small projects viable; that is the point; watch the per-project delivery cost.
