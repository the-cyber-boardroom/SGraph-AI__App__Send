## What This Is

An alignment of the partner's four customer-facing artifacts, the agent trust runtime, the sales deck, the investor deck, and the marketing website, to the agent-mandate strategy, taking the project lead's comments and filling in the repeated corrections: **across all four, the headline should move from identity tells what exists and the solution proves why it acted, to identity tells what is possible and the solution proves why it was possible and who authorised it, because the evidence we hold is for the request, not the decision; the spine should become mandate, blast radius, and risk acceptance, with the trust graph as the place the blast radius is mapped and made to visibly explode when an agent can damage the whole business; the runtime-control and block-what-fails language should be repositioned to visibility and a way to start taking control in days, out of band, since we cannot block in line; the data claims should be honest, we know who approved and which credential was issued, not what data was used or what decision was made; the name should move toward Agent Mandate, not SGraph; and everything should distinguish what works without touching the customer's code from what needs them to integrate.** It is the customer-facing companion to the execution-memo redirection (cross-ref: the v0.33.32 execution-memo-review, how-not-why, why-is-hard, and observability briefs, the v0.33.40 blast-radius and risk-acceptance briefs, and the v0.33.30 naming brief). New contributions: **the cross-artifact corrections, the honest-data table, and the per-artifact re-pointing.**

## The Framing

What this is: **comments on the four artifacts, with the repeated themes filled in.**

The project lead recorded comments on each of the four artifacts the partner built. Several corrections recur across all of them, so this brief states those once as cross-cutting corrections, then applies the artifact-specific points.

## The Cross-Cutting Corrections

The themes that apply to all four:

### 1. Why It Worked And Who Authorised It, Not Why It Acted

The headline correction: **claim what we can prove.**

The project lead: **"identity tells you what exists, and I feel we are underselling it, it then tells you what is possible. The solution proves why it acted, but we do not have that evidence; it is almost like it proves why it worked. What evidence drove a decision, we do not have; we have what evidence drove the request for the decision."** So the headline moves from why it acted, the decision reasoning we cannot see, to why it was possible and who authorised it, which we can; and identity is upgraded from what exists to what is possible (cross-ref: the how-not-why and why-is-hard briefs).

### 2. Mandate, Blast Radius, And Risk Acceptance As The Spine

The missing spine: **the value is the risk you are buying.**

None of the four leads with mandate, blast radius, or risk acceptance. The project lead: **"this should be much more risk-driven. The trust graph is where we map the blast radius, so if something can do a lot of damage, it explodes, this thing can blow up the whole business, versus the blast radius is limited. And after the partner's solution you know what business skill executed and so on, but it is different: it is more about we know what risk you actually are buying with the things you have."** So the spine becomes the authorised universe a mandate opens, the blast radius, and the risk to accept (cross-ref: the blast-radius and risk-acceptance briefs).

### 3. Out Of Band, Not Block-What-Fails

The control correction: **visibility and control in days, not blocking.**

The project lead: **"it says we will block actions that fail, but we cannot control that, it requires in-line. We should position this as giving you visibility, giving you assurance, giving you a way to start taking control of your agents in days, not the blocking."** So the runtime-enforcement and block-what-fails language is repositioned to out-of-band visibility and a path to control, because blocking in line is the single-point-of-failure path we are not taking (cross-ref: the execution-memo-review and nhi-2.0 briefs).

### 4. Be Honest About The Data Gap

The credibility correction: **claim only the data we have.**

The project lead: **"he will know some of it, not all of it. We will have who approved it and which credential was issued, yes, but what data was used, we do not know that, and what decision was made, we do not know that."** So the artifacts must claim only what we hold, and the gap is a feature, not a weakness: starting from reality and saying plainly what we can and cannot see is the self-fulfilling visibility loop.

### 5. Confidence And Visibility As A First-Class Dimension

An addition: **how much we can see and trust the data.**

The project lead: **"a lot of this is about how much visibility and how much confidence we have on the data that we have, that is a key part."** So confidence in the data is itself a dimension to surface, alongside the blast radius and the risk (cross-ref: the observability brief).

### 6. The Code-Change Line

The deployability correction: **separate no-code-change from integration.**

The project lead: **"we must distinguish what requires code changes on the other side from what does not, and be careful not to need code changes we do not control. Split what we can do without touching the code from what we can do when the other party is integrating."** So every capability is tagged by whether it works on day one without the customer touching code, or needs them to integrate.

### 7. The Name

The branding correction: **Agent Mandate, not SGraph.**

The project lead: **"I do not think SGraph is the right name for this; it should be something around Agent Mandate."** This matches the naming brief (cross-ref: the naming brief).

## The Headline Reframe, Across All Four

| Partner's line | The correction |
|----------------|----------------|
| Identity tells what exists; the solution proves why it acted | Identity tells what is possible; it proves why it was possible and who authorised it |
| Block actions that fail attestation in real time | Give visibility, assurance, and a way to start taking control in days, out of band |
| A trust score from six factors, including behaviour consistency | Risk and blast radius, weighted by our confidence in the data we have |
| From who has access to why is it acting | Who has access is not solved; we solve what is possible and the risk it carries |
| Every step proven cryptographically | An opinion on whether that is even possible |
| SGraph | Agent Mandate |

## Artifact By Artifact

### Agent Trust Runtime

Re-point the dashboard to risk and blast radius. Discovery should include privileges, not only agents. The headline metric counts are context-dependent: the project lead notes that tens of thousands of ephemeral credentials could be good or bad, and it is the graph, not the count, that matters. The trust graph is the centrepiece, and it should show how the components fit together and the risk decisions, who decided, who asked, what evidence they showed, and it is where the blast radius is mapped and made to visibly explode for an agent that can damage the whole business, alongside the gaps. Certificates should be presented as provenance certificates, we know where this comes from, or that it does not. The trust score should connect to the blast radius and to our confidence in the data, and behaviour consistency should be softened because we may not have that data. The ledger should be the provenance of who signed what, with which privileges, in which environment, not an execution log. Ask the Graph is the chatbot we already have.

### Sales Deck

Keep the strong premises, your agents are becoming privileged users, and your identity stack was built for humans and service accounts, not autonomous agents acting on your behalf. Map the identity to what an agent can do. Re-point gives you agent trust in days to gives you visibility and a way to start taking control in days, starting from mandates and risk acceptance, and drop block actions that fail. Split the five risk points and the credential-lifecycle claims into what works without touching the customer's code and what needs them to integrate. The after slide is honest only for who approved and which credential was issued; what data was used and what decision was made are not ours to claim, so reframe the slide to we know what risk you are buying with what you have. Rename.

### Investor Deck

Upgrade identity tells what exists to it tells what is possible. Do not claim proves why it acted; claim proves why it was possible and who authorised it, and do not oversell. Do not concede that who has access is solved, that is part of what we solve. We can answer why is it possible to do that, and can we trust it, including the answer you should not trust this, and we do not trust the parts in production. Keep the category-creation ambition, the object model, and the seven ideas, while treating the partner-invented commercial specifics, the ARR curve, the pricing, the $100B claim, the roadmap names, as the partner's to confirm or replace, and dropping the trust score as a load-bearing concept.

### Marketing Website

The hero should say it proves why it worked, not why it acted. Replay-and-explain is only partly true, so claim it modestly. Of the five reframe questions, we can answer why does this agent exist, who authorised it, and what capability it represents; we cannot answer where its context originated, we monitor the request; and for can every step be proven cryptographically, we can offer an opinion on whether that is even possible. Keep the graph-compounds line and the open-core model, re-point the positioning to mandate and blast radius, and rename.

## The Honest-Data Table

What the artifacts should and should not claim:

| Claim | Can we substantiate it? |
|-------|--------------------------|
| Why this agent exists | Yes |
| Who authorised it | Yes |
| What capability it represents | Yes |
| Which credential was issued | Yes, where we issue it |
| What is possible, the blast radius | Yes, this is the core |
| What risk you are buying, whether to trust it | Yes, as an assessment |
| How confident we are in the data | Yes, and we should show it |
| What data was used | No |
| What decision was made | No |
| Why the agent acted, the reasoning | No |
| Where the context originated | No |
| Every step proven cryptographically | Only an opinion on whether it is possible |
| Every action replayed and explained | Partly, at best |

## What To Keep, Re-Point, Drop, Add

| Action | Items |
|--------|-------|
| Keep | The premises, the object model, the seven ideas, the graph-compounds line, open core, the worked example, the pilot and assessment structure |
| Re-point | Why it acted to why it worked and who authorised it; trust score to risk and blast radius; trust-in-days to visibility-and-control-in-days |
| Drop or soften | Block-what-fails, behaviour-consistency as a factor, claims about data used and decisions made, partner-invented ARR and pricing as fact |
| Add | Mandate, blast radius, risk acceptance, the confidence-in-data dimension, the code-change split, the Agent Mandate name |

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: the partner's artifacts are polished and consistent.** Re-pointing the spine is real work. Mitigation: tone and structure are largely reusable; the change is the headline and the claims, not the craft.

**Tension 2: the honest-data table narrows the pitch.** Claiming less than the partner did can feel weaker. Mitigation: it is more credible, and the blast-radius and risk framing is a stronger story than an overclaimed why.

**Tension 3: renaming late is costly.** Everything is SGraph-branded. Mitigation: settle the Agent Mandate frame and the company brand before these go public.
