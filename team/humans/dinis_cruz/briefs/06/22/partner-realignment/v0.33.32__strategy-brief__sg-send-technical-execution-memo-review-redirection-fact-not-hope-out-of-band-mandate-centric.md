# Review And Redirection Of The Technical Execution Memo: Fact Not Hope, Out Of Band, Mandate-Centric

**version** v0.33.32
**date** 22 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Partner, Architect, Security, Strategy, Product, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief (review and redirection)

---

## What This Is

A review and redirection of the partner's technical execution memo, realigning it to the agent-mandate strategy of this week: **the memo is strong and detailed, but it still leans on the why, the in-line enforcement model, and the hope that the agent will do the right thing, whereas the direction is to operate on the fact lens, what privilege exists and what capability was given, out of band rather than in line, answering how an action was possible and who authorised it rather than why the agent chose it, and to leverage the existing ecosystem rather than become the in-line single point of failure; it keeps much of the partner's data model and many of the PRDs but repositions them, certify what a skill asked for and can do, not what it will do, treat skills as state machines with a privilege per step, make risk acceptance the human-in-the-loop feedback that drives least privilege, start from reality and the joiner-mover-leaver gaps that already exist, redefine incidents around expiry, integrate natively with SIEMs, and treat short-lived tokens and attestation as things to enable and review rather than to own.** It connects the partner's spec to the week's strategy (cross-ref: the v0.33.32 how-not-why, why-is-hard, observability, potential-mandate, and mandate-to-operate briefs, the v0.33.40 risk-acceptance and skills-as-code briefs, and the v0.32.3 nhi-2.0 brief). New contributions: **the fact-versus-hope reframe, the PRD-by-PRD realignment, and the deterministic-nature insight.**

## The Framing

The starting point: **a good memo, pointed the wrong way.**

The project lead: **"this document and architecture are still a little bit focused on the whole why, why did the agent do this, the whole in-line mode, which in my view is not what we should be doing, and the latest memos show why that is very difficult. Even when you say scope to least privilege, we can facilitate it, but we do not have the right data to make the call."** So the memo is valuable, and much of it stays, but its centre of gravity, the why and the in-line enforcement, has to move.

## The Core Reframe: Fact Lens, Not Hope Model

The central correction: **we operate on what is, not on what we hope.**

The project lead: **"we operate on the fact that this privilege exists, this capability was given. We do not operate on the hope model, hey, we hope the agent is going to do the right thing. A very key point is the difference between the capability that is physically possible to do and what we are asking the code or the engine to do for us. As soon as you go into, can I do this, let me do it, or asking for permission, that is hope, because you hope the agent asks the right questions and respects what was requested. If the agent has access to the full database, then can I delete ten thousand records, given the evidence, still leaves a token that could delete the whole database. So we position ourselves in a fact world: here is all the scope, and everything that can happen after that is hope. We can make that hope less risky, but it is still risky."** So attest-before-action and ask-for-permission are hope strategies, and too late; the fact-level move is a cookie-cutter permission set that holds only the permission needed, even though most systems do not support that granularity yet (cross-ref: the authorization-ontology brief on capability versus control, and the potential-mandate brief).

## Out Of Band, Not In Line

The architectural correction: **enforcement in line makes you the bottleneck.**

The project lead: **"until you actually control the execution plane, which is application-specific and needs to be implemented, you cannot really do it, and even then you hope that plane is correct. Most decisions do not need to be done in line; it is the damage that needs to be prevented. You can keep a privilege in line as long as you can stop it from being executed within a certain period of time. But we are not in line."** So controlling execution is application-specific, hard, slow to deploy, slow to add value, and makes us a single point of failure; the out-of-band position is the one that scales, and it is enough as long as damage can be stopped within a window (cross-ref: the nhi-2.0 not-in-line brief).

## How And Who, Not Why

The scope correction: **answer how it was possible and who authorised it.**

The project lead: **"I do not think we can effectively answer why this agent acted, and that is not our goal. What we can answer is how it worked, and who authorised it, because that is the data we have. This becomes a self-fulfilling prophecy for visibility, because you start with reality: this is what happened today, yesterday, last month, can we explain it, can we explain who gave the privilege, who made the decision call, and then you peel the engine from there. You can start from both ends of the spectrum, and all of it is based on data."** This is the how-not-why scope, made concrete against the memo's "why did this agent act" framing (cross-ref: the how-not-why and why-is-hard briefs).

## Leverage The Ecosystem, Do Not Become The Bottleneck

The go-to-market correction: **live off the land.**

The project lead: **"we will enable other tools and systems to do this, because we capture a lot of the key data they do not have, and we connect the dots for them. Let us leverage the energy and money already in the market and make existing tools more effective. Issuing short-lived tokens is hard, and as soon as you do it you become a single point of failure; there are already players who have won that space, so let us work with them, review and help them, tell them your short-lived token is still alive to do this, especially connected to the metrics, there are tokens that go to critical damage in seconds and tokens that give you time. We can enable that service, even an MVP, but it should not be our main focus."** So short-lived tokens, attestation, and credential brokering are things to enable, integrate with, and review, not to own in line (cross-ref: the observability and rising-tide briefs).

## The Mandate-Centric Model And Risk Acceptance As The Human Loop

The model correction: **the human belongs in risk acceptance, not removed from the grant path.**

The project lead: **"we will have a score and a graph based on the mandates connected to the agent's identity, and we can find lots of gaps in the mandate phase, which creates a model with much more accountability. Risk acceptance is what allows automated least privilege, because the process of asking for the risk to be accepted forces agent developers to think, do I really need that privilege. So the human has to be in the loop, in the risk-acceptance phase, although we can give mandates within a certain scale that auto-accept."** This directly corrects the memo's goal of removing humans from the grant path: the human stays, in risk acceptance, which is the feedback loop that makes least privilege real, with auto-acceptance within a defined scale (cross-ref: the risk-acceptance and potential-mandate briefs).

## Skills, Reframed

The skills correction: **certify what it asked for, not what it will do; and make it a state machine.**

The project lead: **"we can certify a skill from what it asked for and what it can do, not what it will do, because the skill is code and text and can be prompt-injected, and different mandates on the same skill give different results. The only thing we control is what the skill is allowed to do. We can flag the extra privileges it asked for that it does not need, or, more valuably, that the skill has a bug because it will need to do something it has not asked permission for, which adds value to development now. And a skill should not be given one set of privileges; it should be a state machine, this step then this step, each with a privilege attached, so that if one part is compromised you do not have the union of everything."** So skill certification is about the allowed scope, the over-asks and under-asks are the value-add, and per-step privilege limits the blast radius of a compromise (cross-ref: the skills-as-code brief).

## PRD-By-PRD Realignment

Keeping the memo's structure, repointed:

| PRD | Partner's framing | The redirection |
|-----|-------------------|-----------------|
| 01 Skill Cert and PKI | Certify skills against policy before an agent exists | Keep; certify what it asked for and can do, not what it will do; flag over-asks and under-asks as bugs; map scopes in semantic graphs; skills as state machines with per-step privilege; adds value now |
| 02 Agent Birth Certs and Identity | Bind SPIFFE or X.509 identity per agent | Keep as a side action to the mandate, needed to track mandates; the foundation exists in the toolkit, the SGraph PKI, and vault-to-vault self-certs; but the cert code lives close to the app and the certificate chain is the hard, app-specific part |
| 03 Permission DNA | Derive least privilege automatically, remove humans from the grant path | Reframe: the hard part is knowing what is needed; over-privilege is fine in the Explorer phase, least privilege matters at maturity; risk acceptance is the human-in-the-loop that drives it, auto-accepting within a scale; automation is an evolution, not the start |
| 04 Ephemeral Vault and Credentials | Short-lived scoped credentials per permission set | Vaults support some of this, but in-line credentialing needs heavy code change and is a different, riskier product now; enable it and work with JIT vendors, do not own the in-line path |
| 05 Attestation and Context Provenance | Every tool call passes through an attestation gateway before execution | This is the in-line enforcement point we are avoiding; very hard; facilitate lightly; not the core |
| 06 Transparency Ledger | Append-only signed log reconstructing what happened | We will have a ledger, but of what we issue, tokens, certs, mandates, not a reconstruction of everything from feeds and custom parsers; start from what we can see |
| 07 Trust Scoring | A 0 to 100 score from six factors | Start with reality; behaviour and near-misses come later; the score depends massively on how the agent is built, its deterministic nature, mapped and connected to the observability vectors |
| 08 Agent Lifecycle, JML | Joiner-mover-leaver discipline | A great place to start, and a strong sales entry: how many agent JML gaps do you have today; capture them from what already exists |
| 09 Alert Center and Incident | Severity-tiered alerts in real time | Integrate natively with SIEMs, do not reinvent; redefine incidents around expiry, a large and under-recognised source |
| 10 RBAC and Persona Workspaces | Role-gated views | Comes native from the tech stack, vaults, and websites, using LLMs; out of band |
| 11 Developer Platform | SDK, CLI, API, webhooks | The vault is already API-driven and supports much of this |
| 12 Ask the Graph | An LLM answering why an agent acted | Reframe to how and who-authorised, grounded in our data; the vault already supports access and chat; assume SIEMs may hold more data and may produce better insights on their slice |

## The Deterministic-Nature Insight

An addition worth making explicit: **how the agent is built changes the risk more than its privileges.**

The project lead: **"the risk score of an agent depends massively on how the agent is built. Is it a prompt, heavily prompt-injectable, in line, manipulable, or is it out of band, working from a control graph, pure code with a very limited LLM element, for example only creating semantic graphs. You can have a ridiculously deterministic agent with crazy access that is very safe, and a highly non-deterministic, injectable agent with a small number of privileges that is ridiculously dangerous."** So a real risk model has to capture the deterministic nature of the agent, not just its permissions, connected to the multi-vector observability analysis (cross-ref: the observability brief). This is an addition to the memo's six-factor trust score, and arguably the dominant factor.

## Start With Reality, And JML

The entry point: **the gaps that already exist sell themselves.**

Joiner-mover-leaver discipline for agents is both a strong first build and a strong sales entry: ask an organisation how many agent JML gaps it already has, agents that started, moved, and were never deprovisioned, never cascaded, never retired, and capture them from the privileges and agents already in place, telling them what needs to happen while the heavy lifting stays on their side. And incidents should be redefined around expiry, which causes many incidents and is hard even to discover, this account or certificate is about to expire or be disabled, are you prepared. Starting from reality, what already happened and what already exists, is the self-fulfilling visibility loop.

## Roadmap Realignment

The pace: **MVP-able fast, aligned this way.**

The project lead: **"aligned with these ideas, we can MVP most of this in one, two, three to six months, especially with a dev team from investment. Build them, then let the market guide us to what adds value, what is profitable, what we can sell. And I will be building a lot of these components anyway, because much of this is aligned with what I am already doing, so even without investment, over the next three months, I will probably implement a large chunk of it."** So the redirection does not slow the roadmap; it sharpens it, and the out-of-band, leverage-the-ecosystem direction is what makes the one-to-six-month MVP realistic.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: the partner invested in the in-line model.** The redirection moves away from the attestation gateway as the core. Mitigation: keep it as an enable-and-facilitate capability, not the centre; the data model largely survives.

**Tension 2: out of band gives up some control.** In line can stop an action; out of band reacts. Mitigation: out of band is enough where damage can be stopped within a window, and it is what scales; revisit in line at maturity.

**Tension 3: leveraging the ecosystem means sharing the value.** SIEMs and JIT vendors hold some of the prize. Mitigation: we hold the data they lack and connect the dots, which is a durable position and avoids the single-point-of-failure risk.
