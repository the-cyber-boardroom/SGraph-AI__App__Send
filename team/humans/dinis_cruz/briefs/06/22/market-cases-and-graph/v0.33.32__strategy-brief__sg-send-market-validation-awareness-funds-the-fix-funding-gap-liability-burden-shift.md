## What This Is

The market-validation brief for the approach: **the core hypothesis is that if the multiple stakeholders are made aware, fact-driven and graph-driven, of the real risks they are taking, they will both demand that something be done and fund, or help fund, the fix, which addresses a gap most cybersecurity companies dodge, they know who buys their product but not who funds the changes it generates; the dynamics that make this work are the financial flows and liability of each player, where a provider's liability is often capped at the contract value while the customer carries the real risk, the burden-shift where the provider must prove an exposure is not possible, the reward for companies that do a good job versus those that hand an agent broad access, and the ability to reverse-engineer a security posture from marketing materials, since a vendor cannot offer end-to-end encryption and content search at once; the workflow is to connect the parties and the supply chain into a graph with an evidence chain that shows, at every angle, who has accepted what, with delegation sign-off where authority is required; and the pitch is an honest, pragmatic naming of the hope strategy the customer is already running.** It tests whether the approach has a market (cross-ref: the v0.33.32 the-approach and partner-artifacts-alignment briefs, the v0.33.40 risk-acceptance and inefficiency briefs, the v0.33.31 mandate-is-delegation brief, the v0.33.30 third-party-impact brief, and the v0.33.28 supply-chain brief). New contributions: **the funding-the-change gap, the liability dynamic, the burden-shift, and reverse-engineering posture from marketing.**

## The Framing

The first question: **does the market exist, and is this the way to solve it.**

The project lead: **"the next step is to map examples and workflows of how we add value to the visitors of the website, but the first question is to validate the market: does this problem exist, and is this a way to solve it?"** So before the services, the hypothesis has to be tested.

## The Core Hypothesis

The primitive: **awareness drives both demand and funding.**

The project lead: **"the hypothesis is that if the stakeholders are aware of the risks they are taking today and in the future, they will ask the right questions, and they will fund the fix."** So awareness is the lever, and it produces two effects, the demand for action and the funding of it.

## The Funding-The-Change Gap

The gap most companies dodge: **who pays for the change, not who buys the product.**

The project lead: **"one of the most important questions, which a lot of cybersecurity companies do not address head on, is the financial flow. They know very well who is buying their product, but they do not have a good story for who funds the changes their product generates. When you discover a problem, you have to think about the whole food chain from there to fixed: funding the change, who can do it, the side-effects, the blast radius, who owns it, who checks it, who pays for it. Even if there is a budget, that is time not spent on something else."** So the gap is the path from finding to fixed, and the unanswered question of who funds it; validating that the right risk description makes stakeholders both demand and fund the fix is the heart of the test (cross-ref: the risk-acceptance brief).

## The Players, Financial Flows, And Liability

The dynamic between parties: **liability capped at the contract, risk carried by the customer.**

The project lead: **"there are multiple players, each with different interests and financial flows. There is the one using an LLM, and the company providing a service using an LLM, which either proactively knows about the risk, the best case, or is on the receiving end of, why are you asking for all these privileges? And there is an interesting cyber-insurance dynamic: who is liable? You are asking me to take on a risk, but your liability is up to the value of what we pay you, which is very little."** So a provider's liability is often capped at the contract value while the customer carries the real risk, which is a dynamic the graph can make visible (cross-ref: the third-party-impact brief).

## The Burden-Shift And Rewarding Good Actors

The mechanic: **the vendor must prove it is not possible.**

The project lead: **"based on the permissions, here is the risk, here is this blind spot and that blind spot, based on reality, and it is up to the company to prove it is not possible. You are processing results in line, so you are vulnerable to prompt injection, and it is up to the company to say, we are not, because we do this. Very few can claim a prompt-injection solution. And then: assume prompt injection happens, or your agent is over-enthusiastic, what can it do, how wide is the blast radius? This rewards the companies that do defence and least privilege, versus the ones that just give tons of access to the agent."** So the risk picture shifts the burden onto the provider to show an exposure is not possible, and it rewards good actors over careless ones (cross-ref: the inefficiency and blast-radius briefs).

## Reverse-Engineering The Posture From Marketing

A powerful tool: **the claims reveal the architecture.**

The project lead: **"just by looking at the market materials we can determine a lot about what is actually going on. A lot of file-sharing solutions do not do end-to-end encryption because they offer search and file analysis, and you cannot have both, you cannot hold no keys and still analyse the content. And reality comes into play: every incident and behaviour, even in peacetime, plus the privileges, you say your agent has no access to this, but it is doing that."** So a vendor's own marketing constrains what its architecture can be, end-to-end encryption and content search are mutually exclusive, and the observed privileges and behaviours test the claims against reality (cross-ref: the WhatsApp and Odysseus case studies).

## Supply-Chain Stacking And Provider Settings

The chain compounds: **each layer's posture propagates.**

The project lead: **"the supply chain stacks, one on top of another, this service uses that service, so what are the security requirements of that service. If you use AWS Bedrock or Azure OpenAI connectors with certain settings, then nobody is learning from your data, so you have some governance, but you have to understand how that is done and what assurances are in place, and how each provider maps it."** So the posture of each provider in the chain propagates to the customer, and providers that expose strong, verifiable settings are rewarded (cross-ref: the supply-chain brief).

## The Per-Angle Graph And Who Accepted What

The workflow: **a graph and evidence chain, seen from every angle.**

Connect the parties and the risks into a graph with an evidence chain, so that at each angle the picture is legible: an internal Gen AI team maps the privileges it is using and reports them up to its management, and at every level you can see what is going on and, crucially, who has accepted what. The project lead: **"this is where the delegation angle gets interesting, because if you need to do this, it needs to be signed off by that person, the same way a business has legal counsel sign off, so who authorises the business to sign this off, which is the delegation authority."** So the graph shows the acceptances and the sign-offs, and delegation authority is where the right to accept is checked (cross-ref: the mandate-is-delegation and risk-acceptance briefs).

## The Pitch: Name The Hope Strategy, Honestly

The message: **pragmatic, evidence-based, not manufactured.**

The project lead: **"there is a little fear, uncertainty, and doubt here, but we are not creating it, we are just naming it. The objective is to say, pragmatically, you are literally giving the keys of the castle to that agent. You currently have a hope strategy: you hope the things in place will not happen. There is a dev with a production database on a laptop, so you are hoping that laptop is not compromised, and how many agents does that laptop run? The exposure multiplies. And it is important to base this on evidence."** So the pitch names the hope strategy the customer is already running, in plain and pragmatic terms, grounded in evidence rather than invented fear (cross-ref: the authorization-ontology brief on hope-based authorization, and the evidence work to come).

## The Market Question And The Services On Top

What this validates: **the information, and the services on it.**

The project lead: **"the key premise is, is there a market for giving the multiple stakeholders, across this graph and this evidence, the information they need, and what are the services we can provide on top of this workflow? That is what tells us whether we have a business."** So the validation is twofold: that stakeholders want the information, and that there are services to sell on top of the graph and the evidence.

## What This Asks For

1. **Test the hypothesis** that awareness drives demand and funding.
2. **Map the financial flows and liability** of each player.
3. **Use the burden-shift**: present the risk from the permissions, let the vendor disprove it.
4. **Reward good actors** and surface careless ones.
5. **Reverse-engineer posture from marketing** and test it against behaviour.
6. **Build the per-angle graph and evidence chain** that shows who accepted what.
7. **Name the hope strategy** honestly, on evidence.
8. **Identify the services** sellable on top.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not manufactured fear.** The risk is named from fact and evidence, not invented.
- **Not an accusation.** The burden-shift invites the vendor to show it is safe.
- **Not proven yet.** The funding loop is the hypothesis under test.
- **Not the services themselves.** Those are the next brainstorm.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: the line between honest risk and FUD.** Naming risk can tip into fear-selling. Mitigation: keep every claim fact and evidence based, and invite disproof.

**Tension 2: reverse-engineered claims must be defensible.** Inference is not proof. Mitigation: state what the evidence supports and no more, and let the vendor respond.

**Tension 3: the funding loop is unproven.** It is a hypothesis. Mitigation: that is exactly what this validation sets out to test.
