## What This Is

The agentic-cost case study, applying the agent-mandate framework to runaway token spend: **signing off an LLM without cost controls is the moment of authorisation of a blank check, because token billing has no ceiling unless one is explicitly defined, so the capability to spend is unbounded and relying on people to be frugal is hope-based; the cost is a pure externality, the person burning the tokens does not bear it, and companies actively created a perverse incentive, tokenmaxxing and internal usage leaderboards, so the maximum liability is effectively the maximum the company can spend; the mandate trickles down the chain, every employee given a token without a cap holds unbounded spend authority, amplified by new models that run for hours; the evidence is near-unquestionable, an unnamed enterprise reportedly hit a five-hundred-million-dollar bill in a month with no caps, a major company blew its entire annual AI budget in about four months, and several pulled or migrated licences, which makes this a flagship evidence-driven mapping; the cost-control dilemma is real, a cap can stop applications mid-task, no cap is unbounded, so the questions are whether there is a cap, a maximum, and per-team accountability, and the conclusion is the risk the business is buying.** It is the agentic-cost instance of the evidence-driven mapping (cross-ref: the v0.33.32 evidence-mapping-website, observability, and market-validation briefs, the v0.33.31 moment-of-authorisation and mandate-is-delegation briefs, and the v0.33.40 blast-radius and risk-acceptance briefs). Sources are paraphrased and listed. New contributions: **the blank-check framing, the cost-as-blast-radius mapping, and the evidence base.**

## The Framing

Why this case: **everyone is talking about agentic costs, and it is a perfect externality.**

The project lead: **"I want to bring home this whole risk, especially driven by agents, and the one I want to focus on is agentic costs, because everybody is now talking about how much it costs and how people are spending far too much on tokens. It is a perfect example of the externality of cost, where the real cost is not borne by the person driving it, and we even have a perverse incentive, where companies told employees they should be using more tokens."** So the case is both topical and a clean illustration of cost as an unowned, externalised risk.

## The Blank Check: The Moment Of Authorisation

The core mapping: **no cost control means an unbounded mandate.**

The project lead: **"when the CFO signs off the use of a particular LLM in the organisation, if there are no cost controls, the CFO is signing off a blank check. The maximum liability is effectively the maximum amount the company can spend."** This is the moment-of-authorisation principle in financial form: the grant is the sign-off, and without a control the capability to spend is unbounded. Reporting confirms the mechanism, token billing has no ceiling unless one is explicitly defined, so an agent can expand its own scope, loop, and re-send its full context until the bill is enormous. Relying on users to be frugal is hope-based authorization; the unbounded capability is what actually holds (cross-ref: the moment-of-authorisation and authorization-ontology briefs).

## The Externality And The Perverse Incentive

Why it ran away: **the spender does not pay, and was told to spend more.**

The cost falls on the company, not the person burning the tokens, and through 2025 and into 2026 the incentive ran the wrong way: a culture of tokenmaxxing, internal usage leaderboards at large companies, and senior figures suggesting an engineer who was not consuming a large fraction of their salary in tokens was under-using AI. The externality and the incentive together produced exactly the over-consumption the framework would predict.

## The Delegation Down The Chain

How it multiplied: **every token granted is unbounded spend authority delegated.**

The project lead: **"trickle it down from the CFO to the teams to the execs to the managers to the people on the ground. Once we give each of them a token, give them access to a model that does not have cost control, we are saying any one of them can spend as much money as the agent can consume."** So the blank-check mandate is delegated down the organisation, each grant conferring unbounded spend authority, and the right-to-delegate question applies, did each grantor have the authority to confer that. New long-running models that work for hours, that keep going, amplify the per-grant exposure (cross-ref: the mandate-is-delegation brief).

## The Evidence

Why this is a flagship mapping: **the cases are public and stark.**

The project lead: **"you go by evidence, by companies who have spent half a billion, and companies that blew their budget in two months."** The public record bears this out: an unnamed enterprise reportedly reached a five-hundred-million-dollar bill in a single month after deploying access with no usage caps; a major ride-hailing company exhausted its entire 2026 AI budget in about four months and then imposed a per-employee monthly cap; another large company revoked most internal coding-assistant licences over unsustainable bills; and one developer's experiment reportedly generated over a million dollars of spend in a month, most of it removable by turning off a single high-rate setting. Agents consume many times, by some measures over a thousand times, the tokens of a chat, because each step re-sends the whole context. This is a near-unquestionable, evidence-rich case (cross-ref: the evidence-mapping-website brief).

## The Cloud Precedent

A familiar shape: **the same as cloud spend, with fewer controls at first.**

The project lead: **"this is the same problem we had with AWS and the cloud providers, but there were more controls in place, where now there did not seem to be."** Cloud cost overruns produced a whole financial-operations discipline; token spend repeated the pattern with the guardrails initially missing, and a tooling and standards market, metering, caps, model routing, and caching, is now forming to catch up.

## The Cost-Control Dilemma

Why there is no clean fix: **a cap can break things; no cap is unbounded.**

The project lead: **"there are no perfect solutions. If there is a cost control, are you having applications that suddenly stop working when you hit a certain budget? But are you also making each company and each team accountable for the money they spend?"** So a hard cap can halt an application mid-task, while no cap leaves the blank check open, and the genuine question is per-team accountability, which is a trade-off to be accepted with open eyes, not a clean solution.

## Observability: The Spend Is Invisible

Why it surprises everyone: **token cost does not show up where finance looks.**

Token spend does not appear on a purchase order and is largely invisible to finance until the bill arrives, which is the visibility-and-confidence problem in cost form, and the reason companies were surprised rather than warned (cross-ref: the observability brief).

## The Method: A Few Questions To The Risk They Are Buying

How the case closes: **a handful of questions, then the conclusion.**

The project lead: **"with four or five or six questions we then have a whole set of conclusions based on the risk they are buying in token usage."** The questions: is there a cost control; is there a maximum limit; is each team accountable; who signed it off, and were they authorised to; what is the maximum liability; and does a cap risk stopping applications. The answers map directly to the risk the business is buying, and the case gets the stakeholders to accept that this is the reality of an uncapped agentic mandate (cross-ref: the risk-acceptance and market-validation briefs).

## Honest Notes

- **The five-hundred-million figure is reported and attributed to an unnamed enterprise.** It is widely cited but not independently confirmed here.
- **The named examples are public reporting.** Specifics evolve; confirm before quoting in customer materials.
- **Cost is one blast-radius dimension.** It is the most legible, but the same mandate framing applies to data and action.
- **A cap is not a complete answer.** It trades runaway spend for the risk of halting work.
