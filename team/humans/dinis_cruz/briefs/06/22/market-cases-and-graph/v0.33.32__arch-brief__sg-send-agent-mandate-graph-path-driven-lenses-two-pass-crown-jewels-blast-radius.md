## What This Is

The graph model for the agent mandate, combining the week's ontology into something navigable: **the ontology and taxonomy, decision, authorization, mandate, capability, control, and action, now extended with delegation, becomes a path-driven, text-first graph held in the vault as the database; the uber-graph of thousands of nodes renders as an unreadable blob, so navigation is path-driven, every node carries two distinct relationships, one outward and one inward, mandate given to and mandate received from, authorised to and authorised by, and you navigate by defining a path and then visualising it, starting from many places to show many angles; it is built in two passes, first follow the paths to construct the universe for a chosen topic, the AWS accounts, the agentic workflows, a specific path, rather than everything a CISO cares about, then run focused analysis and queries on that scoped graph; the lenses are the multiple actors, the risk, the stakeholders, and the risk-acceptance workflow; the hypothesis is that a graph shown well makes the blast radius understandable, so map the crown jewels and ask whether a path reaches them and whether it can be detected; the method draws on threat modelling, is authored first on paper in markdown and then visualised in JavaScript and made queryable by an LLM, and moves through a load, extract, transform, and save pipeline.** It is the graph realisation of the ontology (cross-ref: the v0.33.30 ontology brief, the v0.33.31 moment-of-authorisation and mandate-is-delegation briefs, the v0.33.40 risk-acceptance brief, and the v0.33.32 observability brief). New contributions: **the path-driven navigation, the two-pass build, and the crown-jewels hypothesis.**

## The Framing

What this combines: **the prior work, applied to the agent mandate, as a graph.**

The project lead: **"this combines a bunch of what we talked about, applied to the agent mandate use case, which is the graph of it. We started with the ontology and taxonomy, decision, authorization, mandate, which was good; I think we need to add delegation to it, and expand it."** So the graph is the agent-mandate ontology made navigable, with delegation added.

## The Ontology, Extended With Delegation

The nodes and their relationships: **decision, authorization, mandate, capability, control, action, and delegation.**

The ontology and taxonomy already map decision, authorization, mandate, capability, control, and action; delegation joins them. In essence the ontology is the two sets of relationships each node carries, the outward and the inward, which is what makes the graph navigable (cross-ref: the ontology and mandate-is-delegation briefs).

## Path-Driven Navigation, Not The Blob

The core technique: **navigate by path, because the whole graph is a blob.**

The project lead: **"the uber-graph will have thousands and thousands of nodes, which when you render it looks like a big dot, the blob problem with big graphs, though colour-coding gives some sense of it. What I want to focus on is path-driven analysis, where every node has two relationships, one to and one from, that are very distinct, mandate is given to and received mandate from, is authorised to and is authorised by, and all the actions. You navigate the graph by defining the path, then you visualise it, and you can start the graph in multiple places, so you show it from different angles."**

| Relationship | Outward | Inward |
|--------------|---------|--------|
| Mandate | gives mandate to | received mandate from |
| Authorization | is authorised to | is authorised by |
| Delegation | delegates to | delegated by |
| Capability | enables | enabled by |
| Action | performs | performed by |

Each node's distinct outward and inward edges are what let you walk a path through the graph and render just that path, rather than the whole hairball.

## The Two-Pass Build

How to make it tractable: **build the universe for a topic, then analyse it.**

The project lead: **"you have a couple of passes. The first pass creates the universe you are opening up, you follow a set of paths until you have a comparatively massive graph, and then on that graph you do the focused analysis. So instead of, what is everything the CISO cares about, which is massive, you go by topic: first create the graph for AWS accounts, or for agentic workflows, or for specific paths, and then query on top of that."**

```
  Pass 1: build the universe for a topic
     topic = AWS accounts | agentic workflows | a specific path
     follow the relationships outward until the scoped universe exists
            |
            v
  Pass 2: focused analysis and queries on that scoped graph
     lenses = actors | risk | stakeholders | risk-acceptance
     question = is there a path to the crown jewels, and can it be detected?
```

Scoping the universe to a topic first is what keeps the analysis tractable and the rendering legible.

## The Lenses

The angles that make it valuable: **actors, risk, stakeholders, acceptance.**

The project lead: **"the power is when you see the multiple angles with the multiple actors, especially when you look at the risk, and who has to accept the risk, and the current risk-acceptance workflow, and the stakeholders, that is where it gets very interesting."** So the same scoped graph is read through several lenses, who acts, what the risk is, who must accept it, and how acceptance flows today (cross-ref: the risk-acceptance brief).

## The Crown-Jewels Hypothesis

The test of the whole thing: **a graph shown well makes the blast radius obvious.**

The project lead: **"the hypothesis is that when we show the graph and the interconnections in a way that makes sense, they should understand the blast radius. I like the idea of mapping the crown jewels, the things that really matter to an exec, and asking, is there a path there, and can you detect it? When we add the layers of which assets are protected and which are not, everything fits together, and we can take inspiration from threat modelling."** So the graph earns its keep when a person sees it and grasps the blast radius, and the sharpest question is whether a path reaches the crown jewels and whether that path would be detected, which is threat modelling made visual (cross-ref: the observability brief on detectability).

## Text First, Then Visualise, Then Talk To It

How it is built: **markdown in the vault, then JavaScript, then an LLM.**

The project lead: **"the vault is our database, so we author this first on paper, by which I mean markdown, and simulate it, then start implementing the visualisation. We can visualise anything we can do in JavaScript, do multiple paths of analysis, and these days even let you talk to the graph through an LLM, which is super powerful."** The data moves through a load, extract, transform, and save pipeline, and the graph is authored text-first so it can be reasoned about before it is rendered.

## What This Asks For

1. **Extend the ontology with delegation** as a first-class relationship.
2. **Model every node with a distinct outward and inward edge** for path navigation.
3. **Author the graph text-first in markdown** in the vault, and simulate it.
4. **Build in two passes**: the topic-scoped universe, then focused analysis.
5. **Read it through the lenses**: actors, risk, stakeholders, acceptance.
6. **Map the crown jewels** and test for a reachable, detectable path.
7. **Add the JavaScript visualisations and the LLM query layer** after the text model holds.

Estimated effort: moderate to build the text model and the path navigation; the visualisation and LLM layers follow.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not the blob.** It is path-driven, not whole-graph rendering.
- **Not everything at once.** It is topic-scoped, two-pass.
- **Not visualisation-first.** It is authored text-first, then rendered.
- **Not a single view.** It is read through multiple lenses.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: the blob problem is real.** Big graphs do not render usefully. Mitigation: path-driven navigation and topic scoping are the answer.

**Tension 2: two-pass scoping can miss cross-topic paths.** A narrow universe hides connections. Mitigation: make the first pass wide enough, and allow paths across topics.

**Tension 3: crown-jewels detection depends on the data.** A path may exist that we cannot see. Mitigation: pair it with the confidence and observability vectors.
