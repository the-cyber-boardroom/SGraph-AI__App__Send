## What This Is

The side research on companies, especially startups, trying to explain why an agent did something: **the field is crowded, more than twenty agent-observability and explainability platforms, plus governance, context-layer, and event-sourcing players, which confirms the pitch is appealing and the entrants many; but the research also confirms our thesis, that most tools capture what happened, traces, reasoning steps, and tool calls, not why, and several vendors say plainly that tracing alone cannot explain why an agent decided as it did; explaining the why is repeatedly described as needing business context and a context graph, the very idea that faded before and is now being revived, and as an architecture and infrastructure problem, event-sourced immutable causal capture, not a model problem; sovereignty is a recurring constraint, an explainability tool confined to its own cloud is a poor fit; and the regulatory drivers, the EU AI Act and GDPR, are why customers start there; the reading is that these confirm the why is hard and that the mandate-and-capability foundation is what they all need first, while most occupy the in-line, capture-everything, own-cloud position that is a poor place for a startup to start.** It is the companion research to the why-is-hard brief (cross-ref: the v0.33.32 why-is-hard and how-not-why briefs, the v0.32.3 nhi-2.0 brief, and the v0.33.40 graphs-of-graphs brief). Sources are paraphrased and listed. New contributions: **the landscape map, the what-not-why finding, and the positioning reading.**

## The Framing

What was asked: **research the companies trying to explain the why.**

The brief was to survey, especially among startups, who is working on explaining why an agent did something, given the expectation that there are dozens if not hundreds.

## The Field Is Crowded

The first finding: **many players, across five rough categories.**

- **Agent and LLM observability and tracing.** The largest group: LangSmith, Langfuse, Arize with Phoenix, Braintrust, Galileo, Helicone, AgentOps, Maxim, Laminar, Comet Opik, Weights and Biases Weave, Confident AI, Latitude, plus Datadog and Honeycomb modules. Reviews compare more than twenty, and note new entrants every quarter.
- **Explainable AI and model interpretability.** Seekr, Fiddler, WhyLabs: tracing outputs to training data, influence scoring, confidence.
- **Governance and decision provenance.** Kore.ai, DataRobot, IBM, Dynatrace: decision provenance, immutable audit trails, agent identity.
- **Context layer.** Atlan: a context graph connecting agent traces to business, lineage, and governance context.
- **Event-sourcing and decision memory.** Axoniq: an event store of immutable causal records to reconstruct why.

The crowding confirms the project lead's expectation, and the appeal of the pitch.

## Most Capture What, Not Why

The key finding: **a trace is not an explanation.**

Across the observability group, the common capability is capturing traces, reasoning steps, and tool calls, and one comparison notes that every tool can capture traces and the real question is what happens after the trace lands. Several vendors say plainly that tracing alone does not explain why an agent decided as it did, and that most tools stop at tokens and latency. This matches the thesis: capturing what happened is not the same as explaining why, and the why is the hard part.

## The Why Needs Business Context And A Context Graph

The second finding: **the faded idea, revived.**

The context-layer players argue that observability tools lack the business, lineage, and governance context needed to explain why an agent behaved as it did, and they propose a context graph connecting traces to data assets, policies, ownership, and semantic definitions. This is the context-graph idea the project lead described as having started with energy and faded, now revived for agents, and it still depends on the formalised business context most organisations lack, which is exactly the difficulty the why-is-hard brief identified.

## It Is An Architecture Problem, Not A Model Problem

The third finding: **provenance lives in the infrastructure.**

The event-sourcing players argue that explainability is not solved at the model layer but is an architecture and infrastructure problem: you need an event store of immutable, causal records to reconstruct why a decision was made, because ordinary logs capture actions without the full decision context. One vendor frames it with an agent that deleted data on a system never built to remember, and asks whether you can know what your agents did and undo it. This matches the thesis closely: the platforms do not help, the why needs evidence captured and preserved in a temporal state, and the question that matters is how an action was possible, alongside whether it can be reversed.

## Sovereignty And Regulation

The fourth finding: **data residency and the EU AI Act drive it.**

Sovereignty recurs: vendors stress self-hosting and full data ownership, and one notes that an explainability tool confined to its own cloud is a poor fit for organisations with data-sovereignty constraints. The regulatory drivers are explicit: the EU AI Act's transparency provisions, due in August 2026, and GDPR's automated-decision rules are why customers start with the why. This matches both the sovereignty point, a lot of this data can never leave the organisation, and the appeal that draws customers and competitors to the why first.

## The Reading For SGraph

What it means for us: **confirmation, and a clear lane.**

The landscape confirms the why-is-hard thesis on every point: the field is crowded, traces are not explanations, the why needs business context and a context graph that organisations rarely have, it is an architecture and temporal-evidence problem, and it is constrained by sovereignty. The two approaches closest to ours, the context graph and event sourcing, still require the formalised business context and a substantial re-architecture, which is the later, mature stage. Most players occupy the in-line, capture-everything, own-cloud position that is the biggest moat and the biggest single point of failure, a poor place for a startup to start. The lane this leaves is the one we have chosen: start with the mandate-and-capability foundation, binary, objective, and reverse-engineerable, out of band and sovereign, which is what every why-player needs underneath it (cross-ref: the how-not-why and why-is-hard briefs).

## Honest Notes

- **The categories blur.** Many tools span observability, evals, and governance; the map is rough.
- **Funding specifics move fast.** Langfuse was acquired by ClickHouse; others raise often; confirm before citing.
- **Some genuinely add value now.** Part of the market needs the why today; this is not a dismissal.
- **We share infrastructure ideas with the strongest players.** Event sourcing and context graphs overlap our temporal-evidence and graph work; the difference is where we start.
