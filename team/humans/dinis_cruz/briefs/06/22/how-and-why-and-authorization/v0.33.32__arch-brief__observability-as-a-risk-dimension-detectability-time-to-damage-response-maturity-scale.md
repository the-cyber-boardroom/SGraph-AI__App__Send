# Observability As A Risk Dimension: Detectability, Time-To-Damage, And Response

**version** v0.33.32
**date** 22 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Architect, Security, Strategy, @Dev
**type** Arch brief (in the series)

---

## What This Is

The dimension that turns a capability map into a real-impact map: **mapping what an agent can do tells you the privilege, but the real impact of a risk is modulated by whether it can be seen and stopped, so this brief adds objective, fact-driven vectors on a maturity scale, whether we can capture that a privilege was invoked at all and how granularly, the full request and response or only that something happened, how delayed the logs are, real-time or a day or a week, how fast damage can actually be done given real-world limits like exfiltration throughput, whether the monitoring is actually turned on and watched, whether there is a team with playbooks to respond, and whether detection has been simulated and drilled; the reframe is that a loud, detectable, slowly-scaling, well-drilled risk is lower than a quiet, fast, unwatched one of the same capability; and all of it uses data that already exists.** It is the monitoring topic the potential-mandate brief teased (cross-ref: the v0.33.32 potential-mandate brief, the v0.33.40 inefficiency-of-exploitation and agent-blast-radius briefs, the Wardley-maps brief, and the assessment-template brief). New contributions: **the six observability vectors, the maturity-scale framing, and the detectability-modulates-risk reframe.**

## The Framing

What this adds: **another dimension to the captured data.**

The project lead: **"most of what we have talked about is mapping the privileges somebody has access to, and that maps the risk. But when we look at risk, there are more vectors that make a massive difference to the real impact of a risk."** So the capability map is necessary but not sufficient; the impact depends on more.

## The Grounding: Getting In Is Not Getting Away

Why capability overstates risk: **you are not invisible.**

The project lead: **"a zero day, a massive vulnerability, or prompt injection does not make you invisible. You can find your way into the middle of the vault, the Fort Knox, but you still have to get the gold out. You can control an agent with a huge amount of capabilities, but the agent still has to do a bunch of stuff, so you are not invisible at that moment. A lot of this prompt-injection work needs quite a lot of noise."** This is the inefficiency-of-exploitation point: reaching the asset is not the same as extracting value undetected, and the doing is noisy (cross-ref: the inefficiency-of-exploitation brief).

## The New Vectors, On A Maturity Scale

The six dimensions to map, each on a low-to-high maturity scale:

| Vector | The question | Maturity, low to high |
|--------|--------------|------------------------|
| Capture | Can we know when the privilege is invoked, and what was executed? | No log access; only that a request happened; the full request and response |
| Latency | When do we get the logs? | A week; a day; an hour; real-time |
| Damage rate | How much damage can be done in a given time? | Worst case in seconds; minutes; hours; days |
| Real monitoring | Is the monitoring actually turned on and watched? | Off or unwatched; watched in real time |
| Response | Is there a team with playbooks to act? | None; a drilled team with playbooks |
| Detection confidence | Has detection been simulated and drilled? | Never; regularly, with measured confidence |

For five of the six, higher maturity lowers the risk. Damage rate runs the other way: the slower the worst case arrives, the lower the risk, because there is more time to react.

### Capture

The project lead: **"a simple question: do we have data to know when the privilege is actually invoked? There is a spectrum, from not at all, which happens a lot with SaaS providers and companies you do not fully control, to some data, to fully detailed and real-time. Sometimes we have no access to the logs at all. Sometimes the logs only record that a request was made at a moment in time, not what was executed, while another database captures the entire request and even the entire response."** So capture has two axes: whether we get logs at all, and how much of the actual request and response they hold.

### Latency

The project lead: **"the next question is when we get the logs, once an hour, a day, a week? AWS billing data is an amazing resource for understanding what is going on, but it is delayed by about a day. A lot of logs are not available in real time, and the timeframe limits what you can do, because a crazy privilege operation with crazy logging and visibility is safer, since you can react much faster."**

### Damage Rate

The project lead: **"how much damage can be made in a specific amount of time? Is it a case where in seconds you have the worst case, or does it take minutes, hours, days? Take data exfiltration: having the ability to exfiltrate does not mean you can download a terabyte, or even gigabytes, instantly. I remember a system that could only output something like tens of megabytes a minute, which introduced real limitations, you had to make requests in parallel. What we are really asking is how much the attack scales, how you go from a lot of damage to crazy damage, and time is the variable."**

### Real Monitoring, Response, And Detection Confidence

The project lead: **"you could have logs available in real time that give you everything, but they were never turned on, or nobody is looking at them, so they are pointless. Or the best monitoring system on the planet, but you do not get the data. And it is not just whether you can react: do you have a team, do you have playbooks, can you actually act? Then, have you simulated this? How many events have you run in the last day, week, month? How confident are you that in a particular scenario this would be detected?"** So monitoring that is unwatched is theatre, response needs a team and playbooks, and confidence comes from drilling.

## The Reframe: Detectability Modulates Risk

The conclusion: **the same capability is not the same risk.**

The project lead: **"a super-high-profile risk that is easy to attack but easy to detect and handle is much lower risk than a mid-level risk that flies under the radar and chips away slowly, because there the damage is huge."** So the real risk is the capability modulated by detectability, time-to-damage, and response: a loud, detectable, slow, well-drilled risk is lower than a quiet, fast, unwatched one with the same underlying privilege.

## Fact-Driven, From Data That Exists

Why this is sound: **objective metrics, not theory.**

The project lead: **"the power of this is that it is all done with data that exists. This is fact-driven, not theoretical, not something done outside the current flow. These are objective metrics, and this is where you apply maturity models to everything we are doing."** The mapping is often a one-off per system, stable unless the system changes, and it draws on logs, latencies, and throughput that are already measurable.

## Relationship To The Potential Mandate

How the two fit: **one produces the stream, the other measures it.**

The potential-mandate split produces a live stream of privilege use; this brief defines what to measure on it. Together they move control from assignment-only to assignment plus observation: the grant is the first control point, and observability is the second (cross-ref: the potential-mandate brief).

## What This Asks For

1. **Add the six observability vectors** to the captured data, beside the privilege map.
2. **Score each on a maturity scale**, from none to real-time and drilled.
3. **Treat damage rate as time-bounded**, with throughput limits modelled.
4. **Distinguish logs existing from logs watched**, and capability to react from a real team.
5. **Use detection drills** to set confidence.
6. **Modulate the risk score** by detectability, time-to-damage, and response.

Estimated effort: moderate; the data exists, and the work is defining the scales and wiring them into the assessment.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a replacement for the capability map.** It modulates it.
- **Not theoretical.** It uses data that already exists.
- **Not real-time everywhere.** Latency is itself a measured vector.
- **Not a one-time score.** It is re-mapped when the system changes.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: observability is often outside our control.** SaaS and third parties may give no logs. Mitigation: that is itself the lowest capture score, and it is recorded honestly.

**Tension 2: a one-off mapping goes stale.** Systems change. Mitigation: re-map on change; the score is a property, not a constant.

**Tension 3: unwatched monitoring looks good on paper.** Maturity can be overstated. Mitigation: the detection-confidence vector requires drills, not claims.
