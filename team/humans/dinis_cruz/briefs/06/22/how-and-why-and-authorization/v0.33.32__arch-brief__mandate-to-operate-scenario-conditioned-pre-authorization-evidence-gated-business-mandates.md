# Mandate To Operate: Scenario-Conditioned, Evidence-Gated Mandates From The Business

**version** v0.33.32
**date** 22 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Architect, Security, Strategy, @Dev
**type** Arch brief (in the series)

---

## What This Is

A named pattern from incident-response practice, applied to agents: **the mandate to operate, or operation mandate, is where the business grants a real-time, scenario-conditioned mandate to act, I am happy for you to block these accounts, apply this discount, delete this database, do this deployment, spend this amount, given this situation and this evidence; it rides on the potential-mandate mechanism, a central real-time environment holds the privileges and requires evidence before allowing the action, but it adds the business-context-conditioned grant and a real-world precedent; the enforcement gap is the same capability-versus-mandate problem, a mandate to delete ten thousand records is worthless if the token can delete a million, so enforcement needs the digital-twin execution layer and a substantial re-architecture, which makes it a later, more mature stage rather than where to start; the first-generation mode is coarse, the mandate equals the whole permission, and the mature mode adds granularity, business context, and evidence at the moment of action.** It is the separate topic flagged in the how-not-why brief, related to the potential mandate but distinct enough to stand alone (cross-ref: the v0.33.32 potential-mandate, how-not-why, and observability briefs, and the v0.33.31 authorization-ontology brief). New contributions: **the mandate-to-operate pattern, its incident-response precedent, and the coarse-to-granular maturity path.**

## The Framing

Where it comes from: **a CISO practice, transferred to agents.**

The project lead: **"this is what I called the operation mandate, or mandate to operate. In the past, as a CISO, as our incidents evolved, we started to talk to the business to give us mandates to make certain decisions close to real time during an incident. This applies to agents too, because the model is similar."** So the pattern is borrowed from incident response, where responders are granted bounded authority to act quickly under pressure, and applied to the agent case. The name echoes the established authority-to-operate idea from security governance, but here it means a real-time, bounded operational decision, not a one-time system accreditation.

## The Pattern

What a mandate to operate is: **a bounded, conditioned grant to act.**

The project lead: **"in this particular situation, in this particular sequence of events or evidence, we can grant a permission. The business says, I am happy for you to block ten thousand accounts, to apply this discount, to delete the database, to do a deployment, to access these resources, to make these network calls, to spend this amount."** So it is a grant that is conditioned on a scenario and a set of evidence, and bounded by an explicit limit, an amount, a count, a named action, rather than an open-ended permission.

## What It Needs To Work

The infrastructure: **a central real-time environment that checks evidence first.**

The project lead: **"for this to work you would need a central, real-time environment that holds those privileges but receives the evidence before allowing something to happen."** So the grant is not handed over in advance; it is held centrally and released at the moment of action, after the actor presents the evidence the scenario requires (cross-ref: the potential-mandate mechanism).

## The Enforcement Gap

Why holding the grant is not enough: **the mandate must be narrower than the capability, and enforced.**

The project lead: **"it depends on the architecture, because you also have to enforce it, or it is still a hope-based decision. It is a bit like saying you are authorised to delete ten thousand records, but then you give the agent a token that can delete a hundred thousand or a million. For this to work you would need the digital-twin execution layer, but that requires re-architecting the existing systems."** So a mandate to operate that grants ten thousand but is backed by a token that can do a million is hope-based: the authorization is narrower than the capability, and without something enforcing the narrower bound, the wider capability is what actually holds. Enforcement needs the digital-twin execution layer from the potential-mandate brief, and that is a significant re-architecture (cross-ref: the authorization-ontology brief on capability versus control, and the potential-mandate brief on synthetic execution).

## Maturity: Coarse To Granular

The progression: **from the mandate equals the permission, to business-context conditions.**

The project lead: **"what we are doing first is saying the mandate to operate is everything the permission wants to do. A more mature mode goes into granularity, into business context, you start to say, in this situation, this is possible."** So the first generation is coarse, the mandate equals the whole permission, and maturity adds the scenario and business-context conditions that make the grant specific.

## The Evidence Requirement

Where the why lives: **the actor must provide evidence at the moment.**

The project lead: **"what I like about this is that it requires the entity that wants to make the action to provide the evidence at that moment, which is where you can have all the provenance for why you want to do something. That introduces a huge amount of security controls, but it requires a lot of code changes."** So the mandate to operate is where the why is captured: the actor states and evidences why it should act, which is exactly the why thread the how-not-why brief deferred, now given a home (cross-ref: the how-not-why brief).

## Relationship To The Potential Mandate

How they differ: **the mechanism versus the pattern.**

The potential mandate is the mechanism: splitting the right to ask from the means to act, and mediating at the moment of use. The mandate to operate is the business-facing pattern that uses it: a scenario-conditioned, evidence-gated, bounded grant from the business, with a real-world precedent in incident response. One is how the plumbing works; the other is how the business expresses and bounds what it will allow.

## Where It Sits

The honest placement: **a powerful side concept, and a later stage.**

The project lead: **"this is a powerful side concept, but it requires a complete re-architecture of the existing systems, so it is the next level, for a more mature organisation. I would not start there."** So the mandate to operate is on the roadmap, not in the first generation: the first generation stays coarse and out of band, and the mandate to operate arrives with the digital-twin enforcement layer and the cooperating, evidence-providing code.

## What This Asks For

1. **Record the mandate-to-operate pattern** as a named, later-stage concept.
2. **Define the scenario and evidence conditions** that gate a grant.
3. **Bound each mandate explicitly**, by amount, count, or named action.
4. **Require evidence at the moment of action**, capturing the why.
5. **Enforce the bound** with the digital-twin execution layer, not hope.
6. **Place it on the maturity path**, after the coarse first generation.

Estimated effort: significant; it depends on the digital-twin enforcement layer and on cooperating code, and is a later stage.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not the first generation.** That stays coarse and out of band.
- **Not a one-time accreditation.** It is a real-time, bounded operational grant.
- **Not safe without enforcement.** An unenforced bound is hope-based.
- **Not the mechanism.** That is the potential mandate; this is the pattern.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: it needs a full re-architecture.** Cooperating code and digital-twin enforcement are heavy. Mitigation: place it later, for mature organisations.

**Tension 2: an unenforced mandate is theatre.** Granting ten thousand while the token allows a million changes nothing. Mitigation: enforcement is the point; without it, do not claim the bound.

**Tension 3: capturing the why is costly.** The evidence step needs a lot of code changes. Mitigation: it is where the strongest controls live, so it earns its place at maturity.
