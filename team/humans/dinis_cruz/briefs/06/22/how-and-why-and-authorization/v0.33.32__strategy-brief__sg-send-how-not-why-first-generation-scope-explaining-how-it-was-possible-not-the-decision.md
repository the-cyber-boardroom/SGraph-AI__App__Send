# How, Not Why: The First-Generation Scope Of The Solution

**version** v0.33.32
**date** 22 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Architect, Product, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief (positioning)

---

## What This Is

The boundary of the first-generation solution, stated plainly: **it is not about prevention, not about making decisions on privileges that have already been granted, which many startups already do, and not about explaining why something was done, the decision tree and the reasoning, which is a hard, data-and-provenance-heavy problem that others are chasing and that, ironically, needs the capability map we build first; it is about explaining how it was possible, how an agent was able to delete the database or take an action, by capturing the authorization moment, the privileges assigned or potentially assigned to an agent, and so bounding the why by the how and the capabilities; the why is a separate thread, including a future mode where an agent states what it wants to do and why and is granted permission and tokens in real time, and a forthcoming Mandate to Operate memo.** It scopes the solution so it stays shippable and factual (cross-ref: the v0.33.31 moment-of-authorisation and authorization-ontology briefs, the v0.33.32 potential-mandate brief, and the v0.33.40 inefficiency-of-exploitation brief). New contributions: **the how-not-why boundary, the competitive-foundation point, and the why-as-separate-thread scoping.**

## The Framing

What this clarifies: **what the solution is and is not, in its first generation.**

The project lead: **"this is a very important concept. This entire solution, in its first generation, is not about preventing, and not about making decisions on privileges that have been granted. There are a lot of startups already doing that."** So the first move is to say what the solution does not attempt.

## What It Is Not

The three exclusions: **not prevention, not deciding, not why.**

The project lead: **"this is not trying to explain why things were done. Our solution does not try to explain why something was done, the decision tree that led to it, because that is a very complicated question that requires a lot of data and a lot of context, with a lot of people trying to do it, and ironically they need what we created first in order to do it. There is a whole raft of evidence and provenance that has to be done there."** So explaining the reasoning behind an action is explicitly out of scope, both because it is hard and because it depends on the foundation this solution provides.

## What It Is

The single question it answers: **how was it possible.**

The project lead: **"this is about explaining how it was possible. How is it possible that the agent deleted the database? How was it possible that the agent did this? It is about creating the environment where we start to limit the why things are done by the how, and by the capabilities they have to execute. It is about capturing the authorization mode and the decision that happens when somebody physically assigns, or potentially assigns, the privileges to a particular agent."** So the solution captures the moment privileges are granted and maps what those privileges make possible, answering how an action could occur, not why it was chosen (cross-ref: the moment-of-authorisation and potential-mandate briefs).

## The Competitive Point

Why this ordering matters: **the why depends on the how.**

Others are working on explaining why and on deciding about already-granted privileges, and that work is valuable, but it rests on knowing what was possible and what was granted in the first place. By mapping the how and the authorization moment, the solution builds the foundation that the why-and-decision layer needs, which is a stronger position than competing directly in a crowded decision-making space (cross-ref: the rising-tide and go-to-market briefs).

## The Why Is A Separate Thread

What is deferred, deliberately: **two distinct future topics.**

The project lead: **"there is a separate, complete thread about capturing the decisions, the whys. What would be interesting is a mode where an agent describes what it wants to do and why, and then gets given the permission and the tokens in real time. That is a different story. In fact, I will do a separate memo on Mandate to Operate, something we used in the past that is very relevant here, and that starts to touch on capturing the whys, but it is a different topic."** So two things are parked: the real-time describe-then-grant mode, which is the evidence step of the potential mandate, and a Mandate to Operate memo to come. Both belong to the why thread, kept separate from this first generation.

## Why The Boundary Matters

The discipline this buys: **shippable and honest.**

Holding the first generation to how, not why, keeps it bounded, factual, and shippable: it does not over-promise prevention or reasoning, it maps what is possible from data that exists, and it leaves the harder, context-heavy why work to a later layer that will stand on this one. The boundary is a feature, not a limitation.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not prevention.** It does not block; it maps how.
- **Not a decision engine on granted privileges.** That space is crowded.
- **Not an explanation of why.** That is a separate, later thread.
- **Not dependent on context it cannot get.** It uses the authorization moment and capabilities.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: not preventing can sound weak.** Competitors claim prevention. Mitigation: mapping how it was possible is the honest, foundational layer they depend on, and it does not over-promise.

**Tension 2: the why is where some buyers look first.** Decision explanation is attractive. Mitigation: position the how as the prerequisite, and the why as the next layer on top of it.

**Tension 3: the line between how and why can blur.** The real-time describe-then-grant mode touches both. Mitigation: keep it in the why thread and the potential-mandate work, not the first generation.
