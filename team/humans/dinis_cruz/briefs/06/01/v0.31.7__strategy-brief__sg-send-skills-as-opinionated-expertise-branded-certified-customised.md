## What This Is

A refinement of the skills marketplace and skill-vault work (cross-ref: the v0.31.7 skill-base-vaults-creator-economy and skills-marketplace briefs), prompted by conference feedback. Much of this is already captured in those briefs; this one records what is new or sharper: the framing of a skill as **a subject-matter expert's opinionated way of thinking**, and a crisper breakdown of what a creator actually sells, the **branded, certified, and customised versions** of a skill, with the customised version carrying the intellectual property.

The project lead was explicit that the bulk is already documented, so this brief deliberately points to the existing briefs rather than repeating them, and concentrates on the genuinely additive points.

## Signal: Conference Validation

Worth recording as a signal: **the idea was validated at a conference today.** The project lead: **"one of the feedbacks I got today at the conference is how it's a powerful workflow and solution for us, and the vaults are perfectly aligned with that, perfect for capturing skills, version-controlling skills, and customising and selling skills."** This is external, real-world validation of the skill-vault and marketplace direction (cross-ref: the base-vault and marketplace briefs): practitioners at a conference recognised the workflow as powerful and the vaults as well-aligned for capturing, versioning, customising, and selling skills. It strengthens the case for prioritising this line of work, and it confirms the three capabilities the vault brings to skills (capture, version control, customise-and-sell) resonate with the intended audience.

## The Sharper Framing: A Skill Is An Expert's Opinionated Way Of Thinking

The most useful refinement: **a skill is how a subject-matter expert packages their knowledge and their way of thinking, an opinionated view, not just a capability.**

The project lead: **"the key idea is an easy way for a subject-matter expert that has a particular skill or opinionated view about something to package their knowledge and their way of thinking into a skill."** This sharpens what the base vault captures (cross-ref: the base-vault brief): not merely a task capability, but an expert's *perspective*, their opinionated way of approaching something. Two implications:

- **The value is the opinion, not just the function.** What makes an expert's skill worth buying is their particular way of thinking about a problem, their judgment, their opinionated approach. The skill packages that, which is why the creator's brand and reputation matter (cross-ref: the base-vault brief's creator-economy thesis): you are buying *this expert's* way of thinking.
- **Skills are multi-dimensional and context-dependent.** The project lead: **"you don't just have one skill, you have a customised version of skills; the skill has to have context; it has a kind of multiple-dimension thing."** A skill is not a single flat artefact; it has context and dimensions, which is exactly why customised versions exist (below) and why the semantic graph and the rich base-vault contents matter (cross-ref: the base-vault and skills-graph briefs). An expert's opinionated skill, applied in a specific context, becomes a customised version.

This framing is a useful addition to the base-vault brief: the base vault captures an expert's opinionated way of thinking, with all the supporting assets that give it credibility, and the customisation applies that way of thinking to a context.

## The Base Vault Recap (Already Documented)

The project lead re-listed the base vault's contents, which are already documented in the base-vault brief (cross-ref) and not repeated in full here: the main skill text, code samples (probably in multiple languages), guidance documents, background information, evidence, credibility, and certifications, all the assets that give the skill credibility, then published. This matches the base-vault brief's contents table exactly; the one emphasis worth carrying is **credibility**: the project lead stressed evidence, credibility, and certifications as the assets that make the skill believable, which reinforces the base-vault brief's provenance-and-explainability point (cross-ref) and the marketplace's provenance-grounded scoring (cross-ref: the marketplace brief). The base vault is the credible, evidence-backed expression of the expert's opinionated skill.

## The Sharper Breakdown: Branded, Certified, And Customised Versions

The genuinely additive point on the commercial side: **a creator sells not one thing but distinct versions, branded, certified, and customised, with the customised version being the IP-bearing product.**

The base-vault brief framed this as base-then-customised; this memo sharpens it into three sellable forms:

| Version | What It Is | Sold? |
|---------|------------|-------|
| **The base vault** | The creator's canonical, open, credible skill (the foundation) | The open foundation (mostly open/CC; cross-ref: the base-vault brief) |
| **The branded version** | The skill carrying the creator's brand | Sold |
| **The certified version** | The skill certified (by the creator) | Sold |
| **The customised version** | Developed, certified, and maintained by the creator for a context, carrying their brand, ideas, and logic | Sold; the IP-bearing product |

The project lead: **"you have the fundamental first repo-like structure or vault that is used to create the customised version, which is what you sell. You can sell the branded version and the certified version, but more interesting is the customised version, the one developed, certified, and maintained by the creator, taking into account their brand, their ideas, their logic. That is fundamentally what you sell, you maintain it, and that is the one that has the intellectual property element."**

So the model is: the base vault is the open foundation; the branded and certified versions are sellable forms that carry the creator's brand and certification; and the **customised version is the prime product**, the one the creator develops, certifies, and maintains, embodying their brand, ideas, and logic, and carrying the intellectual property. This is sharper than "base-then-customised": it names the branded and certified versions as distinct offerings, and it locates the IP specifically in the maintained, customised version. The creator is selling their opinionated way of thinking, applied and maintained for a context, under their brand and certification, that is the IP.

This also clarifies the open-versus-commercial boundary (cross-ref: the base-vault brief's licensing flexibility): the base vault and the skill idea can be open and shared, while the branded, certified, and especially the customised-and-maintained versions are the commercial products. The IP is not the idea (which is open); it is the creator's certified, maintained, opinionated, context-applied version.

## The Marketplace (Already Documented)

The project lead reiterated the need for a marketplace with easy management and maintenance: **"we need a marketplace for this, an easy way to manage it, an easy way to maintain it."** This is already documented in the marketplace brief (cross-ref: the v0.31.7 skills-marketplace brief), including the trustworthy, provenance-grounded scoring, the community feedback, and the distribution. The one emphasis this memo adds is **maintenance**: the customised version is something the creator *maintains* (it is a living, maintained product, not a one-off sale), so the marketplace must make ongoing maintenance easy, and the version control and provenance (cross-ref: the base-vault and marketplace briefs) support that. The buyer is buying a maintained relationship with the creator's evolving, certified version, not just a static download.

4. **Make maintenance easy and ongoing** (the customised version is a maintained product; version control and provenance support it).
5. **Keep the base vault open** (the idea and foundation are open; the branded/certified/customised versions are the commercial products).

7. **Use the conference validation** (a real signal; prioritise this line).

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a restatement of the base-vault or marketplace briefs.** It records only what is new or sharper.
- **Not a skill as a flat capability.** A skill is an expert's opinionated, multi-dimensional, context-dependent way of thinking.
- **Not a one-off sale.** The customised version is maintained; the buyer gets an ongoing relationship.
- **Not closing the foundation.** The base vault and the idea stay open; the branded/certified/customised versions are commercial.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Distinguishing branded vs certified vs customised could confuse.** Three sellable forms plus the open base could be unclear. Mitigation: define each clearly (the table above); the customised version is the prime product; the others are simpler offerings.

**Risk 2: The IP boundary (open idea vs sold version) must be clear.** Selling a version of an open skill needs a clear line. Mitigation: the base/idea is open; the certified, maintained, customised version is the IP; the licensing flexibility (cross-ref: the base-vault brief) expresses it.

**Risk 3: Maintenance is an ongoing commitment.** A maintained customised version is ongoing work. Mitigation: the version control and provenance support it; the marketplace makes maintenance easy; the maintained relationship is part of the value (and the price).

**Risk 4: Opinionated skills carry the creator's bias.** An opinionated way of thinking is, by definition, a point of view. Mitigation: that is the value (you are buying this expert's view); credibility, evidence, and certification (cross-ref: the base-vault brief) ground it; the buyer chooses the expert whose view they want.
