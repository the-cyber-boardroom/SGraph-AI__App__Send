## What This Is

A research brief on HeyGen (heygen.com): what it is, how SG/Send could use and integrate with it, and how it could help businesses be created around it. It is one of a set of partner-integration briefs aimed at producing library pages and example vaults.

The short version: **HeyGen is an AI avatar video generation platform with a developer API (and MCP and Skills integration paths), turning a script or a prompt into a finished, multilingual, lifelike avatar video.** For SG/Vault, it is a new consumption and communication layer: the vault holds the content, the evidence, the assessment, the debrief, and the artefacts (cross-ref: the visualisation, assessment, and library briefs), and HeyGen turns chosen pieces of that into video, a powerful complement to the text, graphs, and infographics the platform already produces.

## What HeyGen Is

The research picture, current as of this brief:

- HeyGen is an AI video generation platform that turns written scripts (or a single prompt) into finished videos with lifelike digital avatars, supporting a very large number of avatars and languages (reported as 230+ avatars and 140 to 175+ languages), with realistic lip-sync, gestures, and micro-expressions.
- It offers a Video Agent (prompt-to-video: from one prompt it orchestrates script writing, avatar creation, visual assets, voiceover, and layout into a finished, editable video), alongside a Studio editor for granular control and template-based generation.
- It has a developer API (reported as v3, REST/JSON, API-key and OAuth auth, webhooks), covering avatar video generation, text-to-speech, video translation/dubbing (preserving lip-sync), and lipsync, with pay-as-you-go pricing starting around $5.
- Notably, it offers multiple integration paths: MCP, Skills, and Direct API, each with its own authentication. It also supports instant/photo avatars (a digital twin from a short recording or a still) and requires consent for likeness-based avatars.

So HeyGen is an agent-friendly, API-and-MCP-and-Skills-accessible way to generate avatar video from text or a prompt, in many languages.

## Why This Matters To SG/Send

SG/Vault produces a lot of content that people consume: assessments and debriefs (cross-ref: the GDPR, consulting, and vulnerability briefs), evidence packs and CVs (cross-ref: the evidence-CV brief), graph artefacts (cross-ref: the vault-per-standard brief), and the library content (cross-ref: the library-as-shop-front brief). It already serves this content as text, graphs, and infographics (cross-ref: the visualisation and per-page-semantic-graphs briefs). HeyGen adds video as another consumption format: a chosen piece of vault content becomes a presented, spoken, multilingual avatar video.

The strategic alignment is strong on two counts. First, **multi-format consumption**: the platform's principle is to serve content in the form each consumer wants (cross-ref: the per-page-semantic-graphs brief's client-side vs edge-side, and the multi-audience briefs in the security-report and vulnerability demos); video is a high-value format for many audiences (executives, learners, partners), and HeyGen produces it. Second, **the integration paths match the platform's standards**: HeyGen offers MCP and Skills paths, which are exactly the standards SG/Vault is building on (cross-ref: the skills and Netlify-MCP briefs), so HeyGen could be a skill in the skills marketplace (cross-ref: the marketplace brief), a connector (cross-ref: the connector briefs), or an MCP integration.

## How SG/Send Could Use And Integrate With HeyGen

| Use | How |
|-----|-----|
| **Video briefs from a vault** | Turn an assessment, debrief, or audience brief (cross-ref: the assessment and vulnerability briefs) into a spoken avatar video |
| **Multilingual delivery** | HeyGen's translation/dubbing delivers a vault's content in many languages (cross-ref: the Vault App Store's multi-language point) |
| **Video CVs and pitches** | An evidence-CV (cross-ref: the evidence-CV brief) rendered as a personalised video presentation |
| **Library and case-study videos** | The library shop front (cross-ref: the library-as-shop-front and industry-pages briefs) with avatar-presented explainers |
| **HeyGen as a skill / connector / MCP** | HeyGen in the skills marketplace or as a connector (cross-ref: the skills, marketplace, and connector briefs), invoked from a vault |
| **Prompt-to-video from vault content** | The Video Agent turns a vault's content into a finished video from a prompt |

The cleanest integration is **HeyGen as a vault-invokable capability**: because HeyGen offers MCP and Skills paths, it fits the platform's existing integration model. A skill (cross-ref: the base-vault and skills briefs) or connector (cross-ref: the connector briefs) wraps HeyGen, so a vault can turn a chosen piece of its content into video on demand, with the script drawn from the vault's content (and anchored to its provenance; cross-ref: the evidence and vault-per-standard briefs). The vault holds the truth; HeyGen presents a chosen slice of it as video.

A note on provenance and trust, which is where SG/Vault adds value to the video: a HeyGen video generated from vault content can be anchored to that content's provenance, so the video is not an unmoored claim but a presentation of evidence-anchored material (cross-ref: the evidence-CV brief's anchoring and the consistency principle). This matters for AI-generated video specifically, where trust is a concern: a vault-backed video can point to its source.

## How It Helps Businesses Be Created Around It

The combination supports businesses that **turn knowledge into presented, multilingual video at scale**, backed by a provable record. Examples:

- **Assessment and consulting businesses** (cross-ref: the consulting brief) deliver a video debrief of an assessment alongside the assessment vault: the exec gets a spoken summary, the technical audience gets the detailed vault, both from one evidence base.
- **The creator economy** (cross-ref: the base-vault brief): a creator's base vault includes a video explainer (generated via HeyGen), making the skill more consumable and the creator more visible (the brand point); and a HeyGen-wrapping skill is itself a sellable base vault.
- **Training and communication** (HeyGen's core strength): a company turns its vault-held knowledge (procedures, compliance, onboarding) into multilingual avatar-led video, kept in sync with the vault as the source of truth.
- **The library as shop front** (cross-ref: the library-as-shop-front and industry-pages briefs): industry pages and case studies get avatar-presented video explainers, making the shop front more engaging.

In each, the pattern is the same: the vault is the provable, portable record; HeyGen turns chosen pieces into engaging, multilingual video; the video is anchored to the vault's provenance.

## What This Asks For (The MVP)

1. **Integrate HeyGen as a vault-invokable capability** (via Skills, MCP, or a connector; matching the platform's standards).
2. **Generate video from vault content** (assessments, debriefs, briefs, CVs, library content), with the script drawn from the vault.
3. **Anchor the video to provenance** (the video presents evidence-anchored vault content, not unmoored claims).
4. **Use multilingual delivery** (HeyGen's translation/dubbing for multi-language vault content).
5. **Add video to multi-audience delivery** (video as a format alongside text, graphs, infographics; the right format per audience).
6. **Make HeyGen a marketplace skill / connector** (a sellable, reusable HeyGen-wrapping base vault).
7. **Respect consent and likeness rules** (HeyGen requires consent for likeness avatars; honour it).
8. **Produce the library page and example vault** (the page explains the SG/Vault-plus-HeyGen model; an example vault that generates a video brief from its content is the MVP).

Estimated effort: low to moderate; HeyGen offers a REST API plus MCP and Skills paths and pay-as-you-go pricing from a low entry point, so integration is via a skill/connector/MCP wrapper. The new work is the vault-content-to-script flow, the provenance anchoring, and the example. The MVP is a vault that turns a chosen piece of its content into a HeyGen video.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a replacement for text/graphs/infographics.** Video is an additional consumption format, chosen per audience.
- **Not unmoored AI video.** The video is anchored to vault content and provenance.
- **Not ignoring consent.** Likeness avatars require consent; the platform honours it.
- **Not HeyGen-only.** HeyGen is one video capability; the vault and content are independent and portable.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: AI-generated video raises trust and authenticity concerns.** Avatar video can mislead or be misused. Mitigation: anchor the video to vault provenance (it presents sourced content); honour consent for likeness; be transparent that it is AI-generated; the vault is the verifiable record behind it.

**Risk 2: Consent and likeness rules.** Generating avatars of real people needs consent. Mitigation: HeyGen requires verbal consent for likeness; the platform honours it; use stock or consented avatars.

**Risk 3: Cost at scale.** Video generation costs per video. Mitigation: pay-as-you-go from a low entry; the budget discipline (cross-ref: the visualisation brief); generate video for high-value pieces, not everything.

**Risk 4: Dependence on a third-party video service.** Relying on HeyGen. Mitigation: HeyGen is one capability via a standard path (Skills/MCP/connector); the vault and content are independent; other video services could be wrapped similarly.
