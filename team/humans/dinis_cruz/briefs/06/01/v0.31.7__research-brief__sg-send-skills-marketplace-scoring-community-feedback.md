## What This Is

A research-and-mapping brief on building a **skills marketplace with skills scoring and community/usage feedback, on top of SG/Vault.** It builds on the base-vault creator economy (cross-ref: the v0.31.7 skill-base-vaults brief), the skills graph (cross-ref: the v0.31.7 semantic-knowledge-graphs-for-agentic-skills brief), and the Vault App Store (cross-ref: the v0.31.5 Vault App Store brief). It is research-led, because the agent-skills marketplace landscape is now mature and instructive, and the research points directly at where SG/Vault can do something the existing marketplaces structurally cannot: **trustworthy scoring grounded in cryptographic provenance, evals, and an auditable graph, rather than gameable popularity.**

The aim, as with the other integration briefs, is a library page and an example vault: a worked MVP of a skills marketplace where scoring and community feedback sit on the vault's provenance and version control.

## The Landscape: Eight Marketplaces, And A Trust Problem

The research shows a fast-grown, crowded landscape. By Q2 2026 there are around eight significant agent-skills marketplaces (the ecosystem went from one registry in late 2025 to eight major marketplaces), differing mainly in curation and security model:

| Curation Model | Examples (As Reported) | Trade-off |
|----------------|------------------------|-----------|
| **Editorial / curated review** | Curated marketplaces with editorial review and automated security scans | Higher trust, smaller catalogue |
| **Community-run, no central gatekeeper** | Large community registries and hubs | Huge catalogue, minimal curation |
| **Publish-instantly, post-hoc** | Index-everything catalogues (hundreds of thousands of skills scraped from public repos) | Maximum reach, audit-it-yourself |

The reported scoring signals today are mostly **GitHub stars, install counts, update cadence, and security scans**. Notably, the research says update cadence (active maintenance) ranks skills higher in most storefronts than star ratings, and that multi-marketplace publishing is the winning distribution strategy. So discovery, trust, and creator economics are the three things marketplaces are competing on, and most lean on popularity-style signals for scoring.

## The Core Finding: Popularity Signals Are Gameable; Provenance Is Not

The most important research finding for SG/Vault, and the one that defines the opportunity: **in ecosystems without cryptographic provenance verification, popularity signals are trivially gameable and cannot be relied upon as quality indicators**, and ranking manipulation amplifies the reach of malicious skills by exploiting the trust heuristic users apply to highly-ranked packages.

This is not hypothetical. The security research on agent skills is stark:

- A large-scale empirical study scanned 42,447 skills and found 26.1% contained at least one security vulnerability, spanning patterns across prompt injection, data exfiltration, privilege escalation, and supply-chain risks.
- A coordinated supply-chain campaign in early 2026 compromised over a thousand skills in a major community marketplace, roughly one in five packages, delivering a credential-theft payload.
- The barrier to publishing a malicious skill is described as a SKILL.md file and a one-week-old account, and skill-based prompt injection is a qualitatively harder attack class because skill files are composed entirely of instructions with no data-to-instruction boundary.

The forward-looking direction the research points to is exactly SG/Vault's territory: future systems should incorporate **reputation scoring based on behaviour history, community trust signals, and runtime behavioural analysis**, alongside **cryptographic provenance** and end-to-end **provenance tracking** of instruction sources, because signatures prove provenance (where it came from) even if not intent.

So the opportunity is clear: **a skills marketplace whose scoring is grounded in cryptographic provenance, evals, security analysis, and an auditable graph, not gameable stars and install counts.** SG/Vault is structurally suited to this, because the vault provides provenance, version control, security materials, and the graph natively.

## Why SG/Vault Can Do Scoring Differently

The mapping that defines the marketplace: each weakness of today's popularity-based scoring is something the vault addresses directly.

| Today's Weak Signal | The SG/Vault-Grounded Signal |
|---------------------|------------------------------|
| GitHub stars (gameable) | Cryptographic provenance: who authored it, from what source, version-controlled (cross-ref: the commit-queue brief) |
| Install count (gameable) | Verifiable usage from the vault's own access (real usage metrics; cross-ref: the industry-pages metrics) |
| "It has a security scan" (point-in-time) | The base vault's own security materials (threat models) plus the store's security analysis (cross-ref: the base-vault and store briefs) |
| Update cadence (a proxy) | The actual version history and provenance of changes |
| A star rating (opaque) | Tests and evals included in the base vault, run and recorded (cross-ref: the base-vault brief) |
| "It's popular" (heuristic) | The auditable semantic graph: what the skill is, what it relates to, what it conflicts with (cross-ref: the skills-graph brief) |

So a skill's score in this marketplace is not "how many stars," it is a composite of verifiable signals the vault makes possible: provenance (is it from a real, reputed creator, version-controlled?), evals (does it pass its own tests?), security (does the threat model and security analysis check out?), behaviour (does it do only what it claims, per the graph and any runtime signals?), and real usage (genuine access, not gameable installs). This is precisely the reputation-plus-provenance-plus-behaviour direction the research recommends, and the vault is the substrate that makes it real.

## The Scoring Model

A concrete scoring model for the marketplace, composed of vault-grounded dimensions:

| Dimension | What It Measures | Vault Source |
|-----------|------------------|--------------|
| **Provenance score** | Verifiable origin and authorship; version history | The vault's cryptographic provenance and version control |
| **Eval score** | Does the skill pass its included tests and evals? | The tests/evals in the base vault, run and recorded |
| **Security score** | Threat model, security analysis, known-bad patterns | The base vault's security materials plus the store's analysis (cross-ref: the store brief) |
| **Behaviour / safety score** | Does it do only what it claims? Conflict edges? | The skills graph and any runtime behavioural signals (cross-ref: the skills-graph safety surface) |
| **Usage score** | Genuine, verifiable usage | The vault's own access metrics (not gameable install counts) |
| **Community feedback score** | Reviews, ratings, endorsements, references | Community feedback harvested into the vault (below) |
| **Reputation score** | The creator's track record across their skills | Provenance-grounded, portable reputation (cross-ref: the base-vault brief) |

The composite makes the score hard to game: a creator cannot fake provenance, cannot fake passing evals, cannot hide a failed security analysis, and cannot fabricate genuine usage. Popularity is one input among many, and the manipulable ones are outweighed by the verifiable ones. This is the marketplace's core differentiator.

## Community And Usage Feedback (On The Vault)

The community/usage feedback layer reuses a mechanism already designed: **feedback harvesting via shared sub-vaults** (cross-ref: the v0.31.5 evidence-CV brief's feedback-harvesting, where a creator shares a read-only or read-write sub-vault and collects opinions, quotes, and references with controlled access and provenance).

| Feedback Type | How It Works |
|---------------|--------------|
| **Reviews and ratings** | Users submit feedback into the marketplace, recorded with provenance (who, when) |
| **Endorsements and references** | Reputed users or organisations endorse a skill (harvested via sub-vaults; cross-ref: the evidence-CV brief) |
| **Usage feedback** | Real usage signals from the vault's access (genuine, not gameable) |
| **Issue and improvement feedback** | Users report issues or request improvements; the creator iterates (version-controlled) |

Because feedback is recorded with provenance and version control (cross-ref: the commit-queue brief), it is harder to fake and easier to audit than anonymous star ratings: a review traces to a real, identified source (cross-ref: the no-anonymous-users/fingerprint-identity stance from the v0.27.60 authentication and SG/Sentinel briefs), endorsements come from reputed parties, and usage is verifiable. This is community feedback that means something, because the vault grounds it in provenance. And the feedback loops back: it feeds the community-feedback dimension of the score, and the creator iterates on the base vault in response (cross-ref: the base-vault brief), with the version history showing the improvement.

## How The Marketplace Sits On SG/Vault

The marketplace is the synthesis of several existing threads, not a new system:

| Marketplace Function | Built On |
|----------------------|----------|
| **Listing and distribution** | The Vault App Store (cross-ref: the store brief) |
| **The skills themselves** | Base vaults (cross-ref: the base-vault brief) |
| **Discovery and relationships** | The skills graph (cross-ref: the skills-graph brief) |
| **Scoring** | The vault's provenance, evals, security materials, usage metrics |
| **Community feedback** | Feedback harvesting via sub-vaults (cross-ref: the evidence-CV brief) |
| **Trust and safety** | Security materials, the store's analysis, the graph's safety surface |
| **Creator economics** | The base-to-customised commercial model (cross-ref: the base-vault brief) |
| **Reputation** | Provenance-grounded, portable creator reputation (cross-ref: the base-vault brief) |

So the skills marketplace is the Vault App Store plus base vaults plus the skills graph plus vault-grounded scoring plus sub-vault feedback. The new work is the scoring model (composing the vault's signals into a score) and the community-feedback surface; almost everything else is reuse.

It also fits the multi-marketplace reality the research describes: because skills are an open standard and a base vault is a self-contained zip (cross-ref: the base-vault and store briefs), a creator can publish to the SG/Vault marketplace and others, and the SG/Vault differentiator is the trustworthy, provenance-grounded scoring and the rich base vault, not lock-in.

## A Note On Skill Safety (The Marketplace As A Defence)

The security research makes safety the central marketplace concern: skills execute locally with user privileges and are composed of instructions with no data-to-instruction boundary, so a malicious skill is a serious supply-chain risk, and the absence of mandatory security review in many marketplaces makes large-scale supply-chain attacks easy. The SG/Vault marketplace is, by design, a defence against this:

- **Provenance over popularity** (a malicious skill cannot fake a reputed, version-controlled origin).
- **Security materials and analysis** (threat models in the base vault, the store's security analysis, known-bad conflict edges in the graph).
- **Verifiable usage and behaviour** (genuine usage and behavioural signals, not gameable install counts).
- **Identified feedback** (reviews from real, identified sources, not anonymous ranking manipulation).

This directly addresses the research's warning that popularity signals are gameable and amplify malicious skills: by grounding the score in provenance and verifiable signals, the marketplace removes the trust heuristic attackers exploit. Safety is not a feature bolted on; it is the consequence of scoring on the vault's provenance.

## What This Asks For (The MVP)

1. **Build the skills marketplace on the Vault App Store** (listing, distribution, billing; cross-ref: the store brief), with base vaults as the listed skills (cross-ref: the base-vault brief).
2. **Implement the vault-grounded scoring model** (provenance, eval, security, behaviour/safety, usage, community-feedback, reputation dimensions, composed into a score).
3. **Ground scoring in the vault, not popularity** (cryptographic provenance, version control, evals, security materials, genuine usage; popularity is one input among many).
4. **Build the community/usage feedback layer** (reviews, endorsements, references, usage, issues, harvested via sub-vaults with provenance; cross-ref: the evidence-CV brief).
5. **Use the skills graph for discovery and safety** (relationships, conflict edges; cross-ref: the skills-graph brief).
6. **Make trust and safety the core proposition** (provenance over popularity; security materials and analysis; the marketplace as a supply-chain defence).
7. **Support multi-marketplace publishing** (open standard, self-contained base-vault zip; the differentiator is trustworthy scoring, not lock-in).
8. **Make reputation portable and provenance-grounded** (the creator's track record across their skills; cross-ref: the base-vault brief).
9. **Produce the library page and example marketplace vault** (the page explains the trustworthy-scoring proposition; an example with a few scored skills and feedback is the MVP).

Estimated effort: moderate as a synthesis, because the foundations exist (the store, base vaults, the skills graph, provenance and version control, sub-vault feedback harvesting, usage metrics). The new work is the composite scoring model, the community-feedback surface, and presenting the trust signals. The MVP is a small marketplace of scored base vaults with working feedback.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a popularity contest.** Scoring is grounded in provenance, evals, security, and verifiable usage; stars are one minor input.
- **Not an unvetted catalogue.** Trust and safety (provenance, security materials, the graph) are the core.
- **Not anonymous feedback.** Feedback is provenance-grounded and from identified sources.
- **Not lock-in.** Open standard, portable base vaults; multi-marketplace publishing supported.
- **Not a new system.** It is the store plus base vaults plus the graph plus vault-grounded scoring plus sub-vault feedback.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Skill supply-chain attacks are real and serious.** The research documents large-scale compromises. Mitigation: the marketplace is designed as a defence (provenance over popularity, security materials and analysis, conflict edges, verifiable usage); mandatory security analysis on listing; sandboxing (cross-ref: the VIV no-origin/broker model).

**Risk 2: Composite scoring is complex and must be transparent.** A composite score can be opaque or itself gamed. Mitigation: make the dimensions explicit and explainable (the vault's provenance makes each traceable); weight verifiable signals over manipulable ones; show why a skill scored as it did.

**Risk 3: Provenance-grounded reputation needs real identity.** Trustworthy feedback needs identified sources. Mitigation: the no-anonymous-users/fingerprint-identity stance (cross-ref: the authentication and SG/Sentinel briefs); endorsements from reputed parties; provenance on every signal.

**Risk 4: Bootstrapping a marketplace is hard (cold start).** A marketplace needs creators and users. Mitigation: seed with the platform's own base vaults and demos; the trustworthy-scoring differentiator attracts quality creators; multi-marketplace publishing widens reach; the open base vaults draw users.

**Risk 5: Runtime behavioural analysis is non-trivial.** Behaviour scoring is harder than static checks. Mitigation: start with static signals (provenance, evals, security materials, the graph); add runtime behavioural signals over time (the research direction); the vault's auditability supports it.
