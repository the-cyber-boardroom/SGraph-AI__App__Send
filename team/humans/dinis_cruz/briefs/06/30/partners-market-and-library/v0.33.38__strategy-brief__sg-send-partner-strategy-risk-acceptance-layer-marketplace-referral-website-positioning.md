## What This Is

The partner strategy, written also as a brief for the agent building the Risk Mandate.ai website: **many companies are solving the hard problem of identifying what agents are doing, their behaviour, their decisions, and the control plane, with a lot of funding and energy, and Risk Mandate does not compete there; instead it sits on top as the layer that enables the business to figure out the risks, accept them, and find the stakeholders who own the permissions and the accountability, which is what actually drives the funding and energy to fix things; the strategy has two areas of operation, first, partner enablement, giving those companies' findings the depth, the graph, and the toxicity analysis they lack so they can make far better business cases to the business, while they keep the client relationship Risk Mandate does not want; and second, marketplace referral, because Risk Mandate is not in the business of remediation, going inline, or changing company systems, so when customers ask how to detect or remediate it points them to these partners; the open-source, no-lock-in nature of the technology makes it an easy integration point, and the document doubles as website material, a partner page that finds, explains, and demonstrates partnerships.** It is the first brief of 30 June (cross-ref: the v0.33.36 use-case-driven-shipping brief, the v0.33.35 risk-register brief, and the lethal-trifecta material). New contributions: **the two areas of operation, the risk-acceptance-layer positioning, and the website partner-page brief.**

## The Landscape: A Crowded, Well-Funded Detection Space

There is a large and growing field of companies working on the hard problem of seeing what agents do. The project lead: **"there are a lot of companies out there solving the problem of identifying what agents are doing, focused on the behaviour, the decisions, the control plane, and there is a lot of energy and funding there."** It is a genuinely difficult place to build, and it is well resourced. Risk Mandate does not compete in it. That is the first thing the website and every partner conversation should make clear: we are not another agent-detection or control-plane product.

## Where Risk Mandate Sits: The Risk-Acceptance Layer

Risk Mandate sits on top of detection, as the layer that turns findings into owned, accepted business risk. The project lead: **"all we are doing in Risk Mandate is enabling the business to figure out the risks, the risk acceptance, to find the stakeholders, the people who own the permissions and own the accountability for the actions the agents are doing."** And that ownership is not a side effect; it is the thing that unlocks action. The project lead: **"which is what will drive the funding and the energy on actually resolving the fixes."** Detection tells you what is happening; Risk Mandate makes the business own it, which is what gets the fixes funded.

## Area One: Partner Enablement, Better Business Cases

The first area of operation is helping the detection companies make the case for their own value. The project lead: **"we help those companies make much better business cases and show the value they are providing to the business."** Many already produce risk analysis, but not at the depth Risk Mandate brings. The project lead: **"a lot of them already have risk analysis, but not with the depth and the type of graph that we are creating."** Risk Mandate can take a partner's finding and show how dangerous it really is. The project lead: **"we can show how toxic some of those things are."** By adding the graph, the propagation, the stakeholders, and the toxicity, Risk Mandate lets a partner show the business not just that something was detected, but what it would cost and who owns it, which is a far stronger business case for the product the partner is selling. And the division of labour is clean, because the partner keeps the client relationship. The project lead: **"they already have relationships with the clients, which is not what we want to do."**

## Area Two: Marketplace Referral

The second area runs the other way. Risk Mandate's work will repeatedly raise questions it deliberately does not answer itself. The project lead: **"we are not in the business of remediation, we are not inline, we are not making changes to the company systems."** When a customer asks how to detect what agents are doing or how to remediate it, the answer is a partner, not a Risk Mandate product. The project lead: **"we can integrate with all of these companies, driving new business to them."** So Risk Mandate maintains a small, curated set of marketplace recommendations, and when customers ask how to solve the detection or remediation problem, it points them to the right partners. That referral flow drives new business to the partners and keeps Risk Mandate firmly out of a business it does not want to be in.

## Why the Partnership Works

The two areas combine into a relationship with synergy in both directions. Risk Mandate sends partners new business through referral, and it strengthens the business case for the products partners already sell by deepening their risk analysis. Partners bring the client relationships and the detection and remediation capabilities Risk Mandate does not want. The two are complementary rather than competitive, which is the foundation of a durable partnership.

## The Open-Source Advantage

A large part of why this is an easy partnership to enter is that the technology is open and carries no lock-in. The project lead: **"because our technology is open source, there is no lock-in, they can integrate it and add as much or as little as they want in their tech stack, which is a much easier integration point than a closed technology."** A partner can adopt as much or as little as suits them, integrate at their own pace, and never fear being trapped, which lowers the barrier to working together far below what a closed product would allow.

## For the Website Agent: The Partner Page

This document is meant for the agent building the Risk Mandate.ai website, and it should become a partner page. The project lead: **"this is the material that goes on the website to help find and explain potential partners, so when we talk to a partner we point them to a page where they can see it in action."** The page should explain the two areas of operation in plain language, make unmistakable that Risk Mandate complements rather than competes with detection products, and let a partner see the value in action. Concretely, the page should:

1. State the positioning: Risk Mandate is the risk-acceptance and accountability layer, not a detection or remediation product.
2. Explain Area One, partner enablement, with a worked example.
3. Explain Area Two, marketplace referral, with the recommendation format.
4. Show a demonstration a partner can be pointed to during a conversation.
5. Make the open-source, no-lock-in integration story explicit.

## Collateral and Examples

The worked example for Area One is the strongest piece of collateral: take a single detection finding a partner might produce, then show Risk Mandate adding the graph, the propagation, the toxicity, the stakeholders who own the permissions, and the accepted risk, ending in the business case the partner can take to the buyer. A second piece is the marketplace recommendation format, a simple, honest way of listing recommended partners by the problem they solve. A third is a short integration note showing how little is needed to plug Risk Mandate in, reinforcing the no-lock-in point. Having these ready means the positioning can go onto the website as soon as the page exists.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a detection or control-plane product.** Risk Mandate sits on top of those, it does not replace them.
- **Not a remediation business.** No inline operation, no changes to customer systems.
- **Not a competitor to partners.** The relationship is complementary by design.
- **Not the partner list yet.** Naming and ranking candidate partners is the next step.
