## What This Is

A follow-up to the partner strategy, on consulting and time-and-materials firms as a target partner segment: **they already have the customer relationships, they most likely already run agentic-risk projects, they have follow-up services, and they have a customer list to sell into, so rather than compete with them Risk Mandate should empower them; the open-source platform means they can integrate, use, and leverage it and grow the market for the risk-acceptance model; the site should carry a set of pages explaining the rationale, the rules of engagement, and the open-source nature, with templates to work from, and Risk Mandate should seek out firms that operate this way; and these firms are dual-role, they are also customers, because they want to use the technology, not maintain it, so once a deployment becomes mission-critical in production, Risk Mandate can offer a managed and customisation service, cheaper than doing it internally, on their own Risk Mandate environment rather than on any customer system, so the firm can focus on serving its customers.** It is the ninth and final brief of 30 June (cross-ref: the v0.33.38 partner-strategy, black-box, and library briefs). New contributions: **consulting firms as a segment, the dual partner-and-customer role, and the managed-service offering.**

## The Follow-Up: A Specific Partner Segment

This extends the partner strategy to a named segment. The project lead: **"this follows up from the memo on the business model for companies who provide AI services, who already have good solutions we can help define business and drive business to."** Where that brief described the general shape of partnering, this one names consulting and time-and-materials firms as a segment worth going after directly.

## Why Consulting Firms Are the Right Segment

Consulting firms already hold the assets Risk Mandate deliberately does not want to build. The project lead: **"one target audience is consulting companies that sell time and materials, who already have the relationships with the customers, most likely already have projects around agentic risk, have good follow-up services, and have a nice customer list they can sell to."** They own the client relationship, they are already engaged on agentic risk, they can follow up and deliver, and they have a book of customers to sell into. That is precisely the front end Risk Mandate wants a partner to provide.

## Empower Them With the Open-Source Platform

The stance is empowerment, not competition, and the open-source nature is what makes it easy. The project lead: **"we should be empowering them, and because our platform is open source, they can integrate it, use it, and leverage it, and help grow the market for the risk-acceptance model and the workflows we are describing."** A consulting firm can adopt the platform on its own terms, build its practice on it, and in doing so grow the very market Risk Mandate operates in.

## The Pages They Need

Like the broader partner strategy, this needs to live on the website. The project lead: **"we should have a whole set of pages that explain the rationale, the rules of engagement, and the open-source nature, and give them templates to work from, and then look for companies that operate like that."** The pages explain why to partner, how the relationship works, and what open source means for them, and the templates lower the effort of getting started, while Risk Mandate actively seeks out firms that operate this way.

## The Dual Role: Partner and Customer

These firms are not only a channel; they are also buyers. The project lead: **"from a customer point of view they are also great examples of companies that could pay us to customise their environment, because they want to be a user of this technology, they do not want to be maintaining it."** A consulting firm wants to use the platform to serve clients, not to become a platform-maintenance shop, which opens a second, paid relationship alongside the partnership.

## The Managed-Service Argument

The paid relationship rests on a simple truth about production software. The project lead: **"every time you create something that gets used in production it becomes a mission-critical piece of technology that needs to be maintained, so we should be able to offer that service they would be doing internally, cheaper for them, as a capex or an operational cost, so they can focus on what they do better, serving the customers."** Once a firm's Risk Mandate deployment is load-bearing, someone has to maintain and customise it, and Risk Mandate can do that more cheaply than the firm can internally. Two points keep this consistent with the rest of the model: the service is for the firm's own Risk Mandate environment, not for any customer's production systems, so the air-gap stance holds; and because the platform is open source, the firm could maintain it themselves, so this is value offered, not rent extracted through lock-in.

## A Website Page for the Partnership

Like the partner strategy, this becomes a concrete page. The project lead: **"this is another good example of a page that should go on the website, that we can use to understand, to sell to them, and to talk about these partnerships."** The page is both an explanation and a sales surface, the thing a consulting firm is pointed to when the partnership is discussed.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not competition with consultancies.** Risk Mandate empowers them, it does not replace them.
- **Not taking their customer.** The firm keeps the client relationship and the follow-up.
- **Not touching customer systems.** The managed service maintains the firm's Risk Mandate environment, not client production.
- **Not lock-in.** Open source means the service is value offered, not rent.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Partner versus customer | The same firm is a channel and a buyer; the two relationships must stay clear |
| Managed service versus air-gap | The service maintains our tech for them, never the customer's systems |
| Open source versus paid maintenance | They could self-maintain; the offer must be cheaper and better, not coercive |
| Empowerment versus dependence | Growing the market through partners must not quietly recreate lock-in |
