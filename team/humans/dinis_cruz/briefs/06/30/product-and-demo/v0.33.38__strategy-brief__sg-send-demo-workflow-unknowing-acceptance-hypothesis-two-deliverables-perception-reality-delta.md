## What This Is

A map of what the demo and the solution deliver to customers and partners, starting from a hypothesis: **customers are unknowingly accepting far more risk than their risk appetite would allow, and making the business consciously accept those risks triggers a cascade of actions that reduces them; the two measures of success follow directly, the delta between the customer's understanding and the reality uncovered, and the real reduction achieved; the workflow has two deliverables, first a smooth discovery process, ingesting or confirming data even anonymously, connecting it to Risk Mandate's risk taxonomies, ontologies, and examples, and populating an acceptance dashboard of red, green, and action points as risks are accepted, and second the stakeholder-facing outputs, incident triggers, remediation tasks, project plans, integrations, and evidence, with the same material repackaged by the model for many different audiences so each drives the right action; and throughout, Risk Mandate stays air-gapped, it does not touch the systems, it takes feeds in and ideally sends a feedback loop back to update the register and the stakeholders; and the value hypothesis is that moving the market to see this as information every company needs creates the demand Risk Mandate operates in.** It is the eighth brief of 30 June (cross-ref: the v0.33.38 black-box, risk-appetite, and partner-strategy briefs, and the v0.33.36 use-case-shipping brief). New contributions: **the unknowing-acceptance hypothesis, the two success measures, and the two-deliverable demo workflow.**

## The Core Hypothesis: Unknowing Over-Acceptance

The demo starts from a single testable claim. The project lead: **"the hypothesis is that customers are currently, unknowingly, accepting a lot more risk than their risk appetite makes them comfortable with."** They have already accepted the risk by deploying the systems; they simply have not seen it or owned it. And the intervention follows. The project lead: **"when you make the business accept those risks, that triggers a cascading set of actions that reduces the risk."** The act of conscious acceptance is what starts the reduction. (The project lead referred to this by a name that did not transcribe cleanly; the intended label should be confirmed.)

## The Two Measures of Success

The hypothesis gives Risk Mandate two clean measures. The project lead: **"two good measures of success are, one, the delta between their understanding of the risk and the reality we uncover, and two, the real reduction we are able to make in the business."** The first measures how much hidden risk the demo surfaces, the gap between perceived and actual exposure. The second measures how much of it gets reduced once it is owned. Together they capture the whole value: reveal the gap, then close it.

## Deliverable One: Discovery to an Acceptance Dashboard

The first deliverable takes a customer from nothing to a populated picture. The project lead: **"the app is initially a description and discovery process, are you using this, are you using that, what data can we ingest, and it is important that there is a way to do this anonymously, where the customer does not need to put their company name, they just confirm a description or a bunch of data points, or integrate a product we can get an export from."** Anonymity is deliberate: a customer can start without exposing who they are. Then the data meets the model. The project lead: **"we connect them with our risk taxonomies and risk ontologies and examples, and provide their understanding, and as they accept the risks the dashboard starts to populate, with red and green and action points."** The output is an acceptance dashboard that fills in as decisions are made. The project lead: **"the value is for companies to understand their risk thresholds, their buffers, and the spread that exists between perception and reality."**

## Deliverable Two: Stakeholder-Packaged Outputs

The second deliverable turns owned risk into action for many audiences. The project lead: **"the next deliverable is the creation of either an incident trigger, a task for remediation, a project plan, an integration, or evidence and translation to the multiple stakeholders."** These are the same outputs the black-box brief names, produced here for the demo. And the model does the packaging. The project lead: **"one of the powers of the LLM is that we can take the same material and package it for five, ten, twenty different audiences, giving each the right information based on the risks and thresholds that have been accepted, so they drive actions."** One body of analysis becomes many tailored artifacts, each pitched to the audience that has to act on it.

## Air-Gapped by Design

Throughout, Risk Mandate keeps its hands off the systems. The project lead: **"we do not touch the systems, where you touch it you own it, we should be air-gapped in a lot of ways, we get some feeds at the top, and ideally we get the feedback loop back so we can adjust the risk register and update the stakeholders."** This is the black-box handover and the partner strategy made concrete in the demo: feeds come in, understanding and action go out, and the only loop back is data to keep the register and the stakeholders current. Risk Mandate never operates the customer's systems.

## The Workflow, Rendered

```
  DELIVERABLE ONE: understanding
    customer data  (described, confirmed, or exported; anonymous is fine)
         |
         v   connect to Risk Mandate taxonomies / ontologies / examples
         v   as risks are accepted -> ACCEPTANCE DASHBOARD (red / green / action points)
         v   reveals: thresholds, buffers, and the perception-vs-reality spread

  DELIVERABLE TWO: action
    the accepted risks + thresholds
         |
         v   same material, repackaged by the model for 5 / 10 / 20 audiences
         v   incident triggers | remediation tasks | project plans | integrations | evidence
         v   handed to the teams and systems that ACT  (Risk Mandate does not touch them)

  AIR GAP:   feeds in at the top  -->  [ Risk Mandate ]  -->  ideally a feedback loop back
                                                              (adjust the register, update stakeholders)
```

## The Value Hypothesis: Move the Market, Create the Demand

The larger bet is about the market, not just the customer. The project lead: **"if we can move the needle in the market so that this is the kind of information companies need, there will be massive demand for creating it, which is where we operate."** The proposition is high value because it is partly self-fulfilling: establish that the perception-reality gap is information every company must have, and the demand for producing it is the market Risk Mandate sits in.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a system operator.** Where you touch it, you own it; Risk Mandate stays air-gapped.
- **Not a scanner.** It connects discovery to acceptance and action, not detection alone.
- **Not one-size output.** The same material is repackaged per audience.
- **Not a closed intake.** Discovery can be anonymous, without naming the company.
