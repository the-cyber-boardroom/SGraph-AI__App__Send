# Risk Mandate as a Black Box: Outcomes First, Then Inputs and Outputs

**version** v0.33.38
**date** 30 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Product, Architect, Strategy, @Dev
**type** Arch brief

---

## What This Is

A definition of the product by walking backwards from its outcome and treating it as a black-box function: **the ultimate outcome is to reduce the risk of agentic solutions, and the defining constraint is that Risk Mandate does not do the fixes and does not prevent anything directly, it enables the understanding, the actions, the risks, and the funding, and hands over at a well-defined moment to the teams and systems that implement; its outputs are incidents to trigger, explicit tasks and requests for information, funding, or project definition, and the touch points and data it needs back; its inputs are the signals and data the business and integrated tools provide, the risk-decision workflow itself, and the ongoing feedback loops; and the operating philosophy is to be almost a serverless, no-UI company that, while it will always have its own interfaces, aims first to integrate wherever the customer already is, even a spreadsheet.** It is the second brief of 30 June (cross-ref: the v0.33.38 partner-strategy brief, the v0.33.36 use-case-driven-shipping brief, and the mini-apps material). New contributions: **the black-box function model, the handover definition, and the go-where-the-customer-is integration stance.**

## Walk Backwards From the Outcome

The method is to define the product by its result and only then map what flows in and out. The project lead: **"we almost want to walk backwards from the outcomes, and then map the inputs and the outputs, treating Risk Mandate as a box, a function, a black box in terms of capabilities."** Treating the product as a black box is deliberate: it forces clarity about what it is for and what crosses its boundary, before any argument about how it works inside.

## The Outcome: Reduce Agentic Risk

There is a single outcome everything serves. The project lead: **"the ultimate outcome is to reduce the risk of agentic solutions."** Every input, output, and internal mechanism is justified only by its contribution to that end, which keeps the scope honest.

## The Handover: We Deliver, We Do Not Fix

The boundary of the box is a handover, not a fix. The project lead: **"the handover moment is when we deliver the risks and the actions and maybe the guidance to the other systems and other teams who are going to implement the fix."** The richer that handover, the more efficient the downstream work. But the constraint is firm and load-bearing. The project lead: **"we are not going to be the ones doing the fixes, we are not going to be the ones that prevent stuff from happening, what we do is enable the understanding and the actions and the risks, and enable the funding of what needs to happen."** Risk Mandate produces understanding, decisions, owned risk, and funded intent, and then hands them to whoever implements. It does not sit in the remediation path, which is exactly the line the partner strategy draws.

## The Outputs

The box emits three kinds of thing. The project lead: **"the outputs are either incidents that need to be triggered and acted on, or tasks that are explicit requests for more information, funding, or project definition, and the indication of the touch points and data points you need on the way back."**

1. **Incidents** to trigger and act on.
2. **Explicit tasks and requests**, for more information, for funding, for project definition, for example fix a named thing.
3. **The return path**, the touch points and data points Risk Mandate needs back to close the loop.

Every output is a concrete artifact another team or system can act on, and each one names what it needs in return.

## The Inputs

The box consumes three kinds of thing. The project lead: **"the inputs are what the business can give us, or the tools we integrate with can give us: a set of signals and data we consume, the risk-decision workflow, and the regular feedback loops of what is happening."**

1. **Signals and data**, from the business, from integrated tools, from what customers say.
2. **The risk-decision workflow** itself, which is its own set of inputs and outputs.
3. **Feedback loops**, the ongoing reports of what is actually happening.

## The Black Box, Rendered

```
   INPUTS                         RISK MANDATE (black box)            OUTPUTS and HANDOVER
   ------                         ------------------------            --------------------
   signals + data      -->                                    -->     incidents to trigger
   (business, tools,              understand the risk
    what customers say)           find the owner              -->     explicit tasks / requests
                                  accept, for an interval              (info, funding, project def)
   risk-decision       -->        underwrite + propagate
   workflow                       enable the funding          -->     touch points + data needed back
                                                                       (the return path)
   feedback loops      -->        (does NOT do the fixes)     -->     handover to the teams and
                                                                       systems that implement
```

## The Operating Philosophy: Serverless, No-UI, Go Where the Customer Is

The preferred shape of the company matches the black-box framing. The project lead: **"I like the idea of being almost a serverless company, a no-UI company; of course we will always have our own UIs and custom interfaces, but ideally we always integrate with something that already exists, even if it is a spreadsheet."** The default is not to make the customer come to a new surface but to meet them on the surfaces they already use. The project lead: **"we should always aim to go where the customer already is, the tools they already use and the systems they already have, that is always the optimal input and output."** Risk Mandate will always keep its own interfaces, but the optimal integration is the one that lands in the customer's existing tools.

## Why This Framing Helps

Defining the product by its outcome and its handover keeps the scope disciplined, because the moment work drifts toward doing the fixes it has crossed the boundary the box draws. The explicit input and output contract makes integration and partnering concrete, since every partner and every tool connects at a named input or output rather than a vague surface. And the serverless, no-UI stance lowers the barrier to adoption and matches the partner strategy: Risk Mandate is the layer that understands, decides, and funds, wired into whatever the customer and the partners already run.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a remediation product.** It hands over the fix; it does not perform it.
- **Not a prevention product.** It enables action and funding; it does not block directly.
- **Not UI-first.** It integrates where the customer already is by default.
- **Not a closed surface.** Its value is defined by a clean input and output contract.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Rich handover versus staying out of the fix | The more detail we hand over, the closer we edge to doing the work; the boundary must hold |
| No-UI versus our own interfaces | We will always have UIs; the discipline is to prefer the customer's existing tools |
| Integrate anywhere versus consistency | Meeting the customer on a spreadsheet is optimal for adoption but harder to standardise |
| Outputs as tasks versus incidents | The trigger threshold between a task and an incident needs defining |
