# Confidence as a First-Class Property: The Margin of Error on Every Node

**version** v0.33.38
**date** 30 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Architect, @Dev, Product, Strategy
**type** Arch brief (semantic graph)

---

## What This Is

A briefing on making confidence, the margin of error, a first-class property of every node in the ontology: **the point of the model is objective, pragmatic data points backed by evidence and assurance that can survive being challenged, because stakeholders asked to accept a risk will challenge the data in front of them, so every node needs not just a rating but a confidence band that says how sure we are the value is correct and how wide the uncertainty runs; the band matters most when data is thin, and a band too wide for comfort, where the same node could be trivial or could cause serious damage, is precisely what should trigger the get-more-data behaviour, which is one of the acceptance directions; from a graph point of view the model should be able to state the comfort zone it is operating in, so a decision-maker knows the certainty their decision rests on, which matters because today decisions are routinely made on data given more weight of responsibility than its certainty warrants; and confidence, like risk, should connect the dots across the graph.** It is the fifth brief of 30 June, and the next memo covers a related point, that the owners of a piece of data should have a say in where it is used (cross-ref: the v0.33.36 grounding-ladder brief, the v0.33.38 risk-appetite brief, and the v0.33.35 risk-register no-deny brief). New contributions: **confidence as a node property, the uncertainty band, and the graph-level comfort zone.**

## Confidence Is a Property of Every Node

The proposal is to attach a confidence level to everything. The project lead: **"the idea of the margin of error in risk mappings, and in fact in most of the nodes in our ontology, which is almost the confidence level that we have."** Every node, not only risks, carries a statement of how much we trust its value, sitting alongside the value itself.

## Why: Data Must Survive Being Challenged

The reason confidence is not optional is what happens at the moment of acceptance. The project lead: **"the whole point is to have very objective and pragmatic data points, backed by evidence, backed by assurance, that can survive being challenged, because that is exactly what happens when you make stakeholders accept the risk, they challenge the data in front of them."** A stakeholder asked to put their name to a decision will push back on the numbers, so each number has to be able to answer for itself. The project lead: **"we have to add a degree of certainty, a metric that says this is how confident I am that the data you are seeing is actually correct."** Confidence is the field that answers the challenge before it is made.

## A Rating Needs a Band

A single rating is not enough, because it hides how sure we are of it. The project lead: **"every risk, when you classify it high, medium, or low, also has to have a band, where you could say it could be here or it could be there, and this is very important when you do not have data."** The band is the honest width of the estimate, and it is at its widest exactly where the data is thinnest.

```
   a node's rating carries a confidence band, not just a point:

   LOW |----------[####  R  ####]----------| HIGH      wide band  -> low confidence
   LOW |-----------------[# R #]------------| HIGH      narrow band -> high confidence

   band spanning trivial .. catastrophic  ->  too wide for comfort  ->  GET MORE DATA
```

## The Band Triggers Get More Data

The width of the band is what drives one of the most important behaviours in the system. The project lead: **"one of the behaviours we want to capture is I need more data so I can make a good informed decision, and the reason is that the area of ambiguity is too big for comfort."** When the band is narrow, a decision can be made; when it is too wide, the honest move is to reduce the uncertainty first. And the worst bands are the ones that straddle the whole range. The project lead: **"it is too great to have comfort, where this could either be something I do not care about or something that could really cause serious damage to the business, so let us map that out."** A node whose band runs from trivial to catastrophic cannot be accepted responsibly; it has to be resolved into a narrower band first, which is exactly the get-more-data direction of the acceptance workflow.

## The Comfort Zone, From a Graph Point of View

Confidence is not only a per-node field; it is a property of the whole picture. The project lead: **"from a graph point of view we should be able to say this is the comfort zone we are operating in, and that you are making decisions based on this."** The model should be able to state, for any decision, the aggregate certainty underneath it, so the decision-maker knows not just what the answer is but how firm the ground is beneath it.

## The Real-World Problem: Data Given More Weight Than It Deserves

Making confidence explicit fixes a failure that is everywhere today. The project lead: **"this is where it impacts the real world, because today there is a massive problem where people make decisions based on facts and information that should not have that weight of responsibility."** A number with an unstated wide band gets treated as if it were solid, and decisions, and their downstream interconnections, inherit a false certainty. Attaching the band to the node makes the weight a decision can bear explicit, so a shaky input is not silently loaded with responsibility it cannot hold.

## Connect the Dots on Certainty

Confidence propagates like risk. The project lead: **"we should be able to connect the dots in terms of the certainty."** The confidence of a conclusion is a function of the confidence of everything it rests on, so a decision built on a chain of thin evidence should show a wide band no matter how firm the final claim is stated, and the graph is what carries that propagation. This is the grounding ladder viewed through a second lens: how well a node is grounded, down through evidence to a measure and a twin, is much of what sets its confidence, and a documented we-don't-know is simply the widest possible band, honestly recorded.

## Teaser: Data Owners Have a Say in Where It Is Used

This leads directly into the next memo. The project lead: **"the owners of a piece of data also have to have a say on where it actually gets used."** Confidence is about how much a data point can be trusted; the next question is about who controls where a trusted data point is allowed to travel.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not false precision.** The band is the honest width, not a spurious extra decimal.
- **Not a single confidence number.** Confidence is a band, and it propagates.
- **Not a replacement for evidence.** It is a companion to grounding, not a substitute.
- **Not the data-ownership memo.** Who controls a data point's use is the next brief.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Confidence versus false precision | A band must reflect real uncertainty, not invent a number to look rigorous |
| Wide bands versus decidability | Too much honest uncertainty can stall decisions; the answer is get-more-data, not paralysis |
| Propagated confidence | Combining confidences across a chain needs a defined, defensible method |
| Comfort zone as a number | Aggregating per-node confidence into one comfort-zone figure risks its own false precision |
