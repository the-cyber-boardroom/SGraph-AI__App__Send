# Risk Appetite: The Band a Company Reveals, Not the One It States

**date** 30 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Product, Architect
**type** Strategy brief

## What This Is

The companion to the risk-acceptance psychology brief, on risk appetite: **risk appetite is a band on a spectrum, not a single number, the interval between two values that can be wide or narrow and depends on industry and company; it is not one appetite but a fractal network of them, one per division and team, consolidated to the top like risk itself; it already exists in any company that has operated for a while, because every past decision, including how much it invests in security, defines and confirms it, so it is discovered rather than declared; it is computed from two signals, the company's past actions as seen in the register and the risks it has paid to reduce, and the risk-acceptance decisions made going forward, each a fresh data point; it expresses comfort with consequences below a line, risks that are painful but survivable rather than existential; the goal is to operate in the Goldilocks zone inside the band, neither above it, buying risk the owners will not underwrite, nor below it, adding attrition and slowness where none is needed; it takes several rounds to get right and most get it wrong at first, which raises questions of data, information, and mandate; and it becomes one of the most valuable data sets a company can hold, especially once the appetites of consumers, regulators, and supply-chain third parties are added, where misalignment is where the problems live, and where the honest answer is sometimes we don't know.** It is the fourth brief of 30 June (cross-ref: the v0.33.38 risk-acceptance-psychology brief, the v0.33.35 risk-register brief, and the five-whys brief). New contributions: **risk appetite as a revealed band, the two signals, and the Goldilocks zone.**

## Risk Appetite Is a Band, Not a Number

The first correction is that appetite is not a point. The project lead: **"risk appetite is that band, that interval between two numbers, if you think of zero to one hundred in terms of risk, it is a spectrum, and it can be wider or shorter."** Where the band sits and how wide it is depends on the industry and the company's status, but it is always an interval, a range of tolerable risk, not a single threshold.

## A Fractal Network of Appetites

Nor is there only one. The project lead: **"there is not one risk appetite, there is a fractal and a network of risk appetites, because each division and each team has its own, but like risk we can consolidate it to the top."** Every part of the organisation carries its own band, and the enterprise appetite is the consolidation of them, exactly the graph-of-graphs pattern the register already uses.

## It Already Exists, and Is Discovered

A company that has operated for any length of time already has an appetite, whether or not it has ever written one down. The project lead: **"every decision that is made is one that defines and confirms the risk appetite of a business, so if you look back at the decisions, even how much they invest in a cybersecurity team, that already tells you the risk appetite."** The appetite is therefore discovered, read off the history of decisions, not invented in a workshop. And it moves. The project lead: **"it is always not a static number, it changes with the company, the environment, the threat landscape, and the understanding."**

## The Two Signals

The appetite is computed from two streams of evidence. The first looks backward. The project lead: **"the first source is the company's own past actions, what they decided, what they did and did not do; look at the register and you see the areas where the business is happy to take risk and the areas where it wants to invest, and more importantly look at the risks it has mitigated or dramatically reduced in the past, with cost and investment, because that tells you the risk appetite."** What a company has paid to reduce is the clearest statement of what it will not tolerate. The second looks forward. The project lead: **"the second source is risk acceptance, because every time a decision is made you get another signal; when the business decides it is happy to wait a month, two months, six months, it determines where the risk appetite actually is."** Each acceptance, and each interval chosen, is a fresh data point locating the band, which is why the acceptance workflow doubles as an appetite-discovery instrument.

## What Risk Appetite Means: Comfort With Consequences

Underneath the number is a statement about consequences. The project lead: **"risk appetite is saying I am comfortable with the consequences below this number, I am comfortable with risks of this nature because they are not existential, they would be painful but we can live with them, or it is the cost of business."** The line is drawn at survivability: above it, consequences threaten the business; below it, they are painful but bearable, or simply the cost of doing business.

## The Goldilocks Zone

The point of knowing the band is to operate inside it. The project lead: **"if you force companies and security to operate below their risk appetite you introduce attrition, slowness, and process where it should not be; the Goldilocks zone, inside the risk appetite, is where you want to operate, not above, because you are buying risks the business will not underwrite, and not below, because you are slowing down."** Operating above the band means carrying risk the owners never agreed to underwrite; operating below it means spending caution the business did not need, adding friction that slows everything down. The target is neither maximal safety nor maximal speed, but the band the business has actually chosen.

## It Takes Rounds, and Most Get It Wrong at First

The appetite is not found in one pass. The project lead: **"this needs a number of rounds and changes when people change; in the beginning most companies get it wrong, because a lot of risks are underestimated and you do not have full visibility."** Early attempts are unreliable, and every change of personnel shifts the band. That unreliability makes a set of questions unavoidable. The project lead: **"the interesting questions are whether the execs have enough data and correct information to make the decision, whether they had the mandate, and whether they were the ones who should have authorised the risk, because risks have to be mandated and underwritten all the way to the board."** An appetite reading is only as good as the data, the authority, and the mandate behind the decisions that produced it, which is why connecting those dots matters.

## Risk Appetite Is One of the Most Valuable Data Sets

Captured properly, the appetite is an asset in its own right. The project lead: **"risk appetite is something that gets discovered, and it is a hell of a data set, probably one of the most valuable, because it is super valuable to know the risk appetite of your peers."** Knowing your own band is useful; knowing how it compares to your peers, your sector, and your counterparts is more valuable still, and it is a data set Risk Mandate is uniquely placed to build and maintain.

## Adding the Other Parties: Consumers, Regulators, Supply Chain

The appetite gets far more interesting, and more consequential, once other parties are added. The project lead: **"it gets more interesting when you add the risk appetite of the users, the consumers, the citizens, and the third parties, because especially in supply chain you get massive disparities, and a lot of integration problems happen because of misalignment of risk appetite, where one supplier is willing to take far more risk than the parts that depend on it."** A supplier may accept risk for financial or strategic reasons that its dependents would never accept, and that mismatch is where integration problems and incidents cluster. Misalignment of appetite, between a business and its consumers, its regulators, or its supply chain, is where the problems live, and it is a subject for its own memo.

## The Power of "We Don't Know"

Finally, the appetite model has to make room for uncertainty. The project lead: **"it is powerful to have cases where we say we don't know, because that means you have to make an assumption, or we cannot calculate the risk effectively, or the risk has a massive spectrum, and that is important to capture."** Recording we don't know is not a failure; it forces an explicit assumption, or admits that a risk cannot yet be calculated, or that its spectrum runs from trivial to catastrophic, all of which are facts worth having on the register.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a single number.** Appetite is a band, and a fractal network of bands.
- **Not static.** It moves with the company, the environment, and the people.
- **Not the misalignment deep-dive.** Cross-party misalignment is a later memo.
- **Not a regulatory determination.** It describes appetite, it does not set compliance.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Revealed versus stated appetite | The band read from actions may embarrass the one on the policy page; that is the value |
| Consolidation versus local appetite | Rolling team bands to the top can hide real local disagreement |
| Goldilocks precision | The band is discovered over rounds, so early operating targets are approximate |
| We-don't-know honesty | Capturing uncertainty is right, but too much of it makes the appetite unactionable |
