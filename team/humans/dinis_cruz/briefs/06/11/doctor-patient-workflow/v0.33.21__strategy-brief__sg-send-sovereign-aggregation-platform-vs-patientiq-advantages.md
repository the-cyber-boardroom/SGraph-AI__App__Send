## What This Is

A comparison of the sovereign data aggregation and analytics platform (the architecture in the companion brief) with PatientIQ: **the platform architecture closes the one capability gap the earlier PatientIQ comparison had to concede, analytics, benchmarking, and real-world evidence, and it does so without a central unencrypted database, so it now matches PatientIQ on capability while beating it on open, distributed, sovereign, scalable, and safe, and inverting the business model so the data owner participates and is credited while the platform never holds the data. PatientIQ still leads on maturity, EHR depth, its existing dataset and network effect, and certifications, and the strength of the safety claim depends on two design decisions still to be made.** It builds directly on the architecture brief and the earlier comparison (cross-ref: the v0.33.21 sovereign-data-aggregation-platform and patientiq-comparison-doctor-patient-vaults briefs). PatientIQ facts are paraphrased from the research in the earlier brief. New contributions: **the capability-by-capability closure, the advantage-by-advantage read, and the business-model inversion as the headline competitive point.**

## The Gap The Earlier Comparison Had To Concede

The earlier comparison was honest that PatientIQ led on one cluster of capabilities precisely because they require holding and aggregating the data: its analytics engine, its benchmarking against a large real-world dataset, and its sale of de-identified data as real-world evidence. That brief flagged the benchmarking dataset as the one thing a zero-knowledge model appears to trade away, and noted it would need privacy-preserving computation rather than data-holding if it mattered. **The architecture brief is that answer made concrete.** Compute moves to the data, aggregation happens transiently under consented permission, and only results leave, so the capability is delivered without the central dataset that produced the concession.

## Capability By Capability

| PatientIQ Capability | How The Sovereign Architecture Delivers It |
|----------------------|--------------------------------------------|
| Data and outcomes collection | Owner-held vaults; the owner creates the vault and shares results (cross-ref: recipient-created-disposable-vault) |
| Analytics engine (within an organisation) | LETS pipelines run over the organisation's own vaults; results to a result vault |
| Benchmarking against a real-world dataset | A federated query; each owner grants an on-demand, time-boxed permission; ephemeral enclaves compute local aggregates; only the combined aggregate leaves |
| Real-world evidence sold to industry | Consented data participation; the owner grants a scoped computation and is credited; the platform never holds the data |
| EHR integration | Connectors layered onto the substrate over time |
| Compliance certifications | A smaller protected-data surface by design, plus cryptographic provenance on every aggregation |

The first four rows are the point: the capabilities that depend, in the incumbent model, on a central dataset are delivered here by computing next to owner-held data under consent, with only aggregates escaping.

## The Comparison

| Dimension | PatientIQ | The Sovereign Aggregation Platform |
|-----------|-----------|-------------------------------------|
| Aggregation and analytics | Central; data held and processed in the cloud | Compute moves to the data; no central plaintext database |
| Benchmarking | Against a held real-world dataset | Federated, consented, aggregate-only |
| Real-world evidence | De-identified data sold by the platform | Consented participation; owner credited; platform never holds data |
| Source model | Proprietary | Open source |
| Trust model | Trust a certified vendor with the data | Cryptographic; the host cannot read the data |
| Who holds the data | PatientIQ | The owner |
| Data monetisation conflict | Present (platform profits from holding and selling) | Absent (platform cannot read the data) |
| Scale model | Central platform | Distributed; ephemeral enclaves scale out |
| EHR integration | Deep and mature | Layerable; not yet built |
| Compliance | HIPAA, HITRUST, 21 CFR Part 11 | Smaller surface by design; not yet certified |
| Maturity | Mature; 850-plus organisations | Architecture; building blocks exist |

## The Advantages, Axis By Axis

| Axis | Against PatientIQ |
|------|-------------------|
| Open | An auditable open-source substrate, against a proprietary platform; the security model is inspected, not only certified |
| Distributed | Compute scales out across ephemeral enclaves with no central honeypot, against a central platform that is both a bottleneck and a breach target |
| Sovereign | Owners hold their data and consent per query, against a vendor that holds and aggregates it |
| Scalable | The work is where the data is, against centralising everyone's data to analyse it |
| Safe | No central plaintext database to breach, compel, or misuse, plus cryptographic provenance, against trust-the-vendor and server-side encryption the vendor can read |

## The Business-Model Inversion

The headline competitive point: **PatientIQ monetises by holding, aggregating, and selling de-identified data, which is a structural conflict of interest with the data subject, while the sovereign platform lets the owner monetise their own participation and the platform never holds the data.** Where the incumbent's value depends on accumulating a dataset and selling extracts, the sovereign model facilitates consented computations and takes a facilitation fee, never touching the data. Three consequences follow: the conflict of interest between platform and patient disappears; the privacy-sensitive owners and jurisdictions that will not let a vendor hold and resell their data become addressable, so the market is arguably larger; and the owner moves from being the product to being a credited participant. This is not a cheaper version of the same model; it is a different and arguably better-aligned one.

## Where PatientIQ Still Leads

Honesty keeps the comparison credible: **PatientIQ is mature, deeply EHR-integrated, certified, and already holds the dataset and the network effect.**

| PatientIQ Advantage | The Path |
|---------------------|----------|
| Maturity and adoption | The sovereign platform is an architecture; it must be built and proven |
| EHR integration depth | Layerable as connectors, over time |
| The existing real-world dataset and its network effect | The hardest to match; the sovereign model grows a federation of consented participants instead, a different and slower-to-seed asset |
| Compliance certifications | To be earned; the smaller surface helps but does not replace certification |

The dataset network effect is the genuine moat, and the honest read is that the sovereign model does not copy it but replaces it with a federation that has to be seeded; that is a real go-to-market challenge, not a solved problem.

## The Safety Claim Depends On Two Open Decisions

A caveat that must travel with the comparison: **the strength of the safe advantage depends on the two design decisions left open in the architecture brief.** If aggregation runs as transient plaintext in an enclave, the guarantee is real but weaker than if it runs under differential privacy, secure aggregation, or multi-party computation; and the enclave is a transient, scoped trust boundary whose hardening is a choice. The comparison holds at every level (open, distributed, sovereign, scalable), and safe holds against a central plaintext database regardless, but the precise strength of the safe claim should be stated against whichever privacy and enclave choices are made (cross-ref: the architecture brief's Key Design Decisions 1 and 2).

## Recommendations

1. **Lead with the model inversion** (owner-credited participation versus vendor-held-and-resold data; the conflict of interest is the sharpest point).
2. **Claim capability parity carefully** (the architecture closes the gap in principle; it is not yet built; say so).
3. **Compete on the five axes, not on feature maturity** (open, distributed, sovereign, scalable, safe).
4. **Be precise about the safety claim** (state it against the chosen privacy and enclave decisions).
5. **Treat the dataset network effect as the real moat to address** (seed a federation; do not pretend it is already matched).
6. **Reconcile the business models with the existing mappings** (the SG/Send and other-startup models; cross-ref: the architecture brief's note).
