## What This Is

Research on PatientIQ (patientiq.io) and a comparison with the SG/Vault provider-and-recipient vaults (the doctor-and-patient use case, rendered neutrally): **PatientIQ and SG/Vault differ at the level of trust model and business model, not just features. PatientIQ is a mature, proprietary, cloud-hosted SaaS that holds and aggregates patient-reported-outcomes data (and sells de-identified versions of it as real-world evidence), while SG/Vault is an open-source, no-lock-in, zero-knowledge substrate where the host holds no keys, the recipient holds the data, and aggregation-by-the-host is impossible by design. PatientIQ leads on maturity, EHR integration, analytics, and compliance certifications; SG/Vault leads on openness, data sovereignty, zero-knowledge confidentiality, and the absence of a data-monetisation conflict.** It builds on the recipient-created disposable vault model and the open-source thesis (cross-ref: the v0.33.21 recipient-created-disposable-vault and encrypted-spaces-paper-mapping briefs, and the v0.32.3 access-not-security brief). PatientIQ facts are paraphrased from the sources at the end; the SG/Vault facts are from the corpus. New contributions: **the PatientIQ research, the trust-and-business-model contrast, and the open-source no-lock-in positioning.**

## What PatientIQ Is

The research, in brief: PatientIQ is a Chicago-based healthcare technology company whose cloud platform integrates with electronic health records to collect and analyse patient-reported outcomes. Its products are ClinicalPRO (an EHR-integrated outcomes programme), ResearchPRO (an electronic-data-capture platform for trials and registries, with eCRF, eConsent, ePROs, and remote monitoring), and DataPRO (benchmarking against PatientIQ's real-world PRO dataset), plus professional services. It serves health systems, specialty practices, and medical device companies, and reports more than 850 organisations. It raised a 20 million dollar Series B in 2022, is subscription-priced, and is certified for HIPAA, HITRUST CSF, and 21 CFR Part 11, using standard server-side cryptography and role-based access control. A defining detail for this comparison: PatientIQ collects outcomes on behalf of provider organisations, benchmarks across them, and sells de-identified PRO data as real-world evidence to industry partners.

## The Fundamental Distinction

The cleanest way to see the comparison: **PatientIQ's value depends on holding and aggregating the data, while SG/Vault's value depends on not holding it.**

PatientIQ is a vendor-hosted platform that holds the data in its cloud, encrypted server-side, and earns trust through compliance certifications; its analytics, its benchmarking dataset, and its real-world-evidence sales all require it to hold and aggregate the data. SG/Vault is zero-knowledge: the host holds no keys and sees no plaintext, and in the recipient-created model the recipient holds the data and sends back only results (cross-ref: the recipient-created-disposable-vault brief). These are not two points on one spectrum; they are opposite trust models. PatientIQ asks customers to trust a certified vendor with the data; SG/Vault is built so the host cannot read it, which also makes aggregation-and-resale by the host structurally impossible.

## Where PatientIQ Is Ahead

Honesty matters in a competitive read, and PatientIQ leads on several real dimensions: **it is mature, deeply EHR-integrated, purpose-built for outcomes and research, analytically strong, certified, and widely adopted.**

| PatientIQ Strength | What It Means |
|--------------------|---------------|
| Maturity and adoption | Years in market, 850-plus organisations, funded, professional services |
| EHR integration | Deep integration with hospital record systems |
| Purpose-built tooling | Outcomes programmes, trials, registries, eConsent, remote monitoring |
| Analytics and benchmarking | A large real-world PRO dataset to benchmark against |
| Compliance certifications | HIPAA, HITRUST, 21 CFR Part 11 |

SG/Vault does not match these today, and a fair comparison says so. PatientIQ is a turnkey, certified, EHR-integrated outcomes platform; SG/Vault is an early substrate.

## Where SG/Vault Differentiates

The differentiation is exactly the open-source, no-lock-in, zero-knowledge nature the project lead asked to foreground:

**Open source.** SG/Vault is open and auditable; PatientIQ is proprietary. An open codebase means the security model can be inspected, not just certified, and there is no dependence on a single vendor's continued existence or terms.

**No lock-in and sovereignty.** With SG/Vault the organisation and the recipient own and control the data; it is self-hostable and portable, with no migration cost to leave (cross-ref: the access-not-security and webify briefs). With PatientIQ the data lives in the vendor's platform, and the benchmarking dataset is a network effect that deepens lock-in.

**Zero-knowledge confidentiality.** SG/Vault's host holds no keys and sees no plaintext, a categorically stronger privacy posture than server-side encryption where the vendor can read the data. This is the same untrusted-server model the recent academic literature argues for (cross-ref: the encrypted-spaces-paper-mapping brief).

**No data-monetisation conflict.** PatientIQ's model includes selling de-identified real-world evidence, which requires holding and aggregating the data. SG/Vault cannot do this even in principle, because the host cannot read the data, which removes a structural conflict of interest between the platform and the patient.

**The recipient holds their own data.** In the recipient-created model the patient creates the vault, keeps the data, and shares only results (cross-ref: the recipient-created-disposable-vault brief). PatientIQ collects the data into the platform on the provider's behalf.

**A general substrate, not a single product.** SG/Vault is vaults, vault apps, and iframes, a general substrate that can host many workflows, where PatientIQ is a purpose-built outcomes product.

## The Deepest Contrast

Worth stating once more, because it is the crux: **the two are structurally incompatible at the data layer, and that is the differentiation.** PatientIQ must hold and aggregate the data for its analytics, benchmarking, and real-world-evidence business to work; SG/Vault is built so the host can never read the data. A health system that wants turnkey, certified, EHR-integrated outcomes analytics with industry benchmarking will find PatientIQ mature and fit for purpose today. A provider, patient, or jurisdiction that does not want protected health information held and aggregated by a cloud vendor, and that values open source, sovereignty, and no lock-in, is exactly who SG/Vault's model is for. The choice is a trust-model choice, not a feature checklist.

## Honest Positioning: When Each Fits

| Situation | Better Fit |
|-----------|------------|
| Turnkey, certified, EHR-integrated outcomes analytics at scale today | PatientIQ |
| Industry benchmarking against a large real-world PRO dataset | PatientIQ |
| Data must never leave the patient's or organisation's control | SG/Vault |
| Open source and auditable is a requirement | SG/Vault |
| No vendor lock-in; self-hosting; data portability | SG/Vault |
| Jurisdictions or patients wary of vendor-held, aggregated, resold PHI | SG/Vault |
| A general substrate for many workflows, not one product | SG/Vault |

## What SG/Vault Would Need For A Head-To-Head

An honest list of the gaps, with a note on each: **EHR integration, outcomes and registry tooling, analytics, compliance certifications, and maturity.** Several of these can be layered onto the substrate over time (tooling and integrations are applications on top of the vault model), and the zero-knowledge architecture changes the compliance equation rather than only adding to it: holding far less protected health information centrally means a smaller attack surface and a different, arguably simpler, compliance story (cross-ref: the recipient-created-disposable-vault privacy posture). The benchmarking dataset is the one capability SG/Vault cannot replicate by holding data, and would have to approach differently (for example, privacy-preserving computation) if it mattered, which is a deliberate trade for the zero-knowledge guarantee.

## Recommendations

1. **Position on trust model, not feature parity** (zero-knowledge, open source, no lock-in, no data-monetisation conflict, against vendor-held-and-aggregated data).
2. **Target the segments the trust model fits** (sovereignty-sensitive providers, privacy-wary patients and jurisdictions, open-source requirements).
3. **Be honest about maturity** (PatientIQ is mature and certified; SG/Vault is early; lead with the model difference, not a feature bake-off).
4. **Frame compliance as smaller-surface, not absent** (holding less PHI centrally is a compliance advantage, even before certifications).
5. **Treat EHR integration and tooling as layerable** (applications on the substrate, over time).
6. **Use the academic convergence as support** (the untrusted-server model is where the literature is heading; cross-ref: the encrypted-spaces-paper-mapping brief).
