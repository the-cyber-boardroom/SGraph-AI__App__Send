## What This Is

A feature-by-feature scorecard of the Encrypted Collaboration Spaces paper against what SGraph has actually shipped, sorted into three buckets with indicative percentages: **of forty key features the paper describes, roughly 38 percent are already shipped, 30 percent are on the roadmap, and 33 percent need more research and roadmap alignment, and the pattern is clean, SGraph has shipped the substrate, the roadmap is the data-and-key-management layer, and the research frontier is the zero-knowledge verifiability superstructure.** It is the scorecard companion to the overall mapping and the SGit mapping (cross-ref: the v0.33.21 encrypted-spaces-paper-mapping-to-sgraph and sgit-mapping briefs). The paper is paraphrased; the source is listed at the end. New contributions: **the forty-feature scorecard, the three-bucket tally and percentages, and the three-layer reading.**

## A Note On Method

The buckets are a judgement, not a measurement, so the percentages are indicative rather than precise. Each feature is placed in a single primary bucket by its dominant status, with partials noted. Shipped means SGraph has a working implementation (even if less rigorous than the paper). Roadmap means SGraph has named or designed it in the corpus and intends to build it. Research means it needs more research and roadmap alignment before it could be planned. The aim is a shared, honest picture, not a precise audit.

## The Three-Layer Reading

The buckets line up almost exactly with three architectural layers, which is the useful finding:

| Layer | Buckets It Falls In | What It Contains |
|-------|---------------------|------------------|
| The substrate | Mostly Shipped | Confidentiality, no server keys, the changelog, hash commitments, the structure-versus-value split, the signed-update transition, catch-up, the registry, the kernel model |
| The data and key-management layer | Mostly Roadmap | Ordered key-value and tables, secondary indexes, schema and SDK, public-key invite, rekey, the keypair split, hybrid post-quantum, identity linking, alternative deployment |
| The verifiability superstructure | Mostly Research | Tracer proofs, fast-forward proofs, the zkVM, verified authorization, ratcheting, the retention key tree, mVE, transparency consensus, deniability, proven reads |

So SGraph has shipped the foundation, has the data-and-key-management work on its roadmap, and faces the zero-knowledge verifiability and advanced-cryptography work as its research frontier. This is a healthy shape: the parts that are hardest to retrofit (the substrate) are done, and the parts that are research (the proofs) are also the most expensive and the least essential for current users.

## What The Percentages Say

**The shipped third is the part that is hard to add later.** No server keys, content-addressing, append-only history, and the structure-versus-value split are foundational and difficult to retrofit; having them shipped is the strong position.

**The roadmap third is mostly maturation, not invention.** Tables, schemas, indexes, public-key invite, rekey, hybrid post-quantum, and identity linking are known engineering with corpus designs behind them, not open research.

**The research third is mostly the zero-knowledge superstructure.** Tracer and fast-forward proofs, the zkVM, verified authorization, and the advanced key-management constructions are where SGraph would need the most new research, and they are optional for the current trust model (signatures plus version control), becoming valuable only where a fully-malicious-server-with-detectability guarantee is required.

## Roadmap-Alignment Recommendations

1. **Promote the retention system from Research to Roadmap** (it solves real data-lifetime and new-recipient problems; it is the most tractable research item; cross-ref: the recipient-key-management and central-key-management briefs).
2. **Keep hybrid post-quantum on the near roadmap** (already designed; the paper confirms the X-Wing choice; cross-ref: the post-quantum-cryptography brief).
3. **Advance the data layer (tables, schema, secondary indexes)** (the vault-as-database direction; cross-ref: the deterministic-value-indexes and webify briefs).
4. **Treat the zero-knowledge proofs as a standalone research track** (tracer then fast-forward proofs over the SGit substrate; do not block product on them; cross-ref: the sgit-mapping brief).
5. **Re-score periodically** (these are indicative; revisit as the roadmap moves features between buckets).
