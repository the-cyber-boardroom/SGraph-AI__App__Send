# Investor Vaults: Winning On Productivity, Not Security, With Vaults-In-Vaults And Curated Experiences

**version** v0.32.7
**date** 7 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Product, Architect, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief

## What This Is

The promised variation of the tool-replacement strategy, applied to investor vaults and data rooms: **investor vaults have traction but some attrition, because right now they solve a problem investors do not strongly feel (unencrypted data), so the move is to add second-bracket features that let both sides do things they could not before, vaults-in-vaults so each party can see what the other is viewing, and curated experiences that let an investor experience the product rather than read a PowerPoint, winning on productivity, not security.** It applies the strategy from earlier today (cross-ref: the v0.32.7 tool-replacement-strategy brief) and builds on the vault-in-vault and data-room work (cross-ref: the v0.27.62 vault-in-vault-kernel-model and v0.31.9 expert-brand-vaults briefs). New contributions: **the productivity-not-security positioning, the email-as-system-of-record signal, vault-per-investor with mutual visibility, and the curated investor experience.**

## The Problem: Solving A Pain Investors Do Not Feel

The honest diagnosis, straight from the tool-replacement lens: **investor vaults add value on a problem investors do not strongly feel (that data is sent unencrypted), which is why there is traction but also attrition.**

The project lead: **"there is some traction, but a bit of attrition, because right now the vaults add value on a problem they do not have, or have a bit and maybe do not see, which is that data is sent unencrypted. A very interesting signal: a lot of investors like to send things on email, because that becomes a system of record in a weird way. Email almost becomes an archive, not the best one, but one they have."**

| The Value We Lead With | What The Investor Feels |
|------------------------|--------------------------|
| Data is encrypted, not sent in the clear | Not a pain they strongly feel |
| Security | Email already works as their archive and system of record |
| A safer data room | They did not ask to be safer |

So the current pitch (encryption, security) targets a pain investors do not strongly feel, and their existing workflow (email as an informal archive and system of record) is a commodity for them (cross-ref: the tool-replacement-strategy brief's working-tool-as-commodity). This is exactly the be-significantly-better problem: matching or improving on security does not move them. The answer is the strategy's second primitive: features that let them do things they could not before.

## Vaults-In-Vaults: Each Party Sees What The Other Sees

The first new capability, now that a vault can embed another vault: **a company seeking investment can create a vault per investor, and both sides can see, browse, manipulate, and message within the same confidential vault.**

The project lead: **"the power of vaults of vaults, especially now that we can embed a vault inside another vault, is that you can see what the vault the other party is viewing. A company that needs investment creates vaults per investor, and they can see, browse, manipulate, and send messages, the private, confidential email system we added between vaults. This is massive, it allows new capabilities."**

| Capability | What It Enables |
|------------|-----------------|
| Vault per investor | A dedicated, confidential space for each relationship |
| Mutual visibility (vaults-in-vaults) | Each party can see what the other is viewing |
| Private messaging between vaults | The confidential email and messaging system (cross-ref: the inbox brief) |
| Browse and manipulate | A live shared workspace, not a static drop |

So vaults-in-vaults (cross-ref: the vault-in-vault-kernel-model brief) turns the data room into a shared, confidential workspace per investor, where both sides see, browse, and message in private. The messaging is the confidential vault-to-vault comms and inbox (cross-ref: the v0.32.7 vault-inbox and v0.31.12 vault-to-vault-comms briefs), now a feature investors and companies use together. This is a capability email and ordinary data rooms cannot offer.

## Curated Experiences: Experience The Product, Not A PowerPoint

The second new capability, via iframes and more: **curate an experience so an investor can actually try and experience the product, guided, rather than receiving slides.**

The project lead: **"now that we are adding iframes inside vaults and getting better at it, it lets you curate the experience. Imagine you create a folder, an app, a vault app for an investor, and you guide them: here is what the product looks like, here is how it works, here are the prototypes. You do not just send PowerPoints, you share the actual vault, a curated experience to experience the app itself and see how it works. You can do this with an iframe, with videos, and eventually by streaming a desktop of the app: run the app on your EC2 instance, put streaming on it, and stream the app securely. There are no excuses."**

| Way To Curate | What The Investor Gets |
|---------------|------------------------|
| Iframe inside the vault | See what the live web app actually looks like |
| Videos | Guided walkthroughs |
| Desktop streaming (EC2 plus streaming) | The real app, streamed securely |
| A guided vault app | Here is the product, how it works, the prototypes |

So instead of sending PowerPoints, the company shares a curated vault app that guides the investor through the actual product: an embedded iframe of the live app (cross-ref: the vault-in-vault-kernel-model and no-origin-iframes work), videos, or even a securely streamed desktop of the app running on compute. The investor experiences the product rather than reading about it. And the same vault carries integrations, messaging, Kanban boards, documents, document signing, and risk tracking, a rich, interactive workspace.

## The Point: Productivity, Not Security

The thesis, and the whole reason this is the right variation: **these features make investors and companies use our solution because it makes them more productive, not because it is more secure.**

The project lead: **"if we do that, we are adding features for the data room that make them use our solution not because they want it to be more secure, but because the features make them more productive. That is my point."** So the winning move is precisely the tool-replacement strategy realised: do not sell the data room on security (a pain they do not feel); win on productivity and on capabilities they could not have before (the mutual-visibility workspace, the curated product experience, the integrated tools). Security comes along for free, but productivity is what moves them. The encryption is the invisible first primitive (compatibility and safety with no loss); the productivity features are the second primitive (the level above) and the superpowers (cross-ref: the tool-replacement-strategy brief).

## What This Asks For

1. **Stop leading investor vaults with security** (a pain investors do not strongly feel; email is their commodity).
2. **Add second-bracket, productivity features** (let both sides do what they could not before).
3. **Use vault-per-investor with mutual visibility** (vaults-in-vaults; see, browse, manipulate, and message in a confidential shared workspace).
4. **Build the confidential messaging in** (the vault-to-vault comms and inbox between company and investor).
5. **Curate the product experience** (a guided vault app: iframe of the live app, videos, or streamed desktop, not PowerPoints).
6. **Carry the rich tools** (integrations, Kanban, documents, document signing, risk tracking).
7. **Position on productivity, not security** (security is invisible and free; productivity is what moves them).

Estimated effort: moderate and largely assembly; vaults-in-vaults, iframes, the comms and inbox, and the tools exist; the contribution is the investor-vault product and positioning. The MVP is a company creating a vault per investor, with mutual visibility, confidential messaging, and a curated, guided experience of the live product inside the vault.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a security pitch.** Investors do not feel that pain; win on productivity.
- **Not a static data room.** A shared, confidential workspace with mutual visibility.
- **Not a PowerPoint sender.** A curated, guided experience of the actual product.
- **Not a single-tool play.** Messaging, iframes, videos, streaming, and the rich tools together.
- **Not a better-than-email argument.** Things email and ordinary data rooms cannot do.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Mutual visibility is sensitive.** Each party seeing the other's view must be deliberate. Mitigation: vault-per-investor; confidentiality by design; clear controls over what is shared.

**Risk 2: Curated experiences add build effort.** Iframes, videos, and streaming take work. Mitigation: start with the iframe of the live app; videos next; streaming later; reuse existing capabilities.

**Risk 3: Desktop streaming has cost and complexity.** Running and streaming the app is heavier. Mitigation: it is the eventual option, not the first; offer it where it is worth it.

**Risk 4: Attrition persists if we keep selling security.** The diagnosis must change the pitch. Mitigation: lead with productivity and superpowers; let security be invisible.
