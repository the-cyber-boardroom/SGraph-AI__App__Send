# The Personal Risk Acceptance Scenario: A Local LLM, A Question Graph That Builds The Risks

**version** v0.33.34
**date** 24 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** @Dev, Product, Strategy
**type** Dev brief

## What This Is

The first, personal risk-acceptance scenario for Risk Mandate.ai, the validation case: **an early user running Claude or another LLM on their local desktop is walked through a curated graph of questions whose answers populate a per-user graph and generate the risks they must accept; the questions establish the facts, do you use Claude on your desktop, in YOLO mode or accepting each permission, under which account, what credentials are on disk, what accounts you are logged into, Gmail, email, a corporate database, and whether you hold data that is not yours, and each answer is classified as fact, opinion, hypothesis, or evidence; the first-pass evidence is stored in the browser's local storage to start, not a vault; from it the risks are generated, including the lethal-trifecta check, an agent that reads untrusted content, reaches the internet, and can act on its own implies that local data can be exfiltrated, corrupted, or destroyed, and the email-access chain, access to an email account implies that every account that resets through it can be compromised; the risks are then accepted, each for a chosen interval, and the next phase extends to the external stakeholders who must also accept, the delegation question of whether the user even has the right to grant these permissions; much of this is prescriptive and curated, and can run without an LLM.** It is the concrete first instance of the risk-acceptance service (cross-ref: the v0.33.33 risk-acceptance-service and risk-acceptance-workflow briefs, the v0.33.32 agent-mandate-graph brief, the v0.33.30 Odysseus lethal-trifecta case, and the facts-and-evidence framing). New contributions: **the question graph, the evidence-to-risk generation, and the trifecta and email chains.**

## The Framing

What this validates: **go to a user and say, here are risks to accept.**

The project lead: **"this is the validation of the Risk Mandate.ai platform. If I go to somebody and say, I have a bunch of risks for you to accept. Let us take the early users that are running Claude or an LLM in their environment, on their local desktop."** So the scenario is deliberately concrete and personal: a single user, a local LLM, a set of risks to accept.

## The Question Graph

The mechanism: **a curated graph of questions whose answers build the risks.**

The project lead: **"we map a series of questions, and the questions have a graph that we want to ask, and the answers populate the graph for the user, which leads to the answer and the risks to be accepted. Eventually we will have an LLM on this, but a lot of it we can do without LLMs, because the questions and scenarios are curated."** So the engine is a curated question graph: the answers populate a per-user graph, and the graph yields the risks. Because the scenarios are curated, the first version needs no LLM.

## Facts, Opinions, Hypotheses, Evidence

How answers are typed: **each answer is one of four kinds.**

The project lead wants to bring back the facts, opinions, hypotheses, and evidence framing: depending on how the user answers, the system knows what kind of information it holds, a stated fact, an opinion, a hypothesis to test, or a piece of evidence. Typing each answer this way is what lets the graph reason about confidence later.

## The Questions

The personal walkthrough, as a curated sequence:

```
  1. Do you use Claude on your desktop?                         -> yes
  2. In YOLO mode, or do you accept each permission?            -> usually YOLO
  3. Which account are you logged in as?                        -> personal / work
  4. What credentials exist on disk?                            -> AWS, others
  5. What accounts are you logged into?                         -> Gmail, email, corporate DB
  6. Do you hold data on this laptop that is not yours?         -> yes / no
```

The project lead: **"do you use Claude on your desktop, do you use it in YOLO mode or accept the permissions, what account are you logged in as, what credentials exist on disk, what accounts have you logged into, are you logged into Gmail, an email system, a corporate database, and do you, on your laptop, have access to data that does not belong to you."** Each answer is a node that the risk graph builds on.

## Local Storage First

Where the evidence lives, to start: **the browser's local storage, not a vault.**

The project lead: **"once we have this first pass of evidence, we should store it in local storage, and tell the user this is stored locally on your local storage, so we do not put a vault just yet."** So the first pass keeps the evidence in the browser, with the user told plainly that it is local, which lowers the barrier to trying it; a vault comes later.

## Generating The Risks

From evidence to risks: **build the user's risk graph, then have them accept.**

Once the evidence is collected, the system creates the graph of risks that belong to this user, and presents them for acceptance. The risks are derived, not generic: they follow from what the answers established about the account, the credentials, the logged-in services, and the data on the machine.

## The Lethal Trifecta

The central check: **three conditions that together mean serious exposure.**

The project lead: **"we should ask the trifecta: does the agent read data from the outside world, prompts and content files; is the agent connected to the internet; and is the agent able to act on its own."** When all three hold, the risks to accept are concrete:

| Trifecta condition | Present? |
|--------------------|----------|
| Reads untrusted content from outside (prompts, content, files) | yes / no |
| Connected to the internet | yes / no |
| Able to act on its own | yes / no |

If all three are present, the user is asked to accept that:

- **Exfiltration**: all the data currently on the local computer could be exfiltrated.
- **Corruption**: that data could be manipulated and corrupted.
- **Destruction**: that data could be destroyed, rendered useless, not merely deleted.

The project lead: **"it is not just deleted, it could be rendered useless."** This is the lethal trifecta applied to the personal machine (cross-ref: the Odysseus lethal-trifecta case).

## The Email-Access Chain

A second concrete chain: **email access reaches every account that resets through it.**

The project lead: **"if you have access to email, that means every account you have that uses that email, with two-factor authentication, can be compromised."** So access to an email account is not one risk but many: every account whose password reset or second factor runs through that mailbox can be taken over. And, as the project lead notes, this is before even looking at what is actually in the assets, which would surface more.

## Accept, For An Interval

How the risks are accepted: **each for a chosen interval.**

Each risk is accepted using the time-box primitive from the workflow: not denied, but accepted for a chosen interval after which it must be revisited (cross-ref: the risk-acceptance-workflow brief).

## The Views

How the graph is sliced: **stakeholder, assets, and workflows.**

The graph supports several views, a stakeholder view, an assets view, and a workflows view, so the same evidence can be read from the angle a given question needs. Much of this is prescriptive, because the scenarios are curated.

## The Next Phase: External Stakeholders And Delegation

What follows the personal scenario: **the people who also have to accept.**

This scenario is the personal one, a single user. The next phase adds the external stakeholders who must also accept, and with them the delegation question. The project lead: **"do you have the right to delegate these permissions, do you have permission from the stakeholders, the ones who own the data?"** This is where the graph earns its keep, because it can show who owns what and whether the user had the right to grant it (cross-ref: the mandate-is-delegation brief).

## The First Challenges

What has to be built first: **the ontology, the taxonomies, the scenarios, the workflows.**

Because the experience is curated and prescriptive, the first work is the ontology and taxonomies, the curated scenarios, and the workflows that drive the question graph.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. A curated question graph walks a personal user through the local-LLM scenario.
2. Each answer is classified as a fact, an opinion, a hypothesis, or evidence.
3. The questions establish the account, on-disk credentials, logged-in accounts, and access to data that is not the user's.
4. The first-pass evidence is stored in the browser's local storage, with the user told it is local; no vault yet.
5. The system generates a per-user risk graph from the evidence.
6. The lethal-trifecta check yields exfiltration, corruption, and destruction risks when all three conditions hold.
7. Email access yields the account-takeover chain risk.
8. Each risk is accepted for a chosen interval.
9. The graph supports stakeholder, assets, and workflows views.
10. The flow runs without an LLM, on curated scenarios.
11. A next phase extends to external stakeholders and the right-to-delegate question.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not multi-user yet.** It is the personal scenario; stakeholders come next.
- **Not vault-backed yet.** The first pass is local storage.
- **Not LLM-dependent.** The curated graph runs without one.
- **Not generic risks.** The risks are derived from the user's own answers.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: the evidence is self-reported.** The answers are what the user tells us. Mitigation: type each as fact, opinion, hypothesis, or evidence, and treat confidence accordingly.

**Tension 2: the curated chains must be defensible.** The trifecta and email chains must hold up. Mitigation: ground them in known mechanisms and real incidents, and state them plainly.

**Tension 3: local storage is a starting compromise.** It is convenient but not durable or shareable. Mitigation: it is explicitly the first pass; the vault follows.
