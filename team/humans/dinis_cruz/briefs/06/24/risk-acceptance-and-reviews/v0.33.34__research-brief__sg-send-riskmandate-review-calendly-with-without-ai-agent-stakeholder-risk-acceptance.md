## What This Is

A RiskMandate review of Calendly, the market-leading scheduling platform, assessed through the risk-acceptance lens, with and without its AI-agent capabilities: **Calendly is a mature, well-certified vendor, SOC 2 Type II, ISO/IEC 27001, PCI, GDPR and CCPA, yet adopting it still means accepting real risks, because it requires full read and write access to a connected calendar and, optionally, the ability to read and send email and sync contacts, it collects invitee personal data, and its trusted, easy-to-register platform has repeatedly been abused by third parties to deliver phishing and malware; turning on the AI-agent capabilities, the AI Assistant that takes actions on your data, the Callie assistant that books on your behalf, the Notetaker that records and transcribes meetings, and the March 2026 MCP Server that lets external agents such as Claude and ChatGPT act on your Calendly, widens the blast radius and brings the lethal trifecta into play; this review states what Calendly is and accesses, its documented vulnerabilities and abuse history, and the risks each impacted stakeholder must accept, for an interval, underwritten, with no option to simply deny that the risk exists; the certifications cover the static-software risk well, but not the abuse and agentic dimensions, which is exactly the gap a risk-based review surfaces.** It is a worked example of the approach, not a verdict on Calendly (cross-ref: the risk-acceptance-service, risk-acceptance-workflow, personal-risk-acceptance, and adding-AI-is-a-risk briefs). New contributions: **a worked platform review, the with-and-without-AI split, and the stakeholder acceptance table.** Sources are listed at the end.

## The Platform: What Calendly Is and What It Accesses

Calendly is a cloud scheduling service used by more than 20 million people across 230 countries. It connects to a user's calendar to show availability and to book meetings. The connection is made through OAuth for Google and Microsoft 365, and through Exchange; iCloud is deprecated for new connections. Calendly states that it requests the minimum access necessary, but for the full scheduling experience it requires the ability to read, update, create, and delete events, which is full calendar access, not merely free or busy status. Beyond the calendar, Calendly can optionally read and send a user's email and sync contacts from Gmail to power its contact and workflow features, collects invitee personal data through booking forms, processes payments through Stripe, and offers enterprise controls such as single sign-on, SCIM provisioning, domain control, and an audit log. It is hosted on Google Cloud, encrypts data in transit and at rest, and holds SOC 2 Type II, SOC 3, ISO/IEC 27001, PCI, and CSA STAR attestations, with GDPR and CCPA compliance and a data processing addendum built into its terms.

What a user or organisation grants:

| Surface | Access granted |
|---------|----------------|
| Calendar | Full read and write: titles, attendees, create, update, delete events |
| Email (optional) | Read email messages and send email on the user's behalf |
| Contacts (optional) | Sync contacts from the connected mailbox |
| Invitee data | Name, email, and booking-form answers, stored as a processor |
| Payments | Card handling via Stripe, tokenised, not stored by Calendly |
| Organisation | Tenant-level OAuth consent, plus SSO, SCIM, and domain control |

## Known Vulnerabilities and Abuse History

Calendly's own platform is well secured, but two categories of documented risk matter for this review.

The first, and the most significant, is abuse of the trusted platform by third parties. Attackers have repeatedly used Calendly's own features, particularly the custom-invite email and the Add Custom Link function, to insert malicious links into legitimate-looking Calendly pages and emails. Because these messages come from a trusted platform, they often pass email security filters, and because Calendly lets users register without a card or identity check, it is easy to abuse. Reported cases include a 2022 campaign delivering fake fax notifications to harvest Microsoft credentials, a 2023 to 2024 campaign by a North Korean Lazarus subgroup that used Add Custom Link to deliver macOS malware to cryptocurrency targets, and a large, long-running 2025 campaign uncovered by Push Security that impersonated more than 75 brands with Calendly-themed job lures, using attacker-in-the-middle and browser-in-the-browser techniques to steal Google Workspace and Facebook Business and ad-manager accounts. Calendly says it counters this with a web application firewall, fraud detection, anomalous-traffic alerts, and immediate termination of accounts that violate its terms, and recommends a password manager and two-factor authentication.

The second category is third-party components in the surrounding ecosystem. CVE-2026-0868 is a stored cross-site-scripting flaw, not in Calendly itself but in a popular third-party WordPress plugin that embeds Calendly, which let an authenticated contributor inject scripts leading to session hijacking or credential theft. It illustrates that the embedding and integration layer around Calendly is part of the risk picture.

## The Risks Without an AI Agent

Even without any AI feature enabled, adopting Calendly means accepting:

- **Calendar exposure.** Calendly holds full read and write access to the connected calendar, including event titles and attendees, not just availability.
- **Standing OAuth access.** A compromised Calendly session or OAuth token is persistent access to the calendar and, if enabled, the mailbox, until the grant is revoked.
- **Invitee data handling.** Personal data of everyone who books is collected and stored by a third-party processor.
- **The platform as a phishing vector.** A booking page or invite can be abused to host malicious links in the user's or brand's name, and the platform's trust helps those messages land.
- **Brand and employee impersonation.** As the campaigns above show, a brand and its real employees can be impersonated in Calendly-themed lures.
- **Integration and embedding risk.** Plugins, embeds, and connected tools add their own vulnerabilities.
- **Availability and lock-in.** A scheduling dependency on a single external service.

## The Risks With an AI Agent Mode

Calendly's AI capabilities, rolled out through late 2025 and 2026, change the picture. The AI Assistant reviews the user's data in Calendly and takes actions on request. The AI Scheduling Assistant, Callie, schedules, coordinates, and books on the user's behalf from natural-language requests. The Notetaker records, transcribes, and summarises meetings, and can read the calendar to identify attendees. Most significantly for an agentic review, the March 2026 MCP Server lets any Model Context Protocol client, including Claude and ChatGPT, check availability and book directly into a user's Calendly, and third-party agents such as Datagrid and Smith.ai connect to act autonomously on every booking, enriching records and updating a CRM. Calendly states it does not sell customer data or use it to train AI models, stores AI logs under access controls, and offers per-feature choice but no global AI opt-out.

Turning these on adds:

- **Autonomous action.** An agent can book, reschedule, cancel, email, and update connected systems on the user's behalf, so a wrong or manipulated decision acts without a human in the loop.
- **A prompt-injection surface.** Invitee-supplied content, booking-form answers, and meeting transcripts are untrusted text that an agent reads and may act on.
- **Extended reach through MCP.** An external AI tool now has a route to act on the calendar, widening the set of systems that can move the user's data.
- **Meeting capture.** Recording and transcription create a new, sensitive data store and raise consent obligations.

## The Lethal Trifecta in AI-Agent Mode

The agent question is the lethal trifecta. In AI-agent mode, all three conditions are typically present:

| Trifecta condition | In Calendly AI-agent mode |
|--------------------|---------------------------|
| Reads untrusted content from outside | Invitee names, booking answers, and meeting transcripts |
| Connected to the internet and other systems | Calendar, email, CRM, and MCP-connected tools |
| Able to act on its own | Books, reschedules, cancels, emails, updates records |

When all three hold, the risks to accept are concrete:

- **Exfiltration**: calendar contents, contacts, and meeting transcripts could be exposed.
- **Corruption**: bookings, calendar entries, and CRM records could be manipulated.
- **Unwanted autonomous action**: meetings booked, cancelled, or emails sent without intent, triggered by manipulated input.

## The Stakeholders and What Each Must Accept

A material adoption decision is underwritten by the relevant stakeholders, not made by one person. Each accepts the risks below, for a chosen interval, after which they are revisited. There is no deny button; the only escape from a risk that cannot be brought within bounds is the nuclear option, not using Calendly, disabling the AI features, or choosing a self-hosted alternative.

| Stakeholder | Risks to accept (baseline) | Added by AI-agent mode |
|-------------|----------------------------|------------------------|
| The user / scheduler | Full calendar access; optional email read and send; a compromised session exposes both; your page can host malicious links | An assistant books on your behalf; an external agent via MCP can act; your meetings are recorded |
| The invitees / bookers | Their personal data is held by a third party; clicking a link carries phishing risk; they rarely get to choose | Their meeting may be recorded and transcribed; their words feed an agent |
| The organisation / IT | Tenant OAuth consent; processor relationship and sub-processors; brand-impersonation risk; SSO and SCIM configuration | AI has no global opt-out; the MCP server extends access to external AI tools |
| The security team | Calendly links as initial access to Google Workspace or M365; trusted-platform email bypasses filters; standing OAuth access | Prompt-injection via invitee content and transcripts; agent actions may be invisible to the SIEM |
| GRC / compliance | Invitee-data handling, retention, and deletion; certifications cover static software, not abuse | Recording-consent law in two-party jurisdictions; AI-log storage and sub-processors |
| The data owners | Client, patient, or company data touched by the calendar and mailbox is exposed to a third party | That data, and meeting content, is exposed to AI features and sub-processors |

The data-owner row carries the delegation question from the workflow: does the scheduler even have the right to grant a third-party platform, and now its AI, access to data that belongs to clients, patients, or the company? In a regulated setting, healthcare, legal, or finance, that question is sharp.

## The Acceptance Summary

Adopting Calendly is not a yes-or-no debate; it is a set of risks to accept and underwrite. The baseline risks are modest for most personal users and well-mitigated on Calendly's side, but they are real for organisations and for regulated data. The AI-agent mode is a separate, larger acceptance, because it introduces autonomous action and the lethal trifecta. A pragmatic path mirrors the buy-without-AI option: use Calendly's scheduling without enabling the AI Assistant, Callie, the Notetaker, or the MCP server, and accept the smaller baseline; or, where the agentic features are wanted, accept the larger risk explicitly, underwritten by security and GRC, for a defined interval, with recording consent and prompt-injection exposure addressed.

## Honest Notes

- **This is not a verdict on Calendly.** Calendly is a mature, well-certified vendor with strong stated controls; this review concerns the risks a user or organisation accepts in adopting it, not a claim that the platform is insecure.
- **The abuse cases are third-party abuse, not breaches of Calendly.** The phishing and malware campaigns abused Calendly's trusted features; they were not compromises of Calendly's own systems.
- **The CVE is in a third-party plugin.** CVE-2026-0868 affects a WordPress embedding plugin, not Calendly's core platform.
- **The AI features are recent and evolving.** Capabilities, availability, and defaults are changing through 2026; verify current behaviour.
- **This is not legal or compliance advice.** Recording-consent, data-residency, and contractual questions depend on jurisdiction and counsel.
- **Research current as of June 2026.** Drawn from Calendly's own documentation and public security reporting, listed below.
