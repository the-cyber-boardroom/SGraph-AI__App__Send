# Use-Case-Driven Shipping, and the Recurring Risk Acceptance Metric

**version** v0.33.36
**date** 28 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Product, Architect, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief

---

## What This Is

The strategy of building and shipping Risk Mandate.ai through many use cases, from very simple to fully integrated, in order to tell the risk-acceptance story and to discover every piece of the puzzle: **because people do not understand risk acceptance until they experience it, the plan is to ship a stream of examples, simplest first, each one presenting a risk to a stakeholder who then asks the natural questions, what does this mean, how should I think about it, what am I deciding, where is the evidence, with the graph predicting the actions and the digital twins connecting and executing them; the pieces each use case exercises are the storage layer, the graphs, the queries and queryability, the processing, the workflows, the version control, and perhaps infographic generation; each one ships like a product, come up with an idea, map the workflow, create the vault, push it, present it, put it in front of five to ten users, and start exploring the revenue from agent access through bring-your-own-keys; and the north-star metric is not recurring revenue but recurring risk acceptance, how many acceptances happen each day, week, and month, because that is the signal that it works.** It is the first brief of 28 June and the program around the MVP (cross-ref: the v0.33.35 risk-register, digital-twins, and 2FA-MVP briefs). New contributions: **the use-case-driven strategy, the always-same acceptance workflow, and the recurring-risk-acceptance metric.**

## The Strategy: Many Use Cases, Simple to Integrated

The plan is to capture a number of specific use cases, each of which then builds its own scenario, digital twins, and workflow. The project lead: **"the strategy is to use a lot of these use cases, from very simple to all sorts of integrated."** Doing many of them serves two ends at once: it starts telling the story of risk acceptance, and it forces out every key piece of the puzzle the product needs.

## Why Use Cases: People Have to Experience It

The reason to lead with examples is that the idea does not land in the abstract. The project lead: **"a lot of people, until they experience it, will not really understand it, so we need very simple examples and then more complex ones."** Risk acceptance has to be felt, not explained, so the simplest cases come first and complexity is added only once the story is landing. The examples are the product.

## The Always-Same Workflow

Under every use case sits one workflow. The project lead: **"the workflow is always the same: you present the risk to somebody, and that person has a whole bunch of questions, what does this mean, how do you want me to think about it, what decisions am I making, where is the evidence."** The work, then, is to find cases where that exchange is simple and has few moving parts, yet still shows the zooming effect. The project lead: **"we predict the actions on a graph base, and then we use the digital twins to connect everything and make the actions happen."** So the graph anticipates what the stakeholder will need to decide, and the twins carry the decision into effect.

## The Pieces Each Use Case Exercises

Each use case is also a probe that stress-tests the technology. Between them they exercise the storage of the data, the graphs, the queries and the queryability, the processing, the workflows, the version control, and possibly infographic generation. Running many use cases is how the team learns which pieces are ready and which are missing. (One term to confirm: the memo referred to the storage layer as IPFS, whereas the prior briefs established IssueFS, the issues-as-files store; these are different things and the intended one needs settling, see Open Questions.)

## Examples: Start With Agents

A concrete example is a CFO who has to accept and limit the liability for tokens, backed by data, then asks the same questions, where is the evidence, how does it work. The project lead: **"a good example is a CFO having to accept and limit the liability for tokens, backed by data."** And the sharpest source of examples is agents. The project lead: **"let's focus on agents, because they have that extra level of focus on what we are capturing."** Cases drawn from running agents on your own computer give the clearest, most current risks to accept.

## Ship It Like a Product

These examples are not slideware; they are shipped. The project lead: **"this is where we ship, think of it as shipping a product: come up with an idea, map the workflow, create the vault, push it out there, present it, have five to ten users use it."** Each use case runs the full loop, idea, mapped workflow, a vault, a push, a presentation, and five to ten real users, and from the first ones the revenue model is explored too. The project lead: **"if they want to talk to the agent, they will have to bring their own keys, or we provide one."** The end-to-end flow is built out from simple, to medium, to more complex risk.

## The Metric: Recurring Risk Acceptance

The number the company steers by is deliberately not the usual one. The project lead: **"our key metric is not recurring revenue, it is recurring risk acceptance, how many risk acceptances we are having every day, every week, every month, because that is when we know it is working."** Recurring risk acceptance is the signal that the product is doing its job, and counting acceptances per day, week, and month is the primary measure, ahead of revenue.

## Why This Matters

This turns the whole model into a shippable, measurable program rather than a thesis. It gives a unit of work, the use case, a unit of proof, a risk accepted by a real user, and a north-star metric that points straight at the mission, behaviour change through risk acceptance, instead of at a vanity number. It is how the vision becomes a cadence.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a single grand demo.** It is a stream of use cases, simplest first.
- **Not explanation-led.** People have to experience risk acceptance to understand it.
- **Not revenue-first.** The north-star metric is recurring risk acceptance.
- **Not slideware.** Each use case is shipped to real users.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Simple versus representative | The simplest cases must still show the zooming effect, not trivialise it |
| Acceptance as a metric | Counting acceptances must not incentivise empty or rubber-stamp acceptances |
| Revenue exploration early | Bring-your-own-keys raises onboarding friction; weigh against adoption |
| Many use cases versus depth | Breadth of examples competes with finishing the 2FA MVP end to end |

| The first five use cases | Which simple, agent-centred cases to ship first |

## What This Asks For

1. Build a backlog of use cases, ordered from very simple to integrated.
2. For each, run the full loop: idea, map the workflow, create the vault, push, present, five to ten users.
3. Keep the same acceptance workflow underneath every case.
4. Use each case to exercise storage, graphs, queries, processing, workflows, and version control.
5. Start with agent-centred examples, such as the CFO and token liability.
6. Instrument and report recurring risk acceptance as the north-star metric.
7. Explore agent-access revenue through a keys model from the first cases.
