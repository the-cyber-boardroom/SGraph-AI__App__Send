## What This Is

The brief to create a series of Wardley maps for the agent mandate service, capturing the strategic landscape across multiple user needs: **every map starts with the user need at the top and places the components that meet it along the evolutionary axis from genesis to commodity, and each is drawn twice, the reality today and the picture with our service, so the before and after is explicit; the users are several, the exec or stakeholder who wants to use AI safely, the person who needs to understand and propagate risk up and down, the person who needs to delegate mandates without taking on liability, the agent itself, which is also a user that must ask for, receive, and check permissions, make decisions, validate controls, and execute, the security team, the financial team, the vendor of an agentic solution who must present risk to its own customers, and the competitive landscape; the recurring insight the maps should surface is that much of what feels like a product or a commodity is, behind the scenes, weak and custom-built, hope-driven authorization most of all, while execution is mature; the maps build on the earlier risk-dimensions work, are authored text-first in the vault as a graph with position so that map queries are graph queries, and follow the doctrine of starting with user needs.** A companion primer explains what a Wardley map is (cross-ref: the v0.33.32 observability/risk-dimensions, agent-mandate-graph, and how-not-why briefs, the v0.33.40 risk-acceptance brief, the v0.33.31 mandate-is-delegation brief, and the v0.33.28 Wardley-maps brief). New contributions: **the map series, the agent-as-user map, and the before-and-after framing.**

## The Framing

The task: **a series of maps for the agent mandate service, and this is their brief.**

The project lead: **"back to the agent mandate service. Let us create a series of Wardley maps, and this is the brief to create them. Map one as the brief for the maps, and another as the actual attempt to create them, and we also need a primer to explain what a Wardley map is, because some readers will not know."** So this document is the brief; the actual maps and the primer are its companions.

## The Method: User Needs First

The discipline: **start with what the user actually wants.**

The project lead: **"the thing about Wardley maps that is always important is to start with the user needs, so we start with what they actually want, have multiple maps that capture that, then capture the evolution of those elements, then capture the reality and what we are going to do with it, before and after our service. And the user has multiple needs, in fact the agent is also a user."** So each map is anchored at a user need, places the supporting components along the evolution axis from genesis through custom-built and product to commodity, and is drawn as reality today and as the picture with our service. The maps build on the earlier mapping of the risk dimensions, which helped show where to focus.

## Safe Is Not Secure

The distinction under the exec map: **safe means within the risk appetite, not just secure.**

The project lead: **"the exec wants to promote and use a specific AI capability, but in a safe way, and there is a difference between safe and secure. Secure is just the security metrics. Safe is whether it is aligned with the risk appetite, within the threshold of risk the business or the person is willing to accept. So the stakeholder needs to accept the risk, understand the risk they are taking, and have an effective way to do it."** This distinction is what the exec map turns on: safety is alignment with accepted risk, and it requires good data and the ability to enforce.

## The Maps To Create

Each map is anchored at a user need and drawn before and after our service:

| Map | The user need at the top | The evolution insight to surface |
|-----|--------------------------|----------------------------------|
| Exec / stakeholder | Use AI safely: promote and use AI within the risk appetite, accepting and understanding the risk, with good data and the ability to enforce | Hope-driven development is custom-built, not a product; much that feels like a commodity is weak and immature behind the scenes |
| Understanding and propagating risk | Understand the risk faced, with a risk-acceptance flow that propagates up and down | Today there is no good way to do this; the flow is genesis or custom; map cost alongside, since cost correlates with what is actually happening |
| Delegation | Delegate mandates in a way that is safe and creates no liability for the delegator | The safe, liability-free delegation path, and the right to delegate, are not productised; genesis or custom |
| The agent as a user | Ask for, receive, and check permissions; make decisions; validate and exchange controls; execute commands | Execution is mature, APIs, MCP, machines; safe authorization via an LLM is custom-built and immature; the LLM is commodity, but the uninjected, on-task safe decision is not |
| Security team | Understand and reduce the risk of agents, and integrate with existing tools | Detection and observability of agent action are genesis or custom; SIEM integration is product or commodity; live off the land |
| Financial team | Understand and control the cost and spend of agentic AI | Cost visibility and controls are genesis or custom; a FinOps discipline for tokens is only now forming |
| Vendor of an agentic solution | Understand its own in-line LLM capabilities and present a credible risk mapping to its own customers | The ability to present that mapping is genesis; this is where the burden-shift lands |
| Competitive landscape | For buyers and players: who is who, what capabilities, what maturity, how it flows | The landscape map: where each player sits on evolution, and where to focus |

The agent-as-user map is the sharpest, because it sets a mature execution layer beside an immature safe-authorization layer.

## The Before And After

The value is the gap: **draw reality, then draw it with our service.**

Each map is drawn twice. The reality map shows how immature the path is today, especially the hope-driven, custom-built authorization that feels like a product but is not. The with-our-service map shows the same value chain once our service supplies the missing data, the risk understanding, the acceptance flow, and the evidence. The distance between the two is the case for the service.

## The Recurring Insight

What the maps keep revealing: **commodity feel, custom-built reality.**

The project lead: **"what is interesting when you map like this is that you see a lot of things that feel like a commodity or a product to the user are actually very weak and very immature behind the scenes."** Hope-driven authorization is the clearest case: it presents as a working capability, but it is custom-built and fragile. Surfacing that gap across the maps is much of their persuasive power.

## The Maps Are Graphs With Position

The technical continuity: **the maps live in the agent mandate graph.**

A Wardley map is a graph with positional metadata: every component is a node, every dependency is an edge, and the two extra facts per node are its evolution position and its visibility to the user. So these maps are authored text-first in the vault and stored in the same graph as the agent mandate ontology, which means map queries are graph queries and the maps stay consistent with the rest of the model (cross-ref: the agent-mandate-graph brief).

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a single map.** It is a series across multiple users.
- **Not feature-first.** It starts with user needs.
- **Not a snapshot only.** It captures evolution and before and after.
- **Not the maps themselves.** It is the brief; the maps follow.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: evolution placement is a judgement.** Where a component sits is arguable. Mitigation: place from evidence and the risk-dimensions work, and let the placement be challenged.

**Tension 2: many users, many maps.** The series could sprawl. Mitigation: anchor each firmly at one user need, and reuse the shared components.

**Tension 3: the maps assume Wardley literacy.** Some readers will not have it. Mitigation: the companion primer.
