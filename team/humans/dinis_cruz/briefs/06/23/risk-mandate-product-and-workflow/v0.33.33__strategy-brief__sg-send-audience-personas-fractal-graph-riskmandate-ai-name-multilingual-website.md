## What This Is

The audience and persona definition for the website, and the name: **the partner's MVP aimed at three centres of gravity, the CEO for the business, the CSO for security and resilience, and the CIO for data, and this expands the cast to the CFO for the financials, the legal counsel who underwrites and signs off, the GRC team, the business function buying the AI, the internal dev team building it, the third-party company adding a Gen AI module to its product, and the sales or chief revenue officer who has to sell it; the set is fractal, because every player who uses AI also has to sell it upward or outward, so the same roles recur at each company in the chain, which produces the rich, multi-persona graph the product needs; the website must describe each persona, their focus, and their needs, and support multilingual and multicultural customisation; and the product now has a name, Risk Mandate.ai, which puts risk at the centre and names the mandates signed on AI agents.** This resolves the long-open company-name question (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 go-to-market brief, the v0.33.32 agent-mandate-graph brief, the v0.33.30 naming brief, and the v0.33.32 market-validation brief). New contributions: **the name, the expanded persona cast, and the fractal-graph framing.**

## The Name: Risk Mandate.ai

The decision: **the name puts risk at the centre.**

The project lead: **"the good news is we have zoomed in on the name, which is Risk Mandate.ai, and it is good because it literally puts risk at the centre, and the mandates we are signing on the AI agents. The name says it all: what do you do? We help with the risks of the mandates you give to your AI agents. And rm.ai is not a bad little joke, like rm -rf, the recursive delete, a nice nod from a tech point of view."** So the brand is Risk Mandate.ai, with riskmandate.ai and the rm.ai play, and it resolves the company-name question the naming brief left open: Mandate remains the frame, and Risk Mandate is the name that fronts it.

## The Three Centres Of Gravity (The Partner's MVP)

The partner's MVP aimed at three roles, which are three distinct centres of gravity: the **CEO**, the business focus; the **CSO**, the security, resilience, and cyber focus; and the **CIO**, the data focus. These stay, and the cast grows around them.

## The Expanded Cast

The roles to add:

| Persona | Focus |
|---------|-------|
| CFO | The financials, the cost and the liability |
| Legal counsel | Underwrites and signs off; the one who carries the legal exposure, so often the real decision-maker |
| GRC team | Governance, risk, and compliance; a critical and under-served reader |
| The buying business function | The function actually buying the AI solution |
| The internal dev team | The team building the AI capability in-house |
| The third-party vendor | A company adding a Gen AI module to its existing product or service |
| Sales or chief revenue officer | The one who has to sell the AI-enabled product |

The project lead on legal counsel: **"the legal counsel tends to be one of the most important people in the business, because they are the ones who want the right stuff, the ones who really sign off and underwrite a lot of what happens."**

## The Fractal Element

Why the cast multiplies: **everyone who uses AI also has to sell it.**

The project lead: **"this is fractal, because even in the first example, you use AI for something, and you have to sell it, usually upward or outward. And when you look at the commercial company providing the agentic service, not just the CEO and CFO care, but also the chief revenue officer, and the GRC team. So you start to have this very rich graph."** So the same roles recur at every company in the chain, the one using AI, the one selling it, the one buying it, which is exactly why the product needs a rich, multi-persona graph rather than a single dashboard.

## The Selling Company's Double Concern

A persona angle worth its own treatment: **what they build, and what they must answer upstream.**

A company selling an AI-enabled product has two concerns at once: knowing what it is developing internally, and being sure it can meet the compliance requirements that will come from the companies it sells to. This double concern, internal build and upstream compliance, is where the vendor persona meets the question of whether adding AI breaks the product, treated in the third brief.

## The Website

What the site must do: **describe the personas, and customise.**

The site must describe each persona, their focus, and their needs, allow for the multiple angles some of them carry, especially the companies that both build and sell, and support multilingual and multicultural customisation as a first-class part of everything on the site, not an afterthought.

## What This Asks For

1. **Adopt Risk Mandate.ai** as the name and secure riskmandate.ai and rm.ai.
2. **Keep the three centres of gravity** and add the expanded cast.
3. **Model the personas in the graph**, with the fractal recurrence across the chain.
4. **Describe each persona on the website**, focus and needs.
5. **Build multilingual and multicultural customisation** into the site from the start.
6. **Give the selling-company persona** its build-and-comply double view.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not the partner's three-role MVP only.** It widens the cast.
- **Not a single dashboard.** It is a multi-persona graph.
- **Not English-only.** Multilingual and multicultural from the start.
- **Not the risk-acceptance service.** That is the second brief.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: more personas, more surface.** A wide cast risks a diffuse site. Mitigation: the graph holds the personas; the site presents customised views, not one page for all.

**Tension 2: the name change is late.** The partner artifacts are SGraph-branded. Mitigation: settle Risk Mandate.ai now and rebrand before public.

**Tension 3: multilingual adds cost.** Localisation is real work. Mitigation: build the customisation layer once, into the site's foundation.
