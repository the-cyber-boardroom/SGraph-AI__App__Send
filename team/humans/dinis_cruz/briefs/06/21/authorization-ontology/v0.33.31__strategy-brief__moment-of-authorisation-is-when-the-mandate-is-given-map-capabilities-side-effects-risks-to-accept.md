## What This Is

The core principle, stated on its own: **the moment of authorisation is the moment the mandate is given to the agent, not the moment the agent acts, so the risk and the responsibility begin at the grant; a mandate is given in three ways, through an account the agent can use, through a file it has access to, or through a direct or indirect capability it holds; and from there the method is simple, map what the agent can do, connect it to the side-effects of those actions, and so map the risks that all relevant stakeholders need to accept.** It is the single idea the rest of the work derives from (cross-ref: the v0.32.3 nhi-2.0 briefs, the v0.33.40 agent-authorisation-blast-radius and risk-acceptance briefs, the v0.33.40 side-effects brief, and the v0.33.30 ontology brief). New contribution: **the principle stated standalone, with the three channels and the three-step method.**

## The Framing

What this is: **a standalone statement of the core.**

This brief does one thing: it states the principle the whole approach rests on, cleanly, so it can stand on its own and be pointed to.

## The Principle

The core: **authorisation happens at the grant, not at the action.**

The project lead: **"the moment of authorisation is the moment the mandate is given to the agent."** The consequence is that the risk and the responsibility exist from the moment the mandate is granted, not from the moment the agent does something. Nothing has to happen for the exposure to be real; the grant is the event. This is why the right thing to examine is the mandate at the point it is given, before any action (cross-ref: the nhi-2.0 moment-of-authorisation idea).

## How A Mandate Is Given

The three channels: **an account, a file, or a capability.**

A mandate is conferred in three ways, and often none of them is called a mandate at the time:

- **Through an account.** The agent is given access through an account it can use, inheriting whatever that account can reach.
- **Through a file it has access to.** The agent can read or act on a file, and so on everything that file exposes.
- **Through a direct or indirect capability.** The agent holds a capability directly, or acquires one indirectly by chaining capabilities or composing tools.

Each of these is the moment of authorisation, whether or not anyone framed it as granting authority.

## The Method

The three steps that follow: **map, connect, and surface the risks to accept.**

The project lead: **"map what they can do, connect it with the side-effects of those actions, so that we can map the risks that need to be accepted by all relevant stakeholders."**

```
  1. Map what the agent can do        (the capabilities the mandate confers)
            |
            v
  2. Connect it to the side-effects   (the consequences of those actions)
            |
            v
  3. Map the risks to be accepted     (by all relevant stakeholders)
```

So the work flows directly from the principle: because authorisation happens at the grant, you map the capabilities the grant confers, follow them to their side-effects, and from those derive the risks that have to be accepted (cross-ref: the side-effects and risk-acceptance briefs).

## All Relevant Stakeholders

The scope of acceptance: **everyone the mandate reaches, not only the grantor.**

The risks have to be accepted by all relevant stakeholders, which is more than the person who granted the mandate, because a mandate's side-effects can reach other people, teams, and organisations who never granted anything. Mapping the risks means mapping who must accept each one (cross-ref: the third-party-impact and risk-acceptance briefs).

## Why This Is The Core

The point of stating it alone: **everything else derives from this.**

The blast radius, the assessment, the compliance mapping, the supply-chain model, and the third-party mapping are all consequences of this one principle: that authorisation is the moment of the mandate, and that from the mandate you map capabilities, side-effects, and the risks to accept. Stated on its own, it is the thing to agree on first.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not about what the agent did.** It is about what the grant authorised.
- **Not a single channel.** Accounts, files, and capabilities all grant it.
- **Not the grantor's risk alone.** All relevant stakeholders must accept.
- **Not the full method.** It is the principle the method follows from.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: the grant is often invisible.** Access through an account or a file is rarely seen as granting a mandate. Mitigation: naming it as the moment of authorisation is the point.

**Tension 2: indirect capabilities are hard to see.** Chained or composed capabilities hide the true grant. Mitigation: the map must follow capabilities to their indirect reach.

**Tension 3: all stakeholders is demanding.** Acceptance from everyone reached is heavy. Mitigation: the map shows who must accept what, so it is tractable.
