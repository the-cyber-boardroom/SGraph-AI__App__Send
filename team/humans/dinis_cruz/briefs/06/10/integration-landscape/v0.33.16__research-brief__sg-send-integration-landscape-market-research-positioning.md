## What This Is

Executed market research: **a survey and positioning of the companies and products SG/Vault should consider integrating with, organised by the eight integration categories that matter for the platform, with each vendor positioned on what it is, how it charges, and how it relates to SG/Vault.** This is the first of two documents; the second defines a set of visualisations and mappings built on practical implementation and costing criteria (cross-ref: the v0.33.16 integration-landscape-mappings brief). It builds on the platform's own direction (cross-ref: the v0.32.7 central-key-management, v0.33.16 recipient-key-management-options, v0.32.4 ephemeral-compute, and v0.32.7 tool-replacement-strategy briefs). Findings are paraphrased from the sources listed at the end; prices are indicative 2026 figures and move, so treat them as orders of magnitude. New contributions: **the eight-category integration map and the per-vendor positioning.**

## The Eight Integration Categories

SG/Vault's integration surface, derived from the corpus, falls into eight categories: identity and authentication, secrets and key management, cloud and edge compute, object storage, version control, LLM inference, secure sharing and data rooms, and messaging and delivery. Each is surveyed below with the leading players and SG/Vault's likely disposition (integrate, partner, replace or interoperate, or build on).

## 1. Identity And Authentication

The category that lets recipients and providers sign in and that backs the PKI registry's OAuth, GitHub, and DNS connectors. The market splits into per-MAU customer-identity platforms, B2B-SSO specialists, cloud-native options, and open-source self-hosted stacks.

| Vendor | Position |
|--------|----------|
| Auth0 (Okta) | The mature, deep, general-purpose incumbent; per-MAU pricing that scales steeply, with enterprise SSO behind costly plans; the most ecosystem depth |
| WorkOS | B2B-SSO and SCIM specialist; free auth to a very high user ceiling, then per-connection SSO pricing; the clever "free until you land enterprise" model |
| Clerk | Best developer experience for React and Next.js, with drop-in UI; per-MAU plus add-ons |
| Stytch | Passwordless-first and passkey-first, API-polished; per-MAU |
| Amazon Cognito / Firebase Auth | Cloud-native, cheap per-MAU, generous free tiers, but more engineering hours to wire up |
| Kinde | Younger, cheaper B2B bundle; strong price-to-feature for early stage |
| SuperTokens / FusionAuth / Ory | Open-source, self-hostable; high control, high ownership |

SG/Vault disposition: integrate selectively and lean open-source where sovereignty matters. The PKI registry already favours resolving identity through DNS, GitHub, and OAuth connectors (cross-ref: the PKI-registry brief), so the open-source and standards-based options (Ory, SuperTokens, and plain OIDC) align best with the no-lock-in principle, while a B2B-SSO layer like WorkOS is a pragmatic add for enterprise customers. The "log in with Google to hold a key" idea (cross-ref: the recipient-key-management brief) also lives here.

## 2. Secrets And Key Management

The category behind central key management, the recipient key problem, and provider-side secret storage. It splits into dedicated secret stores, cloud-native KMS, and open-source platforms, with one theme that matters intensely for SG/Vault: zero-knowledge.

| Vendor | Position |
|--------|----------|
| HashiCorp Vault | The most capable and flexible; dynamic secrets, encryption-as-a-service, multi-cloud; free self-hosted or expensive enterprise (budget tens to over a hundred thousand a year at scale) |
| AWS Secrets Manager / KMS | Cloud-native, around $0.40 per secret per month plus KMS and API fees; envelope encryption means AWS infrastructure can in principle see plaintext during operations |
| Azure Key Vault | HSM-backed, FIPS-validated, operation-priced; strong for Azure-centric estates |
| Infisical | Open-source, transparent per-user pricing, modern developer experience; zero-knowledge architecture |
| Doppler | Developer-friendly static secrets with sync; simpler, per-user |
| 1Password / Bitwarden | Now first-class key stores too (SSH keys, secrets, passkeys), encrypted client-side (cross-ref: the recipient-key-management research) |

SG/Vault disposition: interoperate, and use this category to sharpen the zero-knowledge story. The sharp point from the research is that cloud KMS uses envelope encryption where the provider can in principle access plaintext during operations, whereas SG/Vault's whole premise is client-side, zero-knowledge encryption. So SG/Vault should let customers bring their own KMS or HSM where they want it, integrate Infisical or Vault for the secrets-store role, and position its own zero-knowledge model as the differentiator the cloud KMS products structurally cannot match.

## 3. Cloud And Edge Compute

The category behind ephemeral compute, the SG compute bastion, and deploying the API as a Lambda into the customer's account. The choice is between the hyperscalers and the edge platforms.

| Vendor | Position |
|--------|----------|
| AWS Lambda | The deployment target today; in-account serverless; deep ecosystem and event integrations; the bastion-and-Lambda model (cross-ref: the AWS-deployment brief) |
| Lambda@Edge | Edge functions for serving public vault data with inline logic (cross-ref: the public-data and search briefs) |
| Cloudflare Workers | Edge-native serverless, strong for low-latency global execution and pairing with R2 storage |
| Azure / GCP Functions | The other hyperscaler serverless options for customers on those clouds |

SG/Vault disposition: integrate across all three hyperscalers, with Cloudflare as a strong edge and storage pairing. The run-in-the-customer's-account principle (cross-ref: the access-not-security and AWS-deployment briefs) means SG/Vault should deploy into whichever cloud the customer uses, so portability across AWS, Azure, GCP, and Cloudflare is the goal, not a single cloud dependency.

## 4. Object Storage

The category that physically holds encrypted vault contents. The decisive variable is egress cost, which is where the bill hides, and it matters doubly for SG/Vault because of the large-file and load-the-whole-vault features.

| Vendor | Position |
|--------|----------|
| AWS S3 | The default; around $0.09 per GB egress, which can dwarf storage cost on read-heavy workloads; deepest ecosystem and event integrations |
| Google Cloud Storage | Similar model, highest egress of the big three (around $0.12 per GB) |
| Cloudflare R2 | Zero egress fees, around $0.015 per GB storage, S3-compatible; the default for egress-heavy workloads |
| Backblaze B2 | Lowest storage cost (around $0.006 per GB), near-free egress through the Cloudflare Bandwidth Alliance; thinner ecosystem and fewer native compute integrations |
| Wasabi | Very low storage cost, but a fuzzy "reasonable use" egress policy |

SG/Vault disposition: integrate via the S3-compatible API and steer egress-heavy workloads to R2 or B2-plus-Cloudflare. Because SG/Vault's differentiating feature is loading and serving large vaults without re-downloading everything (cross-ref: the large-file and deterministic-value-indexes briefs), the chunked, delta-based, content-addressed design directly attacks the egress cost that punishes naive storage use, so the storage choice and the chunking design are a single economic story.

## 5. Version Control And Source Hosting

The category that mirrors the git-like vault, the commit log, and the publishing-mode GitHub Actions path, and that anchors the PKI registry's GitHub connector.

| Vendor | Position |
|--------|----------|
| GitHub | The dominant host; Actions for publishing; the natural identity and provenance anchor (cross-ref: the PKI-registry and publishing-mode briefs); per-user plans |
| GitLab | Integrated DevOps platform; self-hostable, which suits sovereignty-conscious customers; per-user plans |

SG/Vault disposition: integrate for provenance and publishing, not as the store. SG/Vault is itself a versioned store, so the relationship is interoperation: use GitHub and GitLab as identity and provenance anchors and as publishing targets (cross-ref: the publishing-mode brief), while the vault remains the encrypted, versioned home for the data.

## 6. LLM Inference

The category behind in-vault LLM capabilities and the shared-OpenRouter-key-to-child-vaults model. The choice is between aggregating gateways, the AWS-native option, and self-hosted gateways.

| Vendor | Position |
|--------|----------|
| OpenRouter | A single OpenAI-compatible API to 300-plus models from every major provider, passthrough pricing with a small markup, one key and one balance; usage shows but data is not the product (cross-ref: the central-key-management brief) |
| AWS Bedrock | AWS-native, IAM, KMS, PrivateLink, in-account processing, compliance story; a smaller catalogue and roughly a 10 to 15 percent premium |
| Cloudflare AI Gateway / Portkey | Managed gateways with observability, caching, and fallback; generous free tiers |
| LiteLLM / TrueFoundry | Self-hosted or private-gateway options for in-account control and policy |

SG/Vault disposition: integrate OpenRouter for breadth and Bedrock for in-account processing, with a self-hostable gateway option. The central-key-management model already distributes a monitored shared OpenRouter key to child vaults (cross-ref: that brief), and the principle of running compute in the customer's account points to Bedrock or a self-hosted gateway (LiteLLM) for customers who need data to stay in-account. The portability lesson is the same as compute and storage: route through an abstraction so no single provider is load-bearing.

## 7. Secure Sharing And Data Rooms

The category where SG/Vault both competes and can interoperate, and the one the tool-replacement and investor-vault theses speak to directly. It splits into zero-knowledge providers, mainstream server-side providers, encryption layers, and virtual data rooms.

| Vendor | Position |
|--------|----------|
| Tresorit | The enterprise zero-knowledge leader; Swiss, owned by Swiss Post; data rooms, eSignatures, audit logs; strong in legal, healthcare, and finance; around $10 to $12 per user; notably, its end-to-end encryption forces a full re-upload on any file change (no block-level sync) |
| Sync.com | Zero-knowledge, strong storage-per-dollar |
| Proton Drive | Zero-knowledge, Swiss, part of the Proton privacy suite; low entry price |
| MEGA / Internxt | Zero-knowledge; Internxt shards files across nodes |
| Dropbox / Google Drive / Box / OneDrive | Mainstream, server-side encryption (they hold the keys); Google Drive leads share; Box has FedRAMP; convenient but not zero-knowledge |
| Cryptomator | An encryption layer on top of existing cloud storage |
| Papermark / Datasite / iDeals / Ansarada | Virtual data rooms for M&A, fundraising, and diligence; Papermark is open-source and self-hostable |

SG/Vault disposition: interoperate, and replace at the high-assurance end. The single most useful finding is that the leading zero-knowledge competitor, Tresorit, cannot do block-level sync because of its encryption, so editing one line in a large file re-uploads the whole file. SG/Vault's chunked, content-addressed, delta design (cross-ref: the large-file and deterministic-value-indexes briefs) directly solves the exact limitation that hobbles the category leader. The data-room players (Papermark, Datasite) map onto the investor-vaults thesis (cross-ref: that brief), and the mainstream providers are interoperation and migration targets rather than integrations, consistent with the tool-replacement, hundred-percent-compatible-then-commoditize strategy (cross-ref: the tool-replacement-strategy brief).

## 8. Messaging And Delivery

The category behind delivering vault links to recipients by email and WhatsApp and the in-app two-way messaging.

| Vendor | Position |
|--------|----------|
| Twilio | The broad CPaaS; SMS, WhatsApp, voice, and email (via SendGrid); transparent usage-based pricing with a per-message markup plus Meta's WhatsApp template fees; developer-heavy |
| SendGrid (Twilio) | Email API; free low tier then monthly plans |
| Amazon SES | The cheapest high-volume email, AWS-native; pairs with the in-account deployment |
| Amazon SNS | AWS-native SMS and notifications; least overhead for AWS stacks |
| Telnyx / Bandwidth / Plivo | Cheaper SMS than Twilio for technical teams at volume |
| WhatsApp Business API (Meta) | The underlying WhatsApp channel; conversation and template-based fees |

SG/Vault disposition: integrate AWS SES and SNS for the AWS-native path, with Twilio or a cheaper alternative for WhatsApp and SMS. Because the product deploys into the customer's AWS account (cross-ref: the AWS-deployment and shipping-vault briefs), SES and SNS are the natural low-cost email and SMS path, with Twilio (or Telnyx or Plivo for cost) added where WhatsApp and rich SMS are needed.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

Three themes run across every category. First, egress and usage cost is the recurring trap (object storage egress, the auth SSO cliff, per-MAU and per-seat scaling), and SG/Vault's content-addressed, delta, and in-account design is largely an answer to it. Second, sovereignty and zero-knowledge are the differentiators: the categories where SG/Vault wins are exactly where the incumbents structurally cannot offer zero-knowledge (cloud KMS, mainstream sharing) or are hobbled by it (Tresorit's re-upload problem). Third, portability through abstraction is the safe posture in every category: route compute, storage, LLM, and messaging through abstractions so no single vendor is load-bearing, consistent with the no-lock-in principle.

## Honest Limitations

- This is a landscape and positioning survey, not a procurement decision or a security review of any vendor.
- Prices are indicative 2026 figures and move; verify before budgeting.
- The category list is derived from the corpus and may miss adjacencies (CRM, analytics, observability) that are lighter integrations.
- Vendor positioning is directional; a real choice needs a workload-specific cost model (the second document's mappings support that).
