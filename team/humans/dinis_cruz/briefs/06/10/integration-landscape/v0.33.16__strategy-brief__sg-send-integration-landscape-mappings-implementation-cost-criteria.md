## What This Is

The second of two documents on the integration landscape: **a set of criteria, mappings, and visualisations that turn the vendor positioning into practical decisions, scored on implementation effort and on cost, plus strategic fit, sovereignty, and Wardley evolution.** It depends on the first document for the vendor facts (cross-ref: the v0.33.16 integration-landscape-market-research-positioning brief) and applies the platform's principles (cross-ref: the v0.32.7 tool-replacement-strategy, v0.32.3 access-not-security, and v0.32.3 skill-lifecycle-explorer-to-town-planner briefs). New contributions: **the criteria set, the integration disposition map, the effort-versus-value quadrant, the cost-model-and-trajectory mapping, the sovereignty-and-zero-knowledge scoring, the Wardley evolution map, and the now-next-later roadmap.**

## The Criteria

The mappings use three groups of criteria, weighted toward the practical implementation and costing ones the project lead asked for.

| Group | Criterion | What It Measures |
|-------|-----------|------------------|
| Implementation | Integration effort | Drop-in (days), moderate (weeks), or deep (months) |
| Implementation | Standards-based versus proprietary | Open standard (OIDC, S3 API, OpenAI-compatible) eases swap; proprietary locks |
| Implementation | Portability and swap cost | Can it sit behind an abstraction and be swapped |
| Implementation | Operational burden | Managed (low ops) versus self-hosted (high ops) |
| Costing | Cost model | Open-source self-host, usage-based, per-seat or per-MAU, or enterprise contract |
| Costing | Cost trajectory | Flat, linear with use, or a cliff at scale |
| Costing | Cost of leaving | Egress, lock-in, and migration cost |
| Fit | Strategic value | Peripheral to core for SG/Vault |
| Fit | Sovereignty and zero-knowledge | How well it fits run-in-customer-account and zero-knowledge |
| Fit | Wardley stage | Genesis, custom, product, or commodity |
| Fit | Disposition | Integrate, partner, interoperate, replace, or build on |

## Mapping 1: The Integration Disposition Map

The headline decision per category: what SG/Vault should actually do.

| Category | Leading Players | Disposition |
|----------|-----------------|-------------|
| Identity and authentication | Ory, SuperTokens, WorkOS, OIDC, Auth0 | Integrate via open standards; add WorkOS for enterprise SSO; lean open-source |
| Secrets and key management | Infisical, HashiCorp Vault, cloud KMS, 1Password | Interoperate and bring-your-own-KMS; use as a foil for the zero-knowledge story |
| Cloud and edge compute | AWS Lambda, Cloudflare Workers, Azure, GCP | Integrate across all; deploy into the customer's cloud; stay portable |
| Object storage | Cloudflare R2, Backblaze B2, AWS S3 | Integrate via the S3-compatible API; steer egress-heavy use to R2 or B2 |
| Version control | GitHub, GitLab | Interoperate for provenance and publishing; the vault stays the store |
| LLM inference | OpenRouter, AWS Bedrock, LiteLLM | Integrate OpenRouter for breadth, Bedrock for in-account; abstract the provider |
| Secure sharing and data rooms | Tresorit, Proton, Dropbox, Papermark | Interoperate, migrate from, and replace at the high-assurance end |
| Messaging and delivery | AWS SES and SNS, Twilio, Telnyx | Integrate SES and SNS in-account; add Twilio or a cheaper option for WhatsApp |

## Mapping 2: The Effort-Versus-Value Quadrant

Plotting integration effort against strategic value gives four zones. This is the primary visualisation (rendered alongside this brief).

| Zone | Meaning | Examples |
|------|---------|----------|
| Quick wins (low effort, high value) | Do first | S3-compatible storage (R2, B2), OpenRouter (OpenAI-compatible), AWS SES and SNS, GitHub provenance |
| Strategic bets (high effort, high value) | Invest deliberately | The zero-knowledge-aligned identity layer, bring-your-own-KMS plus the zero-knowledge story, multi-cloud compute portability, the interoperate-then-replace play against Tresorit and Dropbox |
| Fill-ins (low effort, low value) | Add when needed | Cheaper SMS (Telnyx, Plivo), encryption-layer tools (Cryptomator), secondary data-room integrations |
| Reconsider (high effort, low value) | Avoid as core dependencies | Deep proprietary lock-in (Auth0 enterprise at scale, single-cloud KMS as the core), building identity from scratch |

## Mapping 3: Cost Model And Cost Trajectory

The costing view: how each category bills, and whether the bill is safe at scale. The recurring trap is the cost cliff.

| Category | Typical Cost Model | Trajectory | The Trap |
|----------|--------------------|-----------|----------|
| Identity and authentication | Per-MAU or per-SSO-connection | Cliff (SSO add-ons, per-MAU growth) | Auth0 SSO and per-MAU scaling; WorkOS per-connection is gentler |
| Secrets and key management | Per-secret, per-operation, or per-user | Linear, can spike on API calls | Per-user managers sting mid-size; self-host trades cost for ops |
| Cloud and edge compute | Usage-based | Linear | Idle and over-provisioned compute |
| Object storage | Storage plus egress | Cliff (egress) | Egress can dwarf storage; R2 and B2-plus-Cloudflare remove it |
| Version control | Per-user | Linear | Manageable; mostly provenance use |
| LLM inference | Per-token passthrough or premium | Linear, watch token growth | Bedrock premium; rising tokens is a bad signal (cross-ref: the artefact-driven-assessments brief) |
| Secure sharing and data rooms | Per-user | Linear | Per-user at scale; the replace play removes it |
| Messaging and delivery | Per-message | Linear | WhatsApp template and marketing fees |

The pattern: the two worst cliffs are object-storage egress and identity SSO-and-MAU, and SG/Vault's design (content-addressed storage, standards-based identity behind an abstraction) is largely an answer to both.

## Mapping 4: Sovereignty And Zero-Knowledge Alignment

Scoring each category on how well it fits SG/Vault's run-in-the-customer's-account and zero-knowledge principles (higher is better).

| Category | Alignment | Why |
|----------|-----------|-----|
| Object storage (S3-compatible) | High | Standards-based, portable, in-account possible |
| Cloud and edge compute | High | Deployable into the customer's own account |
| LLM inference | Medium to high | OpenRouter shows usage not data; Bedrock and self-host keep it in-account |
| Identity (open standards, self-host) | High | OIDC and Ory or SuperTokens avoid lock-in |
| Identity (proprietary at scale) | Low | Per-MAU lock-in and data centralisation |
| Secrets (bring-your-own-KMS, Infisical, Vault) | High | Customer-held keys; zero-knowledge-compatible |
| Secrets (cloud KMS as the core) | Low | Envelope encryption gives the provider theoretical plaintext access |
| Secure sharing (zero-knowledge peers) | High as benchmark | The category SG/Vault competes in; replace, do not depend |
| Secure sharing (server-side incumbents) | Low | They hold the keys; interoperate and migrate, not depend |

The rule that falls out: integrate where the option is standards-based and customer-controllable, and treat any option that requires giving up zero-knowledge or running outside the customer's control as an interoperation or replacement target, not a core dependency.

## Mapping 5: The Wardley Evolution View

Placing the categories on the evolution axis shows where to integrate the commodity and where to build the differentiator (cross-ref: the skill-lifecycle and tool-replacement briefs).

| Evolution Stage | Categories | SG/Vault Stance |
|-----------------|-----------|-----------------|
| Commodity | Object storage, compute, messaging email and SMS | Consume; do not build; pick on cost and portability |
| Product | Identity, secrets management, LLM gateways, version control | Integrate behind abstractions; avoid lock-in |
| Custom to product | Secure sharing and data rooms | Where SG/Vault competes; interoperate then commoditize the next level up |
| Genesis to custom | Zero-knowledge vault-to-vault comms, content-addressed delta sync, PKI-gated capabilities | SG/Vault's own differentiators; build and keep |

The strategic reading matches the tool-replacement thesis: consume the commodities (storage, compute, messaging) on cost and portability, integrate the products (identity, secrets, LLM) behind abstractions, and build and defend only the genesis-to-custom differentiators that are uniquely SG/Vault's, the zero-knowledge, content-addressed, PKI-gated core.

## Mapping 6: The Now, Next, Later Roadmap

Sequencing the integrations by readiness and value.

| Horizon | Integrations | Rationale |
|---------|-------------|-----------|
| Now | S3-compatible storage (R2 or B2), OpenRouter, AWS SES and SNS, GitHub provenance | Quick wins; drop-in, standards-based, high value |
| Next | Open-standard identity plus WorkOS for enterprise SSO, bring-your-own-KMS and Infisical, Bedrock or self-hosted LLM gateway, Twilio or Telnyx for WhatsApp | Strategic bets that need deliberate work |
| Later | Multi-cloud compute portability, data-room interoperation (Papermark, Datasite), the interoperate-then-replace play against Tresorit and Dropbox | High value but high effort; the platform's long game |

## How To Use These Mappings

1. **Start with the disposition map** to decide integrate, interoperate, or build per category.
2. **Use the effort-versus-value quadrant** to sequence: quick wins now, strategic bets deliberately, fill-ins as needed, reconsider the high-effort-low-value.
3. **Check the cost-trajectory mapping** before committing, watching the egress and identity cliffs.
4. **Apply the sovereignty scoring** to keep core dependencies standards-based and zero-knowledge-compatible.
5. **Read the Wardley view** to consume commodities, integrate products, and build only the differentiators.
6. **Follow the now-next-later roadmap** for sequencing.

## Honest Limitations

- The scores and placements are directional judgements from the research, not a quantified model; a real decision needs a workload-specific cost model.
- Vendor facts and prices move (cross-ref: the positioning brief); revisit before committing.
- The quadrant and Wardley placements are simplifications; some vendors span zones.
- The mappings assume SG/Vault's stated principles (no lock-in, zero-knowledge, run-in-customer-account); a change in principle changes the scoring.
