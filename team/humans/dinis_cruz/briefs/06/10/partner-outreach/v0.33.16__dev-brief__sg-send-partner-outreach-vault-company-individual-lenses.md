# The Partner-Outreach Vault: A Targeted Experience Built From The Company And The People

**version** v0.33.16
**date** 10 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Developer (lead), Architect, Product, @Dev (the agent holding the LinkedIn data)
**type** Dev brief

## What This Is

A brief for a vault that reaches out to a specific identified partner company: **build a vault targeted at one partner (LastPass is the worked example) that combines two lenses, the company and the individual contacts, into a personalised experience that is itself the pitch, can be sent privately to contacts and published publicly, and demonstrates the platform while it communicates the message.** It operationalises the partner briefing per company (cross-ref: the v0.33.16 partner-briefing brief), is built by the agent that holds the LinkedIn data (cross-ref: the v0.33.16 linkedin-export-analysis-vault brief), draws on the integration research (cross-ref: the v0.33.16 integration-landscape briefs), and uses the curated-experience idea (cross-ref: the v0.32.7 investor-vaults brief). New contributions: **the per-company outreach vault, the company-and-individual two-lens model, the vault-as-the-pitch experience, and the private-and-public dual distribution.**

## The Goal: Communicate The Message, And Show The Vault Working

Two aims at once, with a clear priority: **communicate the message first, and demonstrate the power of the vault as you do it.**

The project lead: **"this is about creating a vault to reach out to specific companies we have identified as partners. We want to do a couple of things at the same time: really communicate the message, which should always be the priority, but with it, also show the power of the vault and how everything works together."** So the message comes first (cross-ref: the partner-briefing brief), and the medium is the demonstration: the outreach vault both says what the partnership could be and shows the platform in action. LastPass is the running example, a strong potential partner where the project lead has good contacts.

## Built By The Agent That Holds The LinkedIn Data

A direct handoff: **this vault is created by the agent that has access to the LinkedIn data, which maps the contacts.**

The project lead: **"I have four or five contacts there, and a separate session with access to my LinkedIn data, so I can map it there. This vault will be created by that. This is a memo for the agent that has my LinkedIn data."** So this brief is addressed to the implementing agent that holds the LinkedIn export (cross-ref: the linkedin-export-analysis-vault brief): it uses that network data to identify and profile the contacts at the target company and to build the individual lens below.

## Lens 1: The Company

The first slice of the data: **map the target company, its products, product lines, positioning, and strategic direction, and find the synergy and the integration use cases.**

The project lead: **"one lens is the company: map this company with its products, product lines, positioning, the strategic move, ideally connecting to their current moves and direction of travel, even people to work with, their divisions. Where is the synergy? How can we work together? What are the use cases? What is the implementation? What would it look like? A very company-driven focus."**

| Company Lens | Detail |
|--------------|--------|
| Products and product lines | What they offer today |
| Positioning and strategy | Their direction of travel and current moves |
| Divisions and people | Where the relevant work and people sit |
| Synergy | Where SG/Vault and the company fit together |
| Use cases and implementation | What a joint integration looks like |

For LastPass specifically, the project lead names the integration points to map: **"what is their current solution for managing secrets? Where is the integration with the browser? With agents? With the edge? With public and private keys? How can we make that work? These are the flows."** So the company lens grounds the pitch in the partner's real products and strategy (from public sources and the integration research; cross-ref: the integration-landscape briefs) and maps concrete integration points, secrets management, browser, agents, edge, and public-and-private keys, into proposed synergy flows.

## Lens 2: The Individuals

The second slice: **take each direct contact at the company, profile them from LinkedIn, and write a personalised message to their persona.**

The project lead: **"the other lens is each of those individuals, my direct contacts. We put the photos, the descriptions, what we get from LinkedIn, and because I can download their information too, we create a personalised message to their actual persona, explaining what we are trying to do and seeing how they could help. Some might be directly relevant to them, but a lot will not be, so it is how can they help me. These are friendly, people I know."** So the individual lens turns each contact into a personalised approach: their LinkedIn-derived profile (photo, description) and a message written for that person, framed as a friendly ask for help or connection rather than a hard sell, since many will not be the direct buyer.

## The Targeted Experience: The Vault Is The Pitch

The vision, and it is distinctive: **when the contact opens the vault, it is an experience targeted at them, an HTML and infographic page that says who they are and why SG/Send plus their company.**

The project lead: **"imagine when you open the vault, the vault is almost targeted at them. You want an infographic. Remember we can create whatever experience we want with HTML and infographics. We show the page and say, who are you, and why SG/Send plus, in this case, LastPass."** So the outreach vault is not a document attached to a message: it is a curated, targeted experience (cross-ref: the investor-vaults curated-experience and the webify and public-data briefs) built in HTML and infographics that greets the contact, frames the SG/Vault-plus-their-company story, and presents the synergy flows. The vault itself is the pitch and the demonstration.

## Dual Distribution: Direct Messages And Public Publishing

A useful side effect of having nothing confidential: **the vault can be sent privately to the individual contacts and also published publicly as an example of how to work together.**

The project lead: **"I can DM these individuals directly, but I could also publish this publicly, because some of this information I can publish, there is nothing confidential here. This is just an example of how to work together."** So distribution is dual: a private, personalised version sent directly to each contact, and a public version published as an open example of a proposed collaboration. The privacy split matters (see risks): the company synergy pitch is publishable, while the individual contact profiles and personalised messages are for the private, direct version only.

## The Plan

The sequence the project lead sets: **map it out, create the brief, get it working, build a couple of example vaults, and see who connects.**

The project lead: **"let's map this out, create a brief, get the thing working, create a couple of examples of these vaults, and see if we have connection and find partners."** So after this brief, the implementing agent builds a working version, produces a couple of example outreach vaults (LastPass first), and the project lead uses them to open conversations and find partners.

## What This Asks For

1. **Build a per-company outreach vault** (targeted at one partner; LastPass first; the message is the priority and the vault is the demonstration).
2. **Have the LinkedIn-data agent build it** (using the network data to identify and profile the contacts).
3. **Build the company lens** (products, product lines, positioning, strategy, divisions, synergy, use cases, and the integration points: secrets, browser, agents, edge, public-and-private keys).
4. **Build the individual lens** (each direct contact profiled from LinkedIn; a personalised message to their persona; a friendly ask).
5. **Make the opened vault the pitch** (an HTML and infographic experience targeted at the contact: who they are, why SG/Send plus their company).
6. **Support dual distribution** (a private personalised version per contact; a public version as an open example).
7. **Split public from private** (publish the company synergy pitch; keep individual profiles and messages private).
8. **Build a couple of examples and reach out** (LastPass first; then find partners).

Estimated effort: moderate, and a strong demonstration; the LinkedIn-data vault, the HTML and infographic experiences, the public-publishing path, and the integration research exist; the contribution is the two-lens outreach vault and the targeted experience. The output is a working outreach vault, a couple of examples, and live partner conversations.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a generic pitch.** Targeted per company and per contact.
- **Not a document attachment.** The vault itself is the experience and the pitch.
- **Not a hard sell.** A friendly ask of known contacts; how can they help.
- **Not all public.** The company pitch is publishable; the contact profiles are private.
- **Not invented.** The company analysis is grounded in public sources, not fabricated strategy.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Individual contact data must stay private.** Profiles, photos, and personalised messages about real people should not be published. Mitigation: the public-private split; publish only the company synergy pitch; keep the individual lens in the private, direct version (cross-ref: the linkedin-export-analysis-vault privacy boundary).

**Risk 2: Company claims must be accurate.** A pitch built on a wrong reading of the partner's strategy backfires. Mitigation: ground the company lens in public sources and the integration research; do not invent their roadmap; frame synergy as a proposal, not a fact.

**Risk 3: Personalised outreach can feel intrusive.** An over-targeted experience can unsettle even a friendly contact. Mitigation: these are people the project lead knows; keep it warm and helpful; the ask is collaboration, not conversion.

**Risk 4: Public examples represent the partner.** A public collaboration example names a real company. Mitigation: present it as a proposal and an example of working together; nothing confidential; respectful and accurate.

**Risk 5: Scope can balloon across many companies.** A vault per company per contact is a lot. Mitigation: start with one example (LastPass); template it; reuse across companies (cross-ref: the v0.33.2 vault-release-manifest brief).
