# Recruiting Official Partners: A Briefing Document For Identity And Key Management

**version** v0.33.16
**date** 10 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Product, Architect, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief

---

## What This Is

A follow-up to the integration research: **produce a partner briefing document, sharable as a PDF, an infographic, and a LinkedIn post, that recruits official technological partners to implement identity and authentication and secrets and key management for the healthcare vault use case, on a fully public, open-source, no-NDA, collaboration-first basis.** It follows the integration landscape research and mappings (cross-ref: the v0.33.16 integration-landscape-market-research-positioning and integration-landscape-mappings briefs) and addresses the key-management gap (cross-ref: the v0.33.16 recipient-key-management-options and consumer-key-storage research briefs). The briefing document itself is produced as a companion artifact (cross-ref: the partner-briefing-identity-and-key-management artifact). New contributions: **the partner-outreach plan, the rules of engagement, the focus on identity then secrets, and the briefing-document format.**

## The Goal: Find Partners, Do Not Build It Alone

The motivation, stated plainly: **the platform has created a real need it does not yet have a good answer for, and the right move is to partner rather than build it all alone.**

The project lead: **"this is a perfect example of I should not have to do all this by myself. The idea is to integrate with existing companies and startups in this field, find the ones that integrate with us, and do joint projects, joint collaborations, even just a proof of concept. We are now in a really good position to get some partnerships and to get some of these solutions officially made, with official integrators."** So the plan is outreach: use the project lead's network and direct approaches to recruit partners for joint proofs of concept and official integrations, bringing them into the projects, rather than building identity and key management from scratch.

## The Rules Of Engagement

The non-negotiable basis for any partnership: **everything is public, everything is open source, no NDAs, and it is all about collaboration.**

The project lead: **"the rules of the game, from my point of view, is everything I do is public, everything I do is open source, no NDAs I want to sign, and it is all about collaboration."** So the briefing document states these terms up front (cross-ref: the v0.32.3 open-source-not-free and the sovereignty briefs): partners collaborate in the open, on open-source work, without NDAs. This is a filter as much as a principle, it selects for partners who can work this way.

## The Focus: Identity First, Then Secrets And Keys

What the platform actually needs, in order: **identity and authentication first (validating and logging in users), then secrets management and a good home for public and private keys.**

The project lead: **"let's focus first mainly on identity, basically logging in users, authentication, and then authorization. Authentication and secrets management is really what we are looking for. The other one we should also be looking for is a good place to manage public and private keys."**

| Need | What It Covers |
|------|----------------|
| Identity and authentication | Logging in and validating users; the first priority |
| Authorization | Who can do what; follows identity |
| Secrets management | Holding secrets safely |
| Public and private key management | A good home for key pairs and key deployment |

So the brief targets identity and authentication first, then secrets and key management, including the public-and-private-key deployment problem (cross-ref: the recipient-key-management-options brief). Solutions that span several of these are ideal, and cloud providers are explicitly in scope because they already hold several pieces of the puzzle. Two or three providers collaborating is an acceptable answer.

## The Key Technical Requirement

What a partner's solution must do: **validate the user well enough for medical data, for example OAuth with two-factor authentication, while keeping the patient and clinician journeys as simple as possible.**

The project lead: **"the key thing is how do we validate the user, so that the user is logged in, for example using OAuth with two-factor, and all those requirements, and ideally this is for medical data. We want the patient and the clinician journey to be as simple as possible, that is our objective. It is less about cost, more about what tech experience that already exists today we should be leveraging."** So the bar is medical-data-grade user validation (OAuth, two-factor, and the related requirements) delivered through the simplest possible journeys, and the selection criterion is the technology experience that already exists, not cost.

## The Format

How the document should read: **a simple, professional, matter-of-fact one-two-three that maps what we are doing, what we have shipped, and what we need, with the patient and clinician journeys, the flows, and ASCII art the infographic can pick up.**

The project lead: **"a very clear one-two-three document that maps what we are doing, what we have, what we shipped, because it is already working, and what we need. Keep it simple, we should be leveraging their platforms. Link to the work we are doing. Professional focus, matter-of-fact: we are doing this, we just want to find official technological partners to implement it. Describe what we have, the flows, the patient journey and the doctor journey, and do it in a nice infographic style, in this case ASCII art that the infographic will pick up."** So the briefing document is deliberately simple and professional: a one-two-three of what is shipped and what is needed, the two journeys kept simple, the flows as ASCII art for the infographic, and a clear ask, sharable as a PDF and a LinkedIn post.

## What This Asks For

1. **Produce the partner briefing document** (the companion artifact; PDF, infographic, and LinkedIn-post ready).
2. **State the rules of engagement** (public, open source, no NDAs, collaboration).
3. **Focus the ask on identity and authentication first** (then secrets and public-and-private-key management).
4. **State the technical bar** (medical-data-grade validation: OAuth, two-factor; simplest journeys).
5. **Keep it a simple one-two-three** (what we do, what we shipped, what we need).
6. **Include the patient and clinician journeys and the flows** (as ASCII art for the infographic).
7. **Invite multiple providers, including cloud** (two or three collaborating is fine).
8. **Link to the work and make it sharable** (a PDF and a LinkedIn post).

Estimated effort: a document and outreach exercise, not a build; the integration research and the key-management options exist; the contribution is the partner-facing briefing and the outreach. The output is the briefing document plus a LinkedIn post and a list of partners to approach.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a build-it-alone plan.** The point is to partner and integrate.
- **Not NDA-bound or private.** Public, open source, no NDAs.
- **Not complicated.** A simple one-two-three leveraging partners' platforms.
- **Not cost-led.** Led by the technology experience that already exists.
- **Not one rigid partner.** Multiple providers, including cloud, can collaborate.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: The market may be thin for some needs.** Public-and-private-key deployment may have few good answers. Mitigation: the outreach is partly to create the demand and start the conversation (cross-ref: the recipient-key-management-options market observation).

**Risk 2: The public, no-NDA stance narrows the field.** Some partners will not work this way. Mitigation: that is a deliberate filter; it selects collaborators who can.

**Risk 3: A sensitive domain raises the bar.** Medical-data-grade validation is demanding. Mitigation: state the bar clearly (OAuth, two-factor); lean on partners who already meet it.

**Risk 4: A briefing document can drift complex.** Too much detail loses the reader. Mitigation: keep the one-two-three simple; flows as ASCII; detail in the linked work.
