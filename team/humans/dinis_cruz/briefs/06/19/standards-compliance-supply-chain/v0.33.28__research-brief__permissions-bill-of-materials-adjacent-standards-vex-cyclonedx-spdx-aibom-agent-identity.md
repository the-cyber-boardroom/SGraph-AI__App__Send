## The Brief Framing

What was run: **the quick adjacent-standards scan, to connect and augment, not invent.**

The project lead seeded a scan of similar industry ways to map this, to connect to and implement against. This brief reports it, with the consistent finding that the right move is to extend the rich existing ecosystem rather than create a new standard.

## The SBOM Ecosystem Is Mature And Extensible

The foundation: **two mature, modernising formats, with precedent for new BOM types.**

CycloneDX is an OWASP project that has been ratified as the Ecma International standard ECMA-424, maintained with Ecma's technical committee for software and system transparency, and it already supports a family of bill-of-materials types: software, software-as-a-service, hardware, machine-learning, cryptography, manufacturing, operations, a bill of vulnerabilities, vulnerability disclosure reports, VEX, and attestations. SPDX is the ISO/IEC 5962:2021 international standard, now at version 3.0.1 with a 3.1 release candidate, and version 3.0 introduced profiles for security, build attestation, licensing, and AI model and dataset use, with every element independently referenceable, which is effectively graph-shaped. The existence of the cryptography and machine-learning bills of materials shows there is a clear, accepted path to adding a new BOM type, which is where a permissions bill of materials belongs.

## VEX Is The Closest Analog

The key finding: **VEX already does for vulnerabilities what the PBOM proposes for permissions.**

VEX, the Vulnerability Exploitability eXchange, is a companion to the SBOM that communicates whether a known vulnerability in a component is actually exploitable in a specific product, using statuses such as not affected, affected, fixed, and under investigation, with justifications like the vulnerable code not being in the execute path, upstream sanitisation, or sandboxing. It exists precisely because an SBOM alone makes every listed CVE look equally urgent when most are not reachable in a given deployment. VEX is a concept implemented across three formats, OpenVEX, CycloneDX VEX, and CSAF VEX, and CSAF itself is now the ISO/IEC 20153:2025 standard. This is the same reasoning the permissions bill of materials applies: the permissions bill of materials extends VEX's exploitability gating with the authorisation dimension, since reachability plus permission decides whether a vulnerability can actually be weaponised. The PBOM should connect to and complement VEX, not duplicate it.

## Provenance, Attestation, And Controls

The supporting layers: **SLSA, attestations, and OSCAL.**

Provenance and build integrity are covered by SLSA and in-toto, which many CI systems emit natively, and CycloneDX attestations and signing turn a BOM into a verifiable record. Controls and assessment are covered by NIST's OSCAL, the machine-readable language for control catalogues, baselines, and system security plans. The permissions bill of materials sits alongside these: provenance for how it was produced, attestation for trust, and OSCAL for mapping the authorised blast radius to control requirements.

## The Authorization-Inventory Precedent

A near neighbour, and how it differs: **Policy SBOM inventories authorization policy, not the blast radius.**

There is a Policy SBOM concept, which inventories the authorization policy modules, sources, versions, and dependencies in an Open Policy Agent bundle. It is a useful precedent for treating authorization as something to inventory, but it is different from a permissions bill of materials: Policy SBOM lists the policy code, whereas the permissions bill of materials describes what the granted authorisation actually enables, the universe of what can be done, which is the blast radius. The two are complementary.

## The AI And Agent BOM Frontier

The nearest active work: **AI bills of materials and agentic AIBOMs.**

There is active 2026 work extending SBOMs into AI bills of materials and agentic AIBOMs, with schema extensions for agentic orchestration, alongside the SPDX AI profile and the CycloneDX machine-learning bill of materials. The software-as-a-service bill of materials, which inventories services and endpoints, is the nearest existing type to the agent dimension. A permissions bill of materials would extend this frontier, adding the authorisation and blast-radius layer to the agent and service inventories.

## The Agent-Security And Identity Standards Layer

The fast-moving context, and the gap: **the risk frameworks exist, but not the artifact.**

A dense layer of 2026 standards addresses agent security and identity: the OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications (2026), covering identity and privilege abuse, tool misuse, and excessive agency; the OWASP Non-Human Identity Top 10 (2025), on overprivilege and long-lived secrets, which notes that agents mostly amplify existing vulnerabilities rather than create new ones; the NIST AI Agent Standards Initiative (February 2026) and its NCCoE concept paper on agent identity and authorisation, drawing on Next Generation Access Control, zero-trust, and MCP; CoSAI's agentic identity and access management work; the MCP security baselines, OWASP MCP Top 10 and SAFE-MCP, with MCP moving to scoped OAuth tokens; and MITRE ATLAS for AI threat modelling. These describe what to govern and stress continuous runtime authorisation and revocation through delegation chains, but they do not provide a standardised artifact that inventories an agent's authorised blast radius. That artifact is the gap the permissions bill of materials fills. (Notably, the OWASP and NHI framing that agents amplify existing vulnerabilities matches the series' own always-existed-in-software thesis.)

## The Gap And The Recommendation

The conclusion: **augment, do not invent; place the PBOM in the existing ecosystem.**

No existing standard provides a machine-readable, graph-shaped inventory of an agent's authorised blast radius. The recommendation, consistent with the project lead's principle, is to propose the permissions bill of materials as a BOM type or profile within the existing ecosystem, expressed in CycloneDX or as an SPDX profile, wired to VEX for exploitability gating, to the software-as-a-service and AI bills of materials for the agent and service inventory, and to the agent-identity standards, Next Generation Access Control, MCP scoped tokens, and the Non-Human Identity work, for the authorisation semantics. This connects the permissions bill of materials to the standards regulators and buyers already recognise, rather than asking them to adopt something new.

## How It Connects To The Series

The throughlines: **the PBOM extends VEX, the agent standards describe the risks, and the moment of authorisation aligns with continuous runtime authorisation.**

The permissions bill of materials extends VEX's exploitability gating with the permission dimension; the agent-security standards describe the risks the permissions bill of materials would inventory; and the series' moment-of-authorisation principle aligns with the NIST and research direction toward continuous runtime authorisation and pre-action authorisation rather than one-time grants (cross-ref: the agent-authorisation-blast-radius and risk-acceptance briefs).

## Honest Notes

- **Fast-moving and post-cutoff.** The agent-identity standards are early and converging over the next eighteen to twenty-four months; the permissions bill of materials should ride that convergence, not pre-empt it.
- **Format fragmentation.** VEX and SBOM exist in multiple formats; multi-format support is wiser than betting on one.
- **Augment, do not invent.** The strongest position is a profile or BOM type in CycloneDX or SPDX, connected to VEX and the agent standards.
