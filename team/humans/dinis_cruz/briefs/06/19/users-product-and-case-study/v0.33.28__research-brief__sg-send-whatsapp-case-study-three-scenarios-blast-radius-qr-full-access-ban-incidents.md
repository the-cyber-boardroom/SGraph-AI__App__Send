## What This Is

The WhatsApp case study that instantiates the assessment template: **linking an agent to WhatsApp through the QR or linked-device mode gives it near-complete access to the account, all messages, media, and history, and the ability to send as you, so much for end-to-end encryption once an agent sits on either side; three scenarios are mapped and compared, a personal number, a dedicated number, and an official Business account, each with a different blast radius and a different set of parties who must accept the risk, including people who never consented; and it is grounded in real incidents, WhatsApp's own ban policy for unofficial linking, a documented account ban, runaway agents messaging everyone, and the GhostPairing attack as the security mirror of the same mechanism; the research becomes the evidence vault for WhatsApp.** It is the practical example the project lead asked for, showing what an assessment looks like in practice (cross-ref: the v0.33.28 assessment-template brief and the v0.33.40 agent-authorisation-blast-radius and risk-acceptance briefs). Sources are paraphrased and listed. New contributions: **the three-scenario comparison, the consent finding, and the incident evidence.**

## The Framing

What was asked: **research, a prototype, and an example, on WhatsApp.**

The project lead: **"do a research and a prototype and an example of the permission mappings, the risk assessment, and the blast radius for WhatsApp. I want three scenarios, and map the risk between them as a case study, because it is a good example of the blast radius and the understanding of permissions. Simulate what these assessments would actually look like in practice."** So WhatsApp is the test case for the whole approach.

## How The Integration Actually Works

The mechanism, and why it is alarming: **linking gives full account access.**

When people connect an agent to WhatsApp, they typically use the QR or linked-device mode, which syncs a new device to the account. A linked device has near-complete access to the account data: it can read historical conversations, receive messages in real time, and view all media. The session that the agent holds includes the encryption keys and device credentials, which is to say it grants full access to the account. The project lead: **"they use the QR mode, the linked devices, which gives the agent full access to the person's WhatsApp environment, all the messages, all the information, all the encrypted data. So much for having encrypted data, when you should have agents on the other side."** So the much-advertised end-to-end encryption protects the transport, but an authorised linked device sits inside the encryption boundary, and an agent there sees everything.

## The Consent Problem

The blast radius reaches people who never agreed: **the agent on the other side.**

The project lead: **"when I chat with somebody who has an agent on their side, I never gave permission for that agent to access my messages with that person."** So the blast radius is not contained to the account owner. Every contact's messages, and the shared social graph, are exposed to an agent that the contact never authorised, which is a second and third-order side effect that the assessment must surface (cross-ref: the side-effects brief). The same mechanism, linking a device gives full access without breaking encryption, is exactly what the GhostPairing attack abuses, tricking users into linking a rogue device; the agent case is the consensual version of the same risk.

## The Three Scenarios

The heart of the case study: **personal, dedicated, and Business, compared.**

| Dimension | A: Personal number | B: Dedicated number | C: Business account |
|-----------|--------------------|--------------------|---------------------|
| How it connects | QR or linked-device bridge (unofficial) | QR or linked-device bridge on a separate number | Official Business Platform / Cloud API via a provider |
| Official support | No | No | Yes |
| Ban risk | High (unofficial linking) | High, but isolated to that number | None (the supported path) |
| Data in reach | Your entire personal account, all chats, contacts, media | Only what reaches the dedicated number | Only the business conversations, with controls |
| Acts as | You, to everyone you know | The dedicated identity | The business, with per-agent attribution |
| Cost | Free, but highest risk | Cost of a second number | Per-message billing, free service window when the customer initiates |
| Who must accept | You, and implicitly everyone you chat with | You, and contacts of that number | The business, with auditability and device info |

**Scenario A, personal number.** An agent linked to your personal account inherits your whole WhatsApp life: every conversation, contact, and piece of media, and the ability to message anyone as you. The blast radius is maximal and the ban risk is high, because this is unofficial linking. Everyone you have ever messaged is implicated.

**Scenario B, dedicated number.** Using a separate number for the agent isolates the blast radius from your personal account, which is the standard harm-reduction advice. The agent still has full access to that dedicated account, and the linking is still unofficial, so the ban risk remains, but it is contained to a number that is not your personal life.

**Scenario C, Business account.** The official WhatsApp Business Platform, through a provider, is the supported path with no ban risk. It offers proper multi-agent controls, per-agent message attribution, and visible device information, and it bills per message with a free window when the customer messages first. The blast radius is bounded to business conversations and is auditable, at the cost of money and setup.

## Real-World Incidents As Evidence

The case study is grounded, not hypothetical:

- **WhatsApp's own policy.** WhatsApp warns that linking an account to an unofficial app or website, now or in the past, may result in a temporary or permanent ban, because such links can be used to send spam and scams.
- **A documented ban.** A developer using an unofficial linking library reported that WhatsApp flagged the account as linked to an unauthorised application, restricted device linking, and disconnected all previously linked devices.
- **Runaway agents.** The project lead noted accounts banned after an agent messaged everyone and things went out of control; the official guidance for unofficial bridges explicitly warns against bulk or unsolicited outbound messaging for exactly this reason.
- **The security mirror.** The GhostPairing campaign (late 2025) abused the same device-linking flow to hijack accounts, gaining full WhatsApp Web access, reading synced chats, and impersonating victims, without ever breaking encryption. The agent integration is the consensual form of the same full-access mechanism.

## The Blast-Radius And Risk-Acceptance Mapping

Applying the template: the permission map for the personal scenario shows read-messages enabling exposure of all conversations, send-messages enabling impersonation and contact harm, and read-contacts exposing the social graph of people who never consented. The blast-radius score is highest for Scenario A, contained for B, and bounded and auditable for C. The risk-acceptance flow makes the account owner accept explicitly, and surfaces that the people they chat with are implicated, which in a workplace would flow upward to whoever owns the communications risk. The only variables are which scenario is chosen and how long the linking stands.

## The Evidence Vault

This research becomes the WhatsApp evidence vault: a git-compatible vault holding the three scenarios, the permission maps, the blast-radius scores, and the linked incidents, with provenance over time. It is the first concrete instance of the proactive evidence database, built from public information, ready to power both the assessment and a future agent-intel feed (cross-ref: the proactive-evidence-database brief).

## Honest Notes

- **The bridge mechanics evolve.** WhatsApp updates its Web protocol and its detection regularly, so the unofficial scenarios shift.
- **Ban risk is real but variable.** It depends on behaviour and detection; the policy is clear that unofficial linking risks a ban.
- **Pricing is region-specific.** The Business account costs vary by market and message category.
- **The consent problem is structural.** It is inherent to an agent sitting inside the encryption boundary, not a configuration error.
