## What This Is

The run-everywhere principle, one of the most important in the architecture: **the solution runs anywhere, on a laptop, a serverless function, our environment, a customer's environment, Docker, Kubernetes, or any cloud, because at its heart it is only compute and storage, plus maybe DNS or a gateway, which makes it radically portable; this opens many business models and rests on the distinction that customers do not want to maintain it but do want to run it in their own environment, so we maintain it and make it effortless, even through cloud-provider marketplaces, for mass deployability and customization; it defuses the startup-killing curse of big-customer feature demands, because customer-specific customizations live in their own paid forks that keep the main roadmap unpolluted, and because it is open source the relevant bits flow back, so forking is an opportunity not a threat; it delivers real sovereignty and no lock-in, which is why even the provisioning components and the CI pipeline must be open source and productized for the customer to run themselves; and the moat is that the moment a customer touches it they own it, so they would rather we own it for them.** It captures the run-everywhere principle teased in the need-to-ship brief (cross-ref: the v0.33.28 need-to-ship brief, the v0.33.40 open-source-strategy and commercial-model briefs, and the sovereignty thesis). New contributions: **the compute-and-storage portability argument, the run-not-maintain distinction, the forks-defuse-the-curse point, and the ownership moat.**

## The Framing

What this is: **a key principle, worth its own document.**

The project lead: **"this is one of the key principles in this architecture, alongside the no-cost and the open-sourceness: the fact that the solution runs everywhere. By everywhere I mean a laptop, a serverless function, our environment, a customer environment, Docker, Kubernetes, every cloud provider."** So run-everywhere sits with no-cost and open-source as a foundational principle.

## Why It Runs Everywhere

The reason it is portable: **the primitives are tiny.**

The project lead: **"the reason it is possible is that at the heart the solution has very small primitives: compute, storage, maybe DNS, maybe a load balancer or gateway if needed. But the core principle is compute and storage, and that is kind of it. That means it is very portable to deploy everywhere."** So the architecture deliberately depends on only the two primitives present in every environment, which is what makes it run anywhere (cross-ref: the no-databases, serverless constraint in the need-to-ship brief).

## Run, Not Maintain

The distinction that unlocks the model: **customers want to run it, not maintain it.**

The project lead: **"it is important to distinguish: customers do not want to maintain this, but they want to run it in their environment. That is different. We maintain it, we make it super easy, maybe even put it on marketplaces, so a customer just goes to the provider marketplace, buys it, and deploys it without worrying about maintainability. That is the mass deployability and mass customization."** So the offering is run-it-yourself but maintained-by-us, delivered through cloud-provider marketplaces, which is exactly what customers want (cross-ref: the commercial-model hosting tiers).

## Forks Defuse The Big-Customer Curse

A structural advantage: **customizations stay in the customer's fork.**

The project lead: **"a lot of roadmap items kill startups, the curse of bigger customers who demand features that occupy the development team but only matter for that one customer. Here you do not worry, because the thing runs in the customer environment, so you create customer-specific customizations that only run for them, which they pay for. Then, since it is open source, you take the relevant bits back to the main platform. The main roadmap does not get polluted, because the customizations stay in their forks. Forking is not a threat, it is an opportunity, one of the best features we have."** So run-everywhere plus open source turns the classic startup-killing dynamic into an asset: big-customer demands become paid forks, not roadmap pollution, and the good parts flow back (cross-ref: the open-source-strategy and curse-of-knowledge concepts).

## Sovereignty And No Lock-In

The principle it serves: **you cannot claim no lock-in while locking people in.**

The project lead: **"this is the sovereignty: it is independent. You cannot say I am not locking you in when you are locked into the platform. This is also why even the components to provision all the bits of the platform have to be open source, because the customer has to run them themselves. It is not enough to say, here are the core bits; you still need the provisioning. So the CI pipeline is also a product, and it needs to be productized and shared with the customer."** So genuine sovereignty requires that not only the product but its provisioning and CI pipeline are open source and productized, so a customer can stand the whole thing up themselves (cross-ref: the sovereignty thesis and the need-to-ship CI-as-asset point).

## The Ownership Moat

The subtle stickiness: **touch it and you own it, so they would rather not.**

The project lead: **"this introduces very good sticky points and moats, because when the customer touches it, they own it. So they do not want to own it; we will own it for them."** So the moat is not lock-in but maintenance: customers can run and own it, but the moment they touch it they take on the ownership burden, and they would rather pay us to carry that, which is a durable, sovereignty-respecting moat (cross-ref: the rising-tide maintenance hook).

## Why It Matters Across The Board

The reach of the principle: **it affects engineering, business, and everything.**

The project lead: **"run everywhere is one of the most important principles, because it affects engineering, affects business, affects all sorts of things. It is very good for customers, and it opens lots of business models, revenue streams, and much better distribution."** So this is not only an architecture choice; it shapes the engineering, the commercial model, and the distribution at once.

## What This Asks For

1. **Depend only on compute and storage** (plus DNS or a gateway if needed).
2. **Run in any environment** (laptop, serverless, customer, Docker, Kubernetes, any cloud).
3. **Offer run-it-yourself, maintained-by-us** (the key distinction).
4. **Use cloud-provider marketplaces** (effortless deployment).
5. **Keep customizations in customer forks** (unpolluted roadmap, bits flow back).
6. **Open-source the provisioning and CI pipeline** (productized for customers).
7. **Lean on the ownership moat** (we own it for them).

Estimated effort: high but foundational; the primitives keep it portable; the extra work is productizing the provisioning and CI pipeline.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not platform lock-in.** It is sovereignty and run-it-yourself.
- **Not core-only open source.** Provisioning and CI are open too.
- **Not maintenance dumped on customers.** We maintain it for them.
- **Not roadmap pollution.** Customizations stay in forks.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: run-everywhere multiplies the support surface.** Many environments. Mitigation: the customer runs it; we maintain centrally and ship updates.

**Tension 2: forks could fragment.** Many customer forks. Mitigation: open source lets the good bits flow back; the core stays clean.

**Tension 3: provisioning-as-product is extra work.** The CI pipeline must ship too. Mitigation: it is required for genuine sovereignty, and it is also a product.

**Tension 4: the moat depends on us being the easiest maintainer.** If we are not, they self-host. Mitigation: make our maintenance the obvious, effortless choice.
