# The Need To Focus: Profitability First, Projections Later

**version** v0.33.28
**date** 19 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Founders, Investors, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief (in the series)

---

## What This Is

The discipline of focus, applied to business plans and investment: **when creating documents like business plans, be explicit about the phase and maturity we are in, because the main priority is reaching profitability with the first batch of users using the product regularly, and that should be the laser-sharp focus; until we know the traction, the product lines, the services, the cost lines, and what users actually buy, financial projections and future predictions are made-up, because we do not yet have the data; the path is to discover the market and ship quasi-daily, investment only accelerates the path to profitability, and the investors we want are the ones focused on that path and on discovering the product, not on the mature-company structures we can build later.** It sharpens the Explorer-phase conclusion of the Wardley brief into a standalone thesis on focus (cross-ref: the v0.33.28 Wardley-maps brief and the v0.33.40 investment brief). New contributions: **profitability as the single priority, projections-are-pointless-without-data, and the right-investor test.**

## The Framing

What this is about: **being explicit about the phase when you write the plan.**

The project lead: **"the next one is the need to focus when creating documents like business plans and investment mappings. It is very important to be explicit about the phase we are in and the maturity of what is currently happening, taking into account the Wardley maps and the Explorer phase we are in. There is no point creating plans for a company that still has key pieces of the puzzle missing."** So the plan must match the maturity; an Explorer-stage company should not write a mature-company plan.

## Profitability Is The Priority

The single focus: **reach profitability, with real, regular users.**

The project lead: **"the main priority is reaching profitability, the first batch of users using the product regularly, and that should be the laser-sharp focus. Being profitable here is super critical."** So profitability and a first cohort of repeat users is the one goal that orders everything else.

## Why Projections Are Pointless Now

The hard truth: **without the data, predictions are made-up.**

The project lead: **"until we know where the traction is, where the product lines are, what the services are, what the cost lines are, and what users are actually buying, there is no point creating predictions about the future, because they are just made-up. Financial projections are pointless, because we do not know what will hit or what the market will buy. We do not know yet. Maybe in a couple of months we will, maybe even before the first batch of investment, but right now we do not have the data, so there is no point spending time and resources on those documents."** So the projections are deferred not out of laziness but because the inputs do not exist yet (cross-ref: the Wardley brief, on deferring Town-Planner artifacts).

## Discover The Market, Ship Daily

What to do instead: **go to market and ship to discover.**

The project lead: **"we have very clear ideas for the kind of service we can provide, but the super-critical thing is to discover the market and what can be done. At this stage it is more important to go to market, to ship versions all the time, quasi-daily, so that you discover. Only when you have that can you think of the next phase."** So the work is discovery through shipping, not planning in the abstract.

## Investment Only Accelerates

What money is for: **acceleration, not a change of direction.**

The project lead: **"if you get investment, it is just to accelerate that, to accelerate the number of happy customers and reach the path of profitability. From a founder strategy point of view, that is what they should focus on whether they get investment or not. The focus should be getting to that profitable go-to-market release."** So the founder focus is identical with or without investment, and investment simply buys speed toward profitability (cross-ref: the investment brief).

## The Right Investors

A selection test: **the investors you want focus on the same things.**

The project lead: **"you could argue that the investors you want are the ones going to focus on the profitability path and on discovering the product, not on other things you can do later."** So the focus doubles as an investor filter: the right partners care about the path to profitability and product discovery, and the wrong ones push for the mature-company artifacts that do not yet have inputs.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not anti-planning.** It is planning matched to the phase.
- **Not anti-investment.** Investment accelerates the same focus.
- **Not a forecast.** It explains why forecasts are premature.
- **Not permanent.** When the data arrives, the next phase begins.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: investors and boards often expect projections.** Some will ask for a five-year model. Mitigation: show the Explorer plan and the maps, and use the request as an investor-fit signal.

**Tension 2: profitability-first versus growth-investing norms.** Some investors prize growth over profitability. Mitigation: this names the kind of investor that fits, and the kind that does not.

**Tension 3: focus means saying no.** Discovery work is tempting to broaden. Mitigation: the single priority, profitable repeat usage, orders the trade-offs.
