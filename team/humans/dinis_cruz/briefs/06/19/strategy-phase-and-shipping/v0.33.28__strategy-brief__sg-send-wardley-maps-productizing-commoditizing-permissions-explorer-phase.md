## What This Is

The Wardley-mapping pass for situational awareness and strategy: **the most important workflow is to see what we are productising and commoditising, so the maps start from the current permissions and risk landscape, which today is genesis-at-best and custom-built, with hope-driven development visible as low maturity, and then show SGraph commoditising and re-productising a whole range of activities, from permission mapping and distribution to blast-radius understanding, risk acceptance, stakeholder communication, and the feedback loops that reward fewer privileges; once that base is commoditised, the maps show the higher-order opportunities it unlocks, which is where the market actually is, because the capability lets agents be deployed safely and accelerates adoption that risk and side effects are currently blocking; and the maps also place our own offering on the evolution axis, where, from a service-offering point of view, we are still in the Explorer phase, discovering product-market fit, so the first focus is PMF, end-to-end flows, and community-to-profitability, not Town-Planner activities we cannot yet know.** It captures the Wardley memo and, because the latest Mermaid now supports Wardley maps, includes them as renderable maps (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 agent-authorisation-blast-radius, risk-acceptance, and rising-tide briefs, and the Wardley explorer-to-town-planner concept). New contributions: **the four maps, the productise-and-commoditise framing, and the Explorer-phase strategic conclusion.**

## The Series Framing

What this is for: **mapping the landscape, our situational awareness, and the strategy.**

The project lead: **"we need one on Wardley maps, the one where we map out the landscape and our situational awareness and the strategy, a series of maps that show the position, the flows, and how everything fits together. And the cool thing is the latest Mermaid actually supports Wardley maps, so we can have a go at visualising them. This will impact what I want to talk about next, the business plan and other things."** So this is the strategic backbone, and it feeds directly into the business-plan discussion.

## The Core Question: What Are We Productising And Commoditising

The workflow that matters most: **see what we commoditise and re-productize.**

The project lead: **"the most important workflow is to understand what we are productising and commoditising. Today, when you do not use the workflows we describe, you have a genesis-at-best, custom-built solution for understanding permissions, for provisioning them, for controlling what is going on. What we are doing in this project is commoditising and re-productising a whole number of activities: permission mappings, permission distribution, blast-radius understanding, risk acceptance, communication to the many stakeholders, and the feedback loops that reward companies for asking for fewer privileges, for securing agents, and for not doing hope-driven development."** So the strategic claim is that SGraph takes a set of bespoke, immature activities and moves them rightward into products and commodities.

The project lead: **"once you commoditise this, you map the higher-order components that become possible, because that is what happens with Wardley maps: when something is commoditised, a higher order of activities appears, and that is where the market opportunities are. The analogy is that this capability accelerates things, because it lets agents be deployed safely. One of the biggest reasons agents are not widely deployed is the risk, the side effects, that it is too dangerous in real applications and the guardrails become a problem. This solution helps with that."** So the higher order, accelerated adoption, safe deployment at scale, the agent-intel feed, risk underwriting, and a marketplace, is the opportunity, and it only exists once the base is commoditised.

## Alternate Lenses

A reminder from the memo: **evolution is not the only axis.**

The project lead: **"in Wardley maps the evolution does not always need to be genesis, custom-built, product, commodity. You can have other axes: visibility from not-visible to fully-visible, or risk from not-understood to fully-accepted. So you can have multiple angles on this."** So the same components can be mapped against a visibility axis (from invisible permissions to fully visible blast radius) and a risk axis (from not-understood to mapped to fully-accepted), which are useful analytical overlays even though the maps above use the standard evolution axis so they render.

The project lead: **"the second important part of Wardley maps is which phase we are in: Explorer, Villager, or Town Planner. From a service-offering point of view, I would argue we are still in the Explorer phase. We are still discovering the product and product-market fit. We have a bunch of hypotheses, but not the full market. So the first focus is to discover product-market fit, to get the community to profitability, to service customers and do the end-to-end flows. That is where the focus and the investment should be first."** (Explorer, Villager, and Town Planner map to Wardley's Pioneers, Settlers, and Town Planners.)

## What This Means For The Business Plan

The bridge to the previous discussion: **defer the Town-Planner artifacts; do the Explorer plan.**

The project lead: **"there is no point doing things that are typical Villager and Town Planner company maturity when you are still dealing with Explorer ambiguity. Do not spend time creating things that are pure hypothesis you just cannot know, because a lot depends on how everything flies."** This directly answers the earlier question of what is missing for a business plan: some of the missing pieces are Town-Planner artifacts that should be deferred, not rushed. A detailed five-year financial model, a precise market-sizing, and a rigid roadmap are low-value under Explorer ambiguity. What is worth doing now is the Explorer plan: the beachhead and first customers, the end-to-end flows, the evidence of product-market fit, community-to-profitability, and the moat stated as a hypothesis to test rather than a claim to defend. The maps are the situational awareness that plan is built on.

## Note On Tooling

These maps are written in Mermaid's Wardley syntax, declared as the wardley-beta diagram type, available in Mermaid v11.14 and later, which is what the repository's renderer ships. Coordinates are visibility then evolution, the opposite of the usual x,y order, with visibility from infrastructure at the bottom to user-facing at the top and evolution from genesis on the left to commodity on the right. The same component, anchor, link, evolve, and note lines also load directly into OnlineWardleyMaps if a different renderer is preferred. These are a first pass, meant to be argued with and iterated.

## What This Asks For

1. **Map what we productise and commoditise** (the core workflow).
2. **Map the current state** (genesis and custom-built today).
3. **Map the commoditisation play** (move the activities rightward, add feedback loops).
4. **Map the higher-order opportunities** (where the market is).
5. **Use the alternate lenses** (visibility and risk axes).
6. **Place the offering honestly** (we are in Explorer).
7. **Scope the business plan to Explorer** (PMF and end-to-end flows first).

Estimated effort: low to produce and iterate the maps; the value is in the shared situational awareness and the discipline of mapping to the right maturity.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a finished strategy.** It is situational awareness to argue with.
- **Not a Town-Planner business plan.** It scopes the plan to Explorer.
- **Not a single axis.** Visibility and risk are valid overlays.
- **Not static.** The maps are a first pass to iterate.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: Explorer discipline versus investor expectations.** Investors often want Town-Planner artifacts. Mitigation: show the maps and the Explorer plan, and frame deferred artifacts as deliberate.

**Tension 2: the higher-order opportunities are hypotheses.** They depend on commoditisation succeeding. Mitigation: map them as possibilities, not commitments.

**Tension 3: map accuracy.** Component positions are judgement calls. Mitigation: these are a first pass, meant to be challenged.

**Tension 4: tooling currency.** Wardley support in Mermaid is recent. Mitigation: OnlineWardleyMaps is the fallback; the syntax is shared.
