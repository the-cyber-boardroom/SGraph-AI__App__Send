## What This Is

Guidance for someone excited about Gen AI who is good at simplifying and explaining: **help your own past self from before your paradigm shift, because the curse of knowledge turns wonder into commodity fast and the second and third wave of users is now about use cases; focus on the sweet spot of simple, domain-specific workflows that deliver real value (infographics, voice-to-text, documents, presentations, summaries); build on a value-not-rent model of micro-payments and per-use markup rather than monthly fees; concentrate on skills, especially multilingual, local-market, and sector-specific ones; add value rather than replace people; and make it sustainable through revenue.** It applies the simplification and commercialisation thinking to a personal-career frame (cross-ref: the v0.33.27 tool-workflow-simplification, v0.33.26 selling-llm-usage, and sovereignty and open-source theses, and the v0.31.9 webify brief). This is Part Two; Part One is the reflection on why the project lead shares (cross-ref: the v0.33.38 why-share brief). New contributions: **the help-your-past-self frame, the curse-of-knowledge application, and the value-not-rent skills guidance.**

## The Audience

Who this is for: **someone excited about Gen AI, good at simplifying, explaining, and bringing others on board, looking for a business model or a way to add value.**

The project lead frames it as guidance for a person who is good at simplifying and explaining Gen AI and bringing others along, and who is trying to find business models, build a career, or work out where they can add value.

## Help Your Past Self

The core idea: **help yourself from before your paradigm shift.**

The project lead: **"the core idea is to help yourself a couple of paradigm shifts ago, a couple of paradigms before you, so help yourself three months, six months, or a year ago, before you made a certain paradigm shift or discovery. That is the challenge."** So the target user is your own earlier self: the person you were before the thing that now seems obvious to you became obvious. That person is everywhere, and they are who you can help.

## The Curse Of Knowledge

Why your past self is the right target: **wonder normalises into commodity fast.**

The project lead: **"there is a very interesting concept here, the curse of knowledge: when you discover something, even a major paradigm shift, it becomes normalised very quickly, so you stop seeing the wonder and start treating it as a commodity, especially if you work with it regularly. The first ChatGPT was mind-blowing, but if we had never experienced any models and suddenly used an Opus, or GPT-5.5, or open-source ones like the Qwen or Mistral models, it would be mind-blowing for almost anybody. The good news is we evolved with them, but a lot of people have not, and that second and third wave of users is now about the use case."** So the people you can help are not desensitised the way you are; for them the wonder is still new, and what they need is the use case.

## The Sweet Spot: Simple, Domain-Specific Workflows

Where to focus: **simple workflows that deliver real value and make someone a little bit better.**

The project lead: **"a great place to focus is that sweet spot, finding simple workflows that actually deliver value, that make someone a little bit better. Like the thing I published, let me LLM your spreadsheet. Something simple, like creating an infographic, turning a voice memo into text, creating a document or a presentation, summarising something, very simple but very domain-specific. That is where you help people, like what email did to letters, or browsing a website, or having it on your mobile."** So the opening is not grand AI, it is small, concrete, domain-specific help (cross-ref: the tool-workflow-simplification, audio-transcribe, and webify briefs).

## The Value-Not-Rent Business Model

How to charge: **micro-payments and per-use markup, not monthly fees.**

The project lead: **"it is a different business model, not charging a lot of people a big amount, it is about scaling micro-payments, micro-advice, the ability to help somebody a little. Do not view it as rent extraction, view it as added value. I would not build something that gets paid a monthly fee, I would build something paid per use, with a small markup. That is the future."** So the model mirrors the SGraph commercialisation thesis: pay per use, a small markup, value not rent (cross-ref: the selling-llm-usage, image-generation-opportunity, and sovereignty theses).

## Skills As The Focus

The concrete thing to build: **skills, especially multilingual, local-market, and sector-specific ones.**

The project lead: **"skills are a great place to focus. Create skills, and in a multilingual environment create skills in Portuguese, for a local market, for the Algarve, for Loulé or Faro or Almancil. Create skills absolutely focused on a particular sector, industry, place, set of clients, or person."** So the differentiated opportunity is narrow, specific skills: a skill for a language, a town, an industry, a client, where the specificity is the value (cross-ref: the Hermes SKILL.md skills and the SGraph skills).

## Add Value, Do Not Replace People

The framing that matters: **you are helping, not replacing.**

The project lead: **"the good news is you are not replacing people, and you are not telling them this will replace what they do. You are taking something that today is inefficient, something that could help them, especially crystallising ideas and communication, creating something simple."** So the pitch is augmentation, not displacement, which is both kinder and more saleable.

## Why Infographics

A favoured example: **infographics turn complex things into something an audience can grasp.**

The project lead: **"I like infographics because they are a great way to take complex ideas, documents, and topics and break them down for a particular audience, a particular place, a particular outcome."** Infographics are the archetype of the simple, domain-specific, value-adding workflow (cross-ref: the image-transformation-service and agentic-content-website briefs).

## Sustainability Through Revenue

Why revenue matters: **it has to be sustainable, and that is not a bad thing.**

The project lead: **"now is the right time to get on it and understand how to create revenue streams, because it has to be sustainable. It cannot operate purely on goodwill or helping others, because you have to make a business out of it, and making a business just means it is sustainable. That is not a bad thing if it is done from a value point of view."** So revenue is not in tension with generosity: it is what makes the help durable, as long as it is value-based rather than rent-extracting.

## The True Economic Value

The test of whether you are adding value: **save someone time, effort, or money, and always give them value.**

The project lead: **"the true economic value is if you save somebody time, effort, or money, and give them something they do not have, always giving them value. That is why rent extraction is not good, but if you are doing that, you are adding value, you are on the good side of the force. A lot of businesses and activities that before were not cost-effective now are."** So the line between good and bad business is whether you are giving value or extracting rent, and Gen AI has made a whole class of previously-uneconomic help newly viable.

## Open Source As The Enabler

The underpinning, and the model that works: **open-source tools, with a small markup on usage.**

The project lead: **"open-source technologies like the ones I develop help with this. I have a vested interest in seeing my open-source technology used, which adds value to my brand and the tools, but it is open source, so I am not locking anyone in. One good business model is that you create something somebody else uses, and as they use it they pay a little markup to you, for example on LLM usage."** So the recommended model is open-source capability plus a per-use markup: no lock-in, real value, and a sustainable revenue stream (cross-ref: the selling-llm-usage and sovereignty theses).
