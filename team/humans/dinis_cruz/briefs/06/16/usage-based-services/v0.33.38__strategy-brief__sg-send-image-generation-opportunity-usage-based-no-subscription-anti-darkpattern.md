# The Image-Generation Opportunity: Usage-Based, No Subscription, No Tracking

**from** Human (project lead)

## What This Is

A market signal and an opportunity, prompted by a friend being scammed by an online image-generation service: **the market for cheap, on-demand image generation clearly exists and people will pay small amounts for it, but it is full of bad players who win sponsored-link placements and trap users with dark-pattern hidden subscriptions, so the opportunity is a transparent, usage-based, no-subscription, no-tracking image-generation service with a super-simple experience, applying the same OpenRouter-keys and usage model from the audio-tool thread to images.** It extends the commercialisation model to a second tool (cross-ref: the v0.33.26 selling-llm-usage brief and the v0.33.27 audio-transcript-tool-release, workflow-simplification, and finding-early-users briefs) and embodies the value-not-rent positioning (cross-ref: the v0.33.26 sovereignty and open-source-success-paradox theses). New contributions: **the image-generation opportunity, the decoded market signals, and the transparent-versus-dark-pattern differentiation.**

## The Story And The Signals

What happened, in the project lead's account: **a friend searched for image generation, clicked a sponsored link, happily paid a small amount, and was then hit with a hidden monthly subscription.**

The project lead: **"a friend of mine got ripped off by an online image-generation service. They had a photo and wanted to see what it would look like transformed, and had run out of ChatGPT credits. They searched for image generation, clicked one of the sponsored links, which these days tend to be exactly these scams, and were asked for about thirty pence, which they were happy to pay. Then a couple of days later they got a thirty-pound charge, because the fine print said you subscribe to a monthly thing."**

## The Signals Decoded

Four signals worth separating: **the market exists, people pay small amounts, sponsored links skew to scams, and the trap is the hidden subscription.**

| Signal | What It Tells Us |
|--------|------------------|
| The market exists | People want quick image generation and transformation |
| Willingness to pay small amounts | Thirty pence was paid happily; small per-use pricing works |
| Sponsored links skew to scams | Scammers outbid honest services because the scam is worth more per click |
| The trap is the hidden subscription | A dark-pattern monthly charge buried in the fine print |

The willingness-to-pay signal echoes the few-dollars metric from the audio tool: people pay small amounts on demand for something they need (cross-ref: the finding-early-users and audio-transcript-tool-release briefs).

## The Opportunity

Where SGraph fits, in the project lead's words: **cheaper, usage-based, no subscription, no tracking, and smooth.**

The project lead: **"this is a big opportunity. We can do it cheaper, just a markup on top, or give those credits, so our usage-based model is better than this, and we do not have that kind of subscription model. The question is the UI, how to compete at this level to make it super easy to create images. This ties into everything we talked about recently, how to sell OpenRouter keys, how to sell usage, how to provide these workflows. It shows the market exists, and that there are bad players, so how can we compete on the fact that we do not track anything, we just provide the image generation and sell the service, and make it super smooth."**

## The Differentiators

How SGraph wins, honestly: **transparent usage pricing against dark-pattern subscriptions.**

| Differentiator | Against The Scam |
|----------------|------------------|
| Usage-based | No subscription; pay for what you use |
| Transparent | No hidden monthly charge; no dark patterns |
| No personal tracking | No surveillance of the user (see the note below) |
| Cheaper | A small markup, or provided credits |
| Smooth | A super-simple experience |

This is the value-not-rent positioning made concrete: the scam extracts rent through a hidden subscription, while SGraph charges transparently for actual usage (cross-ref: the open-source-success-paradox and sovereignty theses, which frame proprietary rent-extraction as the thing to avoid).

## A Note On No Tracking

Worth getting right before it is marketed: **the claim is no personal tracking, not literally no telemetry.**

The audio-tool work includes an observability vault that captures anonymous statistics (page opens, executions, country) with no PII and no IP. So the honest version of we do not track is no personal tracking and no surveillance, consistent with that anonymous-observability posture, rather than zero telemetry. The two should be reconciled so the privacy claim is accurate (cross-ref: the audio-transcript-tool-release observability posture).

## The UX Question

The thing to design: **a super-simple way to create and transform images.**

The core use case from the story is image transformation (upload a photo, see it transformed), and the experience should be as simple as the audio tool: a clear action, the result, the cost shown, no friction (cross-ref: the tool-workflow-simplification and audio-transcribe-experience briefs). The same maximum-visibility-with-maximum-simplicity principle applies.

## The Model Reuse

Why this is mostly already built: **it is the audio tool's model, applied to images.**

The OpenRouter-keys distribution, the usage-based billing, the key-acquisition journeys, and the cost visibility all carry over directly; image generation is simply another tool in the same family, consuming OpenRouter (or equivalent) image models instead of audio ones (cross-ref: the selling-llm-usage, audio-transcript-tool-release, and openrouter-key-acquisition briefs).

## What This Asks For

1. **Build an image-generation tool on the usage model** (markup or credits; no subscription).
2. **Design a super-simple image experience** (transform a photo; show the cost).
3. **Reuse the key and usage workflows** (OpenRouter keys; the acquisition journeys).
4. **Make transparency the differentiator** (no hidden subscription; no dark patterns).
5. **Reconcile the no-tracking claim** (no personal tracking, consistent with anonymous observability).
6. **Plan discoverability that is not ad-bidding** (see the risk below).

Estimated effort: low to moderate; the usage model, key workflows, and tool pattern exist; the work is the image tool, its experience, and the go-to-market.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a subscription.** Usage-based only.
- **Not a dark pattern.** Transparent pricing, no hidden charges.
- **Not a surveillance product.** No personal tracking.
- **Not a new model.** The audio tool's commercialisation, applied to images.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: the no-tracking claim must be accurate.** Mitigation: reconcile with the anonymous-observability posture; market no personal tracking, not zero telemetry.

**Risk 2: one anecdote is not a market study.** Mitigation: the willingness-to-pay signal aligns with the audio-tool metric, but validate with real users.

**Risk 3: discoverability against scammers' ad budgets.** Scammers outbid honest services on sponsored links because the scam is worth more per click, so SGraph cannot win on ad spend. Mitigation: organic and semantic-graph outreach rather than ad-bidding (cross-ref: the finding-early-users brief).

**Risk 4: the clean model must stay clean.** The differentiation only holds if SGraph never adopts the dark patterns it competes against. Mitigation: keep pricing transparent and usage-based as a principle.
