# Finding Early Users Scientifically: A Mesh Of Semantic Graphs, Public To Private

**version** v0.33.27
**date** 15 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Architect, Data Science, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief

---

## What This Is

A scientific approach to finding early users for the audio transcription tool: **define the goal (enough users that each spend a few dollars of OpenRouter credits, and how long that takes to achieve), then find and segment people through a mesh of intersecting semantic knowledge graphs seen through several lenses (the capability, the outreach targets, the buyers, the investors, and the partners), reusing ontologies and taxonomies from the SGraph website and open schemas like RDF and triplets, building the system and capability graph publicly (which is itself a valid use case) and only then connecting it privately to the LinkedIn data, a clean example of going from public to private.** It builds on the tool release and the LinkedIn work (cross-ref: the v0.33.27 audio-transcript-tool-release brief for the spend metric, and the v0.33.26 linkedin-agentic-team and v0.33.16 company-intelligence-vault and semantic-graph-visualisation briefs). New contributions: **the semantic-graph approach to user-finding, the mesh of lenses, the ontology-of-ontologies reuse, and the public-to-private flow.**

## The Goal

The concrete objective: **get enough early users to each spend a few dollars of OpenRouter credits, and learn how long that takes.**

The project lead: **"this is where we define a set of goals: can I get enough users that will spend five dollars of OpenRouter credits, and how long is that going to take to find users who will try it in their own environment, their own tests, client-side."** So the early-user search has a measurable target (the few-dollars-of-spend metric from the release brief) and a time dimension (how long to reach it), and it is about real client-side trial, not just sign-ups (cross-ref: the audio-transcript-tool-release brief).

## A Note On Privacy And Visualisations

An aside the project lead flags: **the privacy of the models is itself interesting, alongside the visualisations and the explanation.**

Unless content is shared through SG/Send, the privacy of the models is an interesting property to surface, and there are interesting visualisations and explanations to build around what the tool does. These are part of the public story (see the capability lens below).

## The Core Question: Who, Where, And What Message

The question to answer: **who to reach out to, on which channel, and with what message.**

The project lead: **"the question I want to capture is how do I choose the people I reach out to and invite as users. Which ones do I reach out to on LinkedIn, which on Discord, which by email, which do I ping, and what message do I send them, and how does that play out."** So the outreach has three variables per person: the channel (LinkedIn, Discord, email, a ping), the selection (who), and the message (what to say), and the brief is about answering these systematically rather than ad hoc.

## The Key-Distribution Recap

How access is shared, in the simpler variant: **hardcode a key and a decryption key into a shared vault page, where the decryption key retrieves the OpenRouter key.**

The project lead: **"there is the part where I hard-code a key to a vault and share it, and whoever opens that page can see it, and I can also hard-code the decryption key, because you need that decryption key to retrieve the OpenRouter key, which is the bit that is missing."** This is the share-a-key-in-a-vault variant of the distribution, alongside the SG-API-secrets-with-time-to-live approach from the release brief; both deliver the OpenRouter key to a trial user, and the choice between them is the rotation-and-bounding question (cross-ref: the audio-transcript-tool-release brief).

## The Scientific Approach: A Semantic Knowledge Graph For Finding People

The method, and a topic in its own right: **build a semantic knowledge graph to find who will be interested.**

The project lead: **"I want to do this in a scientific way, where we create a semantic knowledge graph for this, which is a nice topic in itself: how do I find who will be interested. The graph finds the interconnections and maps the people. I want to do a lot of this work in a way I can share publicly, because that itself becomes a valid use case, and then use it to connect the dots with my LinkedIn data, but these are two separate processes."** So finding users becomes a graph problem, and the work splits into a public process (the shareable graph) and a private process (connecting it to the LinkedIn data), kept separate.

## Ontology Of Ontologies, Reusing Open Schemas

The structural point: **which graph you get depends on the lens, so reuse ontologies and taxonomies, including open schemas.**

The project lead: **"this is ontology of ontologies and graphs of graphs, because it depends on which lens we look through, on the ontology and the taxonomy. We should reuse ontologies and taxonomies, which eventually come from the SGraph website, and connect to existing RDFs, existing triplets, and other open schemas and open data, to show how that works."** So the graphs are not built from scratch: they reuse the SGraph website's ontologies and connect to open standards (RDF, triplets, open data), and the lens chosen determines the view (cross-ref: the nhi-2.0-semantic-knowledge-graphs and semantic-graph-visualisation briefs).

## The Mesh Of Lenses

The heart of the approach: **several lenses over the same subject, each a semantic graph, intersected.**

| Lens | What It Maps |
|------|--------------|
| Capability | What the tool does and uses: the models, Google or Gemini, audio, OpenRouter, the open-source element, the workflows, the transcription, the live feature, all as components; purely describing what is going on (the public graph) |
| Outreach | Who to contact, and who the project lead would like to reach |
| Buyer | Who will buy it, and how they will buy it (with the multilingual and multicultural dimension, since the models handle many languages) |
| Investor | Which investors should care about this |
| Partnership | Where partnerships fit, and whether they are one of the cohorts |

The project lead: **"you have one graph focused on the capability and what the tool does and uses, and the outreach lens of who to contact, and then a different lens of who will buy it, which is different, and the investor lens of which investors should care, and partnerships, where they fit, are they one of those cohorts. So you have a mesh of semantic graphs we should be intersecting."** There are also news-and-communication and development angles (the CI pipeline, the vault, how it was built, the agentic workflows), each another lens. Intersecting the lenses is what produces targeted, segmented outreach.

## The Public-To-Private Flow

The sequence, and a clean example of the principle: **build the system and capability graph publicly, then connect it privately to the LinkedIn data.**

The project lead: **"do this first from the point of view of just the system, and then take that and connect it to my LinkedIn data, so you connect the dots, and that bit becomes private. That is a perfect example of how SGraph goes from public to private."** So the capability and system graph is public (and a valid use case to share), and the moment it is joined to the personal LinkedIn data it becomes private, which is the public-to-private flow made concrete (cross-ref: the company-intelligence-vault and data-source-mapping briefs, and the linkedin-agentic-team terms stance, which this respects by using the project lead's own data, not scrapers).

## What This Asks For

1. **Set the early-user goal** (enough users each spending a few dollars; track how long).
2. **Build the capability graph publicly** (what the tool does and uses; a shareable use case).
3. **Build the lens graphs** (outreach, buyer, investor, partnership; plus news and development angles).
4. **Reuse ontologies and open schemas** (from the SGraph website; RDF, triplets, open data).
5. **Intersect the lenses for targeted outreach** (who, which channel, what message).
6. **Connect privately to the LinkedIn data** (the public-to-private join).
7. **Keep the OpenRouter-key distribution decided** (the in-vault key variant or the SG-API secret).

Estimated effort: moderate; the semantic-graph, ontology, and LinkedIn work exist; the contribution is the mesh of lenses, the public capability graph, and the public-to-private join.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not scraping.** It uses the project lead's own data and public schemas, within the LinkedIn terms (cross-ref: the linkedin-agentic-team brief).
- **Not spam.** Targeted, segmented, personal outreach, not bulk messaging.
- **Not one graph.** A mesh of lenses, intersected.
- **Not public once joined to LinkedIn.** That join is private.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: outreach can feel like spam.** Mitigation: the lenses produce targeted, personal outreach; keep it tasteful, not bulk (cross-ref: the linkedin-intelligence-ui outreach posture).

**Risk 2: the public-private boundary must be clear.** The capability graph is public; the LinkedIn join is private. Mitigation: keep the two processes separate, as the project lead insists.

**Risk 3: the few-dollars goal may be slow.** Mitigation: that is the honest signal; track how long it takes, as the goal states.

**Risk 4: ontology reuse versus invention.** Building ontologies from scratch is costly. Mitigation: reuse the website's ontologies and open schemas.
