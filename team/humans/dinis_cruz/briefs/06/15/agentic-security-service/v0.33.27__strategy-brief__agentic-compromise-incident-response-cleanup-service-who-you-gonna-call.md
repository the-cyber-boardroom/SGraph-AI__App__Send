# Who You Gonna Call: An Incident-Response Service For Compromised AI Agents

**version** v0.33.27
**date** 15 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Security, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief (service idea)

---

## What This Is

A tongue-in-cheek but genuinely-needed service idea: **as a new generation of agentic exploitation emerges, targeting people who run privileged, well-resourced AI agents in their personal accounts, the attacks behave like rootkits (going for access, resources, credentials, and persistence, not just one-off exploitation), compromise becomes a matter of when not if, and most users cannot safely clean up afterwards, so there is room for a who-you-gonna-call incident-response and cleanup service, aligned with security companies and staffed by experts, that does the rebuild-reset-monitor remediation users cannot do themselves.** It connects to the security work and the hope-driven-development idea (cross-ref: the v0.27.58 sg-sentinel-compliance-as-living-graph brief, the v0.32.3 access-not-security brief, and the v0.33.26 linkedin-agentic-team brief, which is the privileged-agent capability whose flip side this is). This is a defensive remediation concept, not an offensive one. New contributions: **the agentic-compromise threat framing, the rootkit analogy, the when-not-if posture, and the cleanup-service concept.**

## The Threat

The emerging risk the project lead names: **agentic exploitation that targets privileged, resourced AI agents and behaves like a rootkit.**

The project lead: **"there is a lot of conversation about the next generation of agentic exploitation and new types of malware that target people running agents with lots of privileges and lots of resources. They do what rootkits did in the past: they do not just go for the exploitation, they go for the access, the resources, the credentials, they gain persistence and all these capabilities."** So the threat is not a single exploit but a foothold: an attacker that compromises a privileged agent gains its access, its resources, its credentials, and persistence, exactly the rootkit pattern, now aimed at AI agents.

## When, Not If

The posture: **people running agents in personal accounts will be compromised, and at that moment they need experts.**

The project lead: **"this goes back to the hope-driven development we talked about a while back. People running agents in their personal accounts, it is not if but when the accounts are going to be compromised, and they need to talk to security experts at that moment."** The framing is deliberate: running powerful agents in personal accounts without a plan for compromise is hope-driven development (the project lead's prior concept), and the realistic stance is to plan for when, not if.

## The Remediation Reality

Why this is hard, and why users cannot do it alone: **the only real fix is to rebuild, reset, and monitor, and most users cannot do that safely.**

The project lead: **"it is just like corporate infrastructure, where when a laptop is compromised the only solution is to rebuild it, reset all the accounts, monitor for exploitation on all the accounts, and rebuild everything. The reality is that a lot of these users have no idea how to do that, and they cannot do it safely. We need to treat these as rootkits, and the probability that someone hit by a more advanced exploitation can clean up the environment themselves is not very high."**

| Remediation Step | Why Users Struggle |
|------------------|--------------------|
| Rebuild the environment | Most users do not know how |
| Reset all accounts | Hard to do completely and safely |
| Monitor for exploitation | Requires expertise and tooling |
| Rebuild everything | Time, skill, and confidence most users lack |

Treating an agent compromise like a rootkit means assuming the foothold is deep and the only trustworthy path is a full rebuild, which is beyond most individual users.

## The Service: Who You Gonna Call

The idea, with the playful framing intact: **a big incident-response and cleanup service, aligned with security companies, that users call when their agent is compromised.**

The project lead: **"somebody should create a business, almost like Ghostbusters, the help button, who you are going to call when your LLM is compromised. There should be a big service, aligned with security companies, with experts who provide it, because there is a lot of need. Call it an LLM cleanup service, or LLM busters, or LLM exploitation busters, maybe not the right word, but something like that."**

| Element | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| The trigger | A user's privileged agent or account is compromised |
| The promise | Who you gonna call: expert remediation on demand |
| The work | The rebuild, reset, and monitor remediation users cannot do |
| The partners | Aligned with security companies and their experts |
| The names (playful) | LLM cleanup service, LLM busters, LLM exploitation busters |

## The Need

Why it is timely: **the threat is growing and the remediation gap is real.**

As more people run capable agents with real privileges and resources, the population of compromisable, high-value personal-agent setups grows, while the ability of those users to remediate does not. That gap, between the likelihood of compromise and the user's ability to recover, is the opening for the service. The next step the project lead wants is to map the workflows.

## What This Asks For

1. **Map the incident-response and cleanup workflows** (what the service actually does, step by step).
2. **Define the service concept** (the who-you-gonna-call offering).
3. **Align with security companies** (the experts who deliver it).
4. **Frame it around when, not if** (the hope-driven-development counter).
5. **Keep it defensive** (remediation and cleanup, not offence).

Estimated effort: this is a service concept and a workflow-mapping exercise, not a build; the next step is the map.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not offensive security.** It is remediation and cleanup, not exploitation.
- **Not a product yet.** It is a service concept to map.
- **Not a claim that compromise is avoidable.** The posture is when, not if.
- **Not a replacement for prevention.** It is the help button for after.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: the threat framing must stay accurate, not alarmist.** Agentic exploitation is emerging and real, but the service should be grounded in what actually happens, not hype. Mitigation: map the real workflows and ground the threat in evidence.

**Risk 2: consumer remediation is genuinely hard.** That is the premise, but it also makes the service hard to deliver well. Mitigation: align with security companies and their experts.

**Risk 3: the playful naming versus serious substance.** The Ghostbusters framing is memorable but the service is serious. Mitigation: keep the substance rigorous, use the framing only as the hook.

**Risk 4: prevention should not be neglected.** A cleanup service is the after; the before still matters. Mitigation: position it alongside, not instead of, prevention (cross-ref: the access-not-security and compliance-as-living-graph briefs).
