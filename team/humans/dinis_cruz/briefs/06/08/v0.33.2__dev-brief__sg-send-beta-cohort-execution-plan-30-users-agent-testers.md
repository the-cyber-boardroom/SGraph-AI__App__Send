## What This Is

The plan and execution brief for the first beta cohort of the assessment product (the scan, rendered neutrally): **map the whole project as issues on Kanban boards, walking backwards from the milestone of 30 early friendly users running an assessment (or at least shipping the vault to all 30), split into phased validation groups, with five synthetic agent testers, a three-person team, full supporting infrastructure, and AI-native project management.** It is the launch plan for the shippable product vault (cross-ref: the v0.33.2 shipping-vault brief), and it reuses the event capture, the agent-with-personality idea from the tabletop work, and the agentic delivery team. New contributions: **the 30-user milestone and walk-backwards plan, the phased cohort split, the five agent testers with personalities, the supporting infrastructure and sign-off, AI-native project management, the agentic delivery team, and team Claude prompts.**

## The Objective, And Walking Backwards From It

The milestone, stated plainly: **get 30 early friendly users to run an assessment successfully, or at least ship the vault to all 30, and plan everything by walking backwards from that.**

The project lead: **"the key objective of this milestone is to have 30 early users, friendlies, run an assessment successfully, and at least deploy to all of them. Not all of them might do it, but at least ship the vault to 30. So, walking backwards from it: the 30 have to receive an email or WhatsApp message with the vault in place, they go through it, submit the results, which we review, and we comment back, going back and forth."** So the plan is built by walking backwards from the end state (30 users run an assessment) through the user journey to everything that must exist for it. The user journey is the anchor.

## Map It As Issues On Kanban Boards

How to capture the plan: **map the entire project, every milestone and issue, onto Kanban boards (more than one), reusing the vault Kanban already built.**

The project lead: **"map this into issues, because we will have a Kanban board we can use, so we can really go to town and capture this as many issues. We already have a vault where we implemented the Kanban board. Map the multiple boards we need, usually more than one, map all the issues we need to add, and map the entire project with every single milestone."** So the plan lives as issues on Kanban boards (cross-ref: the vault Kanban work and the Email-FS-lite Issues-FS model from the FAQ brief): multiple boards, every issue and milestone captured, visualised and tracked. The Kanban is also how progress is shown to the team (below).

## The User Journey

What every user needs, walked backwards: **receive the vault by email or WhatsApp, go through and submit the assessment, get reviewed and commented on, and have a place to submit bugs and issues.**

| Step | What Is Needed |
|------|----------------|
| Receive | An email or WhatsApp message with the vault in place |
| Complete | Go through the assessment and submit the results |
| Review | The team reviews and comments back; back and forth |
| Report issues | A place to submit bugs and issues, perhaps via the app |
| Prerequisites | All links created, all vaults created, all vaults tested |

The project lead: **"they also need a place to submit issues and bugs, which could be through the app, another way to communicate. And we need all the links created, all the vaults created, all the vaults tested."** So the journey requires links, created and tested vaults, the submission and review loop, and an in-app bug and issue channel (cross-ref: the v0.33.2 vault-append-capability and shipping-vault briefs).

## The Team

Who runs it: **a three-person team, the project lead, the provider, and an admin person, each with responsibilities.**

The project lead: **"we need our own team, made of three individuals: me, the provider, and an admin person, each with their responsibilities."** So the delivery team is three people with defined responsibilities, supported by the agentic delivery team (below). The provider also holds the release sign-off (below).

## The Phased Cohort Split

How to roll out the 30: **split them into phased validation groups, five, five, ten, ten, each testing the whole end-to-end system, with the early groups surfacing the bugs.**

The project lead: **"split the 30 into about four groups: one group of five, us and a couple of friendlies, another group of five, the super friendlies, then two groups of 10 we reach out to in sequence. Each is a validation that tests the whole end-to-end system. The first groups will have stuff that goes wrong, bugs to figure out."**

| Group | Size | Who |
|-------|------|-----|
| 1 | 5 | Us and a couple of friendlies |
| 2 | 5 | Super friendlies |
| 3 | 10 | Reached out to in sequence |
| 4 | 10 | Reached out to in sequence |

So the 30 roll out in phases (5, 5, 10, 10), each a full end-to-end validation, with the first two groups expected to surface bugs to fix before the larger groups. This is the football-team-preparation, practise-before-the-real-thing approach (cross-ref: the incident-response-platform brief's preparation spectrum).

## The Five Agent Testers, With Personalities

A distinctive addition: **five synthetic LLM agent testers, each with a role and a personality, introduced one per phase, that go through the system (often via browser automation) and comment on every screen.**

The project lead: **"we are also going to have five agents, LLM agents, probably one run through Claude, maybe one automatic. Introduce them per phase, as two then one, one, one, so each phase adds an agent. Each agent receives the email or message, opens it, goes to the system automated, some through browser automation, Playwright. Give them personalities: the first one is very forgiving, a tech enthusiast, friendly, and they get more difficult, the last one does not have a lot of time and is critical, why is this, why is that, I like this, I did not like that. Each should comment on every single screen and the whole experience, providing analysis."**

| Agent | Personality | Introduced |
|-------|-------------|------------|
| 1 | Forgiving, friendly tech enthusiast | Phase 1 |
| 2 | Slightly more demanding | Phase 1 |
| 3 | More difficult | Phase 2 |
| 4 | Harder still | Phase 3 |
| 5 | Time-poor, critical, blunt | Phase 4 |

So five synthetic testers, each an LLM agent with a distinct persona (forgiving to harshly critical), are introduced two in phase one then one per later phase, drive the product (often via Playwright browser automation), and comment on every screen and the whole experience. This is the LLM-players-with-personalities idea from the tabletop work (cross-ref: the incident-response-platform brief) applied to beta testing: synthetic users that stress the experience from different temperaments and report back.

## The Supporting Infrastructure

What must be in place around the cohort: **dashboards and visibility, disclaimers, a formal provider sign-off per release, phase reports, an SLA and office hours, documentation, and infographics and checklists on the Kanban and admin site.**

The project lead: **"each milestone needs to be checked. We need documentation, explanations, good visibility, all the dashboards working so we can see who is doing what, all the disclaimers in place and signed off, a formal approval workflow where the provider signs off each release, and reports after each phase. Define an SLA, define office hours, and create the briefs and infographics, a checklist, even on the admin website, because Kanban lets us visualise progress, where we are, what happens next, and the milestones."**

| Element | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| Dashboards and visibility | See who is doing what; which vault, duration, where they get stuck |
| Disclaimers | In place and signed off |
| Formal sign-off | The provider signs off each release |
| Phase reports | A sense of what is happening after each phase |
| SLA and office hours | Defined support commitments |
| Documentation and explanations | For the team and users |
| Infographics and checklists | On the Kanban and admin site; visualise progress and milestones |

So the cohort runs on real infrastructure: visibility and dashboards (built on the event capture; cross-ref: the release-and-event-capture brief), disclaimers signed off, a formal release sign-off by the provider, per-phase reports, defined SLA and office hours, and documentation, infographics, and checklists surfaced on the Kanban and admin site. Visibility is especially important here, because it is what reveals who clicked what, which vault, how long, and where they got stuck, and it is the infrastructure for the wider release.

## AI-Native Project Management

The meta-point, and a sharp one: **use Gen AI for the project management and non-functional requirements of the deployment, because most AI startups are not themselves AI-enabled, and visualisations, screenshots, and infographics keep the team aligned.**

The project lead: **"a lot of people develop AI startups and companies and are not very AI-enabled. What we have here is actually implementing an AI-native way of working. Let's be creative, leverage Gen AI, and use it to do the whole project management, the non-functional requirements of this deployment. Be as professional and logistical as we can. The fact that we can create visualisations, screenshots, and infographics is quite interesting, because it lets us make sure we are aligned, especially for team members who have not fully experienced how this works."** So the project management itself is done AI-natively: Gen AI handles the logistics, the briefs, the infographics, the visualisations and screenshots, which both raise professionalism and align a team where some members have not fully experienced the way of working. The contrast is pointed: many AI companies are not AI-enabled internally; this beta is run the way the product preaches.

## The Agentic Delivery Team

Formalise the team behind the work: **add the agentic delivery team, the roles the project lead already works with, an architect and a developer, plus QA, each a defined role.**

The project lead: **"let's add the agentic team, the multiple roles. These are the agents I work with: I am already working with an architect and a developer, and now we need to add QA. We need to come up with the roles."** So alongside the three-person human team and the five synthetic testers, there is an agentic delivery team of role-based agents (architect, developer, QA, and others to define), which is exactly the audience these briefs are addressed to. The beta formalises that team and its roles.

## Team Claude Prompts

A practical inclusion for the human team: **put the prompts the team should use on the page, because they also have Claude, including prompts to access the data and the vaults.**

The project lead: **"we also need on the page the prompts that the team should be using, because they also have Claude. They should have the prompts to access the data, access the vaults, even some customisations, tell Claude to access the data and the vaults, so they can experience how it feels and plays out."** So the page carries ready-made prompts for the human team to use Claude against the data and vaults, so the team experiences the AI-native workflow first-hand. This is part of aligning the team and dogfooding the product.

The deliverable is the planned beta: Kanban boards of issues and milestones, 30 users in four phases plus five agent testers, full visibility and sign-off, run with AI-native project management.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not an ad hoc launch.** Planned to a milestone, mapped as Kanban issues, phased.
- **Not human-only testing.** Five synthetic agent testers with personalities alongside the 30 users.
- **Not unmanaged.** Dashboards, disclaimers, provider sign-off, phase reports, SLA, office hours.
- **Not non-AI project management.** Run AI-natively, with Gen AI doing the logistics.
- **Not the wider release itself.** This builds the infrastructure for the wider release.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: A sensitive-domain beta needs care.** Real users in a sensitive domain raise privacy and safety stakes. Mitigation: disclaimers signed off; the provider signs off each release; the do-not-enter-confidential-data posture (cross-ref: the release-and-event-capture and shipping-vault briefs).

**Risk 2: Early phases will break.** The first groups will hit bugs. Mitigation: that is the design; small early groups (5, 5); fix before the groups of 10; phase reports.

**Risk 3: Agent testers may not reflect real users.** Synthetic testers are not real people. Mitigation: they complement the human cohort, not replace it; varied personalities; they stress the experience and comment per screen.

**Risk 4: Visibility and tracking are privacy-sensitive.** Tracking who clicked what, duration, and where they get stuck is sensitive. Mitigation: needed for the wider-release infrastructure; combine with disclaimers; minimise; review (cross-ref: the shipping-vault brief's analytics risk).

**Risk 5: AI-native PM can drift into over-production.** Infographics and visualisations can become the work. Mitigation: they serve alignment and logistics; keep them in service of shipping to 30.
