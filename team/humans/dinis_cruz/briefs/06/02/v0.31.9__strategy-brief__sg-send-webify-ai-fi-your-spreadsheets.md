# "Let Us Webify Your Spreadsheets": Spreadsheets As The Painful-But-Working Use Case

**version** v0.31.9
**date** 2 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Product, Strategy, Architect, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief

---

## What This Is

A use case that came out of showing the vault to a friend (a consultant doing business transformation and AI workflows for local companies) and landing on a tagline the project lead thinks should be the headline: **"let us webify, or AI-fi, your spreadsheets."** The underlying principles (don't replace, find painful-but-working use cases; the connector model; shipping is the hard problem; the local-consultant channel) are already documented (cross-ref: the v0.31.5 connector and consulting briefs, the v0.31.7 Tavon brief); this brief grounds them in a sharp, concrete use case and adds the genuinely new contributions: **the tagline, the spreadsheet-as-existing-mini-app insight, and the spreadsheet-as-external-connected-vault (each tab a mini-app, with metadata UIs, infographics, and chat-on-the-data).**

## The Tagline And The Philosophy: Don't Replace, Improve What Already Works

The headline: **"let us webify, or AI-fi, your spreadsheets."** It captures a philosophy the project lead has stated before (cross-ref: the consulting and Tavon briefs' augment-the-human thesis) and restated sharply here: **the logic is not to come in and replace people or rip things out; it is to find use cases that already work but are painful, and make them better.**

The project lead: **"the logic is not to come in and replace stuff, not to get rid of your people. The logic is to find use cases that are working but are painful. Spreadsheets are a great place to start."** This is a low-threat, high-trust message for a business: you are not threatening their people or their systems, you are taking something that already delivers value (but hurts) and improving it. And it answers the same commercial question as the ownership brief (cross-ref: the v0.31.9 ownership brief): **"how much is it worth? How much are they willing to pay for that particular bit?"** The value is concrete and scoped to a painful thing they already rely on.

## The Sharp Insight: A Spreadsheet Is Already A Mini App

The genuinely new and sharp point: **a spreadsheet in most organisations is already a mini app, an app with real business value that works, but riddled with problems.**

The project lead: **"a spreadsheet in most organisations is already a mini app. It's already an app that has real business value and works quite well, but it has tonnes of problems: version control problems, editing problems, UX problems, multiple-people-editing-at-the-same-time problems."**

| The Spreadsheet Today | The Problem |
|-----------------------|-------------|
| **Real business value, already working** | It is genuinely used and relied upon |
| **Version control problems** | No proper versioning; which copy is current? |
| **Editing problems** | Fragile formulas, accidental breakage |
| **UX problems** | Not built for the people who use it |
| **Concurrent-editing problems** | Multiple people editing at once, conflicts |

This is the perfect target precisely because it is not a greenfield idea, it is a proven, valuable, in-use application that happens to be painful. The work is not to invent value (the spreadsheet already proves the value); it is to remove the pain. That is a much easier sell and a much lower risk than "let us build you something new": the use case is already validated by the fact that the business runs on it.

This connects to the vault's strengths directly, because the spreadsheet's problems are the vault's solved problems: version control (cross-ref: the commit-queue brief), controlled editing, provenance, and shareability are exactly what the vault provides. So "webify your spreadsheet" means: take the proven mini app, and give it the version control, the UX, and the controlled editing it never had.

## The New Capability: A Spreadsheet As An External, Connected Vault

The architectural contribution, building on the external/connected-vault idea (cross-ref: the v0.31.5 external-data-connectors and google-drive-connector briefs): **the spreadsheet can be an external, connected vault, or the connector to one, and each tab can become a mini application in its own right.**

The project lead: **"the vaults allow us to integrate with Google Sheets, or any spreadsheet that exists, because that just becomes a connector. As long as the person has access to the online spreadsheet, we can make the calls from the user's browser with the user's authentication and save the data. This is a variation of the external connected-vault idea we talked about (Google Drive), where the connection, or even the vault, is an external spreadsheet or a set of spreadsheets."**

So a spreadsheet plugs into the platform exactly like the existing connectors (cross-ref: the connector briefs): calls made from the user's browser with the user's own authentication (cross-ref: the connector-OAuth-data-broker brief), reaching the data where it already lives. No migration, no lift-and-shift, the spreadsheet stays where it is, and the vault connects to it.

The sharp extension is splitting it up: **"maybe have a spreadsheet with six tabs, but each tab is almost a mini application itself, so we can create metadata UIs, add click infographics from it, or have a chat about the data."**

| From | To |
|------|-----|
| One spreadsheet, six tabs | Six tabs, each treated as a mini application |
| Raw cells | Metadata UIs (a proper interface per tab) |
| Static data | Click infographics generated from it (cross-ref: the visualisation and infographic work) |
| Read-and-squint | Chat about the data (cross-ref: the vault-chat brief) |

And because the connection reaches the live data, the project lead's point lands: **"all those Google things become much easier because you're talking about the latest data, the right information, and you reach you where the data is."** The chat and the infographics are on the current spreadsheet, not a stale copy, which is the whole problem with the version-control mess today. So the vault turns a painful six-tab spreadsheet into six connected mini-apps with real UIs, generated infographics, and chat-on-the-live-data, without moving the data.

## Why Shipping Is The Real Value (And Where The Vault Helps)

The project lead reinforced a point made before (cross-ref: the consulting and library-as-shop-front briefs): **the hard problem is not creating code or an app; it is shipping, releasing something end to end.**

The project lead: **"this goes into the whole thing of shipping a solution, which solves the hard problems. The hard problem these days is not to create code or an app, it's how do you release something. That's where you need to think about all the workflows, and that's where the vaults can really help."** So the spreadsheet use case is valuable not because improving a spreadsheet is clever, but because it is a complete, shippable, end-to-end solution to a real, painful, in-use problem, and shipping end to end is the thing businesses actually struggle with. The vault helps because it carries the workflows, the version control, the connectors, and the delivery (cross-ref: the agent-controlled-websites CI and library briefs) that turn "improve the spreadsheet" into a shipped solution.

## The Go-To-Market: The Local Consultant Who Knows The Business

The delivery model, which cross-references the Tavon channel-partner brief (cross-ref: the v0.31.7 Tavon brief) but is worth stating in its own right: **this is delivered by a local consultant who knows the business and goes there physically, and that local presence is the power.**

The project lead: **"this is a service provided by a local consultant, somebody who knows the business, who goes physically there. That's the power, the message to local businesses: let us make you more efficient. It's easier to get meetings with people who want to leverage AI, the challenge they have is how to actually do it. So follow the documents, follow the workflows they already have, and just make them better."**

| The Local Consultant's Advantage | Why It Matters |
|----------------------------------|----------------|
| **Knows the business** | Understands which spreadsheets matter and why |
| **Goes there physically** | Local presence and trust |
| **Easy to get the meeting** | Businesses want AI; the demand is there |
| **Solves the "how"** | The business wants it but does not know how; the consultant does |

So the demand exists (businesses want to leverage AI), the gap is execution (they do not know how), and the local consultant who knows the business is positioned to deliver the "webify your spreadsheets" service end to end. This is the Tavon channel pattern (cross-ref: the Tavon brief) at the local-consultant scale: the platform provides the substrate (the vault, the connectors, the UIs, the chat, the delivery); the local consultant provides the relationship, the business knowledge, and the physical presence. The method is the recurring one: **follow the documents, follow the workflows they already have, and make them better** (cross-ref: the knowledge-capture and finance-team briefs' capture-how-the-business-works thesis).

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not replacing people or systems.** It improves a painful-but-working thing they already rely on.
- **Not a migration.** The spreadsheet stays where it is; the vault connects to the live data.
- **Not a greenfield app.** The spreadsheet already proves the value; the work is removing the pain.
- **Not a remote-only service.** The local consultant who knows the business and goes there is the power.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Reaching live spreadsheet data has access and security implications.** Calls from the user's browser with their auth touch real business data. Mitigation: the connector-OAuth-data-broker model (cross-ref: the connector briefs); the user's own authentication; least-privilege; the data stays where it is.

**Risk 2: Spreadsheets are fragile and idiosyncratic.** Real-world spreadsheets are messy. Mitigation: treat each as the proven mini-app it is; improve incrementally; the consultant who knows the business guides which matter.

**Risk 3: Concurrent editing and sync are genuinely hard.** Multiple editors on live data is a known hard problem. Mitigation: the vault's version control and commit model (cross-ref: the commit-queue brief); a reactive layer where needed (cross-ref: the Convex brief); scope carefully.

**Risk 4: The local-consultant channel needs the right partners.** Delivery depends on local consultants. Mitigation: the Tavon channel-partner model (cross-ref: the Tavon brief); the platform provides the substrate; cultivate consultants who know their local businesses.
