## What This Is

The investment strategy for SG/Send and the vaults, captured as notes, observations, and guidance for the Alchemist agent that is building the sgraph.ai investor pages (cross-ref: the investor-page disclaimer deliverable, `invest-the-ask-disclaimer-options.md`). It sets out **why now is the right time to raise angel investment, what the investment is for, the positioning, the revenue model and its scenarios, the go-to-market, and the kind of investor to look for.** It is strategy guidance, not financial or investment advice, and the project lead is explicit that the figures here are scenarios to be discovered with the market, not promises.

## A Note On The Workflow (And Why It Is The Proof)

Worth recording first, because it is itself a proof point: **the Alchemist agent created the investor vault, which the dev wired into the investor area of the website, so the agent now manages its own area of the site.**

The project lead: **"the workflow is really cool. I have the Alchemist agent, which understands the blast radius, understands the business strategy, and owns the connections with the investors. The agent created the vault that was then wired up by the dev to the investors area of the website. So now this agent is managing its own area of the website, and it makes sense because that's the agent that has ownership of that content."** This is the platform demonstrating its own thesis: an agent that owns a domain (investor relations) owns and maintains the vault that renders as that part of the site (cross-ref: the agent-controlled-websites brief). The investor pages are not just describing the company; they are a live example of the agent-owned, vault-backed website the company sells. That is a strong thing for the investor pages to embody, the medium is the demo.

## What The Investment Is For

The use of funds is specific: **hire humans who work with the agentic teams and take ownership of areas, each area connected by a web of agents, which is how the company scales.**

The project lead: **"what I'd like investment for is to hire a bunch of humans who work with the agentic teams and take ownership of a number of areas: development, architecture, content, translations, business enablement, creation of customised versions, working with businesses and partners, and the enablement of these use cases. Each one is connected by a web of agents, and that's how we scale. And to cover some more of my time. That's the budget."**

| Area Of Ownership | Human + Agentic Team |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| **Development** | A human owner working with dev agents |
| **Architecture** | A human owner working with architecture agents |
| **Content** | A human owner working with content agents |
| **Translations** | A human owner working with translation agents |
| **Business enablement** | A human owner working with enablement agents |
| **Customised versions** | A human owner creating customised versions (cross-ref: the base-vault brief) |
| **Business and partner work** | A human owner working with businesses and partners (cross-ref: the Tavon brief) |
| **The project lead's time** | More of the founder's time covered |

The model is human-plus-agentic-team per area, with the areas connected by a web of agents, which is the company living its own thesis (humans owning and maintaining, agents scaling the work; cross-ref: the ownership and agent-controlled-websites briefs). This is a clean, credible use-of-funds story: the money buys human owners who, amplified by agentic teams, cover the areas needed to operationalise and scale.

## The Positioning: A Foundational Platform Play

The positioning the Alchemist should convey: **this is not a nice-to-have company; it is a foundational, platform company of this wave, one that enables others, built on sound technology.**

The project lead: **"this is not going to be a nice company. It's a good example of the foundational companies created in this wave of development, and it's one that enables others, so it's a platform play, built on top of sound technology. It's a perfect time. We're even going to have customers who are venture-capital companies."** Three positioning points for the pages:

- **Foundational, not incremental.** A company of the kind that gets built in this wave and that others build on.
- **A platform play (enables others).** The whole skills creator economy, the partner integrations, the blast-radius company, all are built on the vault (cross-ref: the 1 June and 2 June ecosystem briefs); the company's value is that others build on it.
- **Sound technology.** Built on real, working technology, not a pitch.

The detail that VC firms themselves will be customers (cross-ref: the VC-confidential-data demo and investor-groups-for-data-management use case) is a strong proof point for the pages: the people who evaluate companies for a living will be users.

## The Revenue Model And Its Scenarios

The project lead was explicit about caution on projections, which the Alchemist must respect: **"do not over-project. We do not know yet; we need to discover what the market can pay for."** With that caveat, what can be said is the model, not the precise numbers.

### The Model (What Can Be Said)

| Revenue Stream | How It Works |
|----------------|--------------|
| **Consumption / value-based pricing** | Charge the customer per usage, based on value, probably credit-based, often paid in advance (the OpenAI-style model) |
| **Model and capability access from inside the vaults** | Make it easy to access the latest models and capabilities from inside the vaults, with a markup (cross-ref: the OpenRouter-broker brief) |
| **A better gen-AI experience** | The vault as a great way to manage data, chats, images, and gen-AI creations, a better experience people will pay for |

The project lead: **"we are doing consumption-based pricing based on value, charging per usage, probably credit-based, where many pay in advance, like OpenAI. We can also have nice revenue by making it easier to access the latest models and capabilities from inside the vaults, which will have a markup. The vault creates a really good experience for managing data, vaults, and gen-AI creations."** So the model is consumption/credit-based value pricing, plus a markup on model access (the broker substrate; cross-ref: the OpenRouter-broker brief), plus a premium experience for managing gen-AI work.

### The Scenarios (Illustrative, To Be Discovered)

The project lead floated illustrative scenarios, which the Alchemist should present as *scenarios*, not projections: a growth path where revenue reaches **on the order of £100,000 to £200,000 a month across multiple sources, countries, and income streams, which would imply a company valuation on the order of £5,000,000 to £10,000,000 and a reasonable exit at that level**, with the path beyond depending on the growth curve and the option of further investment. These are the project lead's illustrative figures, explicitly subject to discovering what the market pays; they should be framed on the pages as scenarios and ranges, with the model (above) as the substance, not the numbers.

## The Go-To-Market: B2C2B

The go-to-market thesis: **business-to-consumer-to-business (B2C2B), start with sophisticated consumer users, learn and harden the product on them, and each leads to a business connection.**

The project lead: **"a lot of this is what I call B2C2B, business to consumers to business. We have a nice consumer business that lets us fine-tune the technology, make sure it works at scale, learn how to ship it, and work with real customers who give us real features and real fine-tuning. And each of those leads to a business connection, a partner, a final business, an entity that needs our solution."**

| Stage | What It Does |
|-------|--------------|
| **C (sophisticated consumer users)** | Fine-tune the tech, prove it at scale, learn to ship, get real feedback |
| **2B (the business connection)** | Each consumer user leads to a partner, business, or entity that needs the solution |

The test the project lead sets is sharp and honest: **"if we cannot get the C users (who are sophisticated B users) to use it, that's the signal. At the end of the day, I already have enough exposure, from LinkedIn, from my network, even from the existing customers of Secure Send (the file-sharing, not the vault). We need to launch and push the vault capabilities to those customers, which should drive adoption and feedback, and then find more customers. The point is, if we cannot get the users we already have access to, to use it and leverage it, we do not have a product. And I think we are quite close."** So the immediate go-to-market is concrete: migrate and push the vault capabilities to the existing Secure Send file-sharing customers and the founder's network, and the honest internal test of product-market fit is whether those reachable users actually adopt it. Not viral exactly, but: if the people you can already reach will not use it, you do not have a product.

## The Investor Should Become A User (And Understand Open Source)

A guidance point on the kind of investor to seek: **the investor should become a user, and should understand open source, including open source as a sovereignty and future strategy.**

The project lead: **"the investor should also become a user, and that's the path. They should be one who understands what we're doing and the situation, and who understands open source, the play of open source. Open source is not just an open strategy; it is a sovereignty strategy, a future strategy, and the way that lets a lot of conversations happen without attrition, because open source makes everything easy, smooth, and straightforward."** So the ideal investor is an aligned user who understands the open-source thesis (cross-ref: the open-evaluation and blast-radius briefs' open-source-and-sovereignty argument), because that investor will get the platform, become a user (the B2C2B path applies to them too), and the open-source nature reduces friction in the relationship and the diligence.

## Guidance For The Alchemist (How To Use This On The Pages)

Pulling it into direction for the agent building the investor pages:

1. **Lead with operationalisation and product-market fit** (a working platform with real use cases and conversations, not an idea).
2. **Show, do not just tell** (the investor pages are themselves an agent-owned, vault-backed site, the live demo; say so).
3. **State the use of funds clearly** (human owners plus agentic teams per area; how the company scales).
4. **Position as a foundational platform play** (enables others; sound technology; VC firms as customers).
5. **Present the revenue model, not precise projections** (consumption/credit value pricing, model-access markup, premium gen-AI experience; figures as scenarios and ranges, with the discover-what-the-market-pays caveat).
6. **Tell the B2C2B story honestly** (consumer users harden the product and lead to business; the reachable-users test).
7. **Seek an aligned, open-source-literate investor who becomes a user** (frame the open-source-as-sovereignty argument).
8. **Respect the compliance handling** (the investor pages engage the UK financial-promotion regime; use the disclaimer and eligibility-gating from the disclaimer deliverable; not-an-offer-to-the-general-public; adviser sign-off before publication; cross-ref: `invest-the-ask-disclaimer-options.md`).
9. **Confirm internal product names before publishing** (some tooling names were unclear in the source and should be verified).

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not financial or investment advice.** It captures the project lead's strategy as guidance for the agent and the pages.
- **Not hard projections.** The figures are illustrative scenarios; the market is to be discovered.
- **Not a generic raise.** A foundational platform play, with an aligned-investor-as-user thesis.
- **Not bypassing compliance.** The pages need the disclaimer, gating, and adviser sign-off.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Projections can over-promise.** Mitigation: present the model, not precise numbers; figures as scenarios and ranges; the project lead's own discover-what-the-market-pays caveat.

**Risk 2: The reachable-users test is a real test.** If the founder's network and existing customers do not adopt, that is the signal. Mitigation: push the vault capabilities to existing Secure Send customers and the network; honest internal read on adoption; the project lead believes they are close.

**Risk 3: Financial-promotion compliance.** The investor pages engage FSMA s.21 / FCA rules. Mitigation: the disclaimer and eligibility-gating (cross-ref: the disclaimer deliverable); adviser sign-off before publication; not legal advice.

**Risk 4: B2C2B depends on the consumer step working.** Mitigation: the consumer step is also how the tech is hardened and shipped, so it has value even before the business conversion; each consumer user is a potential business connection.

**Risk 5: Unverified specifics on the pages.** Some internal names and the figures need confirming. Mitigation: verify product names; frame figures as scenarios.
