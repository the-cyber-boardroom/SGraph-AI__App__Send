## What This Is

The companion to the blast-radius company thesis (cross-ref: the v0.31.9 blast-radius-cybersecurity-company-2026 brief), mapping **how SG/Vault specifically helps such a company.** The thesis argued for a not-a-choke-point, open-source, live-off-the-land visibility play that builds a living digital twin of an organisation and maps the blast radius of change. This brief shows that almost everything that company needs (a safe place for sensitive data, no databases, graphs, evidence, provenance, version control, packaged delivery, multi-audience visualisation, agent peer review) is what the vault already provides. The capabilities are documented across the corpus; this brief applies them to the blast-radius use case, and adds a marked section of further vault contributions at the project lead's invitation.

## Data Collection, Client-Side Transformations, And Not Becoming The Vulnerability

The first and sharpest contribution: **the vault is a safe place to collect and hold the sensitive data the company needs, so the company itself does not become a security vulnerability.**

The project lead: **"the vault could help with data collection, with running transformations on the client side, client-side managed, and then some information sent to you. It gives you a nice place to put the sensitive data you collect, so that you yourself don't become a security vulnerability. The data you want to collect is quite sensitive, so you need a nice place to put it. The vault is perfect, because you can limit access, it's much easier to manage, you don't have databases left right and centre, and the cost is much lower."**

This is a critical point for a cybersecurity company specifically: a company that collects an organisation's sensitive security data (access maps, configurations, identities, the blast-radius graph) becomes a high-value target and a liability if it holds that data carelessly. The vault inverts this:

| The Risk | The Vault's Answer |
|----------|--------------------|
| Collecting sensitive data makes you a target | Zero-knowledge encryption; you hold it without being able to read it |
| Holding it in databases is a liability | No databases; the vault is the store (cross-ref: the vault architecture briefs) |
| Access sprawl | Limit and manage access cleanly |
| Cost of securing it all | Much lower cost to manage |
| Doing the processing exposes the data | Client-side, client-managed transformations (cross-ref: the per-page-semantic-graphs and prompt-context briefs); only what is needed is sent |

So the vault lets the blast-radius company collect and process sensitive data (client-side, client-managed) and hold it safely, without becoming the single juicy target that a security company holding everyone's security data would otherwise be. For a company whose whole pitch is reducing blast radius, not being a liability itself is essential, and the vault is how.

## Each Blast Radius As A Vault (Or A Vault Of Vaults)

The structural fit: **each blast radius (or each item) is itself a vault, holding everything needed to map it, and a vault can hold many.**

The project lead: **"map each item, or each blast radius itself, as a vault. A vault could have multiple. When you map a blast radius, you need the graphs, the evidence, the information, the connections, the documentation, the visualisations, the tools, the app, the people, the roles, the responsibilities. All of this becomes something you need to deliver."**

| What A Blast-Radius Map Needs | The Vault Holds It |
|-------------------------------|--------------------|
| **Graphs** | The multi-dimensional blast-radius graph (cross-ref: the blast-radius and skills-graph briefs) |
| **Evidence** | The evidence behind the analysis (cross-ref: the evidence briefs) |
| **Information and connections** | The data and the chains between items |
| **Documentation** | The written analysis |
| **Visualisations** | The rendered graph and views (cross-ref: the visualisation brief) |
| **Tools and the app** | The mini-app and tools to explore it (cross-ref: the vault-app briefs) |
| **People, roles, responsibilities** | Who is involved and accountable |

This is the base-vault pattern (cross-ref: the v0.31.7 skill-base-vaults brief) applied to a blast radius: a rich, self-contained vault holding everything about one blast radius, the graph, the evidence, the visualisation, the tools, the people. And because it is a vault, it can be a vault of vaults (cross-ref: the vault-in-vault kernel-model brief): vaults connected to vaults, sub-vaults with different transformations or datasets, each focusing on a piece of the puzzle. The project lead: **"you could have vaults of vaults, vaults connected to vaults, vaults with different transformations or different datasets."**

## Packaged Delivery To The Decision-Maker

The delivery fit: **the blast-radius vault is delivered as a pack to the entity that will make the call.**

The project lead: **"you can deliver this in a pack to the entity that is going to make a call to do that."** Because a blast radius is a self-contained vault holding everything (the graph, the evidence, the visualisation, the recommendation), it is directly deliverable: hand the decision-maker the vault, and they have the complete, explorable, evidenced analysis to make the change decision. This is the create-once-deliver pattern (cross-ref: the security-report-vault and vulnerability-demo briefs): the analysis and its delivery are the same artefact.

## Send For Enrichment And Sign-Off (Advanced PKI)

A strong collaboration fit, and an advanced PKI use: **send the evidence pack to others for enrichment, validation, or sign-off, so they see what you see, including your assumptions.**

The project lead: **"you could send the vault or the evidence pack to somebody for enrichment, and now they have the context, they can see what you see, your assumptions, they can validate your assumptions. If you have data points that need multiple teams to validate or sign off, you send them the vault, and they sign off or connect the dots, and because it's version controlled, it becomes much easier to manage."**

This is the feedback-harvesting and shared-sub-vault model (cross-ref: the evidence-CV feedback-harvesting and the sub-vault briefs), here for validation and sign-off:

| The Need | The Vault's Answer |
|----------|--------------------|
| Others must understand the analysis | They get the full vault: they see what you see, including your assumptions |
| Validate the assumptions | They can check and confirm (cross-ref: the assessment briefs' confirm-not-assert) |
| Multiple teams must sign off | Each receives the vault and signs off or enriches |
| Track who validated what | Version control and provenance record it |
| Controlled, secure sharing | Advanced PKI (cross-ref: the vault-in-vault PKI-per-iframe brief) |

So the vault makes the blast-radius analysis collaborative and auditable: the people who must validate or sign off see the full context (not a summary), can check the assumptions, and their sign-off is recorded with provenance. The project lead flags this as where advanced PKI comes into play, controlled, cryptographically-managed sharing of the evidence pack with the right parties.

## No Databases: The Infrastructure And Cost Win

The infrastructure fit reinforces the company's not-a-choke-point architecture (cross-ref: the blast-radius brief): **the vault gives a clean, no-databases, version-controlled infrastructure, with integrity, confidentiality, and provenance, at much lower cost.**

The project lead: **"the other main thing is not having databases, providing a really nice infrastructure-based way of doing things, where you don't have a massive set of databases and the massive cost of all that. It means they move to a version-controlled solution, and they have integrity, confidentiality, provenance, and they can collect a lot more data."**

| The Company Gets | Via The Vault |
|------------------|---------------|
| **No databases** | The vault is the store; no database sprawl (cross-ref: the blast-radius brief's no-databases architecture) |
| **Lower cost** | No database infrastructure to run and secure |
| **Version control** | Native, on everything (cross-ref: the commit-queue brief) |
| **Integrity, confidentiality, provenance** | The vault's core properties |
| **Collect more data** | Cheaper and safer collection means more of it |

This directly serves the company thesis: the no-databases, all-serverless, deployed-everywhere architecture the project lead recommended for the blast-radius company *is* the vault's architecture, so building on the vault gives the company that architecture for free.

## Semantic Graphs, Ontologies, And Taxonomies

The project lead added (having initially passed over it): **the semantic graphs, ontologies, and taxonomies of the blast radius and the business data are connected in the vault.**

The project lead: **"even the semantic graphs, the ontologies, the taxonomies of the blast radius, the information, the business data, all that can be connected there."** This is the multi-dimensional graph the blast-radius thesis requires (cross-ref: the blast-radius brief and the skills-graph brief): the vault holds the semantic graph with its linked ontologies and taxonomies, connecting the blast radius to the business data, which is exactly the document-to-graph and semantic-graph machinery the platform already builds (cross-ref: the vault-per-standard and per-page-semantic-graphs briefs).

## Mini Apps, Multi-Audience Delivery, And Agent Peer Review

The consumption and analysis fit: **the vault delivers mini-apps and visualisations, adjusts the message to multiple audiences, and supports agent-to-agent analysis with provenance.**

The project lead: **"the mini apps, the ability to deliver a vault app, a visualisation, the message adjusted to multiple players, delivered to multiple audiences, is very powerful. And the ability to talk to things in the vaults, and to have analysis. You could have an analysis done by an agent, and then give that vault to another agent for more analysis, or peer review."**

- **Mini-apps and visualisations** (cross-ref: the vault-app and visualisation briefs): the blast-radius vault is explorable, not just readable.
- **Multi-audience delivery** (cross-ref: the security-report-vault and vulnerability-demo briefs' multi-audience): the same blast-radius analysis is delivered to executives, technical teams, and decision-makers each in the right form.
- **Agent peer review with provenance** (this is a strong one): one agent does an analysis in a vault, and you hand that vault to another agent for further analysis or peer review. The project lead: **"the vault of vaults is powerful, because you can have multiple sub-vaults, each focusing on a piece of the puzzle, even with different types of agents accessing it, but now you have provenance and traceability on what each agent did, what changes were made, and you can revert back, because everything is a branch. It's bringing version control natively to the entire solution."**

This is the vault-in-vault plus version-control model (cross-ref: the vault-in-vault and commit-queue briefs) applied to multi-agent analysis: each sub-vault is a piece of the blast-radius puzzle, different agents work on different pieces, and every agent action is provenanced, traceable, and revertible (because everything is a branch). For a blast-radius company, this is how you run a pipeline of agents (collect, analyse, peer-review, validate) with full auditability of which agent concluded what, which is itself a blast-radius-friendly property: you can see and contain what each agent did.

## What This Asks For

1. **Use the vault as the safe store for collected sensitive data** (zero-knowledge; client-side, client-managed transformations; so the company is not the vulnerability).
2. **Make each blast radius a vault** (graphs, evidence, connections, documentation, visualisations, tools, app, people), with vaults of vaults for sub-pieces.
3. **Deliver the blast-radius vault as a pack** to the decision-making entity.
4. **Send packs for enrichment and sign-off** (others see what you see, validate assumptions, sign off; version-controlled; advanced PKI).
5. **Give the company the no-databases infrastructure** (version control, integrity, confidentiality, provenance, lower cost; collect more data).
6. **Hold the semantic graphs, ontologies, and taxonomies** of the blast radius and business data in the vault.
7. **Deliver mini-apps, visualisations, and multi-audience messaging** from the vault.
8. **Support agent peer review with provenance** (sub-vaults per piece, different agents, full traceability, revertible branches).
9. **Apply the additions** (living-twin vault, connectors, sandboxed agents, public previews, one-graph-with-Sentinel, the company's own methods as base vaults).

Estimated effort: this is a mapping of existing vault capabilities to the blast-radius use case, not a new build; the safe-store, no-databases, semantic-graph, version-control, vault-of-vaults, mini-app, multi-audience, and agent-provenance capabilities all exist. The contribution is showing they are exactly what such a company needs. An MVP is a single blast-radius vault (a real example, for instance an identity blast radius or one mapped from a cloud bill) holding the graph, evidence, visualisation, and an agent analysis with provenance.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a new build.** It maps existing vault capabilities to the blast-radius company.
- **Not the company itself becoming a data liability.** The vault keeps the company from being the vulnerability.
- **Not database-backed.** No databases; the vault is the store.
- **Not a single static report.** Living vaults, mini-apps, multi-audience, agent peer review, all version-controlled.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1: Holding clients' sensitive security data is high-stakes even with the vault.** Mitigation: zero-knowledge encryption (the company holds without reading); client-side transformations; access limits; this is the core reason to use the vault rather than databases.

**Risk 2: Multi-agent analysis needs strong provenance to be trustworthy.** Mitigation: version control and provenance on every agent action; revertible branches; sandboxed agents (cross-ref: the vault-in-vault and Daytona briefs).

**Risk 3: The living twin must stay current.** Mitigation: connectors and feedback loops keep it living (cross-ref: the knowledge-capture brief); provenance marks freshness.

**Risk 4: Sharing packs for sign-off must be controlled.** Mitigation: advanced PKI (cross-ref: the vault-in-vault PKI brief); read-only or scoped sharing; version-controlled sign-off.
