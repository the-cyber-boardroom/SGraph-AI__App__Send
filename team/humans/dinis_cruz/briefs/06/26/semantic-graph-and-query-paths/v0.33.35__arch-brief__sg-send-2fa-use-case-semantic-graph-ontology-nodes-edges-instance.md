## What This Is

A semantic-graph and ontology document for the two-factor-authentication use case, written with the ontologist hat on: **it defines the meta-model, the node classes and edge types the risk register uses, then instantiates the full instance graph for the 2FA scenario, from the vulnerability through the first risk, the five-whys chain, the recursive governance risk, the technical and business owners, the propagation to the board, and the confidentiality, integrity, and availability blast radius; it holds to the project's principle that meaning comes from connectivity and not from properties, so the model is carried by its edges; it renders the instance graph as a diagram, maps it onto IssueFS storage as folders of issues, and shows how every change cascades and how queries replay the story.** It is the formal companion to the worked example (cross-ref: the v0.33.35 risk-register, five-whys, digital-twins, and 2FA-demo briefs). New contributions: **the node and edge ontology, and the wired 2FA instance graph.**

## Modelling Principles

The graph obeys the principles established across the corpus. Meaning comes from connectivity, so a node carries almost nothing on its own and is defined by its edges. Everything in the graph is a real, existing fact; phase one admits no hypotheticals. The endpoints of the graph are twins, each of which either reaches reality or is a tracked air gap. Every change cascades upward to the board, and the absence of a cascade is itself an air gap. Claims are typed as facts, evidence, opinions, or hypotheses, and only facts and evidence are in scope for now.

## The Meta-Model: Node Classes

| Class | Definition |
|-------|-----------|
| System | A real system or component; modelled as a twin |
| Vulnerability | A fact with security implications; binary and real |
| Risk | A business impact arising from a vulnerability or another risk |
| Fact | Something that exists or is true; not knowing is also a fact |
| Evidence | Something that supports or challenges a fact, risk, or acceptance |
| Opinion | A contestable interpretation formed by arguing a proposition |
| Hypothesis | A proposed, unproven claim; out of scope in phase one |
| Asset | A thing of value held by a system, such as data |
| DataClass | The classification of an asset's data, which sets the depth of impact |
| Actor | An entity that acts; a twin. Subtypes: Role, Person, Agent |
| Role | A position in the organisation, such as CEO or HR Director |
| Person | A human bound to a twin |
| Agent | An AI actor bound to a twin, acting on a human's behalf |
| Acceptance | An acceptance event, carrying a direction and an interval |
| Interval | The time-box of an acceptance: 1h, 4h, 2d, 2w, 1m, 6m |
| ThreatAgent | An attacker |
| Attack | An attack technique, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK |
| Control | A mitigation of a vulnerability or risk |
| Obligation | A legal or regulatory duty, such as GDPR or ICO reporting |
| Impact | A consequence dimension: Confidentiality, Integrity, or Availability |
| Register | The risk register; the root graph, ending at the board |
| Change | A versioned transformation of the graph; an IssueFS action |
| AirGap | A risk or system not on the register, or a twin not connected to reality |

## The Meta-Model: Edge Types

| Edge | From to To | Meaning |
|------|-----------|---------|
| has_vulnerability | System to Vulnerability | the system exhibits the vulnerability |
| gives_rise_to | Vulnerability to Risk | the vulnerability creates the risk |
| spawns | Risk to Risk | a risk, or a fact about it, creates another risk |
| matters_because | Risk to Risk or Fact | the five-whys chain, why it matters |
| translates_to | Node to Node | a typed domain translation: security, financial, legal |
| exploits | Attack to Vulnerability | the attack uses the vulnerability |
| performed_by | Attack to ThreatAgent | who performs the attack |
| stores | System to Asset | the system holds the asset |
| classified_as | Asset to DataClass | the asset's data classification |
| exposes | Risk to Asset | the risk puts the asset at stake |
| impacts | Risk to Impact | the consequence dimension, C, I, or A |
| triggers | Impact to Obligation | a consequence triggers a duty |
| reported_to | Obligation to Actor | for example a GDPR breach reported to the ICO |
| validated_by | Risk to Actor | the technical owner confirms the fact |
| owned_by | Risk to Actor | the business owner owns the risk |
| accepted_by | Risk to Actor, via Acceptance | the actor accepts the risk |
| has_interval | Acceptance to Interval | the time-box of the acceptance |
| reports_to | Actor to Actor | the org-chart line |
| propagates_to | Acceptance to Actor | the acceptance flows up the chain |
| underwritten_by | Acceptance to Actor | a co-signer at altitude |
| overrides | Actor to Acceptance | a superior overrides, the original preserved |
| backed_by | Fact or Risk to Evidence | supporting evidence |
| challenged_by | Fact or Risk to Evidence | challenging evidence |
| lacks | Fact to Evidence | absence of evidence, since not knowing is a fact |
| mitigated_by | Vulnerability or Risk to Control | a control reduces it |
| connected_to | Risk to Register | the risk is on the register |
| is_air_gap | Risk, System, or Twin to AirGap | not connected, to the register or to reality |
| represents | Twin to RealEntity | the twin mirrors a real thing |
| acts_as | Twin to Role | the twin plays the role |
| bound_to | Twin to Person or Agent | the actor behind the twin |
| documents | Agent to Reasoning | the agent records its reasoning and persona |
| transforms | Change to Graph | the change is a graph transformation, an IssueFS action |
| cascades_to | Change to Register | every change propagates upward |

## The Instance Graph: The 2FA Use Case

### The Vulnerability and the First Risk

- **SYS-A** (System) has_vulnerability **V1** "Admin accounts lack two-factor authentication" (Vulnerability, binary), backed_by **E1** "Configuration shows no MFA on admin accounts".
- **V1** gives_rise_to **R1** "Admin accounts could be compromised" (Risk).
- **ATK-1** "Credential stuffing" (Attack, MITRE T1110.004) exploits **V1**, performed_by **TA-1** "External attacker" (ThreatAgent).

### The Five-Whys Chain

- **R1** matters_because "Leaked or reused passwords can be tried at scale" matters_because "An admin may have reused a password" matters_because "An attacker could log in unauthorised" matters_because "Sensitive systems become reachable". Each link is a node; the chain climbs toward the business impact.

### The Recursive Governance Risk

- **R1** accepted_by **TWIN-ITD** "IT Director" is challenged_by **E2** "Accepting business risk is not the IT Director's function".
- That fact spawns **R2** "Governance air gap: risk accepted by the wrong owner" (Risk), connected_to Register.
- **R2** owned_by **TWIN-GRC** "Head of GRC", accepted_by an Acceptance with has_interval **4h** (direction: reduce, a P1 to redo the mapping).
- **R2** propagates_to **TWIN-CIO** propagates_to **TWIN-CEO** propagates_to **TWIN-BOARD**, each underwritten at 4h.
- When **R1** is re-homed (below), the condition behind **R2** no longer holds; **R2** is resolved, and a Change cascades_to Register to clear it from the higher views.

### Technical versus Business Owner, and the HR System

- **R1** validated_by **TWIN-ITD** (technical owner) but owned_by the business owner.
- The impacted system is **SYS-HR** "HR System", which has_vulnerability **V2** "Product does not support 2FA", backed_by **E-V** "Vendor confirms 2FA unsupported".
- **SYS-HR** stores **AST-HR** "HR data", classified_as **DC-1** "Sensitive full HR data" (passports, salaries, PIPs, reviews, hires and fires); the alternative classification **DC-2** "Anonymised timesheets" would shrink the blast radius.
- **R3** "Unauthorised admin access to the HR system" exposes **AST-HR**, owned_by **TWIN-HRD** "HR Director" reports_to **TWIN-CPO** "Chief People Officer" reports_to **TWIN-CEO**.

### Acceptance and Propagation

- **R3** accepted_by **TWIN-HRD**, then propagates_to **TWIN-CPO** and **TWIN-CEO**.
- The interval is gated by feasibility and evidence: the **4h** interval is struck off because a fix is not technically possible that fast; **E3** "No evidence of compromise" backs the absence of a clear and present danger. Remaining options: **1m** or **2m** (funded project), **6m** (do nothing), **2w** (incident), **48h** (P1).
- No Control currently mitigates **V2**; there is no compensating control in play for **SYS-HR**.

### The Confidentiality, Integrity, and Availability Blast Radius

- **R3** impacts **Confidentiality**, which triggers **OBL-GDPR** "GDPR breach" reported_to **ICO**, underwritten_by **TWIN-CFO** (potential fine) and **TWIN-CEO**. This yields **R5** "Data incident" and **R6** "GDPR breach" as two distinct risks.
- **R3** impacts **Integrity**, spawning **R7** "Salary or employee-record tampering, or fabricated hires".
- **R3** impacts **Availability**, spawning **R8** "Weekly backup cadence: data loss between last backup and compromise" and **R9** "Restore never tested", backed_by **E4** "Backup logs: weekly" and **E5** "No record of a tested restore".

### The Not-Knowing Fact

- **F5** "Full extent of impact not captured" is a Fact that lacks Evidence; it spawns **R4** "Risk mis-classified and mis-accepted until investigated", which itself must be owned and accepted until evidence closes it.

## Storage in IssueFS

Each node is an issue, a file that holds its definition, and each folder is a set of issues forming a subgraph, for example a folder per system, per actor, or per risk cluster. Edges are links between issues. A change to the graph is an IssueFS transformation, a versioned action that adds, links, or resolves issues, and the commit history is the ordered list of those transformations. The whole graph is folders and files, with no database, yet remains queryable, which is what lets an agent manage it directly through the CLI.

## Querying the Graph

Because meaning is in the edges, the useful operations are traversals: from a vulnerability, find every risk and every owner it reaches; from a risk, climb the acceptance chain to the board; from the register, list every air gap. The narrative engine replays the ordered changes so the story can be watched: the vulnerability appears, R1 propagates, R2 fires and resolves, R3 re-homes, and the C, I, and A branches bloom, each step a timestamped IssueFS transformation.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not property-led.** Nodes are thin; meaning is in the edges.
- **Not exhaustive.** It models the 2FA scenario, not every possible risk.
- **Not hypothetical.** Only facts and evidence are instantiated.
- **Not a database schema.** It maps to IssueFS folders and files.

## Honest Notes

- **The ontology will grow.** New scenarios will add classes and edges; keep it minimal and earn each addition.
- **IssueFS naming to confirm.** The storage mechanism is rendered as IssueFS, pending confirmation.
- **Translation edges are sketched.** The typed domain translations from the five-whys brief need their own modelling pass.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. The node classes and edge types are implemented as the register's schema.
2. The 2FA instance graph is representable end to end with these types.
3. Nodes are thin; relationships carry the meaning.
4. Only facts and evidence are instantiated in phase one.
5. The graph stores as IssueFS folders of issues, linkable and queryable without a database.
6. Traversals answer reach, ownership, propagation, and air-gap queries.
7. Ordered changes replay as the narrative.
