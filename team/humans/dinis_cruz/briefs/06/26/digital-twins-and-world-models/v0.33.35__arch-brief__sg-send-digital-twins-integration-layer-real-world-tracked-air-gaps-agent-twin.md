## What This Is

Digital twins as the integration layer between the risk register, Risk Mandate.ai, and the real world: **because the risk workflow produces risks of risks and every action carries risk, the model has to simulate, and because meaning in the graph comes from connectivity, connection by nodes and edges, not by properties, the graph has to connect to real systems, real organisations, and the real org chart, which is hard because the real world is a mess of different, manual, and uncodified systems; a layer of digital twins solves this by giving three things, control over the schemas the risks map to, a single place to connect the real world, and a place to represent an air gap that is understood and tracked, this has no API, this is updated manually once a week; ideally a twin connects directly to the real system and takes its feed, and twins can stack, a twin of MFA, a twin of the solution, then the real solution, which adds abstraction and isolation; the system only ever sees a twin, so it works in an enterprise way from the first moment, and onboarding becomes describing what you have, which builds the digital twin of the organisation; for Risk Mandate.ai the focus is the digital twin of an agent, whose permissions, capabilities, track record, and credibility become properties of the twin, connected to the real world, with the standing question of how connected the twin actually is to reality.** It is the integration architecture under the register (cross-ref: the v0.33.35 risk-register brief, the v0.33.32 agent-mandate-graph and potential-mandate briefs, and the v0.33.33 S3-compatible vault container, itself a digital twin). New contributions: **the twin as the integration point, the tracked air gap, and the digital twin of an agent.**

## The Framing

This is about the importance of digital twins in risk management, specifically twins that simulate organisations, applications, and capabilities. They are not a side feature; they are how the model touches reality.

## Why You Have To Simulate

The workflow does not produce a single tidy risk. The project lead: **"you do not just have one risk, you end up with risks of risks, and more importantly every action you do carries risk, so we need to simulate this."** This is the same instinct as building several threat models for the same system depending on which features are turned on, so that the different futures can be compared. Simulation is the only way to reason about risks of risks and the risk of each action.

## Meaning Comes From Connectivity

The register earns its meaning from how things are connected. The project lead: **"meaning comes from connectivity, connection by nodes and edges, not via properties, which means you have to connect to the real systems, the real organisations, the real org chart."** That is the ambition, and it is also the problem, because the project lead: **"as soon as you want to do that, everything is different, you have different systems, manual systems, even systems nobody has ever codified."** The real world does not present a clean interface, so connecting to it directly is where the difficulty lives.

## The Core Principle

The non-negotiable behaviour is the same one the register depends on. The project lead: **"every time something happens, the whole graph changes, connected all the way up."** A change has to propagate, and where the graph cannot reach the real system it has an air gap, the same air gap concept from the register. The ambition is always to keep pushing the connection further down, toward the real system.

## What a Digital-Twin Layer Gives You

Inserting a layer of digital twins between the register and the real world provides three things at once:

1. **Control over the schemas.** Inside the twin environment the schemas the risks map to are under your control and kept aligned, rather than being at the mercy of whatever the real systems happen to expose.
2. **A place to connect the real world.** The twin is the single, well-defined point where a real system attaches.
3. **A tracked, understood air gap.** Where there is no clean connection, the twin holds the gap explicitly. The project lead: **"we have a place to create an air gap that we understand and can track, this needs to be manually updated once a week, but the point is we now know where that gap is."** The gap might be that something has no API, does not exist, or must be updated by hand on a schedule; what matters is that the gap is named and located rather than hidden.

## Connect Directly Where You Can

The ideal is always a live connection. For the two-factor authentication example, the twin connects directly to the real identity system, for instance Intune, Microsoft Entra, or Okta, and takes its feed from there, so the twin reflects reality automatically.

## Twins of Twins

Twins can stack, which is where the abstraction pays off. The project lead: **"you can have a twin of MFA with the MFA properties, then a twin of the actual solution, then connect the real solution, which also gives you isolation and security."** So a conceptual twin of MFA carries the MFA properties, a twin of the specific product sits beneath it, and the real product connects below that. The layering creates clean abstraction boundaries and, because the layers are isolated, real security between them.

## The System Only Ever Sees a Twin

This is what makes the product work from day one. The project lead: **"the system only ever sees a digital twin, so from the system's point of view it is already working in an enterprise way."** Because the register always talks to a twin, never directly to the messy real world, it behaves consistently whether the twin is fed live, fed manually, or merely described. Onboarding then becomes simple: someone describes what they have, what exists and what does not, and that description populates the twins, building a digital twin of the organisation, which has many uses beyond risk.

## The Integration Point Is a Digital Twin

This is the key architectural statement. The project lead: **"the integration point between the risk register, the risk mandate, and the real world is a digital twin."** The twin is where the model meets reality. Initially it lets you create the realities by hand, and over time it provides a large space of scalable workflows and architectures, because it puts you in control of the simulation rather than leaving you at the mercy of the real systems.

## The Digital Twin of an Agent

For Risk Mandate.ai the most important twin is the agent. The project lead: **"we create a digital twin of an agent, which represents what it does and does not do, its capabilities, so the permissions, the capabilities, the track record, the credibility become properties of the twin, and then we connect it to the real world."** So an agent is modelled as a twin whose properties are exactly the things the mandate work cares about, what it is permitted to do, what it is capable of, how it has behaved, and how much it can be trusted. The twin is then connected to the real agent, and the standing question, the one that measures how trustworthy the model is, is how connected the twin actually is to reality.

## What This Asks For

1. Build a digital-twin layer as the integration point between the register and the real world.
2. Give twins controlled schemas, a real-world connection, and an explicit tracked air gap.
3. Connect directly to real systems where an API exists, such as the identity system.
4. Support twins of twins, for abstraction and isolation.
5. Make the register only ever talk to a twin, so it works from the first moment.
6. Make onboarding the act of describing the organisation, building its twin.
7. Model each agent as a twin with permissions, capabilities, track record, and credibility.
8. Track how connected each twin is to reality.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a direct coupling.** The register never talks to the messy real world directly.
- **Not properties-only.** Meaning comes from connectivity, not from properties alone.
- **Not a hidden gap.** Where there is no connection, the air gap is named and tracked.
- **Not single-layer.** Twins stack, for abstraction and isolation.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: a twin can drift from reality.** A twin that is not well connected can mislead. Mitigation: track how connected each twin is, and treat a stale twin as an air gap.

**Tension 2: manual air gaps are work.** Hand-updated gaps cost effort and can lapse. Mitigation: name the cadence and surface overdue updates as their own risk.

**Tension 3: twins of twins add complexity.** Layering can obscure as well as clarify. Mitigation: keep each layer's responsibility clear and isolated.
