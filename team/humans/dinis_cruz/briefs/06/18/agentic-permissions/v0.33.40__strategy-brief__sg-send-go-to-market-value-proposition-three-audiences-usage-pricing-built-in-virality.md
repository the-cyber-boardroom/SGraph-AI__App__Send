## What This Is

The go-to-market strategy for the agent blast-radius offering: **finding users is not the hard part, having a service people get value from is, so lead with a focused, binary value proposition (here is your blast radius, here are the examples, which one applies to you) and align with the three audiences who have energy, the agentic users who want to know their blast radius, the buyers who want to know the side effects of the permissions they grant, and the sellers who need to prove they are secure and run minimal privilege; the model rests on extreme granularity, a one-to-one mapping between agentic tasks and steps and permissions, a graph of privileges as deep as you want and never admin-at-the-top; pricing is usage-based and super cheap, even free, because if you cannot get people to use it for free you do not have a system; growth is built-in virality rather than marketing, since every message recipient and data consumer becomes a potential user; so you spend money serving users, not finding them, which is why the agentic team and the investment matter.** It extends the service and commercial work (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 agent-blast-radius-service and commercial-model briefs, the v0.33.40 skills-as-code brief, the v0.33.27 finding-early-users brief, and the v0.33.38 guidance-gen-ai value-not-rent brief). New contributions: **the product-not-users premise, the three energy audiences, the one-to-one mapping, and the built-in virality.**

## The Series Framing

What this covers: **how to hit the market and gain traction.**

The project lead: **"I want to talk about the go-to-market strategy, how you hit the market and get the company that supports this to gain traction."** So this is the distribution pillar of the series.

## The Premise: The Product, Not The Users

The reframing: **finding users is not hard; having something they value is.**

The project lead: **"it is not very hard to find users. The key premise is that we need a service somebody gets value from."** So the go-to-market problem is not acquisition; it is whether the product earns repeat use, which reframes where effort and money should go.

## The Value Proposition: Focused And Binary

How to land it: **here is your blast radius, here are the examples, which one applies to you.**

The project lead: **"this is where the more focused, the more binary, the better: here is the value we provide, do you know this, here is the blast radius, and more importantly, here are the examples of what it is, and which one applies to you."** So the pitch is concrete and self-locating: the prospect sees real examples and recognises their own situation in one of them (cross-ref: the proactive-evidence-database brief, which supplies the examples).

## The Three Audiences With Energy

Who to align with, since it depends who you talk to:

| Audience | What They Want |
|----------|----------------|
| Agentic users | To understand their own blast radius |
| Buyers of agentic solutions | To understand the side effects of the permissions they will grant |
| Sellers of agentic solutions | To prove they are secure and run with minimal privilege |

The project lead: **"there are the people using agentic who want to understand the blast radius, the companies buying agentic solutions who want to understand the blast radius and the side effects of the permissions they will give, and the companies selling who now need to prove they are secure. There are good players who want to say, we do not need a lot of privileges, we can run with minimal privileges, we have thought this through."** So all three have energy, and the sellers in particular want a way to demonstrate restraint (cross-ref: the finding-early-users brief, on aligning with interested parties).

## The One-To-One Mapping

The granularity that underpins it: **one permission per task and step, a graph of privileges.**

The project lead: **"it is not just one privilege, it is a graph of privileges, and ironically it should be more granular, almost a one-to-one mapping between the agentic workflow and a permission, in fact between the agentic tasks and the steps they execute and the permissions they got. We can scale now; it is just how deep down the rabbit hole and how many layers of permissions you want to go. Admin or access-to-everything is at the top, but that is too dangerous."** So the model rewards granularity: a permission per task and step, navigable as deep as needed, with the all-access top deliberately avoided (cross-ref: the skills-as-code and graphs-of-graphs briefs).

## Pricing: Usage-Based, Super Cheap, Even Free

The pricing as a filter: **if you cannot get free usage, you do not have a system.**

The project lead: **"the price is so important. It should be super cheap to use, usage-based. If you cannot get people to use this for free, you do not have a system."** So pricing is both go-to-market and validation: cheap, usage-based access lowers the barrier, and the inability to attract even free usage is the signal that the product is not yet right (cross-ref: the value-not-rent and commercial-model briefs).

## We All Have Access To Users

Where the real constraint is: **everyone has access to users; the question is whether they come back.**

The project lead: **"we all have access to users. The question is, do we have something they want to use, that they come back to, that we use ourselves. You do not need a lot of mass market. I do not think there should be any marketing on this. Every user that uses this should create a couple more users, who create a couple more."** So the strategy is product-led: no marketing spend, just a product good enough that use begets use.

## Built-In Virality

Why use begets use here: **every recipient and data consumer becomes a potential user.**

The project lead: **"a couple of elements of the platform are a bit viral, because every consumer, every recipient of the messaging, every consumer of data becomes a potential user. If a company says here are our permissions, look how secure we are, the receiver goes, this is interesting, I want to use this. And if a company pushes requirements downstream, those downstream parties become consumers themselves. So if this is working, you do not need a lot of early users."** So virality is structural: proving your security to someone, or pushing requirements down your supply chain, exposes the tool to the next party and pulls them in (cross-ref: the vault-append-capability-message and recipient-vault briefs).

## Spend On Serving, Not Finding

Where the money goes: **serve the users, do not chase them.**

The project lead: **"you do not need to spend money finding users. You want to spend money serving them. That is why the agentic team is very important, which I want to talk about next, and why you need investment: it is about serving the customer, serving the use cases, curating the content, making sure the content, the service, the materials, and the experience are really good. You do not need a lot of them, you just need to do that really, really well."** So the investment thesis inverts the usual: money goes to serving, curation, and quality, not acquisition, delivered by the agentic team (the flagged next memo).

## What This Asks For

1. **Lead with the binary value proposition** (here is your blast radius, which example applies).
2. **Align with the three audiences** (users, buyers, sellers).
3. **Build the one-to-one mapping** (permission per task and step; never admin-at-the-top).
4. **Price usage-based and super cheap** (free usage as the validation filter).
5. **Rely on the product, not marketing** (use begets use).
6. **Lean on built-in virality** (recipients and downstream parties become users).
7. **Spend on serving and curation** (the agentic team and quality).

Estimated effort: this is a strategy; the work is the value proposition, the granular mapping, the usage pricing, and the serving capacity.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not acquisition-led.** Finding users is not the hard part.
- **Not admin-at-the-top.** Granularity per task and step.
- **Not marketing-driven.** Product-led, with built-in virality.
- **Not spend-to-find.** Spend to serve.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: free usage must convert to revenue.** Free lowers the barrier but must lead to paid usage. Mitigation: the usage and billable-units model converts use to revenue (cross-ref: the commercial-model brief).

**Tension 2: virality assumes the messaging features work.** The viral loop relies on recipients and downstream parties encountering the tool. Mitigation: the vault messaging and downstream-requirement features carry it (cross-ref: the vault-append-capability-message brief).

**Tension 3: three audiences, three needs.** The value proposition must flex across users, buyers, and sellers. Mitigation: the same blast-radius map, framed for each (cross-ref: the second-third-order-side-effects brief, on recipient language).

**Tension 4: no-marketing is bold.** Relying purely on product and virality is a strong bet. Mitigation: invest the saved spend in serving and quality, which feeds the virality.
