# The Commercial Model: Billable Units, Markup On Tokens, And Recurring Usage

## What This Is

The commercial model for the agent blast-radius offering: **a set of billable units charged across everything provided, the way the cloud hyperscalers charge for compute, storage, bandwidth, and DNS, plus the big one, a markup on the tokens consumed, the OpenRouter model, all to drive usage; the hook is the data, not the technology, so the charge is for managing and maintaining the customer's private data, accounts, and custom environments, connected to how much they use the platform; a business-to-consumer-to-business path of consumer-priced, credit-card, self-serve early adopters opens the businesses, on a consumption model that charges per token including a markup even on bring-your-own-key; direct-to-business SaaS hosting comes with security and privacy tiers from shared to dedicated VPCs and accounts, priced transparently by value into the tens and hundreds of thousands; there are curated content feeds, a security-consultant marketplace, run-in-their-infrastructure for sovereignty, and mass-customised versions of the open-source stack including maintaining the agent mandates for companies; and the metrics are recurring usage and adoption, not annual recurring revenue.** It delivers the commercialisation memo and extends the usage and open-source work (cross-ref: the v0.33.26 selling-llm-usage brief, the v0.33.40 open-source-strategy and agent-blast-radius-service briefs, and the v0.33.38 guidance-gen-ai value-not-rent brief). New contributions: **the billable-units model, the data-is-the-hook point, the hosting tiers, and the recurring-usage metric.**

## The Series Framing

What this covers: **the commercial model and monetisation, the flagged memo.**

The project lead: **"now we talk about the commercial model, the monetisation possibilities. It is good to go back to previous memos on this for more ideas, but the key principle is to have a set of monetisation features."** So this pulls the commercial thinking together (cross-ref: the selling-llm-usage brief).

## Billable Units

The key principle: **lots of small billable units, like the cloud hyperscalers.**

The project lead: **"the key principle is a set of billable units that we charge across the multiple areas of everything we provide. This is what the cloud hyperscalers do: compute, storage, bandwidth, IP addresses, DNS entries, every one of those becomes a billable unit we charge for."** So the model is granular and consumption-shaped, charging for many small units rather than one big licence.

## The Markup On Tokens

The big one: **mark up the tokens consumed.**

The project lead: **"a big one is the markup on the tokens being consumed. A good example is OpenRouter, and a lot of other open-source gen-AI startups that build by consumption: they mark up a little on the usage and make crazy revenues. That is fundamentally what we do."** So the largest stream is a small markup on token consumption, at volume (cross-ref: the selling-llm-usage brief).

## Drive Usage

The aim of it all: **drive usage, because the charge is connected to use.**

The project lead: **"the point is to drive usage: the analysis, the understanding, the retest, the re-analysis. In this first phase we are giving visibility, allowing all sorts of analysis. The charge should be connected to how they use the platform, how often, how many permissions they map, how many simulations they run."** So the commercial engine is usage, and phase one drives it by giving visibility (cross-ref: the agent-blast-radius-service brief).

## The Hook Is The Data, Not The Technology

Where the lock comes from, honestly: **the hook is their data, which is why it is private.**

The project lead: **"as soon as it becomes specific to them, that is the hook. The hook is not the technology or the enterprise features, it is the data, their information, the fact that it is private. We should charge for managing and maintaining that data, their accounts, their custom domains, their custom environments. That gives recurring revenue, but more importantly usage revenue."** So consistent with the open-source strategy, the technology is open and the hook is the customer's own data and customisation, which the company manages (cross-ref: the open-source-strategy brief).

## Business-To-Consumer-To-Business, And Consumption

The way in, and the model: **consumer-priced self-serve opens the business.**

The project lead: **"one bit is the consumer base, the more immediate base, and sometimes it is business-to-consumer-to-business, where the consumer is a business person, so we hit the business via early adopters. If you do not have a service somebody can use and pay a little for, it will not scale. Most execs can pay this on a credit card. Can they start using and paying straight away to gain visibility. It is a consumption-based model, especially if we let them talk to models, do photo analysis and photo views, all charged per token. They can choose the model, or bring their own tokens, and there is nothing wrong with charging an overhead even on bring-your-own-key."** So the wedge is a cheap, self-serve, credit-card service that lands inside companies through individual early adopters (cross-ref: the beta-cohort-agent-testers and openrouter-key-acquisition briefs).

## Hosting And Security Tiers

The direct-to-business SaaS, tiered by security: **from shared to dedicated, priced by value.**

The project lead: **"there is a direct-to-business model where we host it, a pure SaaS business: we provide the service, manage it, the orchestration. We charge for security and privacy. There is the generic, cheaper, shared-infrastructure option with less privacy and sovereignty, and then tighter options, dedicated environments, dedicated VPCs, even dedicated accounts in their environment. We manage and charge for it. We have transparent pricing, we do not make it up per customer, but some setups can cost tens of thousands, up to a hundred thousand, depending on the infrastructure and consumption."**

| Tier | What | Pricing |
|------|------|---------|
| Shared | Shared infrastructure, less privacy and sovereignty | Cheaper |
| Dedicated | Dedicated environments, VPCs, or accounts | By value, tens to hundreds of thousands |

## Content-Driven Feeds

Another stream, content not service: **curated feeds we maintain.**

The project lead: **"there are curated feeds we provide. They do not need to be private, it just means we maintain them, keep them updated, quality control them, and they use them as a service. So there are service-driven revenue streams, but also content-driven revenue streams."** So the company sells maintained, quality-controlled content feeds alongside the service (cross-ref: the data-broker idea in the open-source-strategy brief).

## The Security-Consultant Marketplace

A marketplace, set up quickly: **security consultants analysing on top of our data.**

The project lead: **"an interesting stream is to connect with security consultants and companies who want to do analysis on top of what we have. That marketplace can be set up very quickly, because we facilitate the data and the communications with the vault."** So the company can host a marketplace where third-party experts add analysis, with the vault as the facilitation layer.

## Run-In-Their-Infrastructure

For the sovereignty-conscious: **they run it, we maintain it.**

The project lead: **"they might want to run this in their own infrastructure, and they should, for privacy, security, governance, and sovereignty. They run the environment, but we maintain the tech, the customisations, and make sure the next versions do not break. That is all revenue streams."** So even fully sovereign deployments generate maintenance revenue (cross-ref: the open-source-strategy and sovereignty briefs).

## Mass-Customised Stack And Maintaining The Mandates

The scaling premise, and a hook: **sell mass-customised open-source stacks, and maintain the mandates for companies.**

The project lead: **"the key premise is that we are selling mass-customised versions of our open-source stack. And on integrations, many companies use our technology to expose their own properties, and they might not want to maintain it, so we maintain a lot of the properties, the security, the agent mandates, for a lot of companies. That is a nice hook, because we become a trusted place people go to for information about a product, service, or capability."** So the company maintains the agent mandates (the bundle named in the earlier brief) on behalf of companies, becoming the trusted source (cross-ref: the naming-the-agent-authorisation-bundle brief). The project lead flagged a next memo: how to create much of this proactively, even if the other side does not want to engage.

## The Metrics: Usage And Adoption, Not ARR

How to measure and value it: **track recurring usage and adoption, not annual recurring revenue.**

The project lead: **"the valuation connects directly to revenue, but more important than the number of paying customers or their revenue is the number of new customers adopting, how much they spend, and which way the arrow is going: are they coming back, using it more, spending more, are we scaling, are we not slowing on acquisition, which we should not, because most of it is self-serve. We should not track annual recurring revenue, we should track recurring usage, monthly, weekly, even daily: how much the platform is used. And because it is open source, it becomes adoption: how many people and companies adopt it. The valuation becomes these key primitives."**

| Metric | Why |
|--------|-----|
| Recurring usage (daily, weekly, monthly) | Usage is the engine, not booked revenue |
| New-customer adoption and direction | Are more coming, and are they spending more |
| Self-serve acquisition rate | Growth that does not slow with scale |
| Open-source adoption | How many people and companies use it |

## What This Asks For

1. **Define the billable units** (consumption-shaped, like the cloud hyperscalers).
2. **Mark up the tokens** (the largest stream, at volume).
3. **Charge for managing the data** (the hook, plus usage).
4. **Open with consumer-priced self-serve** (the business-to-consumer-to-business wedge).
5. **Offer hosting tiers** (shared to dedicated, priced by value, transparently).
6. **Sell feeds and a consultant marketplace** (content and ecosystem streams).
7. **Maintain stacks and mandates** (mass-customised, including the agent mandates).
8. **Track recurring usage and adoption** (not annual recurring revenue).

Estimated effort: this is the commercial design; the build is the metering and billing, the hosting tiers, and the marketplace, on the existing usage model.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a flat licence.** Granular billable units and token markup.
- **Not technology lock-in.** The hook is the data, the technology is open.
- **Not opaque pricing.** Transparent, even at high dedicated-hosting prices.
- **Not ARR-driven.** Recurring usage and adoption are the metrics.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: usage metering versus the privacy posture.** Charging by usage means measuring usage, which sits against a no-tracking, privacy-first claim. Mitigation: reconcile the metering with the privacy posture, and be transparent about what is measured (cross-ref: the image-generation-opportunity brief's no-tracking-versus-observability item).

**Tension 2: bring-your-own-key overhead must feel fair.** Charging on top of a customer's own key can feel like double-dipping. Mitigation: transparent pricing and clear value for the overhead.

**Tension 3: high-touch dedicated hosting versus self-serve scale.** Bespoke dedicated deployments do not scale like self-serve. Mitigation: lead with self-serve, treat dedicated as high-value, not the volume engine.

**Tension 4: usage-not-ARR is unconventional.** Some investors expect ARR. Mitigation: show that recurring usage and adoption are the leading indicators for an open-source, consumption business.
