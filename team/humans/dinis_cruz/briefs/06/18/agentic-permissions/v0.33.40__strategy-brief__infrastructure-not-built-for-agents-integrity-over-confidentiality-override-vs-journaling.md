## What This Is

The always-existed-in-software thesis flagged in the first brief, centralised: **our databases, data stores, and applications were never designed for agentic workflows, so they are deeply vulnerable, and while the headlines are about confidentiality, integrity and availability are far more dangerous, because an integrity attack can destroy a company with very little data, since once some records are corrupted you no longer know which to trust, and the core reason our systems cannot survive this is that most databases override data instead of journaling every request, so they cannot identify, diff, or selectively remove malicious changes; we got away with it because attackers were unsophisticated and limited by user interfaces and by their own business models, and because there are no strong integrity laws, creating a market of lemons that hides the scale, but agents remove those limits and traverse the full authorised universe, compounded by overprivilege and multi-system blind spots, so this technological, commercial, regulatory, and ecosystem problem must now be solved.** It is the problem statement under the agent-authorisation series (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 agent-authorisation-blast-radius brief, the v0.33.38 confidence-through-evidence thesis, the v0.32.3 nhi-2.0 briefs, and the v0.33.16 artefact-driven-security-assessments brief). New contributions: **the integrity-over-confidentiality argument, the override-versus-journaling principle, and the market-of-lemons framing.**

## The Series Framing

What this is in the series: **the centralised document on the problem that always existed in software.**

The project lead: **"now that we are capturing the union of the agent's capabilities, the delegated power we give the agent, it is a good moment to create a centralised document on something I have talked about before: our current data stores, databases, and applications were not designed for agentic workflows."** So this brief is the foundational problem statement the rest of the series sits on.

## Not Designed For Agentic Workflows

The starting claim: **our systems are vulnerable, especially on integrity and availability.**

The project lead: **"we have lots of systems that are very vulnerable, from a confidentiality point of view but especially from an integrity and availability point of view."** These systems were built for human-paced, UI-bounded use, not for autonomous agents acting at scale, and that mismatch is the root of the danger.

## Why They Survived So Far

Why the fragility has not yet been fully exposed: **attackers were unsophisticated, did not scale, and were time-bound.**

The project lead: **"the reason they survived is that the attackers, whether malicious or accidents or bugs, manual or automated, were not very sophisticated and never scaled well, and there was a time frame of execution. The exceptions are ransomware and data exfiltration, and those are the canary in the coal mine, because they tell us how insecure, unresilient, and fragile the whole system is. The fact that somebody can deploy to thousands of machines and break them without being detected, or exfiltrate terabytes without being detected, is crazy, and most of our infrastructure allows exactly that."** So the survival was luck and limitation, not resilience, and the big attacks already show what the fragility permits.

## Integrity Over Confidentiality

The core argument: **confidentiality is child's play compared to integrity.**

The project lead: **"the headlines have been about confidentiality, but integrity is far more dangerous. Confidentiality is child's play compared to integrity, because an integrity attack will literally destroy a company, and you do not need large quantities. If a database has corrupted data and you do not know which is true, you do not need a large amount to compromise the whole thing, because even if it is five percent, you do not know which five percent, so it could be twenty or fifty. What you know is that some records are corrupted, so you lose trust."** This is the heart of it: integrity attacks destroy trust in the entire dataset with very little effort, because uncertainty about which records are corrupt poisons all of them.

## The Market Of Lemons

Why we underestimate the problem: **no integrity laws, vested interests, and silence.**

The project lead: **"we got away with it, so we never asked the hard questions. We have laws on confidentiality but not on integrity, at least not strong ones. So every time there is an integrity failure, unless it is undeniably visible, every major player has a vested interest, sometimes even NDAs, not to talk about it. It is a market of lemons: we do not actually know how big the problem is."** So the scale is hidden: without disclosure obligations and with strong incentives to stay quiet, the true extent of integrity failures is unknown.

## The Core Principle: Override Versus Journaling

The structural reason systems cannot recover: **most databases override data rather than journaling every request.**

The project lead: **"most of our databases cannot survive integrity problems because they override data. When you change a record, they override the previous setting, at best leaving a log message. Very few log the entire request, very few have a journaling situation, and very few can selectively go back in time, diff a particular section of changes, and say these are malicious. Take your email or calendar data: if you suddenly have thousands of entries, done at the same time as benign ones, at best you have a backup from a day or two ago, if the restore works, and even then you lose the delta between the last healthy backup and now. We have seen many cases where even with good backups, companies could only restore the last known good state, never saying exactly which records were compromised. With journaling and a strong graph of identity, you could say here is the pattern and here are all the malicious ones. We do not have that."**

| System Behaviour | Consequence |
|------------------|-------------|
| Override the record | The previous truth is gone |
| At best a log message | Not the full request |
| No journaling | Cannot reconstruct what happened |
| No selective time-travel or diff | Cannot isolate the malicious changes |
| Restore last-known-good only | Lose the delta, cannot identify the bad records |

## It Applies To Every SaaS

The scope of the problem: **Salesforce, Slack, monday.com, Jira, all of them.**

The project lead: **"you have to apply this to every database we have. Salesforce, Slack, monday.com, Jira. Last time I looked, Jira backups were a disaster, they did not back up the whole thing. Most backups are of the data but do not back up everything, and companies have not had the time or the business case."** So this is not a niche flaw; it is the default behaviour of the platforms businesses run on.

## The Lock-In Irony

A perverse incentive: **better backup and restore means less lock-in, so companies underinvest.**

The project lead: **"a lot of this is regulatory and client demand. If there is no demand, why would companies invest, especially because the better your backup and your ability to control this, the less lock-in you have, since it is easier to move. That is the irony. And yet it is also in companies' best interests to make backup and restore super efficient and to capture everything."** So the very capability that would protect customers also frees them, which is why vendors have quietly avoided building it well (cross-ref: the sovereignty and no-lock-in thesis).

## The Agents Change Everything

Why now is different: **agents remove the old limits and traverse the full authorised universe.**

The project lead: **"in the past this was limited because users and attackers were bounded by the UI and by what you can do, and were not motivated to find ways around it. Even attackers have a limited business model, so they focus on a specific set of actions, not the universe of what is possible. That is the status quo that led us here, and it is why now it is different. Our identities need a much better, more dynamic system, there is overprivilege everywhere, and it is compounded by the actions, databases, and permissions, especially across integrated systems, where the blind spots become massive, because it is about integrity across multiple systems. In the supply chain, it is the weak links that kill you, the small spots that are not big or sexy but are a key piece of the puzzle, like a factory halting for a missing screw. Supply chain has dealt with this, but in software we have it everywhere."**

This connects directly to the first brief: old attackers did not do the universe of what is possible, but agents can and will, which is exactly why the authorised blast radius now matters (cross-ref: the agent-authorisation-blast-radius brief, and the key-gated supply-chain thesis).

## What This Points To

Where the answer lies, briefly: **journal, do not override.**

The structural answer is the opposite of the default: log every request, keep the full history, and make it possible to selectively go back, diff, and identify the malicious changes against a strong graph of identity. That is the vault content model, which never overrides (head, commit, tree, files, with full history), combined with delta scanning, a dynamic identity graph, and blast-radius containment (cross-ref: the sgit-mapping-to-encrypted-spaces content model, the artefact-driven-security-assessments brief, the nhi-2.0 briefs, and the confidence-through-evidence thesis). The thesis here is the problem; the SGraph approach is built to be the answer.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: it is not just technology.** The project lead is explicit that this is also political, commercial, a visibility problem, and a regulatory and ecosystem problem. Mitigation: treat it as all of those, not a purely technical fix.

**Tension 2: the lock-in incentive works against the fix.** Better integrity protection reduces lock-in. Mitigation: client and regulatory demand, and the sovereignty argument, must drive it (cross-ref: the sovereignty thesis).

**Tension 3: the market of lemons hides demand.** Without disclosure, buyers cannot price the risk. Mitigation: make the authorised blast radius visible, which is the series' value proposition.

**Tension 4: integrity laws do not exist yet.** Confidentiality is regulated, integrity is not. Mitigation: this is a gap the thesis names; regulation may follow as agents force the issue.
