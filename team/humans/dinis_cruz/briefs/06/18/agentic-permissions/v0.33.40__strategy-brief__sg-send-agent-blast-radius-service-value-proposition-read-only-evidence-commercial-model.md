## What This Is

The service, value proposition, and commercial model for the agent blast-radius offering: **the plot twist is to focus on what the agent can do rather than what it did, which means phase one can work off read-only data, needing not a lot of data but just evidence that correlates the primitives of what the agent can do, adding value immediately to a set of parties already in the mix (the agentic companies, the clients and asset owners who want to know what they are signing off, the regulators, and other data-owning actors who must all agree), with the provider's own liability contained; the core is to go to the multiple parties and tell them the current attack surface and blast radius based on the facts, using LLM-built parsers to consume dashboards, reports, configs, and standards into a gigantic enterprise semantic graph; the strategy is value-proposition-first, because this visibility is the foundation other business models depend on and the prerequisite capability is itself a market; and the commercial model is value-not-rent, lots of micro-analysis plus markups on the LLM, the connection of the dots, and customisations.** It is the service deep-dive of the series (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 agent-authorisation-blast-radius brief, the v0.33.38 confidence-through-evidence thesis, the v0.33.26 selling-llm-usage brief, and the v0.32.3 nhi-2.0-semantic-knowledge-graphs brief). New contributions: **the read-only-evidence phase one, the parties-must-agree model, and the value-proposition-first commercial framing.**

## The Plot Twist

What sets this apart in a crowded space: **focus on what the agent can do, not what it did.**

The project lead: **"there is a lot of activity around agentic security, controlling what your agents can do and telling you what your agent has done. The plot twist is that we focus on what the agent can do. This has a lot of advantages and some interesting challenges."** So while others monitor and control behaviour, this service maps the authorised universe, which is the foundation the others implicitly need (cross-ref: the agent-authorisation-blast-radius brief).

## Phase One Works Off Read-Only Data

The biggest advantage, and why it ships fast: **you do not need a lot of data, just evidence.**

The project lead: **"for the first phase, it can literally work off read-only data. You do not need a lot of data, you just need a set of evidence that allows the correlation of the primitives of what you can do. From a blast-radius point of view, the agent goes from cannot do anything to can go nuclear, the worst of the worst case, and it all depends on the privileges that get mapped."** So phase one is low-friction: read-only evidence, correlated into the blast radius, with immediate value and no need for deep integration (cross-ref: the artefact-driven-security-assessments brief, which also works off read-only artefacts).

## The Parties In The Mix

Who the value goes to, and who must agree: **several parties, all of whom need to sign off.**

| Party | What They Need |
|-------|----------------|
| Agentic companies | To say what permissions they want from customers |
| Clients and asset owners | To understand the potential blast radius and what they are signing off, for risk and liability |
| Regulators | To see the attack surface and exposure |
| Other data-owning actors | When a chain (company A uses company B's agent to process a user's data) means the asset owner may not accept a risk that A and B accept |

The project lead: **"you could have a user whose data is processed by a company that uses another company, the one with the agent, and both might be happy with the risk of data destruction, but the customer who owns the asset might not be. All of them have to agree."** So the service mediates a multi-party agreement about the blast radius, which is part of its value (cross-ref: the agent-authorisation-blast-radius risk-acceptance primitive).

## The Liability Flow

A constraint on the provider: **contain your own liability.**

The project lead: **"there is a liability angle. If you are providing a service, you need to make sure you do not take on more liability than you can bear. It has to be contained. Software got away with this in the past, but it will not for much longer."** So the service must be designed so that providing visibility does not transfer unbounded liability to the provider, and the liability flow across the parties must be understood.

## The Core Flow: Tell The Parties The Blast Radius

What the service actually does: **consume the facts, build the graph, tell each party the current attack surface.**

The project lead: **"we add value by going to the multiple parties and telling them, based on the facts and information we can receive, this is the current attack surface, this is the current blast radius. With LLMs we can create parsers for anything: risk dashboards, security reports, configuration data, database configs, mappings, regulators, standards. We create a much richer correlation. It is a gigantic graph, but from an enterprise semantic-graph point of view, an ontology of ontologies and taxonomies, you can see how it all fits together."** So the mechanism is LLM-built parsers feeding a large enterprise semantic graph that correlates everything into the blast radius (cross-ref: the nhi-2.0-semantic-knowledge-graphs and confidence-through-evidence briefs, and the meaning-through-connectivity concept).

## The Value-Proposition-First Strategy

Why start here: **visibility is the foundation every other model depends on, and the prerequisite is itself a market.**

The project lead: **"there are lots of other business models and services, but they depend on having this visibility, which is what a lot of startups do not have. A company says you are going to do X, but to do X you need to do Y and Z, and if you can do Y and Z that is a market itself, so exploit that first, because it has real value and is simpler than X, which is usually a more limited market with gaps in its logic. If we cannot do this workflow effectively, we do not have a market. We start by capturing the market that exists, giving visibility, because risk owners and asset owners want to be aware of the blast radius and will pay for it."** So the discipline is to build and sell the foundational visibility first, rather than chase the harder downstream product before the prerequisite exists.

## The Commercial Model

How it charges: **value not rent, micro-analysis plus markups on buildable units.**

The project lead: **"the commercial model should be no rent extraction, all value proposition. Lots and lots of micro-analysis, then markups on specific actions and specific buildable units, namely the LLM, the connection of the dots, the customisations, and the creation of specific analyses and specific views for the customer."** So it mirrors the corpus commercialisation thesis: per-use markup on real units of value, not a flat rent (cross-ref: the selling-llm-usage and value-not-rent guidance briefs).

## What This Asks For

1. **Build the read-only-evidence workflow** (correlate the primitives into the blast radius).
2. **Serve the multiple parties** (agentic companies, clients, asset owners, regulators).
3. **Mediate the multi-party agreement** (all must sign off the blast radius).
4. **Contain the provider's liability** (understand the liability flow).
5. **Build the LLM parsers and the semantic graph** (consume everything; correlate it).
6. **Lead with visibility** (the prerequisite market, before downstream products).
7. **Charge value-not-rent** (micro-analysis plus markups on the buildable units).

Estimated effort: phase one is low-friction (read-only evidence); the work is the parsers, the semantic graph, the multi-party flow, and the commercial plumbing.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not behaviour monitoring.** It maps what can be done, not what was done.
- **Not data-heavy in phase one.** Read-only evidence is enough.
- **Not unbounded liability.** The provider's exposure is contained.
- **Not rent extraction.** Value-based micro-analysis with markups.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: contained liability.** Providing visibility could attract liability. Mitigation: design the service and contracts so liability is bounded.

**Tension 2: many parties must agree.** Coordination across companies, owners, and regulators is hard. Mitigation: the multi-party agreement is part of the value, not just overhead.

**Tension 3: read-only evidence may be incomplete.** The map is only as good as the evidence received. Mitigation: map the gaps too, and be explicit about confidence (cross-ref: the confidence-through-evidence thesis).

**Tension 4: the graph is gigantic.** Correlating everything is complex. Mitigation: the enterprise semantic-graph, ontology-of-ontologies approach is built for exactly this.
