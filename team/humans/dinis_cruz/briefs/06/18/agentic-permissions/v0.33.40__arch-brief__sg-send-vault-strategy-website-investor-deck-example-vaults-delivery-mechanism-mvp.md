## What This Is

The vault strategy, showing this is buildable on SG/Vaults today: **use vaults to manage the website and investor deck for the company built around this, with multiple vaults per section (investors, functionality, content, news, positioning) as we already do for the sgraph.ai library; make each use case, example, and mapping an individual vault, such as a vault per provider (GitHub, AWS, Salesforce, Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Claude Cowork, local Open Claw) that maps its security properties over time as an evidence base; compose them as a vault of vaults, though for the website they are just a publishing medium and no vault-to-vault is needed yet; and then let the vault become the delivery mechanism for the real solution, the risk-acceptance workflows, the evidence collection, and the customised analysis the customer runs, where the first business model is customised-analysis-as-a-service, the technology exists today for an MVP, and the timing is right.** It delivers the vault-strategy memos and reuses the website and topology work (cross-ref: the v0.33.26 vault-powered-websites brief, the v0.27.62 vault-in-vault-kernel-model brief, the v0.33.40 agent-blast-radius-service brief, and the hope-driven-development concept). New contributions: **the three vault roles, the provider evidence vaults, and the MVP-today framing.**

## The Series Framing

What this covers: **the vault strategy, and what we can do today, the fourth tracked memo.**

The project lead: **"this is the deployment of SG vaults in creating a website and investor deck, for someone setting up a company around this, and also multiple examples. There are multiple pieces of the puzzle that the vaults already solve."** So this brief shows the project can start now on the existing vault technology.

## Role One: The Website And Investor Deck

The first use, the simplest: **vaults manage the website and the investor deck.**

The project lead: **"a good use is to help manage the website about this idea. We deploy a website with all the documents and information about this commercial service, and like we use for the sgraph.ai library, we can have multiple vaults for specific parts: the investors part, the functionality, the content, the news that needs updating, the positioning."** So the website is the vault-powered-website pattern, with a vault per section (cross-ref: the vault-powered-websites brief).

## Role Two: The Example And Mapping Vaults

The second use, the evidence base: **each example and mapping is its own vault.**

The project lead: **"some of the use cases, examples, and mappings can be individual vaults, because we can have all sorts of evidence around them. Say we map a provider, GitHub, AWS, Salesforce, or a specific company, what we know about their data models, even local Open Claw or local deployments, or Claude Code, Claude Desktop, Claude Cowork. We map the security properties and say, if you use this and do that, this is what happens, and the vault maps that over time."** So each provider or scenario gets a vault that accumulates the evidence and security properties over time, which is the evidence-base capture in concrete form (cross-ref: the agent-authorisation-blast-radius and artefact-driven-security briefs).

## The Vault Of Vaults

How they compose, and what is not needed yet: **a vault of vaults, but just publishing for now.**

The project lead: **"this is where you have the vault of vaults, because each individual vault can be the one that implements the stuff. We have vaults of vaults, multiple things, even vaults that communicate with vaults, but in this case we do not need any of that. They can just use the vaults as a publishing medium."** So the full topology exists, but the website and examples need only the publishing medium; the richer composition comes later (cross-ref: the vault-in-vault-kernel-model brief).

## Role Three: The Vault As Delivery Mechanism

The third use, where it gets interesting: **the vault delivers the real solution.**

The project lead: **"the vault can also be the delivery of the rules. It gets interesting with the risk-acceptance workflows, the connection of data, and the collection of evidence. When people look at the specific use case, the vaults themselves become the delivery mechanism: that is what you deliver to the customer, what you run in their environment, and that is when you implement the solution. The vaults become a delivery mechanism when you deliver specific analysis."** So beyond publishing and evidence, the vault is the artefact you hand to the customer to run, carrying the risk-acceptance workflow, the evidence, and the analysis (cross-ref: the agent-blast-radius-service brief).

| Role | What The Vault Does |
|------|---------------------|
| Publishing medium | The website and investor deck |
| Evidence base | The per-provider, per-scenario mapping vaults |
| Delivery mechanism | The customised analysis the customer runs |

## The First Business Model

Where revenue starts: **customised-analysis-as-a-service.**

The project lead: **"the first business model is to say, give us your thing, and here is a customised version for you, a service, a particular analysis."** So the opening offer is a customised analysis delivered as a vault, which is the simplest way to turn the visibility into revenue (cross-ref: the agent-blast-radius-service commercial model).

## MVP-Ready, And The Timing

Why now, and why it is enough: **the technology exists today, and the moment is right.**

The project lead: **"all the key components we already have with the vault, and some coming soon, are more than good enough for an MVP of what this could look like. There is a lot of interesting mileage and education. Even at recent security conferences, people are saying this is too dangerous, to run the models and throw in the agents by themselves. And the older hope-driven-development writing fits nicely. There is good material for a website that implements this."** So an MVP needs no new platform work, and the market is already voicing the concern this addresses (cross-ref: the hope-driven-development concept).

## What This Asks For

1. **Build the website and deck on vaults** (a vault per section).
2. **Create per-provider evidence vaults** (GitHub, AWS, Salesforce, the Claude tools, Open Claw).
3. **Map security properties over time** (the evidence base).
4. **Use vaults as a publishing medium first** (no vault-to-vault yet).
5. **Make the vault the delivery mechanism** (the customised analysis the customer runs).
6. **Start with customised-analysis-as-a-service** (the first business model).
7. **Ship an MVP on what exists today** (plus what is coming soon).

Estimated effort: low; the vault technology, the website pattern, and the topology exist; the work is the content, the evidence vaults, and the delivery packaging.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not new platform work.** It runs on the existing vault technology.
- **Not the full topology yet.** Publishing medium first; composition later.
- **Not hosted by default.** Ideally the customer runs the delivery vault.
- **Not the final commercial model.** The first model is customised analysis.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: do not over-engineer.** The full vault-to-vault topology is not needed for the website. Mitigation: use the publishing medium first; add composition when the use case demands it.

**Tension 2: the delivery vaults need the real solution behind them.** A delivery mechanism is only as good as the analysis it carries. Mitigation: the risk-acceptance workflow and evidence collection (the service brief) are the substance.

**Tension 3: the evidence vaults need maintenance.** Security properties change over time. Mitigation: the vault maps over time; keep it current.

**Tension 4: customised analysis must scale.** Bespoke work is hard to scale. Mitigation: templatise the analysis and let the vault and LLMs do the customisation.
