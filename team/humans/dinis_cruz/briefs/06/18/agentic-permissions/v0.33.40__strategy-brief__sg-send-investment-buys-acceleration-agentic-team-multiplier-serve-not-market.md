# What Investment Buys: Acceleration, The Agentic Team, And Serving Not Marketing

**version** v0.33.40
**date** 18 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Finance, Founders, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief (in the series)

---

## What This Is

What the team needs and what investment buys: **acceleration, not the spark or profitability, because the business should be profitable fast and investment accelerates it; the agentic team is the first real multiplier of 2026, so each business function, customer service, professional services, sales, and fulfilment, gets its own agentic team with custom tools and capabilities, and these functions merge into one continuous serve-the-customer motion; the money goes to great talent, not necessarily full-time, connected to a full agentic team, to doubling down on engineering for quality while also hiring juniors with a great path, and to scaling into a multilingual and multicultural-from-day-one market deliberately, market by market; not to marketing or offices, but purely to serving customers better through features, customisations, mass customisation, mass-providing services, and quality; and investment buys time and focus.** It delivers the agentic-team memo flagged in the go-to-market brief and extends the commercial and open-source work (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 go-to-market and commercial-model briefs, the v0.33.40 open-source-strategy brief, the v0.33.26 linkedin-agentic-team brief, and the v0.33.38 guidance-gen-ai brief). New contributions: **investment-buys-acceleration, the function-level agentic teams, the merged-functions model, and the multilingual-from-day-one scaling.**

## The Series Framing

What this covers: **what team we need, and what investment buys.**

The project lead: **"this is about what team we need, what investment should go for, what the team buys. What does investment actually get."** So this is the team and investment pillar of the series.

## Investment Buys Acceleration, Not The Spark

The key reframing: **we do not need investment for the spark or to be profitable.**

The project lead: **"we do not need investment for the spark, and we do not need it to be profitable. The business should be profitable very fast, apart from the founders' time, because you can have workflows generating revenue quickly. What you need investment for is to accelerate: better customer service, developers, handovers, a whole set of talent. And you can get great talent, not even full-time, and give them a full team to connect to."** So unlike the usual raise-to-reach-profitability logic, here the business is profitable fast and investment buys speed, talent, and reach (cross-ref: the commercial-model brief, on fast profitability).

## The Agentic Team Is The First Multiplier

Why this is new: **the agentic team is the first multiplier of 2026 we did not have before.**

The project lead: **"the agentic team is literally the first multiplier that exists in 2026 that we did not have before."** So the leverage is new: a small number of people, multiplied by agentic teams, can do what large teams used to (cross-ref: the linkedin-agentic-team brief).

## Each Function Gets Its Own Agentic Team

How the multiplier lands: **every business function gets capabilities it never had.**

The project lead: **"the agentic workflows allow each function of the business to have technology, capabilities, and resources that never existed before. The customer service team now has a whole agentic team working for them: custom websites, custom technology, custom tools, custom interactions. They can maintain and build things."** So each function gains its own agentic team and custom tooling, lifting what a single human in that function can do (cross-ref: the tools-in-vault brief).

## The Functions Merge

A structural consequence: **customer service, professional services, sales, and fulfilment merge.**

The project lead: **"in a weird way, customer service, professional services, service delivery, fulfilment, and sales almost merge into one, because they are doing that all the time."** So the agentic teams blur the old functional boundaries into one continuous motion of serving the customer.

## Double Down On Engineering

Where to invest in people: **excellent senior engineers, plus juniors with a great path.**

The project lead: **"engineering is where you double down: amazing developers who read the code and make sure everything has a high degree of quality, control the technical debt and the non-functional requirements, go to the next level. But you can also hire more junior people, give them a great path, a set of tools, and interesting things to do."** So engineering investment goes both ways: senior depth for quality and control, and juniors given tools and a path (cross-ref: the open-source-strategy brief, on engineering excellence).

## Scale: Multilingual And Multicultural From Day One

A big, under-discussed element: **this market is multilingual and multicultural from the start.**

The project lead: **"this is how you scale, and a big element we have not talked about: this market is multilingual and multicultural from day one. But each market needs to be taken and focused, you cannot go to other markets by accident. You hire people in multiple places and let them scale there. That is what you want the money for: to bring the resources, the talent, the people."** So scaling is geographic and linguistic, done deliberately market by market with local people, which is what the investment funds (cross-ref: the guidance-gen-ai brief, on multilingual and local skills).

## Do Not Spend On Marketing Or Offices

Where the money does not go: **not marketing, not offices, but serving customers.**

The project lead: **"I do not think we should spend money on marketing, or on offices and all the stuff companies waste money on. You spend money to fulfil customers better: the features the customer wants, the customisations, the mass customisation, finding the gaps. It is all about quality, building a hell of a product, accelerating and not losing speed as you add features and capabilities, especially the mass customisation and mass-providing services to companies."** So spend follows serving and quality, not the usual cost centres, consistent with the product-led go-to-market (cross-ref: the go-to-market brief).

## The Highly-Effective Professional Services Team

What it produces: **a team that serves more and more customers at once.**

The project lead: **"this is where you have a highly effective professional services team that can support more and more customers at the same time, because they have custom tools we build for them. That is where the money goes. Investment buys time and focus."** So the agentic multiplier turns professional services from a linear cost into a scalable team, and investment buys the time and focus to build it.

## What This Asks For

1. **Be profitable fast** (investment accelerates, it is not the spark).
2. **Build function-level agentic teams** (custom tools and capabilities per function).
3. **Let the functions merge** (one continuous serve-the-customer motion).
4. **Double down on engineering** (senior quality plus juniors with a path).
5. **Scale deliberately and multilingually** (market by market, with local people).
6. **Spend on serving, not marketing or offices** (features, customisation, quality).
7. **Build the scalable professional services team** (custom tools; many customers at once).

Estimated effort: this is the investment thesis and team design; the spend is talent, agentic teams, and serving capacity.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not raise-to-profitability.** Profitable fast; raise to accelerate.
- **Not siloed functions.** They merge around serving the customer.
- **Not marketing or office spend.** Spend on serving and quality.
- **Not accidental expansion.** Each market is taken deliberately.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: profitable-fast is ambitious.** It rests on the founders' time and quick workflows. Mitigation: the usage and customised-analysis models generate revenue early (cross-ref: the commercial-model and vault-strategy briefs).

**Tension 2: merged functions are unconventional.** Blurring service, sales, and delivery is unusual. Mitigation: the agentic teams make it natural; serving is one motion.

**Tension 3: multilingual-from-day-one needs focus.** You cannot enter markets by accident. Mitigation: take each market deliberately with local people.

**Tension 4: juniors plus quality needs strong seniors.** A junior-heavy team risks quality. Mitigation: senior engineers read the code and control debt and non-functional requirements.
