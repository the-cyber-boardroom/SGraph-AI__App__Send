# Naming The Agent Authorisation Bundle: Beyond "Passport"

**version** v0.33.40
**date** 18 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Product, Publication, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief (naming, in the series)

---

## What This Is

The naming question for the concept from the previous brief: **the current candidate is agent passport, but passport falls short because a passport is singular, static, limited, and about authentication rather than authorisation, whereas what we have is the bundle, the union of everything an agent can do, its identities and credentials and its capabilities, where the capabilities are business capabilities and not just technical ones (can it leak data, corrupt data, impact revenue, break the business), so the name has to capture the union of authentication and authorisation plus business impact, and this brief explores candidate names and analogies from security, technology, the real world, and history, recommends a few, and flags the next topic, the toxicity and detectability of access.** It names the concept from the authorisation-blast-radius brief (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 agent-authorisation-blast-radius brief, the v0.32.3 nhi-2.0 briefs, and the v0.33.38 confidence-through-evidence thesis). New contributions: **the passport critique, the union-of-everything definition, and the candidate-name set.**

## The Current Name And Its Limits

The starting point, and why it does not fit: **agent passport is the current name, but a passport is the wrong shape.**

The project lead: **"the current name we are thinking is agent passport, but the challenge with passport is that in the real world you kind of have one passport, the permissions do not change, and they tend to be limited. And passports tend to be about authentication, not necessarily authorisation."**

| Passport Property | Why It Misfits |
|-------------------|----------------|
| Singular | An agent's authority is a bundle of many things, not one document |
| Static | A passport's permissions do not change; an agent's authority is dynamic and composed |
| Limited | A passport grants little; an agent's authority can be vast |
| Authentication | A passport proves identity; what we have is mostly authorisation |

## What The Thing Actually Is

The concept to name: **the union of everything an agent can do, identities and credentials and capabilities together.**

The project lead: **"it is more than just identity. It is this combination of the universe of what the agent can do, a bundle, the union of everything you can do. A better analogy is the security cards that give access to different areas, the lanyards that give backstage access, or the two-factor tokens that give you access to something else."** So the thing is a bundle: the identities and credentials the agent holds, plus the capabilities those unlock, taken as a union (cross-ref: the agent-authorisation-blast-radius brief).

## The Key Reframe: Business Capabilities, Not Just Technical

The point that the name must carry: **the capabilities are business capabilities, measured by the worst that can happen.**

The project lead: **"the way we map it, the capabilities are not just technical, they are business capabilities. It is not just, I have access to this system or that data, but what does that mean. Does it mean you can leak data, corrupt data, gain competitive advantage, impact customers, impact revenue, impact the business strategy, break the business. It is almost, what is the worst that can happen, can this actually destroy your business."** So the unit is not access to a system but the business consequence of that access, up to and including destroying the business. The name should evoke consequence, not just keys.

## The Naming Criteria

What a good name must capture:

| Criterion | Why |
|-----------|-----|
| The union of everything | It is a bundle, not a single credential |
| Authorisation, not just authentication | It is about what can be done, not just who you are |
| Business impact | The capabilities are measured in business consequence |
| Scope that can be broad and dangerous | The worst that can happen must be implied |

## Candidate Names And Analogies

Exploring widely, as the project lead asked, including outside security and into the real world and history:

| Candidate | Flavour | Why It Fits |
|-----------|---------|-------------|
| Agent Mandate | Real-world, legal | A mandate is delegated authority with a defined scope; agents act on your behalf, and a broad mandate is dangerous |
| Power Of Attorney | Real-world, legal | The closest legal instrument: authority to act on another's behalf, broad or narrow, with real potential for harm |
| Warrant, or Letters Of Marque | Historical, legal | A written authority to act; letters of marque historically authorised agents to act with force, a striking analogy for delegated, dangerous authority |
| Authority Envelope, or Capability Envelope | Technical | An envelope bounds the universe of what is possible, like a flight envelope; exceed it and you crash |
| Authority Footprint, or Access Footprint | Technical | The full footprint of what the agent can reach and do |
| Authority Surface | Security | The authorised surface, echoing attack surface |
| Blast Radius | Security (the underlying concept) | The effect of exercising the authority; the consequence, already the project lead's term |
| Keys To The Kingdom | Evocative | Colloquial for total access; captures the union and the danger |

## A Recommendation

A recommendation, with the choice left to the project lead: **Agent Mandate as the primary, with Authority Envelope and Authority Footprint as alternatives, and Blast Radius retained as the underlying concept.**

Agent Mandate fits best because it is a real-world instrument of delegated authority with a defined scope, which is exactly what an agent's authorisation bundle is: agents act on your behalf, a mandate can be broad or narrow, and a broad mandate naturally raises the worst-that-can-happen question. Power Of Attorney is the closest legal analogy and worth using in explanation. Authority Envelope is the strongest technical name, capturing the bounded universe. And Blast Radius remains the right term for the consequence the mandate makes possible. The final choice is the project lead's.

## The Next Topic: Toxicity And Detectability

Flagged for the next analysis: **some access is loud and some is quiet.**

The project lead: **"once we map this, there is also the toxicity of some of those elements, because you can have huge access to something that will cause a lot of alarms to go off, so it is easy to detect abuse, versus systems where you cannot detect abuse."** So beyond the size of the authority, there is its detectability: a powerful but loud capability (abuse triggers alarms) is less dangerous than a quieter one (abuse goes unnoticed). This is a separate analysis to come.

## What This Asks For

1. **Choose a name** (Agent Mandate recommended).
2. **Ensure it meets the criteria** (union, authorisation, business impact, dangerous scope).
3. **Use Power Of Attorney as the explanatory analogy** (acting on your behalf).
4. **Keep Blast Radius for the consequence** (the effect the mandate enables).
5. **Carry forward the toxicity-and-detectability topic** (the next analysis).

Estimated effort: this is a naming and framing brief; the action is a decision.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not just identity.** It is the union of identity, credentials, and capabilities.
- **Not just authentication.** It centres on authorisation.
- **Not just technical.** The capabilities are business capabilities.
- **Not the final word.** The name is the project lead's to choose.
