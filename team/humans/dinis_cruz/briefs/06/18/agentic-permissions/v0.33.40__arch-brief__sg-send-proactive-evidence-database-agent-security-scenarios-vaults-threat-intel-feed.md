# The Proactive Evidence Database: Agent-Security Scenarios As Vaults, And A Threat-Intel Feed

**version** v0.33.40
**date** 18 June 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Architect, Security, Strategy, Product, @Dev
**type** Arch brief (in the series)

---

## What This Is

A proactive, fact-driven evidence database of agent-security scenarios, mapped company by company and use case by use case, built from public data and our own experience without needing the providers to cooperate: **it maps the confidentiality, integrity, and availability properties of a setting plus deeper primitives (request and automation speed, containment speed, abuse-detection speed, backup, journaling, restore, integrity detection, and rogue-agent detection), as specific, unambiguous, often binary scenarios, each stored as a git-compatible vault that holds the evidence over time, the claims, the requests, and the official responses; from a business angle it focuses on the use-case consequences, with integrity as the sweet spot, mapped to MITRE ATT&CK attack trees and segmented by attacker and agent type; it links to existing agentic-incident databases and the OWASP Top 10, is open and community-driven and eventually self-maintained, handled sensitively, and becomes a website and a paid threat-intelligence and agent-intelligence feed where version and tempo matter, forming a gigantic graph linking products, versions, agents, and skills.** It delivers the proactive and evidence-base memos and composes the vault and integrity work (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 vault-strategy, infrastructure-not-built-for-agents, second-third-order-side-effects, and commercial-model briefs). New contributions: **the proactive public-data database, the deeper primitives, the scenario-as-vault model, and the agent-intel feed.**

## The Series Framing

What this is for: **a fact-driven set of examples that show reality and the gaps.**

The project lead: **"it is worth creating one just for this. When you talk about permissions and the union of everything possible, it is important to have a fact-driven, evidence-driven set of examples that show what reality is, show where the gaps are, show the value we provide, and show how most people underestimate the reality and how dangerously many are flying, all backed by evidence."** So this is the evidence engine for the whole series: concrete, evidenced examples of what agents can do.

## We Do Not Need Them To Dance

The proactive angle, and the key freedom: **build it from public data, without the providers' cooperation.**

The project lead: **"the good thing is we do not need them to dance. We do not need others to play and allow our mapping, because we can use public data, information that already exists, and our own experience. But it needs to be open, so companies that initially do not want to be involved can eventually contribute."** So the database is built proactively from what is already public, and being open lets reluctant providers join later rather than blocking the start (cross-ref: the open-source-strategy brief).

## What We Map: CIA Plus Deeper Primitives

What each scenario records: **the security properties, and deeper operational primitives.**

The project lead: **"we map the properties from a security point of view, from CIA, of a particular setting, but we need to go deeper, with primitives like: how fast can you make requests, how fast can you contain them, how fast can you stop and detect abuse, what are the backup situations, can you restore, do you have journaling, how can you recover. Even simple examples: if I create five entries, can I delete those specific five, can I go back in time and delete five corrupted entries, what is the integrity detection, what is the system's ability to detect rogue agents."**

| Primitive | The Question |
|-----------|--------------|
| Request and automation speed | How fast can requests be made |
| Containment speed | How fast can requests be contained |
| Abuse detection | How fast can abuse be detected and stopped |
| Backup and restore | Can you restore, and to what point |
| Journaling | Is the full request journaled |
| Selective recovery | Can you delete exactly the five corrupted entries |
| Integrity detection | Can corruption be detected |
| Rogue-agent detection | Can the system spot a rogue agent |

(Cross-ref: the infrastructure-not-built-for-agents brief, on journaling and integrity.)

## Specific, Unambiguous Scenarios As Vaults

The form each takes: **specific, binary, evidence-bearing scenarios, each a git-compatible vault.**

The project lead: **"we come up with very specific, focused scenarios that are unambiguous, so we can have a straight, effective, evidence-driven conversation, because a lot of these are either you have it or you do not. Each becomes a vault, and the vault contains the evidence, even evidence over time, so as things change, our hypothesis, our claim, the requests, the official responses, all go there. And vaults are git-compatible, so we can run them on git and get contributions."** So each scenario is an auditable vault holding its claims and evidence over time, and git-compatibility opens it to contribution (cross-ref: the sgit-mapping and vault-strategy briefs).

## The Business Use-Case Lens

How to frame it for business: **follow the use-case consequences.**

The project lead: **"from a business point of view we focus on the use case. You can access the email, so you can recover passwords, impersonate users, destroy calendars, spam others, access the database, modify data, corrupt data. What happens if the data is corrupted, what happens if it is deleted."** So a scenario is not a permission in the abstract; it is the chain of business consequences that permission unlocks, which is what makes it land (cross-ref: the second-third-order-side-effects brief).

## Integrity Is The Sweet Spot

Where to focus: **integrity, and the guardrails around it.**

The project lead: **"a lot of the focus should be on integrity, because that is the sweet spot, what a lot of people do not understand: the integrity of data, the mass creation and mass editing of data at specific speed, and the ability to detect, block, recover, and prevent, all the guardrails, when an agent can access in particular ways."** So the database foregrounds integrity: how fast data can be mass-altered, and whether the system can detect, block, and recover (cross-ref: the infrastructure-not-built-for-agents brief).

## MITRE ATT&CK And Agent Types

How to structure the analysis: **map to MITRE ATT&CK, and segment by agent type.**

The project lead: **"we should map to MITRE ATT&CK, because there are great attack trees on it, and we can map by the type of agent: the over-enthusiastic agent, the agent aware something is going on, the agent that keeps trying things and will not stop, the paperclip example, and the malicious agents that digest something and become malicious. Because of prompt injection, almost every time an agent reads something there is a possibility it goes rogue, so the question is how many platforms can contain that."**

| Agent Type | Behaviour |
|------------|-----------|
| Over-enthusiastic | Well-intended, exercises over-privilege |
| Aware | Takes a few deliberate actions |
| Relentless | Keeps trying; the paperclip example |
| Malicious | Acts with intent |
| Turned | Becomes malicious from what it reads (prompt injection) |

(Cross-ref: the why-now-inefficiency-of-exploitation and agentic-compromise briefs.)

## Open, Community-Driven, And Sensitive

How it is run: **open and community-maintained, but handled with care.**

The project lead: **"we link to existing agentic-incident databases and the OWASP Top 10. It should be open and community-driven, eventually self-maintained, the more communities maintain it the better. But some of this might be controversial, some people might not be fully aware, the other side might not like it, so we are sensitive: some things we do not publish, there is no need to fight people."** So the database is community-driven for coverage and openness, but curated sensitively, with the option not to publish where it would simply provoke (cross-ref: the open-source-strategy brief).

## The Website And The Agent-Intel Feed

What it becomes: **a website, and a paid threat-intelligence and agent-intelligence feed.**

The project lead: **"this becomes a website in itself, the vaults support it, each becomes a site, and some use cases are a vault maintained, versioned, shared, and collaborated. This can be one of the most valuable assets the company has. It becomes a threat-intelligence feed, or an agent-intelligence feed, and version and tempo matter, because a deployed version of a product has specific properties, agents have versions, you map to skills, and the whole universe becomes a gigantic graph of relationships. Companies need these feeds and pay for them, and eventually submit or point to where they publish their information."** So the database is also a commercial asset: a versioned, tempo-sensitive agent-intel feed, forming a graph of products, versions, agents, and skills, sold as a service (cross-ref: the commercial-model and graphs-of-graphs briefs).

## What This Asks For

1. **Build the database from public data** (no provider cooperation needed).
2. **Map CIA plus the deeper primitives** (speed, containment, detection, journaling, recovery).
3. **Make each scenario a git-compatible vault** (evidence over time).
4. **Frame by business use-case consequences** (the chains of impact).
5. **Foreground integrity** (mass edit at speed, and the guardrails).
6. **Map to MITRE ATT&CK and agent types** (attack trees; the agent taxonomy).
7. **Run it open, community-driven, and sensitively** (curate; do not provoke).
8. **Ship it as a website and a paid agent-intel feed** (versioned, tempo-aware).

Estimated effort: moderate and incremental; start with a few scenarios as vaults; grow company by company, use case by use case, with community contributions.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not attack instructions.** It maps properties and guardrails for defensive awareness.
- **Not dependent on providers.** Built from public data and experience.
- **Not closed.** Open and community-driven, for coverage.
- **Not reckless.** Sensitive curation; some things are not published.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: sensitivity and controversy.** Mapping providers may upset them. Mitigation: curate sensitively; do not publish where it only provokes; let them contribute when ready.

**Tension 2: accuracy of public-data claims.** Public data can be wrong or stale. Mitigation: evidence over time in each vault, including official responses; label hypotheses and certainty (cross-ref: the side-effects and confidence-through-evidence briefs).

**Tension 3: community moderation and quality.** Open contribution risks noise. Mitigation: quality control, as with the curated feeds (cross-ref: the commercial-model brief).

**Tension 4: keeping it current.** Versions and tempo move fast. Mitigation: version and tempo are first-class; the feed is maintained, and providers point to their own published information.
