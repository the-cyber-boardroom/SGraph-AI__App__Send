# Skills Are Code: Permission Granularity, And Why OAuth Is Not Enough

## What This Is

The skills thesis, which doubles as the long-promised critique of OAuth: **skills are becoming crazy powerful because they are code, English is code now, so they must come with a definition of the permissions they need or they run riot, and the same applies to every unit of execution, the package, the code, the skill, the agent, and the tool, each of which should declare or have dynamically calculated the permissions it needs; OAuth, though a major victory, is not granular enough, because it grants in absolutes, the union of all privileges, all or nothing, is binary at the moment of consent, and forces you to grant a hundred percent when you need ten percent, with no way to scope to just the ten percent in the exact moment it is needed, which is where the graph-based, moment-of-authorisation model comes in.** It delivers the skills memo flagged in the previous brief and addresses the long-parked OAuth critique (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 agent-authorisation-blast-radius brief, the v0.33.38 tools-execution-control-flow brief, and the v0.32.3 nhi-2.0 briefs). New contributions: **skills-as-code-need-permissions, the units-of-execution model, and the OAuth-granularity critique.**

## The Series Framing

What this covers: **skills, and by extension dependencies, the first of the tracked future memos.**

The project lead: **"one we want to cover is this concept of skills, and even of dependencies, but let us talk about skills, because skills are a good example of something becoming crazy powerful."** So this delivers the skills memo, and touches dependencies as another kind of unit (cross-ref: the key-gated supply-chain thesis).

## Skills Are Code

Why skills are powerful and dangerous: **English is code now, and even a simple skill does a lot.**

The project lead: **"skills are code. English is code now, and even a simple skill can now do tons of stuff."** So a skill is not a passive document; it is executable capability, and a small amount of natural-language skill can drive a large amount of action.

## Skills Must Declare Their Permissions

The core requirement: **a skill must come with the definition of the permissions it needs.**

The project lead: **"skills need to come with the definition of the permissions they need, because without a good definition of permissions, skills can just run riot."** So a skill is incomplete without its permission definition; the permissions are part of the skill, not an afterthought.

## Units Of Execution

The broader model: **package, code, skill, agent, and tool are all units of execution, and each declares its permissions.**

The project lead: **"the package, the code, and the skills are units of execution, and the agent is one of them, but the agent also does stuff on top of skills and activities, or hands over to an execution engine like the tool. They will determine what permissions they need, so we apply the same thing to them, because that is how we understand what needs to be done."** So permissions are a property of every unit of execution, dynamically calculable, and they compose: an agent built on skills built on packages inherits and combines their permission requirements (cross-ref: the tools-execution-control-flow brief, where only the required tools are exposed per call).

## Why OAuth Is Not Granular Enough

The critique, long owed: **OAuth is a major victory, but it grants in absolutes.**

The project lead: **"OAuth is good, I am not saying it is bad, just that it is not good enough. It does not have enough granularity on the workflows it allows. OAuth is the provider saying I need these privileges to do this, and the grant tends to be the combination of all the privileges required. So the grant is in absolutes: you can access my email, my calendar, this GitHub repo. And when the user gives the permission, it is binary, access or not. So if I use a tool and only need ten percent of its capabilities, I end up giving it a hundred percent, when I should be giving just the ten percent I want, and in the exact moment it is needed. OAuth was a major victory, better than giving access to the whole account, but we need a lot more granularity."**

| OAuth Limitation | Consequence |
|------------------|-------------|
| Provider-defined privilege bundle | The grant is the union of everything the tool might need |
| Granted in absolutes | Access my email, not access this one thread |
| Binary at consent | You can only say yes or no |
| No scoping to need | Grant a hundred percent to use ten |
| No just-in-time | Granted ahead of time, not in the exact moment |

## The Answer: The Graph World

Where the granularity comes from: **the graph-based, per-unit, moment-of-authorisation model.**

The project lead: **"that is where our graph world, and the things we talk about, come into play."** So the answer is the series' approach: permissions declared per unit of execution, composed across units, scoped to exactly what is needed, and granted at the moment of authorisation rather than in a coarse bundle ahead of time (cross-ref: the nhi-2.0 access-not-security and moment-of-authorisation, and the agent-authorisation-blast-radius brief).

## What This Points To

1. **Make permissions part of every skill** (the definition travels with the skill).
2. **Treat every unit of execution the same** (package, code, skill, agent, tool).
3. **Calculate permissions dynamically and compositionally** (units inherit and combine).
4. **Provide granularity beyond OAuth** (scope to need, not to the bundle).
5. **Grant just-in-time** (the exact moment, the exact scope).
6. **Use the graph world** (per-unit, moment-of-authorisation).
7. **Extend to dependencies** (the supply chain of units).

Estimated effort: this is a model and a critique; the build is the per-unit permission declaration, the dynamic calculation, and the just-in-time granular grant, on the graph approach.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not anti-OAuth.** OAuth was a major victory; it is just not granular enough.
- **Not coarse.** The point is fine-grained, scoped permissions.
- **Not ahead-of-time.** Grants happen in the exact moment needed.
- **Not skill-only.** Every unit of execution is in scope.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: dynamic per-unit calculation is complex.** Computing and composing permissions across units is hard. Mitigation: the graph model is built for composition; calculate from the declared definitions.

**Tension 2: just-in-time granularity needs provider support.** Providers grant coarsely today via OAuth. Mitigation: scope what can be scoped now; push for finer-grained provider grants over time.

**Tension 3: skills as code are a supply chain.** A skill pulls in dependencies, each with permissions. Mitigation: treat dependencies as units of execution too (cross-ref: the key-gated supply-chain thesis).

**Tension 4: over-granularity could be unusable.** Too many tiny grants overwhelm the user. Mitigation: calculate and compose for the user, and grant at the moment in context.
