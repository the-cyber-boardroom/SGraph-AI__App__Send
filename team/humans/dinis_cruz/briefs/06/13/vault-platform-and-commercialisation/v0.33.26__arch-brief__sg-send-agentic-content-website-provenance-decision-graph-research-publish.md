## What This Is

A synthesis of many threads into one platform: an agentic content-creation-and-publishing system, built on the vault-powered-websites pattern, that produces a provenance-rich content website (first case: a provider's guidance site on a specific clinical topic), where **every article carries its source documents, analysis, transformations, and human and agent verifications as a semantic knowledge graph, the review process is modelled as a decision graph rather than a yes-or-no with explicit ownership at each step, facts, hypotheses, and evidence are the unit that gets referenced and checked, billing and observability are built in from the start, the content is multi-language, and the whole thing is delivered as a series of research-to-create-to-publish MVPs, with about 90 percent of the stack already built.** It ties together the freshly-named vault-powered-websites pattern, the agentic team, and the graphs-of-graphs and provenance work (cross-ref: the v0.33.26 vault-powered-websites and linkedin-agentic-team briefs, the v0.32.3 nhi-2.0-semantic-knowledge-graphs brief, the v0.33.16 artefact-driven-security-assessments and semantic-graph-visualisation briefs). New contributions: **the provenance-per-article model, the decision-graph-with-ownership, and the research-to-publish MVP path.**

## The Outcome: A Provenance-Rich Content Website

Starting from the end: **a useful content website on a specific clinical topic, with guidance, links, research, and the latest information, that also generalises to the project lead's own content.**

The project lead: **"the end is a website with content about a specific clinical topic, that has guidance, links, and information for people who need that specific guidance, and that could also have research, the latest documents, and the latest information. This is the kind of stuff I need to create for some of my own work too, like the encryption topics, so there is a lot of correlation."** So the first case is a provider's guidance site, and the same machine produces the project lead's own content (for example on encryption), which is the point of generalising it.

## Provenance As The Core

The central idea, and the differentiator: **every article shows where it came from and who stands behind it.**

The project lead: **"we need very strong provenance on ownership and creation. If you see an article about X, you should be able to see the source document, the source information, the analysis made, the transformations made, the human validations, checks, and ownerships. You can have a spectrum from a hundred percent one LLM, to multiple LLMs mixed, to a hundred percent human-created, to human-created and LLM-verified. What is interesting is to move less about the role of the human and more about the source of truth, the source of evidence, who verified it, and who confirmed it."**

The status quo is the foil: **"most articles do not provide evidence, they provide a link, the link is never followed, and it could go to a site that no longer exists. We do not get a reference of what references what."** The fix is the graphs-of-graphs approach: a semantic knowledge graph for each article, a graph of graphs, even a per-paragraph graph that connects to the evidence, returning to the facts, hypotheses, and analysis idea, even a judge that flags when a stated fact is actually an opinion (cross-ref: the semantic-knowledge-graphs and artefact-driven briefs). The unit of trust is the evidence, not the byline.

## The Architecture

The hosting is the vault-powered-websites pattern: **a static site under a CI pipeline, published to S3, fronted by CloudFront, with content served from a vault.**

The project lead: **"a static website controlled by a CI pipeline, which gives us dev, main, and prod, published to an S3 bucket fronted by a CloudFront distribution. The content is hosted and served from a vault. The CloudFront origin means immutable files are served directly from S3 and cached, so we get that acceleration. We can have multiple vaults for staging, and an agent inside the vault does the research and the creation."**

```
  CI pipeline (dev / main / prod)
        |  publishes
        v
  S3 bucket  ->  CloudFront distribution  ->  visitors
        ^                                        |  static shell + JS loader
        |  immutable files cached                v
  Content vault(s)  ->  head -> commit -> tree -> files  (provenance graphs attached)
        ^
        |  agent inside the vault: research + creation
```

(Cross-ref: the vault-powered-websites brief for the loader and the head-commit-tree-files resolution.)

## The Automated Agent Workflows

The new build, and the moment for it: **airtight, well-defined automated agent workflows for research, curation, classification, the knowledge graph, release, and approvals.**

The project lead: **"this is the moment to get into automated agent workflows. Let us leverage what exists, because AWS Bedrock and others already support these flows well. We need to create a tight harness and well-defined workflows for the research element, the curation element, the classification element, the semantic-knowledge-graph element, and then the release and all the approvals."**

| Workflow Stage | What It Does |
|----------------|--------------|
| Research | Gather sources and evidence |
| Curation | Select and organise |
| Classification | Tag and structure |
| Semantic knowledge graph | Build the provenance and evidence graph |
| Release | Publish through the pipeline |
| Approvals | The decision graph (below) |

## The Decision Graph: Not A Yes Or No

The key conceptual contribution, and one the project lead says has not been captured before: **a decision is never a yes or no, it is a graph that collects evidence, with explicit ownership at each step.**

The project lead: **"a decision should never be a yes or no. A decision should always be a graph that collects a bunch of evidence, and at the top of the pyramid you have yes or no, but throughout the graph you can have all sorts of yes, no, ship, not ship. The question becomes who takes ownership. Whoever creates an article is saying ship this; an agent reviews it and makes a call; a human reviews it and makes a call; another pass; and someone at the end makes the final call. This is where we map how automated the final decision can be."**

| Step | Who | Call |
|------|-----|------|
| Creation | The author (agent or human) | Implicitly, ship this |
| Agent review | A reviewing agent | A call, with evidence |
| Human review | A human | A call, with context |
| Further passes | Agents or humans | More calls |
| Final | A designated owner | The final call |

Two principles attach. First, bring data science to it: **"there is no point having humans in the loop who just approve without context, that is not human in the loop. We need materials that can be processed so you can make accurate decisions, which is why facts, hypotheses, observations, and evidence matter, that is what you reference and check."** Second, present subject-matter experts the information in a form they can process quickly, since they hold the vision for the site and their judgement is only as good as what they are shown.

## The MVPs

The delivery shape: **a series of MVPs from research to document creation to publishing, multi-language, experimenting with the best way to release.**

The project lead: **"a series of MVPs that go from research, to creation of a document, to publishing, and we can now do this in multiple languages, cultures, and angles, experimenting with the best way to release. The first version may have no quality control, pure AI, which is a variation of the thing I did not want to do with news, so let us start there and map it out."** So the path is incremental, and the first MVP may be pure-AI with minimal control, with quality control and the provenance and decision-graph machinery layered in quickly after (see the honest risk on health content below).

## What This Asks For

1. **Build the agentic content workflows** (research, curation, classification, knowledge graph, release, approvals; leverage Bedrock).
2. **Attach provenance to every article** (source, analysis, transformations, verifications, as a graph).
3. **Model review as a decision graph with ownership** (not yes or no; map how automated the final call can be).
4. **Use facts, hypotheses, and evidence as the unit** (referenced and checked; presented for fast human processing).
5. **Host on the vault-powered-websites pattern** (static, CI, S3, CloudFront, vault content).
6. **Build billing and observability in from the start** (cost per article and release).
7. **Deliver as research-to-publish MVPs, multi-language** (with quality control layered in quickly).

Estimated effort: moderate, since about 90 percent of the stack exists; the work is the visualisation, the workflow wiring, the decision-graph model, and the cost and billing model.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a byline-trust site.** The unit of trust is the evidence graph, not the author.
- **Not a permanent pure-AI-no-control site.** That is only the first MVP; control is layered in.
- **Not a context-free human-in-the-loop.** Reviewers get processable materials.
- **Not server-rendered.** Static plus the vault loader.

## Honest Risks

**Risk 1 (most important, given the domain): a pure-AI-no-quality-control MVP is riskier for health content.** Inaccurate or unsafe guidance can harm people seeking it, and the project lead already flags wariness (the news analogy). Mitigation: the provenance and decision-graph machinery is precisely the control; for health content, layer quality control in quickly rather than lingering on pure AI, keep clear not-medical-advice and beta disclaimers, and require human ownership on the final call (cross-ref: the recipient-vault-experience-improvements disclaimer and the open-source-success-paradox AI-slop nuance).

**Risk 2: provenance is hard to build well.** Per-article and per-paragraph evidence graphs are substantial. Mitigation: start simple; build up from a basic structure.

**Risk 3: the decision-graph-with-ownership model is novel.** It needs design. Mitigation: start with the simple chain (creator, agent, human, final owner) and add data science.

**Risk 4: cost control.** Research and generation consume tokens. Mitigation: billing and observability from day one; cost per article and release; prompt optimisation.

**Risk 5: automation versus accountability.** Mapping how automated the final call can be must not remove accountability. Mitigation: explicit ownership at each step.
