## What This Is

Research on REDCap (projectredcap.org) and a comparison with the SG/Vault provider-and-recipient vaults: **REDCap is much closer to SGraph than a commercial SaaS, it is non-profit, self-hosted, institution-controlled, and does not monetise data, so the contrast is precise rather than oppositional: REDCap is source-available but restricted (non-commercial, no redistribution, Vanderbilt owns all rights), it is institution-holds-plaintext rather than zero-knowledge, it is siloed per institution rather than cross-party, and it is a self-hosted server stack rather than a lightweight portable vault, while SG/Vault is genuinely open source, zero-knowledge, distributed, and portable. REDCap leads decisively on maturity, adoption, and depth of research tooling; SG/Vault leads on open source, zero-knowledge, cross-party distribution, and recipient-held data.** It follows the doctor-and-patient and PatientIQ work (cross-ref: the v0.33.21 patientiq-comparison, recipient-created-disposable-vault, and sovereign-data-aggregation-platform briefs). REDCap facts are paraphrased from the sources at the end. New contributions: **the REDCap research, the licensing-reality clarification, and the precise (not oppositional) contrast.**

## What REDCap Is

The research, in brief: REDCap (Research Electronic Data Capture) is a secure web application from Vanderbilt University for building and managing online surveys and databases, built for research data capture and reliable since 2004. It is very large and mature, with more than eight thousand institutional partners across one hundred and sixty-six countries, over 2.7 million projects, more than 4.3 million users, and tens of thousands of citations. Each institution runs its own REDCap system, it is free to non-profit organisations that join the REDCap Consortium, and it supports surveys, multi-site and longitudinal studies, offline and mobile capture, multi-language management, and export to common statistics packages, with compliance options for 21 CFR Part 11, FISMA, HIPAA, and GDPR.

## The Licensing Reality

The most important clarification, because it is widely misunderstood: **REDCap is free to non-profits and source-available to licensees, but it is not open source.** Its non-profit end-user licence agreement makes Vanderbilt the owner of all rights and the copyright, licenses the software for non-commercial research purposes only, and forbids distributing or sharing the software or any derivative works with any third party for any purpose. Eligibility is limited to not-for-profit, governmental, and other non-commercial entities, only Vanderbilt holds commercialisation rights, and derivative works that licensees create are licensed exclusively back to Vanderbilt. Open source, in the accepted sense, means freedom to use, study, modify, and redistribute for any purpose including commercial use; REDCap's licence allows none of redistribution, commercial use, or use by commercial entities, so it does not meet that definition. This is the sharpest and most precise point of difference.

## The Data Model

How REDCap handles data: **each institution self-hosts REDCap and holds the data, including protected health information, on its own servers, secured server-side.** The licence permits installation on servers the institution owns, or on cloud servers where the hosting company has no access to the software or the health information, and makes the institution responsible for maintaining confidentiality and compliance. The institution's REDCap system stores the data in its own database, where REDCap administrators and the server can read it. This is a server-side security model, not zero-knowledge: the institution is trusted with the plaintext.

| Source model | Source-available to licensees, restricted | Open source |

| Research tooling | Deep and purpose-built | Not a focus today |
| Compliance | 21 CFR Part 11, FISMA, HIPAA, GDPR capable | Smaller PHI surface by design; not certified |
| Cost | Free to non-profits in the consortium | Open source |

## Where REDCap Is Ahead

Honesty matters, and REDCap is genuinely excellent on several dimensions: **it is mature, enormously adopted, deeply tooled for research, compliance-capable, and free to non-profits.**

| REDCap Strength | What It Means |
|-----------------|---------------|
| Maturity | Reliable since 2004, two decades of hardening |
| Adoption and community | Thousands of institutions, a large consortium, tens of thousands of citations |
| Purpose-built research tooling | Surveys, longitudinal and multi-site studies, branching, calculations, exports, mobile, offline |
| Compliance track record | 21 CFR Part 11, FISMA, HIPAA, GDPR options |
| Free to non-profits | No licence cost for eligible institutions |

SG/Vault does not match these today, and a fair comparison says so. REDCap is the established gold standard for academic research data capture within an institution.

## Where SG/Vault Differentiates

The differentiation is the open-source, no-lock-in, zero-knowledge nature, sharpened by REDCap's actual licence:

**Genuinely open source.** SG/Vault can be used, studied, modified, redistributed, and used commercially; REDCap's licence allows none of these. For anyone who needs to inspect, extend, redistribute, or build a commercial offering, this is decisive.

**Zero-knowledge.** SG/Vault's host holds no keys and cannot read the data; REDCap trusts the institution with the plaintext in its own database. For data that should not be readable even by the hosting institution, this is a categorical difference.

**Distributed and cross-party-native.** REDCap is excellent within one institution but siloed across institutions, where sharing needs data-use agreements and limited collaborator access. SG/Vault is built for cross-party collaboration, and the sovereign-aggregation model lets parties benchmark across boundaries without centralising data (cross-ref: the sovereign-data-aggregation-platform brief), which is exactly REDCap's hard problem.

**Recipient-held data.** In SG/Vault's model the patient or recipient creates the vault and holds the data, sharing only results (cross-ref: the recipient-created-disposable-vault brief); in REDCap the project and its data belong to the institution.

**Lightweight and portable.** REDCap requires an institution to run and secure a server-and-database stack with real IT effort; SG/Vault vaults are portable and the host holds no keys, so there is no heavy stack to operate.

**Open to everyone.** REDCap is limited to non-profit and non-commercial use; SG/Vault has no such eligibility gate.

## The Crux: A Precise Contrast, Not An Opposite

Unlike the commercial comparison, REDCap and SG/Vault are not opposite trust models, and this is worth stating plainly: **both respect the data owner and neither monetises data, so the differences are specific rather than philosophical.** REDCap is the best-in-class centralised, self-hosted, non-commercial research tool, trusted and deep. SG/Vault is an open, zero-knowledge, distributed substrate. The three real differences are open-source versus restricted licence, zero-knowledge versus server-side, and distributed cross-party versus institutional silo. A reader who values REDCap's mission will find SG/Vault aligned with it, and extending it on exactly the axes REDCap's licence and architecture do not reach.

## Honest Positioning: When Each Fits

| Situation | Better Fit |
|-----------|------------|
| A non-profit institution with IT, needing mature, deep, certified-capable research data capture | REDCap |
| Surveys, longitudinal and multi-site studies with decades of tooling | REDCap |
| Open source, redistributable, or commercial use is required | SG/Vault |
| Data must not be readable even by the hosting institution | SG/Vault |
| Patient or recipient holds their own data | SG/Vault |
| Cross-party collaboration without per-institution silos | SG/Vault |
| No appetite or capacity to self-host a server stack | SG/Vault |
| A commercial entity that cannot license REDCap | SG/Vault |

## What SG/Vault Would Need For A Head-To-Head

An honest list of the gaps: **depth of research tooling (surveys, longitudinal, branching, calculations, exports), maturity, the community and consortium, and a compliance track record.** Several are layerable as applications on the substrate over time, and the open, zero-knowledge, distributed model changes what some of them require: holding less plaintext centrally simplifies parts of the compliance story, and cross-party collaboration is native rather than bolted on. REDCap's two-decade maturity and citation base are the genuine moat, and the honest read is that SG/Vault competes on a different model rather than out-maturing REDCap on its own ground.

## A Complementarity Note

Worth flagging, since the missions align: SG/Vault need not only compete with REDCap. Zero-knowledge, recipient-held capture could feed research without centralising protected health information, and the federated sovereign-aggregation model could let institutions benchmark across their separate REDCap silos without surrendering data (cross-ref: the sovereign-data-aggregation-platform brief). The relationship could be extension as much as replacement.

## Recommendations

1. **Lead with the licence clarification** (genuinely open source versus source-available-restricted; many assume REDCap is open and it is not).
2. **Then zero-knowledge and distribution** (host-cannot-read, and cross-party without silos).
3. **Respect REDCap's mission** (aligned, not oppositional; this lands better with its community).
4. **Target the segments REDCap's licence and architecture exclude** (commercial users, cross-party collaboration, recipient-held data, no-self-hosting).
5. **Be honest about maturity and tooling** (REDCap is deep and proven; compete on model, not on out-tooling it).
6. **Explore complementarity** (feeding and federating across REDCap rather than only replacing it).
