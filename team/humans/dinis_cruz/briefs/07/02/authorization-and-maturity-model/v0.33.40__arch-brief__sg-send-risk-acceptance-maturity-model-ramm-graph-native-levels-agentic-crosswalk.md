## What This Is

A first-pass Risk Acceptance Maturity Model, RAMM, designed graph-native: **it models risk acceptance not as a free-text exception but as a durable decision node linked to the risk, the scoring, the owner, the approving authority, the appetite and tolerance, the compensating controls, the evidence, and the review, expiry, and reassessment lifecycle, so an organisation's maturity can be computed from the graph rather than asserted in a questionnaire; the five levels, ad hoc, repeatable, defined, managed, and optimized, are expressed as Node Type Formulas, path-patterns a graph query can test, so a level is a fact about the graph rather than a narrative claim; it interoperates with rather than replaces existing frameworks, OWASP Risk Rating Methodology as the scoring substrate, the RIMS Risk Maturity Model and public-sector maturity models for the staged and governance semantics, and risk-appetite maturity matrices for the acceptance boundaries, connected through the ontologies-of-ontologies bridge model so that doing a thing here satisfies a box there; it includes an agentic variation, the Agentic RAMM, because agents bring risk and capabilities that were not there before; and because Risk Mandate is itself the graph-native risk-acceptance workflow, adopting it inherently raises an organisation's RAMM level.** It is the sixth brief of 2 July, built on the attached research debrief (cross-ref: the v0.33.36 node-type-formulas, ontologies-of-ontologies, and grounding-ladder briefs, the v0.33.38 risk-appetite brief, and the v0.33.40 agent-authorization brief). New contributions: **the RAMM entity model, maturity levels as Node Type Formulas, and the Agentic RAMM.**

## The Gap and the Approach

The research is clear that the space is open. No dominant framework named Risk Acceptance Maturity Model exists in the OWASP ecosystem or the wider market, so the foundation has to be assembled from three sources: OWASP risk-assessment methods, enterprise risk maturity models, and risk-appetite and governance maturity guidance. The right move is not to compete with these but to overlay them. The project lead: **"in our graph model we should leverage the existing maturity models and risk frameworks and connect to them, so we can say if you do this you check that box, which is a good example of graphs of graphs, ontology of ontologies."** RAMM is a graph-native overlay standard whose specific angle is risk acceptance, and whose job is to link an acceptance decision to everything that justifies it.

## Why Graph-Native: Maturity Computed, Not Asserted

The differentiator, and the reason this belongs to our corpus, is that maturity is computed, not claimed. A questionnaire asks an organisation to rate itself; a graph lets the rating be derived from evidence. This is precisely the Node Type Formula mechanism: each maturity level is a required pattern of typed, directed paths over the acceptance graph, so a level is assigned by a query, not by judgment, and is therefore explicit, testable, explainable, and repeatable. An organisation is at a level when its acceptance nodes satisfy that level's path-pattern, and not before.

## The Entity Model

The debrief's entity classes map cleanly onto five layers, and each maps onto something the corpus already has.

| RAMM entity | Layer | Corpus equivalent |
|-------------|-------|-------------------|
| RiskItem | risk source | the risk node, scoped by the union-of-possible authorization |
| RiskAssessment | evaluation | scoring plus the confidence band and margin of error |
| RiskTreatmentDecision | treatment | mitigate, transfer, avoid, or accept (OWASP TAME) |
| RiskAcceptanceDecision | treatment | the acceptance node: no-deny, accept-in-a-direction, for-an-interval |
| RiskOwner, DecisionAuthority | governance | technical versus business owner, and the underwriting chain |
| RiskAppetiteStatement, RiskToleranceThreshold | governance | the revealed, fractal risk-appetite band and Goldilocks zone |
| CompensatingControl | governance | controls |
| ReviewEvent, ExpiryEvent, ReassessmentTrigger | review | the interval mechanic and the air-gap reassessment |
| EvidenceArtifact | review | the grounding ladder: evidence, measure, twin, reality |
| FrameworkReference | crosswalk | the bridge nodes of the ontologies-of-ontologies model |

Provenance sits across all of them, carried the way the corpus already carries it, as PKI attribution and a commit log that records which standard, tool, or assessment produced each linked artifact.

## The Relationship Patterns

The acceptance decision is the hub, and the edges are directed, so every acceptance has an inward path that grounds it in evidence and an outward path that ties it to authority and appetite. The core patterns are `RiskItem assessedBy RiskAssessment`, `RiskAssessment usesMethod FrameworkReference`, `RiskItem treatedBy RiskAcceptanceDecision`, `RiskAcceptanceDecision ownedBy RiskOwner`, `approvedBy DecisionAuthority`, `boundedBy RiskAppetiteStatement`, `withinToleranceOf RiskToleranceThreshold`, `justifiedBy CompensatingControl`, `evidencedBy EvidenceArtifact`, `reviewedAt ReviewEvent`, `expiresAt ExpiryEvent`, and `reassessOn ReassessmentTrigger`. These are the same directed inward and outward paths the corpus already uses, applied to acceptance.

## The Five Maturity Levels as Node Type Formulas

Each level is the path-pattern its acceptance nodes must satisfy, tested by query across the estate.

| Level | Name | The formula, informally |
|-------|------|-------------------------|
| 1 | Ad hoc | Acceptance nodes may exist but lack the required edges: no owner, no evidence, no expiry; rationale is free text |
| 2 | Repeatable | A fraction of acceptances have ownedBy and approvedBy, but thresholds are partial and evidencedBy is inconsistent |
| 3 | Defined | Every acceptance satisfies ownedBy, approvedBy, expiresAt, reviewedAt, and justifiedBy a control |
| 4 | Managed | Level 3, plus boundedBy an appetite statement, withinToleranceOf a threshold, and an authority-delegation path that reaches the board, with metrics reported |
| 5 | Optimized | Level 4, plus reassessOn triggers that fire on context change, portfolio-level queries, and a feedback path from outcomes back into the appetite |

The point is that each row is a graph predicate. Level 3 is not a claim that acceptance is defined; it is the query all acceptance nodes have the five required edges returning true. Maturity is the highest level whose predicate the graph satisfies.

## Interoperability: Bridges to Existing Frameworks

RAMM connects to the discovered standards through the bridges-not-merges model, each framework kept as its own owned ontology and linked at declared points. OWASP Risk Rating Methodology feeds the RiskAssessment layer as a scoring method. OWASP SAMM anchors governance and maturity semantics; ASVS supplies control requirements; the Web Security Testing Guide, API Security, Mobile Security, and the Top 10 projects supply findings and taxonomies; Threat Dragon and pytm supply threat-model evidence; DefectDojo and the dependency projects supply vulnerability and supply-chain evidence. Outside OWASP, the RIMS Risk Maturity Model anchors the staged progression and appetite semantics, public-sector maturity models inform authority delegation and review discipline, and risk-appetite maturity matrices shape how explicit the standard is about accepted exposure. Each bridge is the crosswalk the project lead described: do this in RAMM, and you satisfy that requirement in framework X. The detailed, per-control crosswalk is a later pass, in super detail; this first pass fixes the bridging approach and the first mappings.

## The Agentic RAMM

The variation the project lead wants is a specialisation for agents. The project lead: **"we can have the main one, or a variation called the agentic risk acceptance maturity model, because agents bring a level of risk that was not there before and capabilities that are very agentic specific."** The Agentic RAMM is the base model plus an agentic overlay of entities, a CapabilityCertificate for the scoped, signed grant, an AuthorizationClosure that models the union of everything reachable rather than the nominal grant, a MomentOfAuthorization, and an AgentTwin carrying the agent's permissions, capabilities, and track record. Its per-level criteria add agentic requirements: at Defined, every agent acceptance links to a capability certificate; at Managed, the authorization closure and the expected-versus-unexpected delta are surfaced and accepted, and the lethal-trifecta status is tracked; at Optimized, the recurring-acceptance metric is instrumented and the agent twin's reassessment fires on capability or track-record change.

## Risk Mandate Raises Your RAMM Level

The positioning falls out of the design. The project lead: **"just by using Risk Mandate you are going to improve your risk acceptance maturity, because the whole thing about Risk Mandate is the risk acceptance."** Risk Mandate is the graph-native acceptance workflow, so it produces exactly the owned, evidenced, interval-bound, appetite-linked, board-propagated acceptance nodes that the higher RAMM levels require. Adopting the product is therefore not a claim of maturity but a mechanism for it, and the RAMM is the standard against which that improvement is measured.

## Example Graph Instances

Three instances are worth building first, per the debrief: an accepted vulnerability, grounded in an OWASP-scored finding with a compensating control and an expiry; an accepted architectural risk, grounded in a threat model with a board-level owner and an appetite bound; and an accepted GenAI or agentic application risk, grounded in a capability certificate, an authorization closure, and a trifecta status, reviewed on an interval. Each exercises a different part of the entity model, and the third exercises the agentic overlay.

## Example Maturity Queries

The queries are the standard in action, and several should be first-class: expired acceptances still above tolerance; accepted risks with no compensating control; acceptances justified by an obsolete framework version; acceptances with no owner-to-board propagation path; and, for the Agentic RAMM, agent acceptances whose authorization closure exceeds the nominal grant, and agent acceptances with a full trifecta and no capability certificate. Each query is both an assurance check and a maturity probe.

## Design Decisions to Resolve

The debrief's questions for the follow-on agent, with the ones the corpus can already answer. The representation should be dual-published, a conceptual ontology and an implementable schema, because the corpus already treats types as computable path-patterns. Maturity should be inferred from evidence, not declared, for the same reason. The mandatory attributes for a valid acceptance are owner, approver, rationale, expiry, review cadence, compensating control where exposure is unresolved, appetite reference, residual score, and evidence provenance. Temporary exceptions are distinguished from durable acceptances by the interval and the reassessment trigger, a short interval with a hard expiry is an exception, a policy-aligned bound is durable. The open ones are the target graph representation, the scoring granularity, and the crosswalk versioning strategy.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a replacement.** RAMM overlays and interoperates with OWASP, RIMS, and the rest.
- **Not a questionnaire.** Maturity is computed from graph evidence, not self-rated.
- **Not the detailed crosswalk.** The per-control mapping is the next, super-detailed pass.
- **Not final.** The levels and entities are a first draft to be refined into normative statements.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Overlay versus standalone | RAMM must add value without forcing adoption of a whole new framework |
| Computed maturity versus messy data | Deriving a level from real graphs needs tolerance for partial evidence |
| Bridges versus drift | Crosswalks to external frameworks must be versioned as those frameworks change |
| Base versus agentic | The agentic overlay must extend, not fork, the base model |
