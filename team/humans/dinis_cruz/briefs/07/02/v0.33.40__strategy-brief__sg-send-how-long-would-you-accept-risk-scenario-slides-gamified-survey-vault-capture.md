# How Long Will You Accept This Risk? A Scenario Series to Make Agentic Risk Visceral

**version** v0.33.40
**date** 2 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Product, Strategy, Website Agent, Design
**type** Strategy brief

---

## What This Is

A concept and a scenario set for a short slide series, and eventually a gamified survey, built around one punchline, not would you accept this risk but how long will you accept it: **each scenario opens with a would-you question most people instinctively refuse, lets them say this is not relevant to me, and then reveals that it is already happening the moment they run a local model or grant an OAuth token; the format comes in two versions, static images to post on LinkedIn and a dynamic, branching one that responds to the user's answer, and the dynamic version can be gamified as a mini survey whose responses are captured in a vault, which both yields data on people's first instincts and demonstrates the technology; this brief supplies ten ready scenarios and the slide structure, so mock-ups can follow.** It is the third brief of 2 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.35 lethal-trifecta material, the v0.33.36 OAuth-Klue brief, and the v0.33.34 personal-risk-acceptance scenario). New contributions: **the how-long-not-whether punchline, the you-already-do reveal, and ten scenarios.**

## The Core Move: How Long, Not Whether

The whole series turns on one shift in the question. The project lead: **"the punch line is not would you accept this risk, but how long will you accept this risk for."** Whether to accept is a yes or no that people dodge; how long forces them to name an interval, an hour, a day, a month, and an interval is a commitment. The punchline lands the product's signature mechanic, accept for an interval, in a single question the reader answers about their own life.

## The Reveal Structure

Each scenario runs the same three beats. The project lead: **"show a scenario that is already happening today, and you can even say I do not think this is relevant to me, and then the next slide says, actually you do."** First the visceral question the reader refuses. Then the escape hatch, this is not my company, not my problem. Then the reveal that it already is, the instant they run a local agent or grant a token. The refusal and the reality are the same act seen from two angles, and the gap between them is the whole point.

## The Two Formats

The series ships in two forms. The project lead: **"there is a static images version I can post on LinkedIn, and a more dynamic one that depends on the user's answer."** The static set is a fixed slide sequence for the feed. The dynamic set branches on what the reader answers, and it can be gamified. The project lead: **"we can gamify this as a mini survey, and use the vault to capture the data, to see people's first instinct, and then show them this is already happening."** Capturing the responses in a vault does double duty: it yields real data on people's first instincts, and it is a live demonstration of the vault technology carrying the very survey that teaches the risk.

## Ten Scenarios

Each is written as hook, reveal, and punchline. The hook is the refusal, the reveal is the agentic reality, the punchline is always how long.

1. **Every email you own.** Hook: would you hand a stranger the keys to every email account you have ever registered anywhere? Reveal: that is what a local agent with inbox access holds, and since every password reset lands in that inbox, it is the keys to every other account too. Punchline: so how long will you let it?

2. **Your calendar.** Hook: would you let someone quietly delete and scramble your entire calendar? Reveal: an assistant with calendar write access can, in one call. Punchline: for how long is that acceptable?

3. **Your private messages.** Hook: would you let an app forward every private message you have ever sent to anyone it chooses? Reveal: an agent with messaging access can exfiltrate the lot. Punchline: how long do you accept that?

4. **The company card.** Hook: would you give a brand-new hire an unlimited company card on day one, unsupervised, overnight? Reveal: that is an agent with payment or billing API access. Punchline: for how long?

5. **The OAuth token.** Hook: would you sign a blank power of attorney and hand it to a vendor you have never audited? Reveal: that is an OAuth token grant, and it bypasses the password and the second factor. Punchline: how long will it stay signed?

6. **All your repositories.** Hook: would you let a bot rewrite or delete every one of your code repositories? Reveal: an agent acting on your behalf across your account can. Punchline: for how long?

7. **The production database.** Hook: would you give an unsupervised script write access to your production database with no undo? Reveal: that is an agent with the database credentials. Punchline: how long is that a good idea?

8. **Your unlocked laptop.** Hook: would you invite a stranger to sit at your unlocked laptop while you make coffee? Reveal: that is a local agent that reads untrusted content and then acts on your machine. Punchline: how long do you leave them there?

9. **A database of customers' passwords.** Hook: would you keep a database whose contents are, in effect, your customers' passwords? Reveal: that is a store of customer OAuth tokens, and it was the Klue breach. Punchline: how long do you hold it?

10. **One customer reaching another.** Hook: would you let one customer's request reach into another customer's data? Reveal: an agent with broad access and one poisoned input can. Punchline: how long can you accept that path being open?

## Slide Structure

For the static version, each scenario is a small sequence. Slide one poses the hook as a plain yes or no. Slide two offers the escape hatch, not relevant to me. Slide three delivers the reveal, this is already happening when you run X. Slide four turns the whether into a how long, and shows the interval choices, an hour, four hours, a day, a week, a month, six months, with the small Risk Mandate brand at the foot. The dynamic version collapses the same four beats into a branch that responds to the reader's tap.

## Why This Educates What Risk Mandate Does

The series teaches the product in miniature. It makes the lethal trifecta visceral, an agent that reads untrusted input and can exfiltrate, corrupt, or destroy. It lands the you-have-already-accepted-this insight that underpins no-deny. It puts the interval question, how long, in the reader's own mouth. And the vault-captured survey is a self-referential proof, the technology carrying the lesson about the technology.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not fear-mongering.** The scenarios are real, and the point is the interval, not panic.
- **Not whether-to-accept.** The question is always how long, never a simple no.
- **Not generic.** Each reveal ties to a concrete act: a local agent, a token, a credential.
- **Not the mock-ups.** Those are the next step, built from these scenarios.

## Honest Notes

- **The escape hatch is the hook.** Letting the reader say not relevant is what makes the reveal land.
- **Static and dynamic share content.** The same ten scenarios feed both formats.
- **Vault capture is opt-in.** Survey data should be captured transparently and with consent.
- **Ten is a starting set.** Three or four per post is plenty; the rest are a bank to draw from.
