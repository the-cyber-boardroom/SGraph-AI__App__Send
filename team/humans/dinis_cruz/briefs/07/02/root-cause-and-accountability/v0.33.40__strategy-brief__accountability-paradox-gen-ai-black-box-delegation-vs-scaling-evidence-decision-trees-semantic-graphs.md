**from** Human (project lead)

## What This Is

A briefing on the accountability paradox of Gen AI, two opposing forces pulling in different directions: **the first, which makes things worse, is delegating more and more decisions to models we do not understand, black boxes whose reasoning we cannot follow and whose explanations are often unconvincing, which produces fuzzy accountability, the machine decided, and which too often hides behind a human-in-the-loop that is really accountability-in-the-loop, a person given the blame without the agency, time, or context, whose judgment decays under approval fatigue, and these systems fail in ways humans do not, across a much wider spectrum; the second force, which is the real opportunity and the piece worth focusing on, is to use LLMs not as the decision-maker but as the means to scale the control flows, decision trees, facts, evidence, and the collection of the data and the state behind a decision, so that mission-critical decisions come back to explicit logic, if this then that, at a scale only now possible, documented, traced, and connected in a semantic knowledge graph; the payoff is more accountability and informed risk-based decisions rather than hope-based ones, a different paradigm built on visibility; and the friction is that visibility exposes inefficiency and runs against the self-interest of those who benefit from not being accountable.** It is the fifth brief of 2 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.36 accountability-in-the-loop, the v0.33.40 hope-driven, and the v0.33.36 node-type-formulas briefs). New contributions: **the two-force paradox, use-LLMs-not-to-use-LLMs, and scaled accountability via graphs.**

## The Paradox: Two Forces

Gen AI pulls accountability in two directions at once. The project lead: **"I want to capture the accountability paradox of Gen AI, there are two forces at play, one makes things worse and the other makes things better."** Both are real and both are happening now, which is why the situation is a paradox rather than simply a problem or an opportunity. The article should cover both, while focusing on the second.

## Force One: Delegating to Black Boxes

The first force degrades accountability by handing decisions to systems no one can follow. The project lead: **"one path is delegating more and more decisions to agents and Gen AI without fully understanding how the decision was made, giving black boxes we do not understand the ability to make decisions that are then not accountable, or where the accountability is very fuzzy."** When the machine decided, no human holds real agency, and accountability dissolves into the model.

## Accountability in the Loop, Not Human in the Loop

The usual defence, a human in the loop, is often a fiction. The project lead: **"we already have a lot of human-in-the-loop solutions that are actually accountability-in-the-loop, a way to assign blame to a human who is not given enough agency, time, or context, and the quality of the judgement goes down over time because it is not being exercised."** And the mechanism of the decay is mundane. The project lead: **"if you have to click yes, yes, yes, approve, approve all the time, of course you get fatigue, especially if you are asked for things without context."** A rubber stamp is not oversight, and a human placed there only to absorb blame is worse than none.

## They Fail in Ways Humans Do Not

The black boxes also fail unlike anything we are used to. The project lead: **"we are delegating capabilities to systems we do not understand, that work in unpredictable ways and fail in unpredictable ways, that can work quite well and then fail in very specific ways."** This is the point Bruce Schneier and Nathan Sanders made in IEEE Spectrum: human errors cluster at the edges of knowledge, track fatigue, and come with an admission of ignorance, whereas LLM errors are scattered evenly across the whole knowledge space, arrive without warning, and are delivered with the same confidence whether right or wrong. Our entire apparatus for catching human mistakes assumes human failure patterns, so it does not catch these. The spectrum of success and failure is simply far wider, and our mistake-correcting instincts do not fit it.

## Force Two: Scale the Accountability

The second force is the one that matters, and the article should centre on it. The project lead: **"the real power of LLMs is not to introduce more black-boxiness, but for the first time to scale the control flows, the decision trees, the facts and evidence, the data points, and the collection of the evidence and the decisions and the state used to make a decision."** The opportunity is not to add another opaque decision-maker, but to use the technology to make decisions more legible than ever before, by scaling the scaffolding of logic, evidence, and traceability that good decisions have always needed and rarely had at scale.

## Use LLMs, Not to Use LLMs

The distinction is the crux. The project lead: **"the power here is to use LLMs, not to use LLMs, because when you get into mission-critical decisions it should all be about logic, decision trees, if this then that, but at a bigger scale, and every one documented, every trace mapped, every bit of information connected."** LLMs are the means, not the decider. For decisions that matter, the decision itself should be explicit, deterministic logic, and the LLM's job is to build, populate, and connect that logic at a scale humans never could. This is the same conclusion Schneier and Sanders reach from the other side, confine the model to what it is actually good at, and keep the consequences of its mistakes in view.

## Semantic Knowledge Graphs Scale It

The structure that makes this possible is the graph. The project lead: **"that is why semantic knowledge graphs are so important, because they are a great way to scale this."** The decision trees, the evidence, the traces, and the state all become nodes and edges, and the graph is what lets the whole thing grow complex without becoming opaque. It gets complex fast, but complexity that is designed, documented, and connected is the opposite of a black box.

## Risk-Based, Not Hope-Based

The payoff is a change in the quality of decisions. The project lead: **"the power of LLMs is to increase accountability and to have people making effective risk-based decisions, not hope-based decisions or blind risk-based decisions."** This connects directly to hope-driven development: the alternative to hoping is deciding on visible, connected evidence. The paradigm shift is toward making things visible and accountable rather than fast and opaque.

## The Friction: Visibility Against Self-Interest

The obstacle is not technical but human. The project lead: **"this exposes a lot of inefficiency, and it can be against an individual's or a company's self-interest to have this level of visibility, because it brings accountability for a lot of things that today are not accountable."** Making decisions legible surfaces inefficiency and assigns ownership where there was none, and plenty of people and organisations quietly benefit from the current fog. This is the prefer-not-to-know force again, now aimed at the whole enterprise, and it is the real resistance the second paradigm has to overcome.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not anti-AI.** The second force is the case for using LLMs, heavily, in the right role.
- **Not a claim of explainable models.** It routes decisions to explicit logic, not to model introspection.
- **Not human-in-the-loop theatre.** A blame-absorbing stamp is rejected, not endorsed.
- **Not friction-free.** Visibility runs against real self-interest, and that resistance is expected.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Black-box power versus legibility | The models are useful precisely where they are least transparent |
| Scaling logic versus complexity | Explicit decision trees at scale get very complex, though designed, not opaque |
| Confidence without ignorance | An LLM gives no signal when it is wrong, unlike a human |
| Visibility versus self-interest | The main resistance is that accountability is unwelcome to those who lack it |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |

| Where is the line for mission-critical? | Which decisions must return to explicit logic |
| How do LLMs populate the decision graph? | Using the model to build the scaffolding, not to decide |

| How is the self-interest resistance addressed? | Making visibility worthwhile to those it exposes |
