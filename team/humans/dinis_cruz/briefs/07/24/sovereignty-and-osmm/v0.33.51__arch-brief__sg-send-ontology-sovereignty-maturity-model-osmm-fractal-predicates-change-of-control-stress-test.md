## What This Is

A maturity model for ontology sovereignty, designed graph-native and fractal: **it narrows the sovereignty question from the whole technology stack to the ontology alone, on the grounds that the ontology is the most concentrated representation of an organisation that will ever exist and therefore the place where sovereignty is won or lost, and that a narrow model can be made testable where a broad one can only be asserted; it defines sovereignty not as a licence badge but as five predicates that can be evaluated against any node at any altitude, Readable, Portable, Modifiable, Survivable, and Provable, which together form a single Node Type Formula applied recursively; it is fractal in the corpus's precise sense, the same five predicates, the same validators, and the same query engine apply unchanged to a property, an object type, a domain ontology, a platform ontology, a tenant estate, and a national estate, so a maturity score at one altitude composes into a score at the next without a new format; it expresses five maturity levels as path-patterns a graph query can test rather than as questionnaire rows, so a level is a fact about the graph and not a narrative claim; its distinctive contribution over existing sovereignty frameworks is the Change-of-Control Stress Test, which encodes the empirical finding that an OSI licence does not survive an acquisition but a copyright structure does, making Survivable depend on distributed copyright and foundation-held trademark rather than on the licence file; and it bridges rather than merges to CADA's four assurance levels, the Open Source Definition and SPDX, the W3C semantic stack, and FAIR, so that satisfying a level here demonstrably satisfies a requirement there.** It applies the RAMM pattern to a new domain (cross-ref: the v0.33.40 RAMM brief, the v0.33.36 node-type-formulas, ontologies-of-ontologies, and grounding-ladder briefs, the v0.33.48 fractal-semantic-graphs brief, and the v0.33.26 sovereignty-requires-open-source thesis). New contributions: **the five sovereignty predicates, the fractal altitude ladder, the Change-of-Control Stress Test, and the counter-evidence queries.**

## Why Ontology Rather Than Technology

The scope decision is the first design decision, and it is the right one for three reasons.

**The ontology is where the concentration is.** The 13 June thesis established that the ontology is the most concentrated representation of an organisation that will ever exist, every entity, relationship, decision, and rule in one place. A technology maturity model spreads its attention across compute, storage, network, identity, and application, where the sovereignty stakes are real but diffuse. An ontology maturity model aims at the single artefact whose capture is most consequential.

**Narrow scope is what makes it testable.** A whole-stack sovereignty model can only ever be a questionnaire, because no single graph spans it. An ontology is already a graph, which means every predicate in this model can be evaluated by query against the thing itself. That is the difference between a model that is scored and one that is computed, and it is only available at this scope.

**The market is arguing about the wrong layer.** Vendors compete on where data is hosted and under whose jurisdiction the operating entity sits. Both are properties of deployment, and both are snapshots that an acquisition or a subpoena can change. The ontology's licence, copyright structure, and semantic portability are properties of the artefact, and they survive the vendor. Pointing the model at the layer that persists is the point.

## The Five Predicates: One Formula, Applied Recursively

Sovereignty over any ontological node is the conjunction of five predicates. Each is a required path from the node to an evidence artefact, in the grounding-ladder sense, so each is testable and each grounds down to something real rather than asserted.

| # | Predicate | The question | Grounded by |
|---|-----------|--------------|-------------|
| 1 | **Readable** | Can I obtain the definition of this node at all? | An export artefact, retrievable without vendor assistance |
| 2 | **Portable** | Can a different engine reason over it without loss? | A W3C-standard serialisation (OWL / RDF / SHACL) or an equivalent open schema |
| 3 | **Modifiable** | Can I change it and run the changed version? | A round-trip test: export, modify, execute on a non-vendor engine |
| 4 | **Survivable** | Do I still hold it if the vendor is acquired, sanctioned, or fails? | A copyright and trademark structure that no single party can unilaterally alter |
| 5 | **Provable** | Can I demonstrate the above to a regulator without the vendor's cooperation? | A signed, versioned evidence artefact anchored to a commit |

The ordering is deliberate and monotone. Readable without Portable is Palantir's ontology JSON export, real but not sufficient. Portable without Survivable is a permissively licensed schema whose next version can be closed. Survivable without Provable is a claim you believe but cannot evidence in a procurement.

**Readable, then Portable, then Modifiable, then Survivable, then Provable**: each predicate presupposes the ones before it. This is what makes the levels below well-formed rather than an arbitrary staircase.

## Fractal: The Same Formula At Every Altitude

The model is fractal in the corpus's precise sense, one grammar at every altitude, not a decoration. The same five predicates, the same validators, and the same queries apply unchanged from a single property to a national estate.

```
   ZOOM OUT                                          ZOOM IN
   national / sectoral estate
     +-- organisation estate
           +-- platform ontology
                 +-- domain ontology
                       +-- object type
                             +-- link type / action type
                                   +-- property / definition

   same five predicates, same validators, same query engine,
   same evidence requirement, at every altitude
```

Three properties follow, and they are what a non-fractal model cannot offer.

**Composition.** The score of a graph is a function of the scores of its nodes, so an estate score is computed rather than sampled. The default composition rule is the weakest link: a domain ontology is Portable only if every object type in it is Portable. Strict conjunction is the right default because a single non-portable object type in a critical path defeats the migration it was supposed to enable.

**Zoom without reformulation.** A regulator asking about a national estate and an engineer asking about one link type are running the same query at different altitudes. There is no separate executive framework and technical checklist to keep in sync.

**Locality of failure.** Because the score composes, a failing estate score resolves by traversal to the specific nodes that caused it. The model does not just grade, it points, which is what turns an assessment into a backlog.

## The Five Maturity Levels As Node Type Formulas

Each level is the path-pattern its ontology nodes must satisfy, tested by query across the estate. Maturity is the highest level whose predicate the graph satisfies, not the level an organisation reports.

| Level | Name | The formula, informally |
|-------|------|-------------------------|
| 0 | **Opaque** | The ontology exists only inside the vendor's runtime. No export path. Nodes have no outward path to any artefact the customer holds. |
| 1 | **Extractable** | Every node satisfies Readable: an export exists and the customer can trigger it unaided. Serialisation is vendor-shaped; no path to a W3C artefact. |
| 2 | **Portable** | Level 1, plus every node satisfies Portable: a standards-serialised form exists, and a round-trip test has been executed and evidenced on a second engine. |
| 3 | **Operable** | Level 2, plus Modifiable: the reasoning engine is OSI-licensed, a non-vendor deployment has executed the ontology in anger, and the exit is rehearsed rather than theoretical. |
| 4 | **Survivable** | Level 3, plus the Change-of-Control Stress Test passes: distributed copyright or foundation-held trademark, schema under CC0/CC-BY, and demonstrated fork capacity. |
| 5 | **Provable** | Level 4, plus every claim above is a signed, versioned evidence artefact anchored to a commit, re-tested on an interval, with drift alerting when a predicate stops holding. |

The point is that each row is a graph predicate. Level 2 is not a claim that the ontology is portable; it is the query *all object types have a `serialisedAs` edge to a W3C artefact and a `roundTripTestedBy` edge to a passing test* returning true.

Note the deliberate consequence: **most self-described sovereign platforms today sit at Level 1 or 2.** Palantir's Foundry is a clean Level 1 on the ontology (the JSON export is real, the customer can trigger it, and it is genuinely not nothing) and fails Level 2 because the serialisation is Foundry-shaped rather than OWL. Vendors claiming sovereignty on the basis of EU jurisdiction and Iceberg tables are typically Level 1 on the ontology while being Level 3 on the *data*, which is precisely the asymmetry this model exists to surface.

## The Change-of-Control Stress Test

This is the distinctive contribution, and it exists because the empirical record contradicts the naive form of the open-source argument.

An OSI licence is irrevocable for code already published, but the copyright holder may ship the next version under different terms. Every catalogued relicensing between 2018 and 2024 followed the same legal pattern: a single corporate copyright holder, having aggregated contributor copyright via a CLA, exercised the right to change terms going forward. Distributed-copyright projects could not be moved the same way, because no single party had standing. And acquisition entrenches rather than reverses the closure: HashiCorp went BUSL, IBM acquired it, and the licence stayed.

Therefore **Survivable is not a property of the licence file. It is a property of the copyright and trademark structure.** The test has four legs, each independently evidenced:

| Leg | Passing condition | Failing pattern |
|-----|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Copyright** | Distributed: DCO, inbound=outbound, no assignment | Single-entity CLA with relicensing rights |
| **Trademark** | Held by a neutral foundation with charter-level transfer | Held by the operating company |
| **Schema licence** | CC0 or CC-BY on the ontology definitions, separately from the code | Schema unlicensed, or bundled under the code EULA |
| **Fork capacity** | A named party with the headcount and mandate to run the fork | "The community would fork it" with no named party |

The trademark leg is the one most often missed and is empirically the load-bearing one: the NATS case turned on charter-level trademark transfer, and the licence integrity followed from name control rather than from the licence text. The fork-capacity leg encodes Tension 1 of the 13 June thesis, that holding the code confers nothing without the capacity to run it: the forks that survived the relicensing wave were the ones with committed engineering headcount from day one.

**The stress test is a query, not an interview.** "Show me the CLA" and "show me who holds the trademark" are answerable from public artefacts, and a vendor that cannot answer them has answered them.

## Bridges, Not Merges

Following the ontologies-of-ontologies model, each external framework stays its own owned ontology, linked at declared points, so that doing a thing here demonstrably checks a box there.

| External framework | Bridge point | Crosswalk claim |
|---|---|---|
| **CADA assurance levels 1 to 4** | Deployment and control | CADA grades *where and under whose control the service runs*; OSMM grades *whether the artefact survives that control changing*. A CADA Level 3 provider can be OSMM Level 1. The two are orthogonal and both are needed. |
| **Open Source Definition / SPDX** | Levels 3 and 4 | OSMM Level 3 requires an OSI-approved licence on the engine; SPDX identifiers are the evidence artefact. Source-available licences satisfy Readable, never Modifiable. |
| **W3C OWL / RDF / SHACL** | Level 2 | The standards-serialisation requirement is discharged by a valid OWL or SHACL artefact; this is what makes Portable machine-testable rather than argued. |
| **FAIR principles** | Levels 1 to 2 | Findable and Accessible map onto Readable; Interoperable and Reusable map onto Portable and Modifiable. FAIR is the research-data vocabulary for the same idea. |
| **RAMM** | Governance overlay | An OSMM level below the organisation's declared appetite is a RiskItem; accepting it is a RiskAcceptanceDecision with an owner, an expiry, and a compensating control. |

The CADA bridge is the most commercially useful and deserves emphasis. CADA already legislates that jurisdiction of operation is insufficient: Level 1 is satisfied by an EU subsidiary of a US hyperscaler, and control only enters at Level 3. OSMM extends that logic one step further: control of the *operator* is itself a snapshot, and the ontology's survivability is what remains when the operator changes hands. Presented that way, OSMM is not a competitor to CADA but the missing column.

## Counter-Evidence Queries

The queries are the standard in action, and each is both an assurance check and a maturity probe. They are framed as counter-evidence deliberately: each looks for the fact that would *falsify* a claimed level, because a model that only gathers confirming evidence grades optimistically.

1. Object types with no `serialisedAs` edge to a W3C artefact: falsifies a claimed Level 2.
2. Round-trip tests older than the last schema change: the evidence is stale, so Level 2 has lapsed.
3. Ontology definitions with no separate licence node: the schema is travelling under the code EULA.
4. Engine dependencies whose SPDX identifier is source-available rather than OSI-approved: falsifies Level 3.
5. Projects whose copyright path terminates in a single legal entity: falsifies Level 4 regardless of the licence.
6. Trademark nodes held by the operating company rather than a foundation: the NATS backstop is absent.
7. Fork-capacity claims with no named party and no headcount: Tension 1 unaddressed.
8. Predicates that held at last assessment and do not hold now: drift, the Level 5 requirement.
9. Estates whose composed score is dragged below appetite by fewer than five object types: the highest-leverage remediation backlog.
10. Nodes where OSMM level is below the RAMM-accepted threshold with no acceptance node: an unaccepted sovereignty risk.

Query 9 is the one that makes the model usable rather than merely correct: because the score composes, the traversal that produces a failing estate score also produces the shortest list of nodes that would fix it.

## What Adopting The Graph Does To Your Score

The positioning falls out of the design, as it did for RAMM. A platform whose ontology, validators, and engine are forkable artefacts, whose tenant state is a portable versioned vault, and whose audit log is the commit history, produces Level 5 evidence as a by-product of running rather than as a compliance exercise. The commit anchor is the Provable predicate. The vault is the Survivable one. Adopting the architecture is therefore not a claim of maturity but a mechanism for it, and OSMM is the standard against which the improvement is measured.

The corollary is the honest one and should be stated in public: **this model scores us too, and we should publish our own score before we score anyone else.** A maturity model published by a vendor that has not run it against itself is marketing. Run against ourselves first, with the failing queries shown, it is a standard.

## Honest Tensions

**Tension 1: strict conjunction may be too harsh.** Weakest-link composition means one non-portable object type drops a whole domain. That is correct for migration realism and wrong for progress reporting. The likely resolution is to report both, a conjunctive level and a coverage percentage, and to resist the temptation to let coverage become the headline.

**Tension 2: the model advantages us.** We designed a standard our architecture scores well against. That is not disqualifying, every framework encodes its authors' commitments, but it must be disclosed, and the model is only credible if a competitor could score Level 5 without adopting our product. Check that property deliberately, and if it fails, the model is a moat and not a standard.

**Tension 3: W3C serialisation is not automatically semantic equivalence.** A valid OWL export can still lose the operational semantics, the actions, the permissions, the temporal state. Portable therefore needs the round-trip execution test and not just a schema validation, and even then equivalence is asserted at the granularity the test covers.

**Tension 4: Level 5 has an ongoing cost.** Interval re-testing and drift alerting are real operational expense, and organisations will rationally stop at Level 3 or 4 for non-critical ontologies. The model should say so explicitly rather than implying that Level 5 everywhere is the goal.

**Tension 5: sovereignty remains a spectrum with dependencies below.** The ontology can be fully sovereign while the chips, the cloud, and the upstream package registries are not, which is Tension 2 of the original thesis and is not solved by narrowing scope, only bracketed by it.

**Tension 6: fork capacity is the softest predicate.** Copyright, trademark, and licence are documentary and testable. Capacity is a judgment about headcount, funding, and mandate, and it is the leg most open to generous self-assessment.

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Conjunctive level plus coverage, or a single composed score? | Tension 1; affects every downstream report |
| What is the minimum round-trip test that certifies Portable? | Schema validity is insufficient; execution equivalence is expensive |
| Who could hold the trademark if we foundation-ise? | The Level 4 leg we cannot currently pass ourselves |
| How is fork capacity evidenced rather than asserted? | The softest predicate needs an objective proxy |
| Does OSMM publish as CC BY, and the schema as CC0? | The model must satisfy its own Level 4 schema-licence leg |
| What is the re-test interval for Level 5 by criticality tier? | Tension 4; drives the operating cost |
| Do we publish our own score first? | Recommended yes; decide before any external use |
