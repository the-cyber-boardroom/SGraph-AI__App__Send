## What This Is

The second thing to know about pulling the plug, after knowing it exists: **the off-switch is rarely surgical, because the only way to stop one misbehaving agent is often to take down the whole service it runs inside, the entire website, the shared database, the production line, so pulling the plug has a blast radius of its own and a set of side effects, the lost transactions, the broken sessions, the downstream systems that fail when their dependency vanishes, the contractual breach when an SLA is missed; this is why in practice the plug so often goes unpulled, not because nobody has it but because pulling it hurts almost as much as the thing it stops, which means the real control is not merely having an off-switch but having a precise one, a plug that halts the agent without taking the estate with it, and the absence of a precise plug is itself a finding worth owning, because a business that can only stop its agent by stopping its business does not really have a usable plug at all.** This is piece 1 of the Who Can Pull The Plug series (cross-ref: the series-plan document, the pillar, the v0.33.50 core-primitives brief, and the v0.33.49 fractal-registers brief). New contributions: **blast radius and side effects as the second and fourth dimensions of the plug profile, the precise-plug versus blunt-plug distinction, and the absence of a precise plug named as a finding.**

## The Off-Switch Is Often The Power Switch

Ask how you would actually stop a specific production agent and the answer is frequently blunt. The agent runs inside a service, shares a database, holds a credential that other things also use, sits on a network segment with its neighbours. Stopping it cleanly would require reaching exactly its process and only its process, and often that reach does not exist. So the honest off-switch is a bigger one: take the whole application offline, revoke a credential that a dozen systems share, cut the network segment, roll back the deployment for everyone. The plug is a sledgehammer, and the thing it smashes is much larger than the agent.

## Blast Radius Is A Measurement, Not An Assumption

Blast radius is the scope of what falls when you pull the plug, and it is a property to measure rather than assume. Two agents with identical behaviour can have completely different plug blast radii: one has a dedicated, scoped credential and a process you can kill in isolation, the other shares a service account and a runtime with the checkout flow. Stopping the first costs almost nothing. Stopping the second stops revenue. The register should carry that number, because a plug with a small blast radius is a genuine control and a plug with a huge one is a decision nobody will actually take in the moment.

## Side Effects Are The Cost Of The Stop Itself

Side effects are distinct from blast radius: blast radius is what else goes down, side effects are the consequences of it going down. Pulling the plug mid-transaction loses the transaction. Cutting the credential breaks every session using it. Halting the line strands work in progress. Missing an availability commitment breaches a contract. These are real costs, they are caused by the stop rather than by the risk, and they belong in the profile, because a decision-maker weighing whether to pull the plug is weighing the harm the agent is doing against the harm that stopping it will cause, and if the second is unknown, the decision is made blind.

```
   BLUNT PLUG                          PRECISE PLUG
   stop the agent = stop the service   stop the agent, service keeps running
   shared credential, shared runtime    scoped credential, isolated process
   huge blast radius, heavy side effects small blast radius, contained cost
   nobody pulls it in the moment        someone actually will
```

## Why The Plug Goes Unpulled

The uncomfortable consequence is that a plug with a large blast radius is a plug that does not get pulled. When stopping the agent means stopping the business, the person with the authority hesitates, escalates, waits for certainty, and the agent keeps acting through the delay. A plug nobody will pull in time is close to no plug at all in effect, even though it exists. This is why the mere existence of an off-switch is such a weak assurance, and why the profile, not the switch, is what an organisation should actually track.

## The Precise Plug Is A Control Worth Building

The constructive reading is that a precise plug is an investment with a measurable payoff. Giving an agent its own scoped credential rather than a shared one, running it in a process you can terminate in isolation, putting it behind a gate you can close without closing the service, all shrink the blast radius and the side effects, which turns a plug nobody dares pull into one somebody actually will. The absence of that precision is a finding in its own right, ownable and acceptable like any other: this agent has no precise off-switch, the only plug takes the whole service down, and here is who accepts that until it is fixed.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a claim that the plug is missing.** It exists; it is just blunt, and bluntness is the finding.
- **Not enforcement advice.** RiskMandate measures and owns the blast radius; it does not build or pull the plug.
- **Not a demand to isolate everything.** Precision is an investment to be prioritised by profile, not a universal mandate.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Precise plug versus cost | Isolating every agent has real engineering cost, and the profile is what says which agents are worth it |
| Measuring blast radius versus knowing the estate | A true blast radius needs a map of shared credentials, runtimes, and dependencies that many organisations do not have |
| Blunt plug exists versus counts as a plug | An off-switch nobody will pull in time is technically present and practically absent, and the profile has to capture that honestly |
