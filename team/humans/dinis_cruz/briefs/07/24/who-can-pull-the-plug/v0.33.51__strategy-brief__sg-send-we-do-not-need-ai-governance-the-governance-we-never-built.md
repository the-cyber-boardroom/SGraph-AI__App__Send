# We Do Not Need AI Governance

**version** v0.33.51
**date** 24 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Board, Executive, Security, Risk, Public
**type** Strategy brief (series)

*Piece 7 of the Who Can Pull The Plug series. The governance reframe. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

---

## What This Is

The reframe that places the whole plug question in a longer story: **the plug question is not new and not specific to AI, it is the old question of what data can enter a system, what its output may contain, where results may go, and who authorised an action, and organisations have owed answers to those questions for years, mostly paying the debt late or not at all, and getting away with it only because consequences moved slowly enough to manage; the habit each time a new technology arrives is to invent a new governance discipline, cloud governance, data governance, now AI governance, rather than to fix the accountability that failed underneath, so a new committee is stood up and a new set of principles is filed while the old gap stays open; the honest conclusion an outside governance argument reaches, and the one this series shares, is that we do not need AI governance, we need the governance we never built, and the reason agents force the issue is that they remove the slow-consequence tolerance that hid the gap, since a system acting hundreds of times in seconds makes the distance between a written policy and a working control visible at once; RiskMandate is positioned as that missing governance, computed from evidence rather than asserted in a questionnaire, with one honest difference from the outside argument, that where it would embed automatic controls in the infrastructure, we make the plug profile legible and owned and rate the enforcer rather than becoming it.** This is piece 7 of the Who Can Pull The Plug series (cross-ref: the series-plan document, the pillar, the v0.33.40 RAMM brief, and the maturity piece). New contributions: **the plug question placed in the pattern of governing labels instead of systems, the alignment with the we-do-not-need-AI-governance argument, and the single stated difference of legibility over embedded enforcement.**

## The Pattern

The story repeats. A new technology arrives and the existing governance fails to contain it. Rather than ask why the governance failed, organisations invent a discipline named after the technology. Cloud arrived, and cloud governance was invented instead of fixing change management and access control. Data became strategic, and data governance was invented instead of fixing ownership, quality, and accountability. Agents arrived, and AI governance is being invented instead of fixing the same accountability that failed before. Each cycle names the new problem and leaves the old one unsolved, governing the label rather than the system, the document rather than the decision.

## We Govern Inputs And Outputs, Not Tools

There is a useful intuition in how mature governance already works for ordinary tools. We do not govern the spreadsheet; we govern what data may go into it, what may be saved, where it may be shared, and who may open it. We do not govern the aircraft; we govern what may be brought onto it. The tool is treated as neutral and the governance lives around its inputs, outputs, and access. For a great deal of enterprise use, AI can work the same way, because what data may enter, what the output may contain, where it may be stored, and who authorised the action are exactly the old questions, not new ones.

## Where The Analogy Breaks, And The Plug Appears

The analogy holds until autonomy. A spreadsheet does not act on its own and an aircraft does not reroute itself because it found a better path, but an agent does. It makes decisions in the gaps between human checkpoints and takes actions no one explicitly authorised, because the system gave it the permission and the opportunity. So governing the inputs is necessary but no longer sufficient, and a second question appears that the old tools never raised: who can stop it when what it is doing is not what anyone intended. That is the plug question, and it is the point where governing inputs has to be joined by governing the off-switch.

## Why Agents Force The Old Question

The reason the deferral stops working is not that the questions changed but that the tolerance did. Organisations answered the access and accountability questions badly, or treated them as a future-us problem, and survived because the consequences arrived slowly. Agents remove that tolerance. When a system can take hundreds of actions in the time it takes to notice one, the gap between having a policy and having a functioning control is visible immediately, and every deferred answer comes due at once. The governance was always needed. Agents are the enforcement mechanism that finally makes not having it hurt.

## The Positioning, And The One Difference

RiskMandate is the governance an organisation already needed, expressed as something computed from evidence rather than asserted in a questionnaire: owned acceptances, named plug-holders, measured profiles, all derivable from the graph rather than claimed in a form. On this the corpus and the outside argument agree almost entirely, that the answer is structural governance rather than another discipline, and that clear accountability for who can stop the system is the heart of it. There is one honest difference worth stating rather than blurring. The outside argument leans toward embedding controls in the infrastructure so the stop happens automatically, which is an enforcement posture. RiskMandate is never in line. It makes the plug profile legible and owned, and it rates the automatic control as a tool whose value is conditional on it working, rather than becoming that control. We supply the accountability; the enforcement stays someone else's job, rated by us.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a new discipline.** The argument is the opposite: the governance was always needed and never built.
- **Not a dismissal of AI's difference.** Autonomy is real, and it is exactly why the off-switch question is new.
- **Not full agreement with the outside argument.** The one difference, legibility over embedded enforcement, is stated plainly.
- **Not a framework to file.** The point is governance computed and enacted, not principles approved and forgotten.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Borrowing the argument versus the one difference | The outside thesis is close but not identical, and honesty requires stating the enforcement difference rather than implying agreement |
| Old questions versus genuine novelty | Most of the questions are old, but autonomy adds a real new one, and overstating the not-new claim would be its own error |
| Governance computed versus messy reality | Deriving governance from evidence assumes the evidence exists, and many estates cannot yet supply it |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |

| What evidence must the estate supply? | The minimum an organisation needs before its governance can be computed rather than asserted |
