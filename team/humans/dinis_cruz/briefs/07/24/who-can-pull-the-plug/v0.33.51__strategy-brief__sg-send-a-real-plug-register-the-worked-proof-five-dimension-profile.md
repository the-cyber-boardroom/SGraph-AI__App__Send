## What This Is

The series made concrete on a real register: **an existing RiskMandate scenario, an agentic browser-isolation deployment, already carries a plug for every risk, and re-expressing that register in the five-dimension profile does two things at once, it shows the full range of what a plug actually looks like, from the surgical and fully recoverable, kill the isolated session, revoke the scoped credential, through the blunt and slow, exit the platform contract, to the board's only lever, fund the fix, and it demonstrates the correction at the heart of this series in a single move, because the rows the original register marked as having no plug, a data breach, a foothold on a trusted desktop once used, an unattributable transaction, were never really missing an off-switch, you can always disconnect, revoke, or shut down, they were rows where recoverability is zero, so restating them as plug exists but recoverability is zero turns a frightening blank into an honest, ownable finding that points straight at prevention; this is the proof a practitioner can hold, the piece closest to the data the register already stores, and the one a partner could productise most directly.** This is piece 10 of the Who Can Pull The Plug series (cross-ref: the series-plan document, the pillar, the recoverability piece, and the v0.33.49 fractal-registers brief). New contributions: **a real register re-expressed in the five-dimension profile, and the correction demonstrated on live rows by restating no-plug as plug-exists-but-recoverability-zero.**

## The Register, Re-Expressed

The scenario is a real one from the product: an agent running inside a browser-isolation deployment, with the risks that arrangement creates. The original register recorded, for each risk, whether a plug existed, who held it, and how it worked. Re-expressed in the five-dimension profile, the same risks reveal their true range. The table below is illustrative, drawn from the scenario rather than from any customer, and shortened to show the span.

| Risk (illustrative) | Who holds the plug | Blast radius | Speed | Recoverability |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Agent misuses the isolated session | IT / isolation platform | Small: that session only | Fast | High: contained on stop |
| Agent abuses a scoped credential | IT | Small to medium | Fast | High: revoke and rotate |
| Agent misuses the platform itself | COO / procurement | Large: the whole platform relationship | Slow: contractual | Medium: exit and migrate |
| Foothold on a trusted desktop, once used | IT can wipe the host | Medium: may have spread | Uncertain | Low: the foothold may already have paid off |
| Data leaves the boundary (breach) | Anyone can disconnect | n/a to the damage | Irrelevant once out | Zero: cannot be un-happened |
| Unattributable transaction | Anyone can halt the flow | n/a to the damage | Too late once cleared | Zero: cannot be reversed at will |
| Governance residual | The board | n/a | Slowest | Lowest: funding undoes the least |

## What The Re-Expression Reveals

Read across the table and the shape of the deployment's exposure becomes legible in a way the original binary could not show. The top rows are the comfortable ones: a plug that is fast, surgical, and fully recoverable, exactly the kind an organisation wants for as many risks as possible. The middle row is the honest cost of depending on a platform: the plug exists, but it is slow and blunt, and pulling it means a migration. And the bottom rows are the ones that matter most, because they are the irreversible exposure of the whole arrangement, and they were previously hidden behind a blank.

## The Correction, Demonstrated

The most important thing this register does is prove the correction on live data. The original recorded a data breach, a used foothold, and an unattributable transaction as having no plug. That was the wrong description, and re-expressing it shows why. There is always a plug: you can disconnect the network, revoke the credential, shut the host down, halt the flow. What those rows lacked was never the off-switch. It was recoverability. Restating them as the plug exists, but recoverability is zero moves them from a frightening and slightly mysterious blank into a precise and ownable finding, and the finding points somewhere useful: because no plug helps after the event, these rows are governed by prevention and belong to the most senior acceptance in the organisation. The blank said stop looking. The corrected profile says here is exactly what to do.

```
   ORIGINAL BINARY                 CORRECTED FIVE-DIMENSION PROFILE
   plug: none  (breach)      -->   plug exists (disconnect), but
                                    recoverability = ZERO
   a frightening blank             an ownable finding: prevent, senior-accept
```

## Why This Is The Proof Piece

Every other piece in the series makes an argument. This one shows the argument already implemented. The register exists, the plugs are recorded, and the only work to reach the five-dimension profile is to drop the binary exists field, which is always true, and carry who, blast radius, speed, side effects, and recoverability instead. That closeness to the live data is also why this is the piece a partner could productise most directly: the scenario, the risks, and the plugs are already there, and the task is to render them in the profile the rest of the series has defined. It is the shortest path from the introduction to something a reader can see running.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not customer data.** The scenario is a product deployment example, shortened and illustrative, not any customer's register.
- **Not the whole register.** A representative span is shown to convey the range, not the full risk set.
- **Not a claim that the old register was wrong to build.** It was right; it simply described the hardest rows with a blank that the profile now fills.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Illustrative versus real | A worked example persuades only if it is honest about being an example, while still being recognisably real |
| Publishing a register versus exposure | Even a demo register reveals how an organisation thinks about its exposure, and the published version must be curated with that in mind |
| Re-expression versus re-work | Dropping the binary for the profile is small in principle and touches real stored data in practice, which is migration, not just wording |
