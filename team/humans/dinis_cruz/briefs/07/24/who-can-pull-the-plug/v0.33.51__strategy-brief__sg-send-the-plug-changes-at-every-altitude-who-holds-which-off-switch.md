## What This Is

The structure that turns the diffuse chain of blame into an owned map: **there is not one plug for a risk but a different plug at every altitude of the organisation, and each has its own profile, a database administrator kills a session, an engineer revokes a scoped credential, IT cuts a network segment, a chief operating officer exits the platform contract, a data protection officer withdraws processing consent, and at the top the board's only plug is to fund the fix, so the same incident is stopped by different hands with different reach, different speed, and above all different recoverability, since the lower plugs are fast and surgical while the higher ones are slow and blunt, and the board's lever, funding an adjustment, acts slowest of all and undoes the least; a healthy organisation knows which plug sits at which altitude, and that map is exactly what the fractal register produces, one register per accepting role, each holding the plug it can actually reach and a derived view of the plugs above and below, so that a risk which cannot be stopped at one level rolls up to the level that can, and the vague sense that someone somewhere can stop this becomes a specific, owned, testable structure.** This is piece 8 of the Who Can Pull The Plug series (cross-ref: the series-plan document, the accountability-gap piece, the v0.33.49 fractal-registers brief, and the recoverability piece). New contributions: **the plug modelled per altitude with its own profile, the observation that recoverability worsens as you climb, and the fractal register as the map that assigns each plug to the level that holds it.**

## One Risk, Many Plugs

The plug question implies a single switch, and for a real risk there is rarely just one. Consider an agent misbehaving against a production database. The database administrator can kill the running session. An engineer can revoke the agent's credential. IT can cut the network segment it operates on. The business unit can suspend the workflow that invokes it. The chief operating officer can exit the contract with the platform it runs on. The board can fund the project that removes the underlying exposure. Every one of these is a plug, they sit at different altitudes, and they are pulled by different hands for different reasons. The question is never simply who can pull the plug; it is which plug, at which altitude, with which profile.

## Each Altitude Has Its Own Profile

The five dimensions do not stay constant as you climb. The low plugs, the killed session, the revoked credential, are fast, surgical, and their damage is usually recoverable, because they stop a specific thing without wide collateral. The middle plugs, cutting a segment, exiting a contract, are slower and blunter, with larger blast radius and heavier side effects. And the high plug, the board funding a fix, is the slowest of all, because it acts through budget and time rather than through a switch, and it undoes the least, since money commissioned today does not un-happen what occurred yesterday. Reading the profile by altitude reveals an uncomfortable pattern: the faster, more surgical plugs are held low, and the authority to make the biggest decisions sits where the plug is slowest and weakest.

```
   ALTITUDE        THE PLUG                  SPEED     RECOVERABILITY
   DBA / engineer  kill session / revoke     fast      high (surgical)
   IT              cut the segment            medium    medium
   business unit   suspend the workflow       medium    medium
   COO             exit the contract          slow      low (blunt)
   board           fund the fix               slowest   lowest (undoes least)
```

## Recoverability Worsens As You Climb

This is the sharpest point in the altitude view and worth stating on its own. The higher the plug, the less it can undo. A killed session stops the harm in progress and usually leaves the world repairable. Funding a fix, the board's only real lever, changes the future and touches the past not at all. So an organisation that can only reach a risk with a high plug is an organisation that can only respond slowly and recover little, which is another way of saying that a risk with no low or middle plug is far more dangerous than its impact score alone suggests, because the only hand that can touch it is the one that can undo the least.

## The Fractal Register Is The Map

The map that assigns each plug to its altitude is exactly what the fractal register already produces. One register exists per accepting role, in that role's own language, holding the risks and plugs that role can actually reach, plus a derived view of the registers above and below. The database administrator's register holds the session kill; the board's holds the funding lever; each sees enough of the others to know where a risk goes when it cannot be stopped locally. A risk that has no plug at one altitude rolls up to the altitude that has one, and if it reaches the top with no plug below, that fact is itself the finding: the only available response is the slowest and weakest one. The chain of blame becomes a structure of ownership, and the vague reassurance that someone can stop this becomes a specific claim that can be checked.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a single plug per risk.** Real risks have several, at several altitudes, with different profiles.
- **Not a claim the top can fix anything.** The board's plug is the slowest and undoes the least, which is the point.
- **Not a new hierarchy.** The altitudes are the organisation's own; the register maps plugs onto them, it does not invent them.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Many plugs versus a clear owner | Multiple plugs per risk can revive the diffusion the accountability piece warns against unless each altitude's plug has a single named holder |
| Low plug preferred versus not always available | The fast surgical plug is best, but many risks genuinely have only a high, slow one, and the model must show that starkly |
| Mapping altitudes versus organisational reality | Real reporting lines are messier than a clean ladder, and forcing the map can distort as much as it clarifies |
