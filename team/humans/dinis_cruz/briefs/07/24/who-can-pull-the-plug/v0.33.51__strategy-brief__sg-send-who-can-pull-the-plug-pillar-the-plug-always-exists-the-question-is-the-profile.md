## What This Is

The one question every board understands in a sentence and almost no organisation can answer for its agents: **who can pull the plug, and the honest starting point is that the plug almost always exists, because you can always stop a system, end the process, revoke the credential, cut the power, take the site down, halt the line, so the question is never whether an off-switch exists but five things about using it, who has both the authority and the capability to pull it, how large the blast radius is when they do, how fast it can be pulled against how fast the damage spreads, what side effects the stop itself causes, and how much of the damage can be recovered afterwards, with that last dimension the one that decides everything, because some damage is irreversible at any price; the reason this matters now rather than five years ago is that organisations deferred the underlying questions for a long time and survived only because consequences moved slowly enough to manage, and autonomous agents remove that grace period by acting hundreds of times in the gaps between human checkpoints; and the reason it is the right way to meet RiskMandate is that answering it fully is the product, since a stated plug profile is an owner, an interval, an acceptance, and a place on the register, while RiskMandate itself never pulls the plug, because it is never in line, it tells you who can, how fast, at what cost, and whether any of it can be undone.** This is the pillar of the Who Can Pull The Plug series (cross-ref: the series-plan document, the v0.33.50 core-primitives brief, the v0.33.49 interval-ladder brief, and the v0.33.40 RAMM brief). New contributions: **the question posed as an introduction to RiskMandate, the plug-always-exists framing, and the five-dimension profile as the real content of the question.**

## Ask It About One Agent

Pick a single agent already running in production, one that can change something that matters, a coding agent with write access to a repository and a cloud account, a support agent that can issue refunds, a procurement agent that can place orders. Now ask the plain question: if this agent starts doing something no one intended, who stops it, how fast, and what does stopping it cost? In most organisations the sentence that follows is a pause. The model owner assumes the platform team has it. The platform team assumes the business unit. The business unit assumes somebody in IT holds a kill switch. Often nobody does, and the more precisely the question is asked, the longer the pause gets.

## The Plug Almost Always Exists

It is tempting to conclude from that pause that there is no plug, and that is the wrong conclusion. There is nearly always a way to stop a system: kill the process, revoke the token, disconnect the network, power down the host, pull the deployment, halt the line. Assume the plug exists. The information that actually matters is not its existence but its profile, and the profile is where every uncomfortable truth hides.

## The Five Questions That Are The Real Question

"Who can pull the plug" unfolds into five, and a mature organisation can answer all five for every consequential agent it runs.

```
   who ........... who has BOTH the authority and the capability to pull it
   blast radius .. what else goes down when they do
   speed ......... how fast can they pull it  vs  how fast the damage spreads
   side effects .. what the stop itself breaks
   recoverability  how much comes back afterwards   <-- the one that decides
```

Each is a fact, not an opinion. Who is a name with a real ability attached, not a title on an org chart. Blast radius is measured, because the off-switch for one agent is often the power switch for the whole website. Speed is a race against how fast the harm moves. Side effects are the lost transactions and broken sessions the stop causes. And recoverability is the amount of the damage that can be undone once the plug is pulled, which for a whole class of risk is none.

## Recoverability Is The One That Decides

Four of the five describe stopping. The fifth describes what stopping cannot fix, and it should be read first. Most risk is quietly recoverable: you stop the thing, clean up, restore from backup, refund the customer, and with enough time and money the state is repaired. A serious class of risk is not. A data breach cannot be un-happened once the data is outside the boundary. Destroyed data with no clean backup does not come back. A real-world action, an environmental release, a physical safety event, cannot be recalled. For those, all of the plug's value is spent before the event, because after it, pulling the plug changes nothing that already happened. A risk that is catastrophic but fully recoverable and a risk that is smaller but irreversible are different objects, and treating them as the same because their impact scores match is the most common and most expensive error in the whole discipline.

## Why The Question Bites Now

Organisations have owed answers to the data, access, and accountability questions for years, and most paid the debt late or never. They got away with it because the consequences of a missing control arrived slowly enough to manage by hand. Agents end that. When a system can take hundreds of actions in the seconds it takes a human to notice one, the gap between having a policy and having a working control is visible immediately, and the slow-consequence cushion that hid every missing plug and every unknown recoverability is gone. The question was always fair. Agents are what make leaving it unanswered dangerous.

## What This Has To Do With RiskMandate

The question is the product, seen from the outside. A complete answer, who pulls it, the blast radius, the speed, the side effects, and the recoverability, stated for a real risk and owned by a real person, is exactly an acceptance on a register: a named owner, a committed response, an interval, and the evidence behind it. RiskMandate is the graph-native workflow that produces and carries those answers. It is not the thing that pulls the plug, because it is never in the request path and makes no runtime decisions. It is the thing that makes the plug profile legible, keeps it owned, and rates whether the plug actually works. The rest of this series takes the five questions one at a time, and it ends where the answers live, on a register you can compute rather than a claim you have to trust.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a claim that plugs are missing.** The plug almost always exists; its profile is the content.
- **Not a scare.** The five questions are descriptions of reality, and recoverability is a fact about the world.
- **Not a kill switch pitch.** RiskMandate is never in line; it makes the plug legible, it does not pull it.
- **Not the whole answer.** This piece poses the question; the series answers it dimension by dimension.
