# We Do Not Pull The Plug. We Tell You Who Can, How Fast, And What It Costs.

## What This Is

The correction the whole series depends on: **asking who can pull the plug invites the assumption that we are the plug, and we are not, because RiskMandate is never in the request path and makes no runtime decisions, so it does not stop agents, revoke tokens, or block actions, and the moment it tried it would become one more brittle enforcement point competing with an entire industry that already builds them; what it does instead is the job nobody else is doing, which is to state the plug profile for every consequential agent, who holds the off-switch, how large the blast radius, how fast it can be pulled, what side effects it causes, and how much can be recovered afterwards, to keep that profile owned by a named person, and to rate whether the plug actually works, treating the isolation platform, the identity revoke, the enforcement gateway, the kill switch, as controls whose value is real only if they do what they claim; this is why never in line is not a limitation to apologise for but the position itself, because the enforcers are many and interchangeable while the thing that knows who can stop what, how well, and at what cost is missing, and that missing thing is the product.** This is piece 4 of the Who Can Pull The Plug series (cross-ref: the series-plan document, the pillar, the v0.33.50 core-primitives brief, and the v0.33.50 investor-response brief). New contributions: **the never-in-line position stated for a public audience, the plug as someone else's tool that we rate as a conditional control, and the argument that legibility, not enforcement, is the unmet need.**

## The Assumption To Correct First

Every reader who takes the plug question seriously arrives at the same next thought: so you are the kill switch. It is a natural inference and it is wrong, and leaving it uncorrected would misrepresent the entire product, which is why this piece comes early rather than late. RiskMandate does not pull the plug. It is never in the path where an agent's action is allowed or denied, it makes no decision at runtime, and it holds no switch. If that sounds like less than expected, it is actually the harder and more valuable half of the problem.

## Why We Stay Out Of The Path

Being in line, adjudicating each action as it happens, is a specific and crowded business. It requires deep integration with every system an agent touches, it is different in every customer, and it puts a brittle decision point in the middle of production traffic. An entire industry already builds these enforcement points, and they will succeed at it to varying degrees. Joining them would mean competing on their ground with their weapons, and it would mean abandoning the thing that is actually scarce. The decision to stay out of the path is not caution, it is a choice about where the unmet need is.

## What We Do Instead

We make the plug profile legible and keep it owned. For each consequential agent, RiskMandate carries the answer to the five questions the rest of this series poses, and attaches that answer to a named accountable person and a place on the register. The value is not in stopping the agent, it is in the fact that, before anything goes wrong, the organisation can already say who would stop this one, how fast, at what cost, and whether the damage could be undone. That is the difference between an organisation that discovers its plug profile during the incident and one that decided it in advance.

## The Plug Is Usually Someone Else's Tool

The off-switch itself almost always belongs to another system: the isolation platform that kills a session, the identity provider that revokes a credential, the enforcement gateway that blocks a tool call, the platform kill switch that halts an agent. RiskMandate does not replace any of them. It models each as a control, and it rates that control the way it rates any other: the plug reduces the risk, conditional on the plug doing what it claims to do. If the enforcement gateway only covers connection-level access and the agent has another path, the plug is weaker than it looks, and the register should say so. Rating the plug is a different and more honest job than being the plug.

```
   THE ENFORCERS (someone else's tools)      RISKMANDATE (never in line)
   isolation platform  -> kills a session     names WHO holds each plug
   identity provider   -> revokes a token     measures blast radius, speed
   enforcement gateway -> blocks a call        rates whether the plug WORKS
   platform kill switch-> halts the agent      keeps it owned on the register
                                               conditional_on: works only if
                                               the tool does what it claims
```

## Legibility Is The Scarce Thing

The market has many ways to stop an agent and almost no way to know, in advance and across the whole estate, who can stop which agent, how fast, and whether the stop would even help. That legibility is what is missing, and it is missing precisely because it is not an enforcement feature that any one tool can ship, it is a cross-cutting, owned, evidenced picture that sits above all the enforcers. Building it does not require being in the path. It requires consuming what the enforcers and the estate already expose and turning it into a profile someone is accountable for. That is a larger job than holding a switch, and it is the one worth doing.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a kill switch.** No runtime decisions, no enforcement point, no switch held.
- **Not a competitor to the enforcers.** They are rated inside our register, not replaced by it.
- **Not passive.** Making the plug profile legible and owned in advance is active work with a real output.
- **Not a claim the plug does not matter.** It matters greatly; we make sure someone owns it and knows if it works.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Never in line versus the buyer's reflex | A buyer under pressure wants the thing that stops the agent, and a tool that explicitly will not stop it has to sell the harder value of legibility |
| Rating the plug versus verifying it | Saying a plug works, conditional on it working, is only as good as the evidence that it does, which has to come from somewhere |
| Legibility versus urgency | In the moment of an incident, a picture is less satisfying than a switch, even when the picture is what prevents the next incident |
