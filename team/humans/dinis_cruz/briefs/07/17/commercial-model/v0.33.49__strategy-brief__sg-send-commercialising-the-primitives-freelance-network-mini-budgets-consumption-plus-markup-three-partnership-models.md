# Commercialising The Primitives: The Freelance Network Turned Inward, Mini Budgets, Consumption Plus Markup, And Three Partnership Models

**version** v0.33.49
**date** 17 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Commercial, Strategy, Product, Operations

**type** Strategy brief

*Eighth of 17 July, a go-to-market memo turning the freelance model onto our own primitives. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

---

## What This Is

A go-to-market plan for the primitives the team has already built, using the freelance network as the mechanism: **the core primitives have reached a level of maturity that justifies pushing them out as standalone tools rather than holding them as internal components, and the way to do that is to bring in freelancers for go-to-market and commercialisation, taking a budget and issuing mini projects with mini budgets against it; the deeper motive is capability, because the team needs to learn how to actually ship products and run commercial products that charge on demand, with the scalability and billing capabilities that the other projects will require, so this is a way of building the commercial muscle the whole portfolio depends on; the target is profitability as fast as possible, defined concretely as revenue that at least covers the team and the tokens consumed around it, which is a break-even bar rather than a growth target; the commercial model starts as on-demand consumption cost with a markup on top, in the shape of what OpenRouter does, with a set of small billable units attached to isolation, privacy, implementation of the solution, and help deploying into the customer's own environment; the opportunity is amplified by the pace of the latest models and by how many people have never experienced things the team treats as routine, such as generated infographics or transcription, so a large surface of mini tools already exists and needs partners rather than more building; and three partnership models are on the table, freelancers hired and paid directly, joint revenue distribution where both sides take a cut for a period, and venture creation with actual companies and equity, with the whole thing framed as a strong learning opportunity for someone with time who wants to understand how to ship products and build with Gen AI.** It is the eighth brief of 17 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.49 consulting-model brief, the v0.33.49 open-source brief, the v0.33.42 commercial-model brief, the v0.33.44 evidence-packs brief, and the v0.33.46 ai-in-defence brief). New contributions: **the freelance network turned inward onto our own primitives, mini projects against mini budgets as the unit of go-to-market work, break-even defined as covering the team plus the tokens, consumption plus markup as the starting commercial model, billable units around isolation, privacy, implementation, and customer-environment deployment, the untapped mini-tools surface, and the three-model partnership ladder from direct hire through revenue share to equity.**

## The Primitives Are Ready To Leave The Building

The trigger is maturity, not necessity. The project lead: **"now that we have a good set of products, and we started to use the freelance network effectively."** The primitives built to serve the platform have reached a point where they can stand alone. The project lead: **"the commercialization of some of the core primitives that we have created, that I believe have now reached a good level of maturity, that we should be pushing them as standalone tools."** This is the open-source thesis from earlier today running in the other direction: if uncommercialised technology stays available to the team, then at some point some of it should be taken out and sold, and the judgement being made here is that the moment has arrived for a subset of it.

## Shipping Capability Is The Real Product

The stated goal is not only revenue from these tools. It is learning to do the thing the whole portfolio will need. The project lead: **"really start to learn how to actually ship products and how to actually have commercial products that charge on demand, that have that sort of scalability, that sort of billing capabilities that I actually need for my other projects."** So the primitives are the training ground, and the durable output is a working commercial machine, on-demand charging, metering, and billing, that can then be pointed at anything else the portfolio produces. That reframes the exercise: even a modest revenue result leaves behind the capability, which is the part the other ventures cannot proceed without.

## Mini Projects, Mini Budgets

The delivery unit mirrors the consulting model from earlier today, but pointed inward at our own go-to-market rather than outward at a client. The project lead: **"find similar sort of on-demand freelancers, take a budget, and then start to issue kind of mini projects and mini budgets."** Small, bounded, individually funded pieces of work keep the commitment low and the feedback fast, which is the same zero-based logic that made the client-facing model attractive: nothing is committed beyond the block in flight, and a piece of work that is not landing can stop after very little spend.

## Break-Even: Cover The Team And The Tokens

The financial target is stated plainly and modestly. The project lead: **"the idea is to reach profitability as fast as possible, and the logic would be to get to the point where we can generate revenue from these on-demand practices in a way that at least covers the team and the tokens used around it."** Covering the team and the token spend is a survival bar rather than an ambition, and it is the right bar for this stage, because a portfolio that pays for its own compute and people has bought itself unlimited time to find the larger business.

## The Commercial Model: Consumption Plus Markup

The starting model is deliberately borrowed from something already proven in the market. The project lead: **"the commercial model here to start with is fundamentally the sort of on-demand costs on top of markup that we provide, so very similar to kind of what OpenRouter does."** The reference point is a marketplace take-rate on consumption rather than a subscription or a seat licence, which suits tools that are used in bursts and by people who will not sign a contract to try something. It is worth being precise about the reference, because the mechanism differs from the shorthand: OpenRouter passes provider token rates through untouched and takes its fee on credits purchased rather than marking up each inference. That distinction matters if this model is adopted literally, since a take-rate on funds loaded and a margin on each unit consumed produce different revenue curves and very different customer perceptions of fairness.

## The Billable Units

Consumption alone is thin, so the memo attaches paid units to the things customers actually worry about. The project lead: **"a set of billable units that are sort of related to isolation, to privacy, to basically implementation of the solution, and or even help to deploy on their environments."** Each of those is a place where a customer will pay beyond raw usage, and notably they are the same properties the vault architecture already provides, isolation and privacy being structural features of the design rather than add-ons that must be built. Deployment into the customer's own environment is the fourth, and it is the one that turns a tool into an engagement.

```
   BASE            on-demand consumption + markup      (the meter)
   PLUS UNITS      isolation                            (structural: vaults)
                   privacy                              (structural: PKI)
                   implementation of the solution       (service)
                   deploy into their environment        (service)
```

## The Mini Tools Nobody Has Tried Yet

The market read is that the team is further ahead than it realises, and that ordinary capabilities are still novel to most people. The project lead: **"leverage the fact that there is lots of people who have not even experienced the creation of infographics that you can get from ChatGPT, and the transcriptions, for example, that I have added; there is lots and lots of mini tools that we have."** The bottleneck is therefore not invention. The project lead: **"the logic here is to find the partners."** That is the whole shape of this brief: the tools exist, the models keep getting faster, and what is missing is people to carry them to market.

## Three Partnership Models

The memo lays out three ways to bring someone in, which form a ladder of increasing commitment on both sides. The project lead: **"there is the model of on-demand freelancers that I hire and pay directly."** Then a shared-upside arrangement. The project lead: **"joint revenue distribution, again for a period of time, so both of us get a cut of the revenue created."** Then full company formation. The project lead: **"venture creating companies around this, and then have shares and equity on it."** Each rung trades cash certainty for upside, and each implies a different relationship, a different legal structure, and a different answer to who owns what. The memo does not choose between them, which is appropriate at this stage, but selecting per person and per primitive is the operational question left open.

```
   RUNG 1   direct hire        pay cash per mini project      lowest commitment both ways
   RUNG 2   revenue share      both take a cut, time-boxed    shared upside, no entity
   RUNG 3   venture creation   a company, shares, equity      highest commitment both ways
```

## A Learning Opportunity, Honestly Framed

The last element is who this suits. The project lead: **"this is a great opportunity for somebody who wants to learn, somebody who has time, somebody who wants to get their head around how to ship products and how to develop things using Gen AI; I think this is a brilliant learning opportunity for them."** That is a real offer, because access to a mature set of primitives and a live commercialisation attempt is genuinely hard to get. It also needs care in how it is presented, since a learning opportunity paired with revenue share or equity rather than cash can read as unpaid work if the exchange is not made explicit, which is picked up in the tensions below.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a build phase.** The primitives exist; the missing element is partners and go-to-market, not more engineering.
- **Not a growth target.** The bar is covering the team and the tokens, which is break-even, not scale.
- **Not a single partnership model.** Direct hire, revenue share, and venture creation are all on the table.
- **Not a subscription business.** The starting model is on-demand consumption with a markup, plus discrete billable units.
- **Not separate from the platform.** The billable units, isolation and privacy, are properties the vault and PKI architecture already provides.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Three models versus focus | Running direct hire, revenue share, and equity in parallel multiplies legal, admin, and relationship overhead for a small team |
| Learning opportunity versus fair exchange | Offering upside instead of cash to people who want to learn can shade into unpaid work unless the exchange is explicit and honest |
| Thin markup versus covering the team | A take-rate on consumption is a thin margin; the billable units, not the meter, are what would actually cover people |
| Commercialising primitives versus core focus | Pushing primitives out as standalone tools competes for attention with RiskMandate itself, at the moment its MVP is being defined |
| Mini tools versus commodity | Infographics and transcription are cheap and widely available; the defensible part is isolation, privacy, and deployment, not the tool |
| Speed to break-even versus readiness | Pressure to cover costs quickly can push a primitive out before it is ready to be someone else's dependency |
| On-demand partners versus continuity | Freelancers engaged per mini project may not stay for the go-to-market they began, and go-to-market rewards persistence |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Which primitives ship first, and against what criteria? | Maturity, market readiness, and distance from the core platform |
| What is the markup, and on what base? | Whether the model is a margin per unit consumed or a take-rate on funds loaded, given they behave differently |
| How is the partnership model chosen per person and per primitive? | The rule for placing someone on rung one, two, or three |
| What is the actual break-even number? | The concrete monthly figure for team plus tokens that defines success |
| Who owns the IP under each model? | Particularly under revenue share and venture creation, and how it interacts with the open-source stance |
| Is this the same network as the Algarve consulting model? | Whether one freelancer pool serves both client delivery and our own go-to-market |
| What does onboarding look like for someone learning? | The path that makes the learning opportunity real rather than nominal |
