# What We Actually Sell: Your Risk Mandate, The Mandate To Operate, Delivered As A Vault

**version** v0.33.49
**date** 17 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Product, Architect, Board

**type** Strategy brief

*Second of three from one memo. Companions: the fractal-registers brief and the acceptance-interval brief, both 17 July. Offered as a proposal to be challenged, not a settled answer.*

---

## What This Is

An answer to the founding question of what the product actually is: **the question underneath everything is what we are selling and what we hand a customer, and the answer this memo proposes is that we give every part of an organisation its risk mandate, the mandate of risks that part is currently taking on behalf of the business, not because anyone chose to but because the risks already exist and somebody is carrying them whether or not it has been written down; the phrase that makes it click is that a risk mandate is a mandate to operate, since the thing a role receives from the business is permission to act along with the exposure that permission creates, and stating it explicitly turns an invisible liability into a document with a name and a clock on it; the register is then not the product but one of its outputs and one of its integration points, which is a useful demotion, because the mandate is what a person receives and the register is how it is presented, and the mandate also unifies something the corpus has been circling, that the authorization point and the risk point are the same object, the identities, the files, the capabilities, and the authenticated browser sessions we hand to a person or an agent are literally the mandate, so granting access and granting a risk mandate are one act described twice; the delivery follows from that, not a dashboard but a personal vault, because the vault already supports sending data to another vault using public and private keys, so with the recipient's vault identifier and public key, and the recipient configured to accept from us, a mandate can be delivered into someone's own sovereign vault rather than into a login on our system, and that feature exists today and needs operationalising rather than inventing; the consequence for the build is that the MVPs should re-centre on the individual role, which becomes the middle of everything; and the whole thing is offered to be challenged, because the strongest objection is obvious, that a mandate is a statement of what you are personally carrying, which nobody has ever asked to receive.** It is the second brief of 17 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.49 fractal-registers brief, the v0.33.45 operational-mandates brief, the v0.33.45 personalised-vaults brief, and the v0.33.42 commercial-model brief). New contributions: **the risk mandate as the answer to what we sell, the mandate-to-operate framing, the demotion of the register to an output and integration point of the mandate, the identification of the authorization grant with the mandate itself, and delivery by vault-to-vault rather than by dashboard.**

## The Question This Answers

The memo starts from the foundational question rather than a feature. The project lead: **"this goes down to the initial question of what are we selling in risk mandate, what are we actually providing to the customer?"** One honest answer is integration. The project lead: **"ultimately we are connecting stuff that exists with the existing risk register."** True, and insufficient, because it describes the plumbing rather than the thing the customer receives. The proposal here is that the thing they receive has a better name.

## The Answer: We Give People Their Risk Mandate

The move is to name the artifact after what it does. The project lead: **"maybe we give them a risk mandate, that is not a bad idea, because it is the mandate to operate."** A mandate to operate is exactly what a role has: permission to act, and the exposure that permission creates, which are the same grant seen from two sides. And the content is not aspirational, it is a statement of current reality. The project lead: **"what is the mandate of risks that he is currently taking for the business, because he has to accept."** The reason there is no choice in it is the no-deny principle. The project lead: **"we give each part of the organisation the current risk mandate that they receive from the business, that they are having to accept, because the risks exist."** So a mandate is not a request to take on risk; it is a description of the risk a role is already carrying, made explicit so it can be owned, priced, and time-boxed. Answering the product question that way is unusually clean. The project lead: **"then we answer the question, what do we give to the organisation? We basically give risk mandates left, right, and centre."**

## The Register Becomes An Output, Not The Product

This reorders things we have been treating as central. The project lead: **"the risk register is one of the outputs, or one of the integration points, for the risk mandate."** That demotion is useful rather than diminishing. The register is how a mandate is presented and how it meets an organisation's existing GRC tooling, so it remains the integration surface with everything that exists, and it stays the fractal structure the companion brief describes. But the thing a person receives is the mandate. The register answers what risks exist here; the mandate answers what you are carrying, which is the question people actually have and the one nobody currently answers.

## The Grant Is The Mandate

The idea that ties the corpus together is that the authorization point and the mandate are the same object. The project lead: **"the risk mandate consolidates that idea that the authorization point is the identities, or the files, or the capabilities, or the authenticated browser pages that we give to an agent, so fundamentally those are the risk mandates that we give to the individuals."** Every grant is a mandate: an identity handed to a person, a file shared, a capability enabled, an authenticated session handed to an agent. Each one is simultaneously permission to act and an assumption of exposure, and the corpus has hit this from several directions already, permission-grant-as-acceptance at the OAuth click, the closure as the blast radius, the token scope as the whole argument in the isolation case. Naming it the mandate collapses those into one primitive: to grant is to mandate, and to mandate is to accept.

## Delivery: A Vault, Not A Dashboard

If a mandate is a personal instrument, it should not live behind a login on our system. The project lead: **"what we provide is this sort of risk mandate, not just dashboards, but mini vaults, and then they are delivered through these vaults."** And the transport already exists. The project lead: **"the vault already has support for sending data to another vault, using public and private keys, if I give the public key of another vault and the ID of the vault, it is possible to send a message to the other vault, as long as the other vault is also configured to receive messages from this particular vault, so that feature already exists."** So delivery is a solved problem waiting to be applied. The project lead: **"what we need is to operationalise this."** The properties this inherits are the ones the sovereignty argument has been claiming: the mandate arrives in the recipient's own vault, versioned, portable, and theirs, which means their mandate survives us, which is exactly the anti-lock-in proof the sprawl-fatigued buyer wants and almost no vendor can offer.

```
   RISKMANDATE VAULT                          RECIPIENT'S VAULT
   (the org's register)                       (the role's mandate)

     mandate for role X   --- public key --->   arrives, versioned
     mandate for role Y       + vault ID        owned by them
     mandate for role Z       + accept rule     survives us

   the transport exists; what is missing is the operationalisation
```

## Re-centre The MVP On The Role

The build consequence is concrete. The project lead: **"we change some of the MVPs that we have been doing to now be very focused on an individual, so the individual, his individual role becomes the centre of everything."** Today's demos centre on a scenario or a register; this re-centres them on a person opening their own vault and seeing what they are carrying, in their language, with the path lit to the board. That is also a better first meeting than any dashboard, because it answers a question the person in the room actually has about themselves.

## The Objection, Stated Fairly

The memo asks for this to be challenged, and it deserves the strongest version of the objection. The project lead: **"it is worth exploring this and exploring from the multiple angles and challenge it."** So: a mandate is a statement of what you are personally carrying, and nobody has ever asked to receive one. Every incentive in a large organisation pushes toward not knowing, because knowing converts a vague background exposure into a documented, dated, named one, and the moment it exists the person cannot claim they were unaware. That is precisely the point of the product and precisely why it may be resisted, which means the value has to be framed as protection rather than exposure: an undocumented mandate does not mean you are not carrying it, it means you are carrying it without the ability to hand it up, argue it down, or fund the work to reduce it. The companion interval brief sharpens this, since a risk not accepted above you is one you are personally holding right now, which is bad for the business and worse for you. The mandate is what lets someone say, in writing, this is what I hold, here is what I need, and here is who else signed.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a settled answer.** It is proposed as the product thesis and explicitly offered to be challenged.
- **Not a replacement for the register.** The register remains an output and the integration point with what exists.
- **Not a dashboard.** The mandate is delivered into the recipient's own vault, which they keep.
- **Not a new grant of risk.** A mandate describes what a role is already carrying, because the risks already exist.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Receiving a mandate versus wanting one | Nobody asks to be handed a documented personal liability, which is both the value and the resistance |
| Mandate versus register naming | Two words for adjacent things will confuse buyers unless the distinction is stated crisply every time |
| A vault per role versus operational load | Sovereign delivery is the trust argument and multiplies the vaults to be provisioned and kept in sync |
| Protection versus exposure framing | The mandate protects the individual and also removes their deniability, and both are true at once |
| Explicit mandate versus legal weight | Making personal accountability explicit is the goal, and it may attract scrutiny nobody has invited |

## Open Questions

| How does a mandate meet an existing GRC register? | The integration seam where the mandate's register output lands in what the customer already runs |
