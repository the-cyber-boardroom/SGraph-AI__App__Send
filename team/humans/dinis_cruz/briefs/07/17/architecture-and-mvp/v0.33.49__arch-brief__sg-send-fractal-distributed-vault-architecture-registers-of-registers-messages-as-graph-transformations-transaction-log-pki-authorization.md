# The Fractal, Distributed Vault Architecture: Registers Of Registers, Messages As Graph Transformations, A Transaction Log, And PKI Authorization

**date** 17 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Engineering, Architecture, Security, Product

*Seventh of 17 July, mapping how the pieces deploy and fit together. The messaging protocol and the PKI layer are each flagged for their own dedicated brief. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

## What This Is

The system architecture for RiskMandate, stated as a fractal, distributed, file-based graph: **every risk mandate is a vault, so every player has one, and vaults can hold a single exec or several, or split apart, which is the fractal element, while the distributed element is that the whole thing runs on a file-based store, IPFS, that is distributed by design and open source, so the deployment can be a vault of vaults or start as small as a folder of folders anywhere in the organisation and grow organically; registers compose into registers of registers, Lego-fashion, each with a scope bound to the business assets and to the roles and responsibilities it covers, and each with defined inputs and outputs, so a department register is a combination of the individual registers beneath it; the dangerous case the architecture is built to expose is the risk a stakeholder holds but has not passed to an upstream owner, because that stakeholder is left holding the bucket and held accountable, when accountability should flow all the way to the top; the registers do not share a database, they talk, through a messaging protocol that is a JSON schema, where a risk-register message is itself a transformation, the command that changes the graph, a new asset, a new risk, a risk accepted or awaiting acceptance, data changed, and taken to its natural extreme every action on a register and on the issues that underpin it is one of these messages, which turns the system into an event stream that is eventually consistent, because risks change constantly and the dots connect over time, and because every message is stored on the file system the by-product is a transaction log that gives provenance, determinism, explainability, and resilience, and a clean input and output that LLMs can parse and that serves even the MVPs; and access is settled not by complex rules but by cryptography, a PKI in which the public and private keys are what let a party read, write, and communicate across vaults, so a vault can be shared widely yet stay unreadable without the key, while selected metadata can be left lightly encrypted or symmetric-key encrypted so that monitoring and anomaly detection, such as a key touching data it should not, remain possible.** It is the seventh brief of 17 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.49 fractal-registers brief, the v0.33.48 browser-local-databases brief, the v0.33.48 fractal-semantic-graphs brief, the v0.33.46 who-holds-the-bag brief, and the v0.33.47 where-authorization-should-live brief). New contributions: **every risk mandate modelled as a vault on a distributed file-based store, registers-of-registers composition with scope bound to assets and responsibilities, the unforwarded-risk-as-holding-the-bucket danger, the risk-register message as a graph transformation and the everything-is-a-message event log that yields provenance and eventual consistency, messages as the LLM-parseable input and output of the system, and PKI as authorization by cryptography rather than by complex rules that backfire.**

## Every Risk Mandate Is A Vault

The architecture starts from one identity that collapses several earlier ideas into a unit of deployment. The project lead: **"every risk mandate, if you think about it, is a vault, so every player has a vault."** That is the atom. The flexibility on top of the atom is what makes the model fractal, because a vault is not tied to exactly one person. The project lead: **"we can have more than one exec in the same vault, so theoretically we can have all of them in the same vault or all of them separate."** The same content structure holds whether the deployment is one vault per player, one vault for a whole team, or a single vault for everyone, which is what lets the topology be chosen rather than baked in. Underneath it, the movement of data between these vaults is the real subject. The project lead: **"the tech solution is implemented based on the idea that data is provided between the multiple vaults."**

## Registers Of Registers

Because a register is a unit that composes, the same shape appears at every level of the organisation. The project lead: **"you can have risk registers of risk registers."** Each register is anchored, not free-floating. The project lead: **"the scope of the risk register should be connected to the business assets and to the roles and responsibilities of that individual, or that department."** Beyond scope, a register is defined by what flows through it. The project lead: **"then it is about the inputs and the outputs, the mapping, what inputs you are going to get and what outputs you are going to be providing."** That input-output discipline is what makes composition mechanical rather than bespoke. The project lead: **"it is kind of like a Lego structure where one feeds to the other."** And composition is what lets the system be adopted incrementally. The project lead: **"the graphs can start small, can start anywhere in the organisation, and then grow from it."**

## Accountability Flows Up, Or Someone Holds The Bucket

The architecture is built to surface one specific failure, the risk that is real but has gone nowhere. The project lead: **"when you have a risk that a stakeholder has that has not been given to an upstream risk owner, they are the ones holding the bucket, the ones held accountable."** In most organisations that outcome is silent and accidental, and the memo treats it as the thing to fix. The project lead: **"the accountability has to flow all the way to the top."** Registers of registers plus upward messaging is precisely the mechanism that makes an unforwarded risk visible instead of letting it sit with whoever happened to notice it. This is the who-holds-the-bag problem from the earlier corpus, now given a structural answer rather than only a name.

## File-Based And Distributed

The substrate is deliberately plain, which is what buys the distribution. The project lead: **"it is all a file-based system, and it is using IPFS, which is also by design distributed."** Nothing here depends on a central server; the store is content-addressed and spreads by its nature, so the deployment shapes described above, a vault of vaults or a folder of folders, are all just arrangements of files. And the whole stack stays adaptable because it is not proprietary. The project lead: **"it is all open source, so we can be quite flexible with this."** This is the same vault-as-source-of-truth, no-backend posture the browser-local-databases work established, extended outward into a distributed, multi-vault setting.

## The Messaging Protocol: Registers Talk In Transformations

Registers do not read each other's storage; they exchange messages, and the memo flags this as worth its own document. The project lead: **"the communication between the risk registers should be done using a specific messaging protocol, which fundamentally is like a JSON file, like a schema, a message that should go from one risk register to the other, in some cases from vault to vault if you are crossing vaults."** The message is not a notification about a change, it is the change. The project lead: **"the risk-register message ultimately is the acceptance, the non-acceptance, the mapping of the asset; you can think of it as the command, the action that is transforming the graph."** Modelling the write path as commands that transform a graph is a clean event-sourcing stance, and it is deliberate.

```
   VAULT A                           VAULT B
   register-a  --- msg (JSON) --->   register-b
      |          transformation         |
      |          (accept / new risk /   |
      |           asset / data change)  |
      v                                 v
   every message is appended to the TRANSACTION LOG
                     |
                     v
        distributed file store (IPFS, open source)
        => provenance, determinism, replay, eventual consistency
```

## Everything Is A Message: The Transaction Log

Pushed to its natural conclusion, the messaging protocol stops being one feature and becomes the whole write model. The project lead: **"you can go to an extreme and say that every action on the risk register, and on the issues that underpin it, is created by such a message, and the message is fundamentally a transformation."** A system whose every mutation is an ordered message is an event stream, with the consistency properties that implies. The project lead: **"the system itself eventually will be consistent, because risks are changing all the time; it is a matter of eventually connecting the dots."** The valuable by-product is free. The project lead: **"as long as all these messages are stored in some file system, what we actually have is a transaction log, so every single transaction that has happened in the ecosystem is logged and captured."** From that log the guarantees follow. The project lead: **"this creates a hell of a provenance, and also determinism, explainability, but also a lot of resilience."** And the same stream is the natural interface for both agents and MVPs. The project lead: **"the way that two risk registers talk to each other is fundamentally through messages; that is how they add and remove and accept risks."** Because the messages are structured, an LLM can produce and parse them, which is what makes this a usable input and output for the earliest builds.

## PKI: Authorization By Cryptography, Not Complex Rules

The access model is the part the demo brief left open, and the answer is to make possession of a key, rather than evaluation of a rule, the thing that grants access. The project lead: **"you will need the public and private keys in order to write and read and communicate between multiple vaults."** This decouples sharing from readability, which is the property that makes wide distribution safe. The project lead: **"you can afford to give access to some vaults to some users, but unless they have the private key they will not be able to read or write to the vault."** The model is not all-or-nothing, because some layers can be left readable on purpose. The project lead: **"some parts of the metadata could be not as encrypted, or maybe encrypted just with a symmetric key, and that means you can also do monitoring, you can detect a key accessing data it is not supposed to access."** And the reason to prefer keys over a permission engine is stated plainly. The project lead: **"the private key is where we do authorization based on PKI, not on complex rules."** The project lead: **"you basically have authentication and authorization through cryptography, versus through complex rules that always tend to backfire."** This is the cryptographic instantiation of the earlier position that authorization should be removed from variable rules and made structural.

```
   share the vault widely             authorization = keys, not rules
   ----------------------             ----------------------------
   has vault, no private key   -->  cannot read or write
   has the private key         -->  can read, write, and message
   light / symmetric metadata  -->  monitoring and anomaly detection
                                    (e.g. a key touching data it should not)
```

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a shared central database.** Registers talk through messages; they do not read each other's storage.
- **Not rule-based authorization.** Access is granted by cryptographic keys, not by a complex permission engine.
- **Not strongly consistent.** The system is eventually consistent by design, because risks change constantly.
- **Not a fixed deployment topology.** Vault of vaults or folder of folders, one exec per vault or many, chosen not baked in.
- **Not closed.** The file store is IPFS and the stack is open source.
- **Not the final word on protocol or PKI.** Each is flagged as deserving its own dedicated brief.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Eventual consistency versus liability | At any instant two registers may disagree on whether a risk is accepted; where accountability is time-sensitive, that window is a real hazard, not just a technical nuance |
| Keys as authorization versus key management | Cryptographic authorization is clean, but rotation, revocation, delegation, and loss are hard; lose the private key and the vault is gone |
| Key-gated access versus the open demo | The demo shares a read-only vault for anyone to try; production gates on a private key; the two access models need an explicit reconciliation |
| Everything-is-a-message versus volume | Modelling every mutation as a stored message gives provenance but can explode in count and complexity; how far to take it is an unresolved dial the memo itself flags |
| Readable metadata versus confidentiality | Leaving metadata lightly or symmetric-key encrypted enables monitoring, but it is a deliberate information leak whose boundary must be defined |
| Accountability upward versus willingness | Making unforwarded risk flow to the top is the point, yet upstream owners may resist automatic accountability landing on them; a social obstacle, not a technical one |
| Total topology flexibility versus a decision | One vault or many, folder or vault, is powerful but postpones a choice that drives keys, messaging, and blast radius |
