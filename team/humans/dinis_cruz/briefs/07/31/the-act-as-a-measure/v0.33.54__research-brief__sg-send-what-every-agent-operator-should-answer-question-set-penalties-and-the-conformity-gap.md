## What This Is

The set of questions anyone running an agent should be able to answer, drawn from the instrument and deliberately separated from whether they are legally in scope: **the memo asks for the top articles and the top questions for anyone deploying, running or embedding an agent, and the useful finding is that the questions divide into three tiers by their legal status rather than by their importance, since a small number of obligations already bind everyone regardless of risk classification, a second set arrives for everyone on 2 August 2026, two days from now, and the largest set is gated to high-risk systems and was deferred to December 2027 by the amendment that came into force four days ago, yet the third set is where almost all the substantive engineering discipline lives and is worth answering whatever your classification; the memo also asks what breach actually costs, and the instrument is specific, with three tiers of administrative fine, up to thirty-five million euro or seven percent of worldwide turnover for prohibited practices, up to fifteen million or three percent for most operator obligations including those of deployers, and up to seven and a half million or one percent for supplying incorrect information, taking the higher of the two figures for most undertakings and, in an inversion that matters for smaller companies, the lower of the two for small and medium enterprises and start-ups, with the penalty framework already applicable, with non-monetary corrective measures including orders to withdraw a system from the market, and with the amendment leaving the fine tiers untouched; and the third finding answers the memo's question about whether to build a maturity model, because the official conformity route is real, is under construction and is not yet usable, since the European standards bodies have been developing harmonised standards under mandate since 2023, the quality management standard is the most advanced and closest to citation, and as of mid-2026 none of the deliverables grants presumption of conformity because none has yet been cited in the Official Journal, which leaves a genuine and time-limited gap between what buyers need to ask today and what the official machinery will eventually supply.** It is the ninth document of 31 July (cross-ref: the companion business-case brief, the v0.33.54 no-canonical-Act research brief, the v0.33.53 accepted-is-not-acceptable brief, the v0.33.51 plug-profile series plan, and the v0.33.52 outbound maturity model). ## Three Tiers, By Legal Status Not Importance

The single most useful organising insight is that the provisions relevant to an agent operator do not sit at one legal altitude, and conflating them is what makes the Act feel impenetrable.

```
   TIER 1  binds everyone, already in force
           prohibited practices, AI literacy

   TIER 2  binds everyone, from 2 August 2026  (two days away)
           transparency about interacting with, and content from, AI

   TIER 3  gated to high-risk, deferred to 2 December 2027
           risk management, data governance, documentation, logging,
           human oversight, accuracy and robustness, deployer duties,
           monitoring and incident reporting
           <- almost all the engineering substance lives here
```

The asymmetry is the point. Tier 3 is where the Act says what good practice actually looks like, and it is the tier most operators are currently exempt from. That is precisely the gap the companion brief argues should be closed voluntarily.

## The Question Set

Framed so that each is answerable from memory by the person running the system, without a lawyer, and each tied to the provision it comes from. This is offered as a working set for the assessment flow rather than as a legal checklist.

**Tier 1, already binding on everyone.**

| # | Question | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does your system do any of the things the instrument prohibits outright, such as social scoring, emotion inference in workplaces or education, or untargeted scraping of facial images? | Article 5 |
| 2 | Do the people operating and affected by this system have sufficient understanding of it to use it responsibly? | Article 4 |

**Tier 2, binding on everyone from 2 August 2026.**

| # | Question | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Do people know when they are interacting with an AI system rather than a person? | Article 50 |
| 4 | Is synthetic content it produces marked as machine-generated in a machine-readable way? | Article 50 |

**Tier 3, gated to high-risk, and worth answering regardless.**

| # | Question | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | Do you have a risk management process for this system that runs continuously rather than once, and can you say what level of residual risk you have judged acceptable? | Article 9 |
| 6 | Do you know what data it was trained, validated and tested on, and what you have done about bias in it? | Article 10 |
| 7 | Could you produce documentation of what this system is and how it works, today, without a project? | Article 11 |
| 8 | Does it keep logs automatically, and for how long, and could you reconstruct what it did last Tuesday? | Article 12 |
| 9 | Do the people using it have the information they need to use it correctly, including its limitations? | Article 13 |
| 10 | Can a human meaningfully oversee it, intervene in it, and stop it, and has anyone tested that? | Article 14 |
| 11 | Do you know its accuracy, its robustness under unexpected input, and its cybersecurity posture? | Article 15 |
| 12 | If you are running somebody else's system, do you use it according to their instructions, monitor it, and keep the logs you generate? | Article 26 |
| 13 | Do you monitor how it behaves after deployment, and would you know if it drifted? | Article 72 |
| 14 | If it caused serious harm, do you know who you would have to tell, and how quickly? | Article 73 |

Two features of that set are worth noting. Question 10 is the plug question the corpus has been developing independently, which is why the human oversight provision keeps recurring as the external anchor for that work. And question 5 carries the accepted-versus-acceptable distinction established this week, which is where the instrument mandates a judgement and declines to define its standard.

## What Breach Actually Costs

The memo asks about the implications, and the instrument is specific. Article 99 establishes three tiers of administrative fine.

| Tier | Applies to | Maximum |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prohibited practices under Article 5 | 35 million euro or 7 percent of total worldwide annual turnover |
| 2 | Most operator obligations, including providers, importers, distributors, deployers and the transparency provisions | 15 million euro or 3 percent |
| 3 | Supplying incorrect, incomplete or misleading information to authorities | 7.5 million euro or 1 percent |

Five details matter more than the headline numbers.

**The direction of the comparison flips for smaller companies.** For most undertakings the fine is the higher of the fixed sum or the percentage. For small and medium enterprises and start-ups it is the lower of the two, which is a statutory cap keyed to size rather than regulatory discretion, and it materially changes exposure for exactly the kind of company that assumes it cannot afford to care.

**Fines are not the only consequence.** Corrective measures include orders to bring a system into conformity, to withdraw it, or to remove it from the Union market. For a product business, withdrawal is the more serious outcome.

**The framework is already live.** Penalties have applied since August 2025 and the prohibited-practices tier since February 2025, with national market surveillance authorities gaining full investigation and sanction powers from 2 August 2026.

**The amendment did not touch it.** The Digital Omnibus deferred high-risk obligations and added new prohibited practices, and left the fine tiers and amounts unchanged.

**Documented compliance reduces exposure even after an infringement.** The instrument lists factors the authority must weigh, and cooperation, documentation and proactive disclosure sit among them, which means an organisation that can show its working is in a materially better position than one that cannot even when both were in breach.

One inconsistency in the public sources should be recorded: the third tier is reported by some as one percent and by others as one and a half percent, with at least one source explicitly correcting the higher figure. The operative text should be checked before this is used anywhere external.

## The Conformity Route Exists And Is Not Ready

This is the finding that answers the memo's question about building a maturity model, and it is more favourable than expected.

The Union did not intend the Act to be self-assessed against prose. Under Article 40, compliance with harmonised standards whose references are published in the Official Journal grants a presumption of conformity, which shifts the burden of proof onto authorities. The European standards bodies established a joint technical committee in 2021 and accepted a standardisation mandate in 2023, updated in 2025, to produce those standards in four areas: a trustworthiness framework, risk management, a quality management system, and conformity assessment.

The state of that work as of mid-2026:

- The quality management standard is the most advanced, at approval stage, and is expected to be cited first. Notably, the committee judged the existing international AI management standard insufficiently aligned with the Act's requirements and wrote a bespoke European standard rather than adopting it.
- The bias standard is much earlier, with a working draft consultation closed in April 2026.
- **None of the deliverables currently grants presumption of conformity, because none has been cited in the Official Journal.** Both conditions are required and neither is met.
- The Commission expects the first harmonised standards to be published during 2026, after which its own review begins before citation can follow.

Three consequences follow directly.

**There is a real gap and it is time-limited.** Buyers are asking questions now, the official instrument that would answer them authoritatively does not yet exist, and the high-risk obligations it supports have moved out sixteen months. Something usable in the interval has genuine value.

**Alignment to draft standards has real but limited value.** It builds readiness and audit evidence and confers no presumption, which is exactly the honest position a maturity model should occupy.

**The boundary is legally sharp.** Presumption of conformity is reserved to harmonised standards cited in the Official Journal. Any private assessment, ours included, must therefore be explicit that it measures maturity against the instrument's principles and confers no legal status whatsoever. That is not a disclaimer to bury; it is the difference between a defensible product and a misrepresentation.

## What Could Not Be Verified

Recorded so the gaps are visible. The question set is derived from the structure and subject matter of the named articles as described in secondary sources rather than from a clause-by-clause reading of the operative text, and should be checked against the amended instrument before external use. The Article 99 third-tier percentage is disputed between sources. The standardisation status is a snapshot from sources dated between late 2025 and June 2026 and may have moved. And whether the amendment altered any of the named articles beyond Article 10 and the transparency timing was not exhaustively checked.
