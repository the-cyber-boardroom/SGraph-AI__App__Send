# When The Business Downgrades Everything: External Anchors Are The Defence, And The Liability Attaches To Concealment Rather Than Acceptance

## What This Is

The case where the system is gamed not by non-participation but by participation in bad faith, with everything rated low: **the memo is candid that the model can be abused from the inside, and generous about who does it, since an executive downgrading risks may be poorly informed, may run a business genuinely in an exploratory phase where high appetite is correct, may simply not believe a category matters, or in the limit may be compromised, and the system's answer is not to prevent the downgrade but to make it visible and to anchor it to something the business does not control; two things happen below such an executive, either people disagree and it becomes an explicit difference of view that time will resolve, or they align with the more powerful voice, and the important property is that both outcomes are on the record; the move that defeats systematic downgrading is the meta-risk, because even when every individual rating has been reduced, a higher-order risk remains available, that the organisation does not have a healthy risk assessment, or that its ratings are inconsistent with how its industry operates, or that on the specific requirements of a named regulation it is not doing several things it is obliged to do; that last form is the powerful one, because an external standard is measurable in a way an internal opinion is not, so the business can still override it and say the exposure is low, but overriding a regulation's requirement is a visibly different act from disagreeing about a severity, and the override is itself recorded; the memo's own answer to the associated passive behaviour is the sharpest line in it, that accepting something for six months is always available and therefore not doing something becomes a measurable action; and the reported enforcement cases add something the memo does not claim, because the executives who have faced personal liability were pursued for concealment and for misleading public statements rather than for deciding to carry a risk, which means an explicit dated acceptance is closer to a defence than to an admission, and inverts the natural objection that writing it down creates evidence against yourself.** It is the twenty-first document of 31 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.54 design-for-non-cooperation brief, the v0.33.54 register-maintains-itself brief, the v0.33.53 accepted-is-not-acceptable brief, the v0.33.50 no-judgement brief, and the v0.33.54 decouple-in-scope brief). New contributions: **systematic downgrading treated as a distinct attack from non-participation, external standards identified as the anchor that survives downgrading, the meta-risk named as a recurring family with four instances now, not-doing recorded as a measurable action, revealed calibration as a track record accumulating over time, and the finding that reported liability attaches to concealment rather than to acceptance.**

## The Scenario, And Why It Is Not Always Wrong

The memo is unusually careful here and the care should be preserved, because it is what makes the position defensible. The project lead: **"the system by itself contains a built-in abuse mechanism."** And the range of people who do it. The project lead: **"execs who don't have a good understanding of risks, companies and execs that are at the phase where they're willing to take lots of risks, maybe startups, exploration."** Through to the limit case. The project lead: **"and even malicious or even compromised systems."**

Crucially, the memo does not treat high appetite as a failure. The project lead: **"this could be okay, there's scenarios where there was not a lot of data, we're flying dangerously, it's a high risk operation, so you accept it, because at the end of the day it's the cost of what they're doing."**

That matters for the product's posture. An organisation in an exploratory phase, taking large risks deliberately, is behaving correctly by the corpus's own road analogy. The system's job is not to stop it. The project lead names the two conditions that make it acceptable: **"it's important that over time the consequences are within the accepted thresholds, and the people impacted also have a voice."**

Both are testable. The first is the accepted-versus-acceptable distinction from earlier this week: an outcome outside the stated threshold is a fact, not an opinion. The second connects to the customer-voice idea from 23 July, and it is the one nobody currently implements.

## What Happens Below

The memo describes the two responses available to people working under a systematic downgrader, and observes that both leave a record. The project lead: **"you either have a scenario where the individuals in the company don't agree with their boss, which is a source of tension"**, or the alternative. The project lead: **"people sometimes would align themselves with the more powerful voice, but again, it's on the record."**

The first outcome is healthy and the memo says so. The project lead: **"the people below either go against the execs, which is exactly how it should be, because at the end of the day you're going to have different points of view, and over time one of them will be right and one will be wrong."**

That last clause is more interesting than it looks and is picked up below under calibration.

## The Meta-Risk Is The Move

Here is the mechanism that defeats systematic downgrading, and it is the most important idea in the memo. The project lead: **"even in those scenarios, you could still extract a risk to say you don't have a healthy risk assessment, or the current risk assessments are contradictory with the way the industry works."**

The logic is recursive. Individual ratings can be reduced by whoever has the authority to rate them. But the *pattern* of reductions is itself a fact about the organisation, and it can be raised as a risk at a higher altitude, where a different person holds the pen. An executive can downgrade a hundred risks. They cannot as easily downgrade the observation that they downgraded a hundred risks, because that observation belongs to a level above them.

**Downgrading defends against the register one entry at a time; the meta-risk operates on the pattern.**

## The External Anchor Is What Makes It Measurable

This is where the memo turns a rhetorical point into an operational one, and it connects the whole of this week's regulation work to the integrity of the register. The project lead: **"that can actually be measured, GDPR is a good example, so you're not doing this, you're not doing that, so in a way you are breaking GDPR compliance, so you have a high level risk to say we are not GDPR compliant."**

The distinction that matters: **an internal severity rating is an opinion, and an external requirement is not.** A business can rate its own exposure low. It cannot rate a regulation's obligation low, because the obligation is not its to set. What it can do is decline to meet it, which is a different and far more visible act.

The memo anticipates the response and it is the right one. The project lead: **"of course the business can turn around and say actually we don't think that's a big deal, we think that's a medium or a low, so I override it and I change it."**

That override is the point rather than a defeat. Somebody has to put their name on the statement that a specific regulatory obligation is not being met and that this is acceptable, with a date and an interval. Nothing prevents them. But it is a materially different artefact from a quietly adjusted severity score, and the difference is exactly what makes the anchor work.

This is also why the AI Act work of this week matters beyond compliance. An instrument supplies obligations the business did not write, which is precisely what an internally captured register lacks.

## A Family, Not A Trick

Worth naming, because this is now the fourth instance of the same pattern in the corpus and it has not been called a family before.

| Instance | The meta-risk |
|---|---|
| 23 Jul | You do not know how many agents you have |
| 28 Jul | You have never defined your acceptable level |
| 31 Jul | You have not assessed this provision at all |
| Today | Your risk assessment is not healthy |

The shape is consistent: **when a party will not engage with a risk, the non-engagement is itself a rateable risk at a higher altitude, with an owner and an interval.** It is recursion used as a defence mechanism, and it is available whenever the direct route is blocked. Naming it as a pattern makes it teachable rather than something rediscovered each time.

## Not Doing Something Is A Measurable Action

The sharpest line in the memo, and it generalises the anti-attrition property from the companion brief. The project lead: **"that's why the no deny and the kick it to the curb option here is to accept it for six months; that's why that works quite well, because suddenly not doing something is also a measurable action."**

The mechanism is worth stating plainly. Because there is no option to deny and no option to defer indefinitely, the person who wants to do nothing must still choose something: accept it, for a stated period, with their name on it. Doing nothing is not on the menu. So the passive behaviour that normally leaves no trace is converted into a dated record that says a named person considered this and chose to carry it for six months.

The memo also identifies where this behaviour concentrates. The project lead: **"these are scenarios where it's more likely that you have a passive aggressive approach to not want the data to be captured."** Which is consistent: an organisation systematically downgrading has the strongest reason to resist the mapping in the first place, since a good map makes the downgrades legible.

## What The Liability Cases Actually Show

The memo refers twice to fiduciary duty and to people knowing they will be held accountable. The reported cases support the general claim and contain a distinction the memo does not make, which strengthens the argument considerably.

The two landmark matters are well known. A former chief security officer at a ride-hailing company was convicted by a federal jury in October 2022 of obstructing a regulatory proceeding and misprision of felony, over the concealment of a 2016 breach affecting more than fifty million riders and six hundred thousand drivers, having arranged a hundred-thousand-dollar payment to the intruders characterised as a bug bounty and obtained non-disclosure agreements from them; he was sentenced in 2023 to three years of probation, community service and a fifty-thousand-dollar fine rather than the custodial term sought, and the conviction was upheld on appeal. Separately, in October 2023 the securities regulator charged the chief information security officer of a software company individually alongside the firm, reportedly the first time it had charged a cybersecurity executive personally, over allegedly false statements about security posture in public filings. Reporting from late 2025 indicates that much of that case was subsequently dismissed, with the court observing that post-incident conduct is easy to second-guess and requiring proof of intentional misleading, leaving claims focused on pre-incident public statements.

**The distinction that matters: in both matters the alleged wrong was concealment or misrepresentation, not the decision to carry a risk.** Neither executive was pursued for having accepted an exposure. One was convicted for hiding an incident; the other was charged over what the company said publicly about its posture.

That inverts the objection the register most often attracts, which is that writing a risk down creates evidence against you. On the reported record the opposite is closer to true. **The exposure lies in the risk that was known and undocumented, and in the public statement that did not match the internal reality.** A dated acceptance, made in good faith, with the information available recorded alongside it, is the artefact that demonstrates a decision was taken openly rather than buried. The court's reluctance to second-guess judgement made in good faith points the same way.

Two honest qualifications. This is a description of reported cases in one jurisdiction and not a general rule, and outcomes turn on their facts. And the professional reaction was not uniformly positive: the sentencing drew a large number of character references and a warning signed by around fifty practitioners about the chilling effect on security decision-making, with a former senior government official cautioning about the effect on recruitment. Personal liability changes behaviour in both directions, and some of that change is defensive rather than better.

## Revealed Calibration

An implication of the memo that deserves separating out. The project lead: **"if you have somebody crying wolf all the time, if you're in a place where there's a lot of wolves, that's great; if it's a place where there's no wolves, then you lose credibility."** And earlier. The project lead: **"over time one of them will be right and one will be wrong."**

Over a long enough period a register does not only record decisions; it records who was right. Somebody who consistently rated high in an environment where nothing happened is revealed as poorly calibrated. Somebody who consistently rated low in an environment where things did happen is revealed the same way in the other direction. Neither is visible in any single decision, and both become visible in the accumulated record.

That is the reference-class idea from the project manager brief applied to people rather than projects, and it is genuinely valuable, because calibration is currently invisible and therefore never improves. It is also the most dangerous artefact the system can produce, for the reason recorded in the mental health brief: a record that shows who was wrong becomes a blame instrument in the wrong hands, and its value depends entirely on being used to improve judgement rather than to allocate fault.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a claim that high appetite is wrong.** Deliberate risk-taking in an exploratory phase is legitimate and the system should not obstruct it.
- **Not a mechanism to prevent downgrading.** It cannot and should not; it makes the pattern visible and anchors it to something external.
- **Not legal advice.** The cases are described factually, in one jurisdiction, and turn on their own facts.
- **Not a claim that documentation eliminates exposure.** It changes its shape, and the reported cases concern concealment rather than acceptance.
- **Not a performance management tool.** Calibration data exists to improve judgement, and using it to allocate blame would destroy the system it came from.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| The meta-risk versus who accepts it | It relocates the decision one level up, and if the level above shares the appetite the recursion simply repeats until it runs out of altitude |
| External anchors versus their reach | A regulation supplies obligations for its own scope only, and much of what matters most sits outside any instrument |
| The register as a defence versus as evidence | The reported cases support the protective reading, and a documented acceptance is still a document, and the reading depends on facts nobody controls in advance |
| Personal liability as a corrective | It demonstrably changes behaviour and a substantial part of the profession warned it makes decisions more defensive rather than better |
| Revealed calibration versus its misuse | The most valuable long-term artefact and the one most likely to be turned into a blame instrument |
| Naming an unhealthy register | The meta-risk is accurate and it is also close to telling an executive their judgement is poor, which is rarely survivable in the room |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| What triggers the meta-risk automatically? | A downgrade rate, a divergence from an external anchor, or a pattern of overrides, computed rather than raised by a person |
| Which external anchors are available? | Regulations supply some, industry norms others, and the coverage gap is where downgrading remains unchecked |
| How is the people-impacted voice implemented? | Named as a condition of legitimacy and never built, and it is the harder of the two conditions |
| When does calibration data become usable? | The number of resolved decisions before a track record means anything |
| How is calibration protected from misuse? | The governance that keeps it a learning artefact rather than a performance record |
| Does the recursion terminate? | If every altitude shares the appetite, the meta-risk reaches the board and stops, and what happens then is undefined |
