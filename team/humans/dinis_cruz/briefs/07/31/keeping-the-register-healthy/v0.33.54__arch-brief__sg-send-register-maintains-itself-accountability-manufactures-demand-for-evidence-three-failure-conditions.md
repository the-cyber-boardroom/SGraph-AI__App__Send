# The Register Maintains Itself: Accountability Manufactures The Demand For Evidence, And The Three Conditions Under Which It Stops

**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Product, Strategy, Board, Engineering

*Fourteenth of 31 July. The answer to the most common question asked about the workflow. Research on accountability effects is grounded and cited, including findings that identify how the mechanism fails. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

## What This Is

The property that makes the acceptance workflow self-maintaining, which is the answer to the question the project lead is asked most often about it: **how does a risk register keep itself honest, resist gaming, and stop risk being quietly buried rather than passed upward, and the answer is that no separate policing mechanism is required because the accountability itself does the work; three primitives produce it, that every risk must be accepted because the risk already exists as a fact rather than as a proposal, that acceptance attaches to a named person, and that the decision is visible to that person's manager and to their manager in turn; from those, the decision surface collapses to two choices, how long to accept for and what action to take, which strips out the negotiation about whether a risk is real and leaves only questions the business is actually equipped to answer; three things are then on the mind of the person accepting, whether they have enough data to be comfortable being held accountable, whether their boss will be content with the decision when they review it, and whether the decision belongs to them at all or should be escalated, and the second of those is the engine, because a person who expects to be reviewed by someone whose reaction they cannot fully predict demands better evidence before deciding, which means the appetite for accurate data is created by the same mechanism that assigns the accountability and nobody has to fund a separate data quality programme; the psychological research supports this precisely, since accountability to an audience whose views are not known produces more effortful, self-critical, multidimensional thinking before the decision, and accountable professionals write more thorough justifications under exactly those conditions; and the same research names three conditions under which the mechanism reverses, when the reviewer's preference is guessable so the accountable person simply conforms to it, when the person is already committed to a prior position so effort goes into defending it rather than reconsidering, which is precisely what repeated acceptance at intervals invites, and when accountability broadens the appetite for information without improving discrimination about which information matters, which is an argument for the evidence layer doing the discrimination that the psychology says people will not do for themselves.** It is the fourteenth document of 31 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.46 risk-acceptance-redefined brief, the v0.33.49 interval-ladder brief, the v0.33.53 scenarios-are-the-MVP brief, the v0.33.53 accepted-is-not-acceptable brief, and the v0.33.50 no-judgement brief). New contributions: **the self-correction property stated as the answer to the gaming question, the two-decision surface, the three considerations on the accepting stakeholder's mind, the identification that accountability manufactures the demand for evidence as a by-product, and the three research-grounded failure conditions with re-acceptance identified as the most exposed.**

## The Question This Answers

It is the objection every experienced practitioner raises, and it deserves a direct answer. The project lead: **"one of the questions that I get asked quite a bit on the risk acceptance workflow is how does the system maintain itself, how does the self-controlling mechanism work, and how does the system make sure that it doesn't get gamed, or people don't abuse it, or risk doesn't propagate upstream."**

The reason the question is asked is that everybody has seen a risk register rot. Entries age, owners leave, severities drift downward without anyone deciding they should, and the whole artefact becomes a compliance exhibit nobody consults. The usual response is to add process: a review board, a quality function, an audit. Each adds cost and none addresses why the register decayed.

The answer here is structural rather than procedural. The project lead: **"the reason why that works very effectively is because what that does is that contains a built-in correction mechanism."**

## The Primitives That Produce It

Three, and the property emerges from their combination rather than from any one.

**The risk already exists.** Acceptance is not agreement that something might happen; it is acknowledgement of something already true. The project lead: **"they always have to accept the risk because the risk exists, so this is designed for fact-based risks that exist already; we might have a margin of error, but the risk exists."** That removes the most common evasion, which is to dispute whether a risk is real rather than to decide what to do about it.

**It attaches to a person.** Not a team, not a function.

**The decision is seen upward.** The manager reviews it, and so, in principle, does theirs.

## Only Two Decisions

The decision surface is deliberately narrow, and the narrowness is what makes the workflow tractable. The project lead: **"the only two main decisions are for how long you need to choose it, and what's the action that you want."**

The action set is small and each option is a real commitment rather than a deferral. The project lead: **"the action can be: you need more details, you want to fund it, depending on a particular urgency, or you want to assign it to a team, you want to fund it to a new team and a new project, or you want to review it, accept it again in a couple of months from now."**

```
   HOW LONG                     WHAT ACTION
   the interval ladder          need more detail    -> fund an investigation
   an hour to six months        fund the fix        -> fund a remediation
   the interval implies         assign to a team    -> give it an owner
   the committed response       re-accept later     -> a dated review
```

What has been removed matters as much as what remains. There is no option to deny the risk, no option to defer indefinitely, and no negotiation about severity as a route to inaction. Those are the three moves by which registers usually die.

## What Is Actually On The Stakeholder's Mind

The memo's most useful contribution is a plain psychological account of the moment of decision, and it names three considerations.

**Sufficiency.** The project lead: **"do I have enough data to make an accurate decision that I can be held accountable for, that I am happy to be held accountable for, based on what I know and based on what I don't know."** The phrasing matters: not whether the data is complete, but whether it is enough to defend a decision, including an honest account of the gaps.

**Review.** The project lead: **"will my boss, when he reviews my decision, be happy with it?"**

**Standing.** The project lead: **"the alternative is I cannot really make the decision, this needs to be my boss's decision."**

The second is the engine. The first produces care and the third produces escalation, but anticipating a reviewer whose reaction is not fully predictable is what converts care into a demand for evidence.

## Technical Confirms, Business Accepts

The memo draws the distinction cleanly and it is the same one established on 28 July under different names. The project lead: **"the stakeholder is more of a technical stakeholder to confirm; I agree and I confirm that this is the thing, but I'm not accepting the business risk of this."** And the separation. The project lead: **"that's the difference between a technical stakeholder and a business stakeholder; the business stakeholder has accountability, the technical stakeholder just confirms that it's actually correct and that it is working the way it's supposed to work."**

Confirmation is a factual act available to whoever is close to the system. Acceptance is a judgement about exposure available only to whoever can commit the organisation. Keeping them separate protects both: an engineer is not asked to carry a business consequence they cannot size, and an executive is not asked to vouch for a technical fact they cannot verify.

## The Property: Accountability Manufactures The Demand For Evidence

This is the mechanism, and it is worth stating as plainly as the memo does. The project lead: **"the person that is reviewing the risk is now very motivated to ask and to ensure that he has the right information, that the data is accurate, and that person is comfortable with their boss reviewing it, and of course their boss's boss reviewing it."** Producing the result. The project lead: **"you have this really strong chain of accountability and strong chain of demand for facts and demand for accurate information."**

The elegance is that **the appetite for good evidence is a by-product of assigning the accountability.** Most organisations run data quality as a separate programme, funded centrally, resented locally, and perpetually behind. Here nobody funds it, because the person who will be held to the decision becomes the person demanding the evidence, and they demand it at exactly the moment it is needed and about exactly the thing that matters to them.

```
   assign accountability to a named person
            |
            v
   they anticipate being reviewed
            |
            v
   they demand better evidence BEFORE deciding
            |
            v
   the evidence base improves as a side effect
            |
            v
   the register stays current because staleness is now somebody's exposure
```

This is the third time the corpus has reached for this idea, and the first time it has a mechanism. The no-judgement brief in July observed that the system governs itself because people notice when claims about their own work are wrong, and the collaboration brief in late July repeated it. Both were true and neither said why. The reason is that being reviewed by somebody you cannot fully predict is what makes you check your work.

## The Research Agrees, And Names Three Ways It Breaks

There is an extensive experimental literature on precisely this, and it is worth reading carefully because it validates the mechanism and then specifies its boundary conditions.

The central finding supports the memo directly. Accountability to an audience whose views are unknown, applied before the decision is made, produces what the literature calls preemptive self-criticism: more effortful, flexible, multidimensional thinking as the person prepares to defend a position against objections they cannot anticipate. Professionals working under high accountability with unknown reviewer views were found to write more thorough justifications for their decisions. That is the memo's mechanism, measured.

Two features of the corpus's design happen to match the effective conditions. The accountability is **pre-decisional**, since the person knows before accepting that the decision will be recorded and reviewed. And it is **to a person rather than to a form**, which is what makes anticipation possible at all.

The same literature identifies three conditions under which the effect reverses, and each maps onto a real design risk here.

**One. If the reviewer's preference is guessable, people conform instead of thinking.** The research is explicit that unknown-audience conditions fail to produce self-criticism when participants believe they can guess what the audience wants, at which point they abandon the effort to reach a defensible position and simply shift toward the presumed view. Applied here: if everyone learns that a particular manager always approves a twelve-month acceptance, the workflow collapses into a formality and the demand for evidence disappears. **The mechanism depends on review being genuinely evaluative rather than a predictable rubber stamp**, which is a cultural property the software cannot supply and can help expose, since a reviewer who has never once pushed back is visible in the record.

**Two. Commitment produces defence rather than reconsideration, and re-acceptance is where this bites.** The research finds that people accountable for positions to which they feel committed devote most of their effort to justifying those positions. This is the sharpest warning for this design, because the interval ladder means the same person re-accepts the same risk repeatedly. At the second acceptance they are no longer a neutral judge; they are the author of the previous decision, and the psychology predicts they will defend it. Three mitigations are worth considering: lead the re-acceptance with what has changed rather than with the prior decision, make the prior rationale less prominent than the current facts, and consider whether a re-acceptance after some number of cycles should require a different person. The corpus already holds that the level is a dated ledger of owned adjustments, and this suggests the ledger should be presented in a way that invites revision rather than confirmation.

**Three. Accountability widens the appetite for information without improving judgement about which information matters.** One study found that accountable participants took in a wider range of information, including material of no diagnostic value, but did not become better at telling useful information from useless. That is a direct argument for the corpus's own architecture: if accountable people will demand more evidence but will not reliably discriminate its quality, then **the evidence layer has to do the discrimination that the psychology says people will not do for themselves.** Provenance, grounding, the distinction between what is claimed and what is confirmed, and a chain back to a source are not decoration; they are the compensating control for a known limitation of the very mechanism the workflow relies on.

## Gamification, Honestly Framed

The memo acknowledges the game and does not apologise for it. The project lead: **"there's a bit of gamification here, which is sort of part of the point."** With the intended outcome. The project lead: **"so that we actually get the risks to be properly documented, and the facts and the evidence and the fixing properly funded."**

That is an honest framing. The system is designed so that the self-interested behaviour of a person who does not want to be embarrassed produces the behaviour the organisation wants: better documentation, better evidence, funded remediation. Designing incentives so that self-interest and institutional interest point the same way is what mechanism design is, and calling it gamification is more candid than most descriptions of the same move.

## Risk-Driven Projects

The end state the memo names is worth recording because it is the commercial consequence of the whole property. The project lead: **"then you end up having projects that are risk-driven."**

If accepting a risk requires choosing an action, and the actions include funding an investigation or a remediation, then the register stops being a record and becomes a source of demand. Work gets funded because somebody accountable decided it needed funding, with a dated decision and a named owner behind it. That is a materially different origin for a project than a roadmap item, and it is the mechanism by which security work acquires a business sponsor rather than an advocate.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a policing mechanism.** There is no separate quality function; the accountability does the work.
- **Not a claim that judgement is removed.** The judgement is where the business is comfortable, and only that.
- **Not automatic.** The property depends on review being real, which is cultural and cannot be enforced by software.
- **Not proof against defensive behaviour.** Re-acceptance is the exposed point and needs deliberate design.
- **Not a substitute for good evidence.** Accountability creates appetite for evidence and does not create the evidence.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Review as the engine versus review as a formality | The whole property rests on the reviewer being unpredictable, and in most organisations managers become very predictable to their reports within months |
| Re-acceptance versus defensive bolstering | The interval ladder is central to the model and creates precisely the committed-position condition the research identifies as the failure mode |
| Demand for evidence versus who pays for it | The appetite is free and the evidence is not, so the mechanism generates cost somewhere, which is a good outcome and still a cost |
| Escalation as a valid choice versus escalation as avoidance | Passing a decision upward is legitimate and is also the safest personal move, so the top can silt up with decisions people simply preferred not to make |
| Gamification versus theatre | Making accountability salient can produce visible diligence rather than actual diligence, and the two look identical in a register |
| Accountability improves care versus improving discrimination | The research says it does the first and not the second, so the system inherits a known limitation and must compensate for it structurally |
| Fact-based risks versus contested facts | The same accountability that demands accurate data gives an accountable person a reason to dispute inconvenient data, which is healthy in moderation and corrosive beyond it |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| How is re-acceptance presented? | Leading with what changed rather than with the prior decision, to interrupt the commitment effect |
| Should a re-acceptance ever require a different person? | After some number of cycles, or where nothing has changed, or where the level has drifted |
| Is reviewer predictability measurable? | A manager who has never returned a decision is visible in the record, and surfacing that may be the most useful metric in the system |
| What stops escalation becoming the default? | The mechanism that distinguishes a decision genuinely above someone's standing from one they preferred to avoid |
| How is sufficiency expressed? | The accepting person's own statement of what they knew and did not know, which is the honest form of the first consideration |
| Does the evidence layer discriminate well enough? | Since the psychology says the human will not, the grading of evidence quality becomes load-bearing rather than nice to have |
