## What This Is

An answer to the question of what somebody who wants to read the AI Act today can actually obtain: **the project lead's understanding is confirmed, the amendment has been adopted and is in force, and the relevant instrument is Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, the Digital Omnibus on AI, adopted on 8 July 2026, published in the Official Journal on 24 July and entered into force on 27 July, which is four days before this brief and unusually fast, since it took effect on the third day after publication rather than the customary twentieth because the general application date of 2 August was too close to leave the text unsettled; it amends the AI Act along with the aviation and machinery regulations, and because it is a regulation amending a regulation it applies directly in all member states with no transposition step, which means the Act that anyone read in June is a materially different document today; the finding that answers the memo's actual question is that **no consolidated version exists**, since the consolidated text on the Union's own legal portal is identified as 02024R1689-20240712 and carries a document date of 12 July 2024, incorporating nothing at all, and the most widely used browsable version states openly that its text is the Official Journal version of 13 June 2024; testing the popular sites against Article 10, as the memo suggests, reveals three distinct states rather than one, some carrying the original text with honest labelling, some carrying it without any labelling, and at least one carrying what appears to be a pre-adoption negotiating draft, which is worse than stale because it never was the law; the best private consolidation available was produced by a law firm in May 2026 and is itself now superseded, because it consolidated the provisionally agreed compromise text rather than the adopted regulation; and the practical consequence is that a person who wants the current law must open two documents, read the amending instructions, and perform the amendment themselves, which is precisely the gap the corpus is building into.** It is the first document of 31 July (cross-ref: the companion implementation brief of today, the v0.33.53 every-paragraph-is-a-graph brief, the v0.33.53 customised-standard brief, the v0.33.53 regulation-graph brief, and the v0.33.53 accepted-is-not-acceptable brief). New contributions: **confirmation that the amendment is adopted and in force with its exact identifiers and dates, the finding that no consolidated version exists on the official portal, the Article 10 diagnostic applied across the public browsable sources, the classification of those sources into three states of currency, and the observation that the best private consolidation was built on a superseded draft.**

## Confirming The Position

The memo asks for confirmation rather than assuming it, which is the right instinct given how much has moved. The project lead: **"just confirm that my understanding again is that it has been approved and that it has been enforced, and I want to confirm that I'm not missing something here, because it seems to me quite a big gap."**

The understanding is correct on both counts, and the gap is real. The amending regulation exists, is adopted, is published, and is in force. The gap is that being in force and being readable are two different things.

## The Amending Instrument

**Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**, known as the Digital Omnibus on AI. Adopted by the Parliament and Council on 8 July 2026 in Strasbourg, following the Parliament's position of 16 June and the Council's decision of 29 June. Published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026. **Entered into force on 27 July 2026**, on the third day after publication rather than the usual twentieth, which is a deliberate acceleration explained by the proximity of the Act's 2 August general application date.

It amends three instruments: the AI Act itself, the Basic Aviation Regulation and the Machinery Regulation. Because it is a regulation amending a regulation, it applies directly across all member states with no national transposition, so there is no implementation lag and no divergence between jurisdictions.

## What It Changed

The structural point is that it did not touch the risk tiers or the substantive obligations. It moved dates, adjusted a small number of provisions, and expanded the governance arrangements. The changes most relevant here:

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| Annex III standalone high-risk obligations | Deferred from 2 August 2026 to **2 December 2027** |
| Annex I embedded high-risk obligations | Deferred to **2 August 2028** |
| Systems already on the market | Subject to those requirements only if substantially modified after the new date |
| New prohibited practices | Added, applying from 2 December 2026 |
| Article 10(5), data and data governance | Legal basis for processing special category data for bias detection extended beyond providers of high-risk systems, under a strict necessity standard with mandatory safeguards; no obligation to perform bias detection is created |
| National regulatory sandboxes | Deadline extended from 2 August 2026 to 2 August 2027, with a new Union-level sandbox |
| Article 50 transparency | **Unchanged, still applies from 2 August 2026**, with only the machine-readable marking obligation in Article 50(2) receiving a grace period to 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market |

That last row matters commercially and is widely misread. The deferral is not general. Two days from now, transparency obligations begin to apply and enforcement powers arrive, while the high-risk regime moves out by sixteen months.

## There Is No Consolidated Version

This is the direct answer to the question and it is unambiguous. The Union's own legal portal publishes consolidated texts, which are the version of an instrument with all amendments incorporated. For the AI Act, the consolidated text carries the internal identifier **02024R1689-20240712** and a document date of **12 July 2024**, which is the date of Official Journal publication of the original. Its recorded first date of entry into force is the same. It therefore incorporates no amendments whatsoever, and there is no later consolidated version.

Consolidated texts on that portal also carry a standing caveat that they are of documentary value only and are not authentic. So even when one appears, it will not be the law; it will be a reading aid.

The practical position four days after entry into force is that **the authoritative statement of the current law exists only as two separate documents**: the original regulation, and the amending regulation containing instructions about what to substitute, insert and delete.

## The Article 10 Test

The memo proposes a specific diagnostic and it works well. The project lead: **"one that I noticed that it's a good one to find is if you look at the changes to Article 10, there's a couple of substitutions and then the paragraphs that got removed; so if the text for that online has those paragraphs and has not been changed, then they haven't done it."**

Article 10 is a good probe for three reasons. It was amended rather than merely re-dated, so a stale copy is detectable by content rather than by a date stamp. The amendment touches paragraph 5, which is short, distinctive and easy to compare. And it is a heavily read provision, so if anywhere is current, it should be.

Applying it across the public sources produces a clearer picture than expected, and it is not a single picture.

## Three States, Not One

**State one: current text, honestly labelled as historical.** The most widely used browsable version of the Act, the explorer maintained by a research non-profit, presents Article 10 in its original form and states plainly on the page that the text used is the Official Journal version of 13 June 2024. That is stale but transparent, and a careful reader is warned.

**State two: original text, unlabelled.** Several other browsable versions and law-firm reproductions present the same original Article 10 text, including the paragraph 5 wording that ties the bias-detection basis to providers of high-risk systems, without any indication that the provision has since been amended. A reader arriving from a search engine has no way to know.

**State three: a text that never was the law.** At least one searchable version presents an Article 10(5) referring to points that do not appear in the adopted regulation, using wording characteristic of the negotiating drafts rather than the final text. This is the worst of the three, because it is not a superseded version of the law but a version that was never in force, and nothing on the page distinguishes it.

The classification matters more than any individual site, because it means a user cannot resolve currency by cross-checking two sources. Two sources agreeing tells you only that both are old.

## Even The Best Private Consolidation Is Superseded

One serious attempt at a consolidation exists. A large international law firm published a consolidated version in May 2026 applying the Omnibus amendments to the Act, with a clear statement of scope, an explanation of where inserted articles were placed, and an explicit note that the exercise was a reading aid rather than legal advice.

It was, however, consolidated against the **provisionally agreed compromise text**, a Council document dated 13 May 2026, not against the adopted regulation. The provisional text then went through formal endorsement and legal-linguistic revision before adoption on 8 July. So the most careful public consolidation available is built on a draft that was superseded seven weeks later, and it says so.

This is worth recording without criticism. The firm did the work early, documented its sources precisely, and stated its limits. That it is now out of date is a property of the situation rather than of the effort, and it illustrates the underlying problem: consolidating a moving instrument is expensive and perishable.

## What A User Actually Has To Do Today

Assemble it themselves. Obtain the original regulation, obtain the amending regulation, read the amending article's instructions, and apply each substitution, insertion and deletion by hand, tracking which provisions take effect on which of at least four different dates. There is no published artefact that does this for them, and no way to verify their result against an authoritative one.

For a document of this length, on which commercial and legal decisions are being made two days from now, that is a substantial gap.

## Why The Gap Exists

Not incompetence, and worth stating fairly. Consolidation on the official portal follows publication by a lag that is normal and unhurried, and the amendment here entered into force in an unusually compressed timeframe. Independent sites are maintained by non-profits, vendors and law firms, mostly as public goods or marketing, and a full re-derivation of a long regulation is real work that arrives after the analysis pieces that get published first. The predictable consequence is that commentary about the change circulates well ahead of the changed text.

## What This Means For Us

Three things follow directly.

The regulation graph the corpus is building is not a convenience. If no authoritative consolidated artefact exists, a graph that carries the base text, the amending instructions and the resulting projection, with provenance for each, is producing something the market does not currently have.

The dates-as-versioned-properties conclusion from 28 July is now demonstrated rather than argued. An instrument whose provisions take effect on at least four different dates cannot be represented as text with a date stamp.

And the amendment mechanism is the interesting part rather than an obstacle, which is the subject of the companion brief.

## What Could Not Be Verified

Recorded so the gaps are visible. The exact list of substitutions, insertions and deletions applied to Article 10 was not read from the amending regulation's own operative text; the effect is described from secondary analysis, and the precise instructions should be taken from Article 1 of the amending regulation directly. Whether a consolidated version has been prepared and is pending publication is not knowable from outside. Only a sample of the browsable sources was checked, and the three-state classification is a characterisation of that sample rather than a census. And whether the transparency obligations arriving on 2 August are affected by any provision not surfaced here should be confirmed against the amending text before anything is published externally.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| A gap worth filling versus a gap that closes itself | The official consolidation will arrive, and a product justified by its absence needs a reason to exist afterwards, which is the graph rather than the text |
| Naming the state of public sources versus fairness | These are mostly free public goods maintained by small teams, and a survey of their currency should not read as criticism of people doing unpaid work |
| Reading the effect from commentary versus the instrument | This brief describes the Article 10 change from secondary analysis, which is exactly the second-hand pattern it criticises elsewhere, and is flagged rather than hidden |
| Four days is very early | The absence of a consolidation four days after entry into force is unremarkable in itself; the finding is the user-facing consequence, not institutional failure |
| Three states versus a moving target | The classification is accurate today and several of these sites may update this week, so the artefact is a snapshot and should be dated wherever it is used |
