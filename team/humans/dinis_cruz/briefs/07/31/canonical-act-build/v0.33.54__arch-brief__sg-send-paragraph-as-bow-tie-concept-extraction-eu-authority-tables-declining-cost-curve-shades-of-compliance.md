## What This Is

What happens to a paragraph once the structural model exists, and the economics that decide whether the ambition is affordable: **the shape is a bow-tie with the provision as the knot, structure running upward into the instrument and meaning running outward into everything the provision refers to, and the outward work is concept extraction, finding the words that carry meaning and connecting each to something that defines it, because a term connected to nothing is only a word; those connections should terminate in external authorities rather than in nodes we invent, which answers the memo's own disambiguation question directly, since the Union's Publications Office already maintains authority tables with stable identifiers for exactly the entities an instrument names, so the way to know you are linking to the right institution is to link to the identifier that institution publishes about itself; the same infrastructure answers the translation worry as a side effect, because those vocabularies and the accompanying multilingual thesaurus attach labels in every official language to one language-independent concept, which is the anchor-node primitive the corpus already holds, and it means a nuance survives translation by being carried in the graph rather than in a word; the economics are the part that decides feasibility, and the memo states the curve correctly, that the first passes are expensive and produce an explosion of nodes while the marginal cost of each subsequent paragraph falls as the shared vocabulary accumulates, which combined with the content-hash caching established earlier today means the cost of the second instrument is materially lower than the first and the cost of the tenth is lower again, so the business model rests on a curve that has to be demonstrated rather than assumed; the definitions article is the natural first target because it is where the instrument defines its own terms, and it recurses, since the definitions are themselves built from words that need meaning, and the instrument's definition of risk as a combination of probability and severity is the clearest case, because each of those words opens a further question the instrument does not answer; and the point of all of it is anchoring, since every provision needs a line to a risk, a fact and a core node, which is what lets a claim run from a paragraph up to an executive, and what that produces is not a compliance verdict but a shading, because an organisation does not have one level of compliance across an instrument, it has a different level per provision, per part of the business and per product.** It is the eighth document of 31 July (cross-ref: the companion document-ontology brief, the v0.33.54 paragraph-is-a-folder brief, the v0.33.53 accepted-is-not-acceptable brief, the v0.33.53 every-paragraph-is-a-graph brief, and the v0.33.49 fractal-registers brief). New contributions: **the bow-tie with structure upward and meaning outward, external authority tables proposed as the termination point for entity links and as the answer to disambiguation, the multilingual thesaurus identified as carrying the translation nuance, the declining marginal cost curve named as the thing the business model rests on, the recursion of the definitions article, and shades of compliance per provision, per business unit and per product rather than one level.**

## The Bow-Tie

The memo works out the shape while describing it. The project lead: **"a paragraph or a bullet point fundamentally is the bow tie of a specific graph, because upwards you have all the relationships with the actual standard, and downwards you have all the things that it means."**

The companion brief handles the upward half, which is a taxonomy and is mechanical. This brief handles the outward half, which is an ontology and is open-ended.

```
   UPWARD (taxonomy, finite, mechanical)
   bullet -> paragraph -> article -> chapter -> instrument
                    ^
                    |
              [ PROVISION ]  the knot
                    |
                    v
   OUTWARD (ontology, open-ended, judged)
   defined terms . concepts . entities . obligations
   actors . thresholds . risks . facts . external vocabularies
```

## Every Word That Means Something

The extraction rule is stated plainly. The project lead: **"we need to start extracting every single concept that is defined here, and that needs to be either defined by an ontology or defined by something else, because or else it's just words."**

That last clause is the corpus's meaning-through-connectivity principle arriving from a different direction: a term with no connections carries no meaning the system can use. And the operative question for each candidate term is whether it resolves to something. A term that resolves to a definition is anchored. A term that resolves to nothing is either noise or a finding, and distinguishing between those two cases is where the value is, because the second case is exactly what surfaced the acceptable problem earlier this week.

The stopping rule proposed this afternoon applies here and keeps the extraction bounded: a word earns annotation if removing it would change who must do what, by when, or to what standard.

## Link To Authorities, Do Not Invent Nodes

The memo raises a problem and then does not solve it, and the solution exists. The project lead: **"even just take into account EU institutions, I'm sure there's all sorts of different ones; how do you know you're linking the right one?"** And the requirement that follows. The project lead: **"we need to make sure that the terms are defined, and we need to make sure that we then remove ambiguity on the definition of those terms."**

The Publications Office of the European Union maintains authority tables, also called named authority lists, as controlled vocabularies with stable identifiers, published specifically so that institutions and data producers refer to the same things in the same terms. The corporate body list is exactly the case the memo worries about: a controlled list of Union institutions, bodies and agencies, each with its own permanent identifier under a published concept scheme.

So the answer to how you know you are linking to the right one is that **you do not decide, you defer**. An entity reference terminates at the identifier the Union itself publishes for that body rather than at a node we created and named. Three things follow. Ambiguity is resolved by an authority rather than by our judgement. Anyone else linking to the same authority is automatically interoperable with us. And when a body is renamed or restructured, the authority table already models it, since the identifier stays stable while superseded labels become historical concepts with validity dates.

The general rule is worth stating beyond entities: **where an external authority publishes an identifier for a thing, link to it; only mint a node where no authority exists.** That keeps the graph small, keeps it interoperable, and moves an enormous amount of curation work onto institutions already doing it.

## The Translation Worry Is Answered By The Same Infrastructure

The memo's aside is a real concern. The project lead: **"I'm very curious to see how this is done from a translation point of view, because we already have a whole thing about risk acceptance and risk acceptable, which are very different, and how you're going to get these nuances across multilingual without more context is quite interesting."**

The concern is well founded, since the distinction established this week between an act performed by a person and a threshold set by a business is carried in English by two closely related words, and there is no reason another language would preserve that shape.

The infrastructure above answers it structurally. The Union's multilingual thesaurus covers the activities of the Union in twenty-four official languages, and the accompanying ontology exists specifically to express authority lists and vocabularies in multilingual form. The pattern is that **a concept has one identifier and many labels**, one per language, so the concept is language-independent and the word is a projection of it.

Applied here, accepted and acceptable become two distinct concepts with distinct identifiers, each carrying labels in every language. The nuance survives translation not because a translator preserved it but because it was never stored in a word. That is the anchor-node primitive the corpus already holds, now with an official vocabulary to anchor to.

And it generalises in the direction the memo cares about most. The project lead: **"the objective here is to translate what we do, not just different languages, but to stakeholders and to customise it."** Translating a provision from a lawyer's language into an engineer's is the same operation as translating it from one natural language into another: one concept, several labels, and the reader receives the one that fits them. The multilingual case and the multi-altitude case are one mechanism.

## The Cost Curve Is The Business Model

The memo states the economics precisely and this deserves elevating, because the standards-as-a-service argument rests on it. The project lead: **"in the beginning we're going to have an explosion of the graph because we don't have a lot of these things, but the idea will be that the cost of mapping the graph goes down as the more we do this; in fact the more documents we do, but especially the more we process a document, the cost of each paragraph."**

Two distinct effects are running here and they should be separated.

**Within a document**, cost falls because vocabulary accumulates. The first paragraph mentioning a defined term does the work of defining it; every later paragraph reuses it. Since instruments are internally repetitive, most terms are resolved early and the tail is cheap.

**Across documents**, cost falls because the shared vocabulary transfers. The second instrument arrives with the entity links, the external authority connections and much of the conceptual scaffolding already built, and needs only its own specific terms. The memo's own observation that the AI Act already references other instruments means those connections are made once and reused in both directions.

Alongside these sits the third effect established this afternoon: content-hash caching means unchanged text is never reanalysed, so maintenance scales with change rather than with size.

```
   cost per paragraph
     |
     |*  first document: vocabulary being built
     | *
     |  **
     |    ****            second document: much already linked
     |        *******
     |              **************  tenth document: mostly reuse
     +----------------------------------> paragraphs processed
```

**This curve is the business model.** A service converting standards into graphs is only viable if the tenth costs materially less than the first, and the strategy brief written in the parallel session priced the offering on exactly that assumption. So the curve should be instrumented from the first article: cost per paragraph, proportion of terms already resolved, and new nodes minted per paragraph, tracked over time. If it does not bend, the pricing needs revisiting rather than the ambition.

## The Definitions Article Recurses

The memo identifies the right first target and the trap inside it. The project lead: **"the definitions, I think it's Article Three, that's a good example, but we need that hyperlinked on graph; in fact the definitions itself is made of a whole bunch of other words that need to have meaning."**

Article 3 is indeed the definitions article, and it is the highest-value place to start because it is where the instrument anchors its own vocabulary, so every term defined there resolves cleanly and is reused everywhere.

The recursion is the interesting part and the memo picks the sharpest example. The project lead: **"when they define risk as probability versus impact, that needs to be connected; what's probability, how do you calculate probability, how do you calculate impact?"**

One correction for precision, since this is a definition the corpus will cite. Article 3(2) defines risk as the combination of the probability of an occurrence of harm and the **severity** of that harm, not impact, and it says combination rather than specifying multiplication. That wording repays attention for two reasons. Severity is narrower than impact and is tied to harm. And because combination is unspecified, the instrument does not mandate that risk be the product of two numbers, which leaves room for the recoverability argument made on 24 July, where an irreversible outcome is a different object from a recoverable one of the same magnitude.

The recursion then continues, exactly as the memo says: probability, occurrence, harm and severity are each words the instrument uses and does not define. That is the used-but-undefined category again, and Article 3 is where it is densest.

## Every Provision Needs A Line To A Risk

This is the requirement that connects the whole graph to the rest of the product. The project lead: **"it's critical that every single paragraph in there has a direct line to a particular risk and a particular fact and a particular core node that we're going to be using in other places."** And the reason. The project lead: **"we also need to capture how do you measure that, how do you know if you're compliant or not."**

The memo also identifies the meta-risk, which is the same shape the corpus has found twice before. The project lead: **"if you have not mapped this particular article into play, then that's a risk."** Not having assessed a provision is itself a rateable, ownable finding, exactly as not knowing your agent population was in July and not having defined your acceptable level was this week.

And the payoff is the corpus's central claim made concrete. The project lead: **"we're going to be able to connect all the way from a paragraph all the way to the stakeholder and the executive."** A risk in a board register cites a provision; the provision resolves to a paragraph in a published graph; the paragraph resolves to a file and a commit. That chain is the evidence layer the plug work has been waiting for.

## Nobody Has One Level Of Compliance

The best line in the memo, and it should become product vocabulary. The project lead: **"organisations don't have one level of compliance with the EU Act; they have a shade of it."** With the dimensions named. The project lead: **"you also have to have context on which part of the organisation, and even products and things need to have this."**

That is right and it has real consequences. Compliance with an instrument is not one boolean, it is a value per provision, per organisational unit and per product. A firm can satisfy the transparency provisions in one business line and not another, meet the data governance requirements for one system and not the next. Reporting it as a single percentage destroys the only information anybody can act on, which is where specifically the gap is.

The memo also anticipates that some provisions are not binary at all. The project lead: **"some of these might even be maturity models."** A provision requiring a risk management system is satisfied by degrees rather than by a yes, which is precisely what the corpus's maturity models exist to express, and it means a provision node may point at a maturity level rather than at a state.

Two consequences for the build. The compliance shading is a per-provision, per-unit, per-product matrix rather than a score. And it composes with the fractal register directly, since a provision assessed differently across business units is the same object seen from different altitudes, which the corpus already knows how to represent.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not the structural model.** That is the companion brief; this is what attaches to it.
- **Not a private entity vocabulary.** Entity links terminate at published authorities wherever one exists.
- **Not a translation of words.** Concepts carry identifiers and labels attach per language and per stakeholder.
- **Not a compliance score.** The output is a shading per provision, per unit and per product.
- **Not affordable by assumption.** The declining cost curve is the load-bearing economic claim and needs instrumenting.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| The cost curve versus evidence for it | The business model depends on it and nothing has yet demonstrated it, so the first article should be instrumented rather than merely completed |
| Linking to authorities versus their coverage | Authority tables cover Union institutions well and will not cover most of the conceptual vocabulary, so a large minted-node population remains |
| Concept identifiers versus real translation | One concept with many labels is right structurally and does not settle whether the labels chosen actually carry the nuance, which still needs expert review |
| Shades of compliance versus what buyers ask for | A matrix is more truthful than a percentage and harder to put on a board slide, and somebody will ask for the number anyway |
| Every provision needing a risk line versus provisions that create none | Some provisions are definitional or procedural, and forcing a risk from each would manufacture noise |
| Extraction by model versus by person | The volume demands automation and the judgement about what a term means is exactly what automation gets confidently wrong |
