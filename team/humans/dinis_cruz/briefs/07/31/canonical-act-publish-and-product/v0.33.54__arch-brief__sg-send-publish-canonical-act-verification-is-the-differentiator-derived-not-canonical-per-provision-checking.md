## What This Is

The plan for publishing the consolidated Act as a public artefact, and the argument that the invitation to check it is worth more than the artefact itself: **the vault has already done the hard part, consolidating the instrument, loading the data, mapping it out and presenting a version that is genuinely pleasant to consume, which makes it a case study for the vault work as much as a product in its own right, and the next step is to put it on a dedicated site alongside references to the other public versions rather than in place of them; the opportunity is real and was verified independently this morning, since no consolidated version of the Act incorporating the July amendment exists anywhere, the official consolidated text still carries a 2024 date, and the best private attempt was built against a draft that was superseded seven weeks before adoption; but the framing matters more than the opportunity, because publishing a derived text and calling it canonical claims an authority that even the Union's own consolidations explicitly disclaim, and the memo's own alternative phrasing, an attempt at mapping the canonical version, is both the honest description and the stronger position; that is because the differentiator here is not having a consolidation, which anyone with a weekend could produce, but having one whose working is visible and which has survived being checked, and nobody else can make that claim, as demonstrated by the fact that a superseded consolidation sat publicly for weeks without anyone catching it, since nobody could; so verification has to be designed rather than merely invited, which means publishing the derivation per provision so that a checker verifies one paragraph rather than a document, accepting issues against individual provisions, and exposing content hashes so that a checker can state exactly which version they examined; and the mechanics are already in place, a repository working the way the existing library does, drawing content from the vault, published through the read-key mechanism already in use, with a pipeline to a dedicated domain under the existing brand, and export to several formats which is what lets a lawyer, a website, a graph tool and an agent all consume the same artefact.** It is the fourth document of 31 July (cross-ref: the companion business-model brief of today, the v0.33.54 no-canonical-version research brief, the v0.33.54 paragraph-is-a-folder brief, the v0.33.53 customised-standard brief, and the v0.31.9 vault-per-standard brief). New contributions: **verification designed rather than invited, with per-provision derivation, provision-level issues and published hashes as its three mechanisms, the argument that the invitation to be checked is the differentiator, the recommendation to publish as a derived consolidation rather than as canonical, and the confidentiality review positioned as a gate rather than a task.**

## The Vault Already Did The Work

Worth recording because it is a result rather than a plan. The project lead: **"the vault, which is by the way a great case study of the vault development, is already proven to be ridiculously useful; it's already successfully able to consolidate, load the data, map it all out, and actually present a consolidated version of the act in a really great to consume way."**

Two things follow. The consolidation problem identified this morning has in fact been solved internally, which means the gap in the public record is not a gap in capability. And the vault architecture now has a demonstration that is independent of the risk product, which is worth more to that architecture than another internal use would have been.

## The Opportunity, Verified Rather Than Assumed

The memo states the opportunity and it holds up, because it was checked this morning rather than assumed. The project lead: **"there's an interesting opportunity here, because right now there isn't a canonical version, there isn't a version out there that actually has both original plus amendments."**

The research brief of the same date establishes this precisely. The consolidated text on the Union's own portal carries a document date of 12 July 2024 and incorporates nothing. The most widely used browsable version states openly that its text is the Official Journal version of 13 June 2024. Several other public reproductions show the pre-amendment text with no notice, and at least one shows wording from a negotiating draft that was never in force. The most careful private consolidation available was produced against the provisionally agreed compromise text and was superseded by the adopted regulation seven weeks later.

So the artefact would be the only one of its kind, four days after the amendment took effect.

## Call It What It Is

The memo offers two framings and they are not equivalent. The project lead: **"publish this on a site that we can basically say this is the current canonical version, or an attempt at mapping the canonical version."**

The second is the one to use, and not only for caution. Official consolidated texts carry a standing warning that they are of documentary value only and are not authentic; a privately derived consolidation sits further from authority still. Publishing something described as canonical invites reliance the artefact cannot support, and the first person to act on an error will be right to complain.

But the honest framing is also the stronger one. **A consolidation presented as finished asks to be trusted. One presented as derived, with its working shown and its verification openly incomplete, asks to be checked**, and being checked is the thing no competing version has been. The weaker claim is the more defensible position and the better invitation.

## Verification Is The Differentiator, So Design It

The memo identifies verification as one of the two parts of the job. The project lead: **"we actually need independent verification of this, so we need to actually get the community on the case and see if we can get people to help to actually verify that what we've done is correct."** With the enabling condition already met. The project lead: **"the good news is the code is all available, all the mappings are available, information is available, so we're just leveraging the open source element of it."**

Openness is necessary and not sufficient. An invitation to check a long regulation, unaccompanied by structure, produces nothing, because the task is too large for anyone to start. Three mechanisms make it tractable, and all three fall out of the storage model specified earlier today.

**Publish the derivation, not just the result.** For each provision, show the original text, the amending instruction applied to it, and the resulting text. A checker then verifies one paragraph against one instruction, which is a two-minute task, rather than reading a regulation against another regulation, which is a week.

**Accept issues against provisions.** The paragraph-as-folder model already makes a provision the unit that comments and issues attach to. A reader who spots a problem in Article 10 paragraph 5 raises it there, and it stays attached to that provision permanently, which also produces a public record of what has been examined.

**Publish the hashes.** A checker who says the consolidation is correct is making a claim about a specific version. Exposing the content hash per provision lets them say precisely which text they checked, and lets a later reader see whether it has changed since.

Together those turn a vague appeal into a verifiable process, and the accumulating record of checked provisions becomes the asset. That is the claim no competing consolidation can make.

## The Illustration Is Already Available

The strongest argument for designing verification this way is what happened without it. A serious consolidation, produced by a capable firm with its sources documented and its limits stated, was built against a text that was subsequently superseded. It sat publicly through that change. Nobody publicly caught it, not because nobody was paying attention but because there was no mechanism by which anyone could check it short of redoing the work.

That is the failure mode this design avoids, and it is worth citing as the reason rather than asserting the principle abstractly.

## The Mechanics

Mostly assembly of things that already exist. The project lead: **"we're going to create a GitHub repo; the repo will work like the SGraph AI library, which basically leverages the content of the vault."** With the publishing route already proven. The project lead: **"we are going to be hosting some of the files via read key on the public environment, which is a platform that we already use quite well, so we just need that, and then the CI pipeline goes from there."** And the destination decided. The project lead: **"we can put it under the risk mandate, the AI brand."**

One item in that list is a gate rather than a task, and should be treated as one. The project lead: **"I don't think there's anything confidential in the vault, we might just need to double check it, or maybe create a sub vault with that information."** Publishing a vault publicly is irreversible; a read key handed out cannot be recalled from anyone who has already used it. The safer default is the sub-vault containing only published materials, created deliberately rather than deciding that the existing vault happens to be clean. That is a small amount of work now against a class of error that cannot be undone.

## The Export Formats Are The Distribution

The memo mentions the existing capability almost in passing and it is more strategically useful than it sounds. The project lead: **"this site already has some cool features like download PDF, download HTML, download Markdown, download graph."**

Those four formats reach four different consumers. A PDF is what a lawyer files and cites. HTML is what a search engine indexes and a reader browses. Markdown is what another repository absorbs, and what an agent reads without an integration. The graph is what a tool queries.

The last two matter most for the corpus's own arguments. The position taken on 28 July was that material must live where agents can read it, and a markdown and graph export means this artefact is the first regulation anyone can hand to an agent without writing a scraper. That is a genuine reason for the artefact to spread that has nothing to do with marketing.

## Reference The Others

A small point in the memo worth honouring. The project lead: **"similar to the ones that exist out there with the mappings, which probably should also include reference to all of the others."**

Linking to the other public versions is generous and is also the strongest possible statement of the differentiator, because a reader who follows those links finds the states catalogued this morning: one honestly labelled as the 2024 text, several unlabelled, one showing a draft that was never law. The comparison makes the case without anyone having to make it.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not canonical.** A derived consolidation, described as such, with its working shown.
- **Not authoritative.** Even official consolidations disclaim authenticity; this is further from it.
- **Not finished.** Verification is openly incomplete, and the incompleteness is the invitation.
- **Not a replacement for the other versions.** They are linked to, not competed with.
- **Not a marketing asset first.** It is a public good that happens to demonstrate the platform.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Publishing a legal text versus reliance | Somebody will act on it, and the disclaimers that make that safe are the same ones that reduce its perceived value |
| Inviting verification versus getting it | A designed process improves the odds and does not guarantee that anyone shows up, and an unverified artefact claiming to invite verification is weaker than one that never asked |
| Being first versus being right | The window is open because the amendment is four days old, and speed is exactly the condition under which consolidation errors happen |
| Open code and mappings versus copyable output | Everything that makes verification possible also makes the artefact trivially reproducible by anyone, which is the intended trade and should be stated |
| The brand versus the public good | Publishing under a product brand aids the funnel and slightly undercuts the claim that this is a neutral resource |
| The confidentiality gate versus momentum | The sub-vault is the right answer and adds a step to a build everyone wants to ship this week |
