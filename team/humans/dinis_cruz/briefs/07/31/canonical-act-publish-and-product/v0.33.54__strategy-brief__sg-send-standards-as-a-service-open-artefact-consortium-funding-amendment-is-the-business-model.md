# Standards As A Service: The Market Is Proven And Entirely Proprietary, And The Amendment Is The Business Model

**version** v0.33.54
**date** 31 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Commercial, Strategy, Partners, Board

**type** Strategy brief

*Fifth of 31 July. The business half of the memo; the publishing plan is the companion document. External market claims are grounded and cited. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

---

## What This Is

The business that could be built from the capability now demonstrated on one instrument: **the proposal is a service, and eventually a company, that converts standards and regulations into this format and then keeps them current, prompted by the observation that users shown the first version immediately asked for a different standard they were struggling to understand; everything produced stays open source and Creative Commons, with a contribution path so that others can add their own mappings through a peer review workflow, and the commercial model is that organisations pay for the mapping to be produced, at something like ten or twenty thousand a time, or several of them chip in together in a way closer to crowdfunding than to a licence, after which the freelance network already established does the work; the argument that they would pay is not speculative, because the market exists, is large, and is entirely proprietary, since the incumbent harmonised compliance database carries on the order of a hundred thousand mandates, ten thousand common controls and a thousand mapped authority documents, sells enterprise access at tens of thousands of dollars a year, charges over two thousand dollars per person merely to train a certified mapper, and sits underneath the compliance products of several major vendors, which means the question is not whether organisations pay for mapped standards but whether an open alternative can take that ground; the same incumbent also validates the workflow, since its mapping process requires three distinct roles of mapper, reviewer and approver, which is the peer review shape the memo proposes and which is worth copying rather than inventing; the crucial commercial consequence of staying open is that access cannot be sold, so the recurring revenue has to come from somewhere else, and it comes from maintenance, because a regulation that amends is a regulation that must be re-derived, and the work established yesterday means the cost of doing that scales with change rather than size, which turns amendment from a burden into the subscription; and the value compounds across instruments, because standards reference each other, so each new mapping adds edges to the ones already done.** It is the fifth document of 31 July (cross-ref: the companion publishing brief of today, the v0.33.49 open-source-as-strategy brief, the v0.33.51 sovereignty-bounties brief, the v0.33.50 freelance-next-steps brief, and the v0.33.54 paragraph-is-a-folder brief). New contributions: **the identification that the harmonised-standards market is proven, large and entirely proprietary, the incumbent's three-role mapping process as a validated peer review shape, the conclusion that an open artefact cannot charge for access so maintenance must carry the recurring revenue, the reframing of amendment as the subscription rather than the burden, and the compounding argument from cross-referencing instruments.**

## The Demand Arrived Unprompted

The most useful signal in the memo is that nobody went looking for it. The project lead: **"even with this first version, we're already seeing a couple of users that we talked to who said, hey, this will be quite useful for this other standard that I need to get my head around, which I don't fully understand."**

That is the right kind of evidence, because it is a request for a different instrument rather than praise for this one. Someone who says the artefact would be useful for a standard you have not built has told you the format has value independent of the content, which is exactly the claim a productisable service rests on.

## The Market Is Proven, Large, And Closed

The memo assumes organisations would pay and does not argue it. The assumption is correct and the evidence is stronger than the memo suggests.

The established player in harmonised compliance content maintains a database on the order of one hundred thousand individual mandates, ten thousand common controls, and over a thousand mapped authority documents, with a quarter of a million interconnected words and phrases in its dictionary. It is sold commercially through a controls hub with a limited free tier, where multi-user and enterprise licences are reported to run into the tens of thousands of dollars per year. To become a certified mapper on its platform, an individual pays over two thousand dollars for the training course, and an organisation must license the tooling for a minimum of three people. It provides the underlying content for the compliance products of several large vendors.

Three conclusions follow.

**Organisations demonstrably pay for mapped standards**, at a scale that makes ten or twenty thousand for a single instrument look modest rather than optimistic.

**The market is entirely proprietary.** The largest harmonised compliance resource is a commercial database, and the mappings underneath several major products are licensed rather than public. There is no open equivalent, which is the differentiation and the opportunity.

**The word-level ambition is commercially serious, not just intellectually interesting.** A quarter of a million interconnected words and phrases in the incumbent's dictionary is the same shape as the annotation work specified yesterday, and it is a substantial part of what customers are paying for.

## The Incumbent Also Validates The Workflow

The memo proposes contributions with review. The project lead: **"there is definitely a mode where others could contribute to this, so they can add their own mappings, which we could then read a peer review workflow."**

That shape is already proven at scale. The incumbent's mapping process requires three distinct roles, a mapper, a reviewer and an approver, each trained and certified, and its tooling cannot be licensed for fewer than three people because the separation is structural rather than optional.

Adopting the same triad has two advantages beyond correctness. It is legible to exactly the buyers being targeted, who may already know it. And it gives the open contribution path a quality gate that a pull request alone does not, which matters when the artefact is a legal text and a wrong mapping is worse than a missing one.

## Open Means You Cannot Sell Access, So Sell Maintenance

This is the commercial consequence the memo does not draw out, and it determines the shape of the whole business.

The incumbent's revenue comes from access: the content is proprietary, so the licence is the product, and it recurs because access must be renewed. That model is unavailable here by choice. The project lead: **"everything we're going to be doing is open source, and Creative Commons."** Once an artefact is published openly, nobody can be charged to read it, and the seven companies who funded it cannot be charged again next year for the same thing.

So the recurring revenue has to come from somewhere, and there is only one honest candidate: **the instrument keeps changing, and somebody has to keep the mapping true.**

That is not a workaround, it is the strongest possible position, for three reasons.

It is genuinely valuable, as this week demonstrates. An amendment entered into force on 27 July and by 31 July no public consolidation reflected it. An organisation relying on a mapping produced last month is relying on something wrong, and has no way to know.

It is cheap for us and expensive for them, which is the right asymmetry. The work established yesterday means re-deriving a mapping after an amendment costs in proportion to what changed rather than to the size of the instrument, because content hashing localises the change and caches everything untouched. An organisation doing it internally pays the full cost each time.

And it is defensible in a way access never is, because it is a service rather than a rent. The corpus argued on 17 July that lock-in belongs in quality, maintenance, versions and certification rather than in withholding the artefact. This is that argument with a customer attached.

**The reframing worth carrying: the amendment is not the burden that makes this business hard, it is the recurring revenue that makes it a business at all.** A one-off mapping is a project. A moving instrument is a subscription.

## The Funding Model

The memo proposes two shapes and they suit different customers. The project lead: **"for companies to pay us ten, twenty grand a pop or more, or even chip in; you can have five or six or seven companies chipping in, almost like a crowdfunding sort of solution."**

The single-payer version is straightforward and suits an organisation with an urgent need for one instrument. The consortium version is more interesting, because it fits what the artefact actually is. A published mapping is a public good in the strict sense: once it exists, nobody can be excluded from it and one organisation's use does not diminish another's. Markets systematically under-produce those, which is why they get funded collectively when they get funded at all. Several companies splitting the cost of something none would fund alone is the textbook response, and the corpus already designed a milestone-based instrument for exactly this shape in the sovereignty bounty work.

The justification the memo gives is the one to lead with in a sales conversation. The project lead: **"this will make a lot of commercial sense for a lot of companies, because they're actually spending more money internally trying to make sense of these standards."** That is almost certainly true and is also unmeasured, which makes it the first thing to quantify with a real prospect rather than assert.

## Delivery Is Already Designed

The memo notes that the operational half exists. The project lead: **"once we have that, we can organise the freelance network to create this and map it up."** And the reason it is repeatable. The project lead: **"we got the formula, we got the structure, we got the flow, we got the inputs and the outputs."**

That is accurate. The engagement model, the scoping discipline, the requirement that partners arrive with a working agentic setup, the public-by-default working practice and the payment mechanics were all specified in July. What this adds is a demand-side pipeline for that network, which the earlier work explicitly identified as the missing piece.

One caution the earlier work does not cover. Producing a mapping of a legal instrument is closer to a regulated professional output than producing a transcription tool, and a wrong mapping carries a different kind of consequence. The review triad above is part of the answer; how the artefact is described is the rest, and it should carry the same derived-not-authoritative framing as the publishing brief specifies.

## It Compounds

The last observation in the memo is the one that decides whether this is a service or a company. The project lead: **"the AI standard already links to another of the standards, another acts that you need to be able to access as a graph, so there's a lot of value in how everything can actually work together."**

Instruments cross-reference each other constantly. The AI Act refers out to data protection regulation, to product safety and machinery legislation, and to sectoral rules. Every one of those references is an edge that currently terminates in nothing. Map a second instrument and the edge resolves; map a third and two more resolve.

So each mapping increases the value of the ones already done, which is a genuine compounding effect rather than a linear pipeline of projects. It also changes the sales conversation, because the tenth customer is buying access to a connected corpus rather than a single document, while the first customer is buying a document. That is a strong reason to sequence the early instruments by how densely they cross-reference each other rather than by who pays first.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a licensing business.** The artefacts are open; access cannot be the product.
- **Not a one-off consultancy.** The recurring revenue is maintenance, and the amendment is what makes it recurring.
- **Not a competitor to the incumbent on its own terms.** The differentiation is openness and provenance, not a bigger proprietary database.
- **Not a claim that internal spend is quantified.** The comparison is plausible and unmeasured, and should be measured with a real prospect.
- **Not risk-free delivery.** A wrong mapping of a legal instrument is a different class of error from a wrong transcription.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Open artefacts versus recurring revenue | Maintenance is the honest answer and it is a weaker commercial position than a renewable licence, which is the price of the openness |
| Consortium funding versus who goes first | A public good funded collectively still needs a first payer, and the first payer subsidises everyone who waits |
| Ten to twenty thousand versus what it costs to produce | The price is anchored against the incumbent rather than against delivery cost, and the margin is unknown until one is delivered end to end |
| Freelance delivery versus legal-adjacent output | The network was designed for tools, and mapping a regulation carries consequences a transcription tool does not |
| Compounding versus sequencing | Densely cross-referenced instruments compound fastest and are not necessarily the ones anyone will pay for first |
| Copying the incumbent's workflow versus its overhead | The three-role triad is validated and is also why their process is slow and expensive, which is the thing being competed against |
| Demand from two users versus a market | Two unprompted requests is the right kind of signal and is not evidence of a market |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| What does one instrument actually cost to deliver? | End to end on the second standard, which sets the margin and validates the price |
| What do organisations currently spend internally? | The comparison the sales case rests on, unmeasured and answerable by asking two prospects |
| Which instrument is second? | Sequenced by cross-reference density for compounding, or by demand for revenue, and those differ |
| Single payer or consortium first? | The consortium fits the artefact and needs more coordination than a first sale can usually carry |
| How is maintenance priced? | Per instrument per year, per amendment, or by change volume, given the cost scales with change |
| What is the contributor agreement? | Licensing of contributed mappings, and the review path from contribution to publication |
| What is the liability position? | For a derived mapping of a legal instrument that somebody relies on |
