## What This Is

A briefing on introducing time, learning, past events, and credibility as first-class metrics in Risk Mandate, so the system learns whose statements to trust: **at every moment stakeholders are asked to make decisions and state facts under uncertainty, and the thing to measure is not whether they are right on the day but how often they are right over time, because time is brutal, a prediction that an event will happen next year is either borne out or not, and with good industry data across past years the record can be checked; from that record the system can assign credibility, a track-record-based weight on a source's statements, which decouples the weight a statement carries from the volume, power, or rank of the person making it, so the quiet person who is usually right is heard and the loudest or most senior voice does not automatically win; and this is what makes the system self-improving and self-detecting, because it learns who to believe from what actually happened rather than from who spoke loudest.** It is the fifth brief of 4 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.38 confidence and data-accuracy briefs, and the v0.33.40 near-misses brief). New contributions: **credibility as a track-record metric, the time-resolves-predictions loop, and decoupling weight from power.**

## The Metrics to Introduce

The proposal is to add a temporal, learning layer to the system. The project lead: **"some important metrics we should introduce, especially in a system like Risk Mandate with feedback loops, are time, learning, past events, and credibility."** These are not four separate features but one loop: time resolves predictions, past events are the ground truth, learning is what the system extracts, and credibility is what it assigns as a result.

## Decisions Are Made Under Uncertainty

The reason this matters is that the register is built from human judgments made without full information. The project lead: **"at any moment we are asking stakeholders to make decisions, to state points and facts, and what matters is what kind of decisions you are making with the data you had available."** A statement is not just true or false; it is a judgment made under a particular degree of uncertainty, which is the confidence band from the earlier brief, the certainty spread the project lead notes we had another name for. Credibility is the source-level companion to that node-level band.

## Measure Over Time: How Often Are They Right

The key move is to stop judging a single call and start tracking the record. The project lead: **"it is important to measure over time and to connect how many times people are right in their analysis, especially when you do not have enough data."** Under thin data, everyone is guessing, so the only honest way to tell a good judgment from a lucky one is to watch many judgments resolve. Counting how often a source's analyses hold up is the measurement, and it is exactly the calibration that forecasting research uses to separate real skill from confidence.

## Time Is Brutal

The resolution mechanism is simply the passage of time. The project lead: **"time is quite brutal, because if you look back at the data across past years, events either happened or not."** A prediction commits its author, and the future is an unforgiving grader: the event occurred or it did not, the incident happened or it did not. There is no arguing with the record once it exists.

## Good Data Is the Ground Truth

The loop only works if the past can be read accurately, which is why industry data matters. The project lead: **"that is why it is important to have data from the industry, because you either have the events, incidents, and initiatives, or you do not, and the better that data is, the more we can learn."** The richer and more accurate the record of what actually happened, across the organisation and the wider industry, the sharper the learning and the more reliable the credibility it produces.

## From Track Record to Credibility

The output of the loop is a weight. The project lead: **"the more we learn, the more we can see who should have more or less credibility for making statements."** Credibility is a track-record-based weight attached to a source, earned by being right over time and lost by being wrong, and it attaches through the same provenance and attribution the system already carries, so a statement arrives already annotated with how much its source's past record justifies believing it.

## Decouple Weight From Power

The point of measuring credibility is to break the link between how much a statement counts and who made it. The project lead: **"sometimes it is the quiet person in the room who has the most accurate statements, so we need to make sure the louder or more powerful person, or the one higher on the org chart, is not the one whose statements get the weight used to make decisions."** This is the highest-paid-person's-opinion problem stated precisely: volume, seniority, and force of personality routinely capture the oxygen, while the person who is quietly, consistently right goes unheard. A credibility metric grounded in track record gives the weight to the record, not the rank.

## Self-Improvement and Self-Detection

Together this turns the workflow into one that gets better on its own. The project lead: **"this is how the whole system becomes efficient, and how you build self-improvement and self-detection mechanisms into the workflow."** Because the system checks its own past statements against what happened, it learns who and what to believe, and it can flag when a highly credible source dissents or when a low-credibility source is driving a decision. Self-improvement is learning who is right; self-detection is noticing when the weighting has gone wrong.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not punishing being wrong.** Under uncertainty, being wrong sometimes is expected; it is calibration, not blame.
- **Not silencing anyone.** Low credibility weights a statement down, it does not gag the speaker.
- **Not an authority score.** Credibility is earned from the record, never conferred by rank.
- **Not certainty.** It measures how calibrated a source is, not whether any one call is right.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Track record versus new voices | A newcomer has no record; the system must not entrench incumbents |
| Calibration versus rare events | Scoring predictions about rare events needs many resolutions to be fair |
| Credibility versus context | A source strong in one domain may be weak in another; credibility is scoped |
| Decoupling from power | Powerful people may resist a metric that lowers the weight of their word |
