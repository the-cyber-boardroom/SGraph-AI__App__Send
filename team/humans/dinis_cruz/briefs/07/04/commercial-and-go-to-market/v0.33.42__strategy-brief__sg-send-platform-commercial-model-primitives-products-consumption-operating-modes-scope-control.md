## What This Is

The commercial-model positioning: **Risk Mandate is not a tiered SaaS product but a platform in the shape of a hyperscaler, a small set of strong Risk Acceptance primitives at the foundation, many small, super-focused products composed on top, and value-based consumption as the economic engine; the three tiers are reframed as three operating modes, self-hosted open source, managed consumption, and sovereign enterprise, that are orthogonal to the products, because every mode runs the same products on the same primitives, so the customer chooses how they operate, not which features they get; billing follows the hyperscaler pattern of compute, bandwidth, and storage, mapped to agent execution, workflow runs, token usage, vault and graph storage, evidence processing, and integration traffic, with no artificial layers and no rent-like monthly charge; the danger of unpredictable, illegible billing is answered by turning the product's own visibility and risk-acceptance discipline on itself, so cost is shown before it is incurred and accepted like any other risk; and the model's most important and least obvious property is that it keeps the tech stack and the enterprise-feature and certification burden under control, because the answer to a demand for a bespoke SSO integration or a specific certification is run the platform in your own environment and do the integration yourself.** It is the sixth brief of 4 July, deepening the monetization strategy (cross-ref: the v0.33.42 monetization and PMF briefs, the product roadmap, and the open-source and sovereignty material). New contributions: **the primitives-products-consumption platform, operating-modes-not-tiers, and scope-and-certification control by design.**

## The Reframe: Platform, Not Tiered SaaS

The starting move is to stop thinking in plan features and start thinking like a hyperscaler. A tiered SaaS gates features behind price bands and charges rent for access. A platform exposes primitives, lets products be composed on them, and charges for what is consumed. Risk Mandate is the second kind. This is not a pivot but the completion of a direction the site already states, that sovereignty is the floor and not the premium, and that what differs is how the product is operated, not which features are unlocked. The commercial model makes that explicit and extends it into a full catalogue.

## Layer One: The Risk Acceptance Primitives

The foundation is a small set of strong, stable primitives, the equivalent of a hyperscaler's core services: risk acceptance, mandate ownership, blast-radius mapping, evidence, provenance, workflow execution, agents, environments, organisations, teams, storage, and integrations. These have to be crisp and stable, because everything above composes on them, and their stability is what lets many products be built without touching the core. Defining and locking this canonical primitive list is the enabling step for the whole model.

## Layer Two: The Products

On top of the primitives sit many small, super-focused products, and this is the monetizable surface. A capability that a tiered SaaS would gate behind a plan becomes, here, a product. Each product is treated as a real business, with its own business model, architecture, service offering, customer value proposition, ROI case, profitability model, explicit dependency on the core primitives, and the token and unit consumption it drives. The catalogue grows by composition rather than by enlarging the core, which is the same discipline the roadmap uses: build the primitives once, build many surfaces on them.

## Layer Three: Consumption as the Economic Engine

Revenue is value-driven consumption, not a subscription boundary. Following the hyperscaler pattern, the billing primitives are the analogues of compute, bandwidth, and storage: agent execution, workflow runs, token usage, vault storage, graph storage, evidence processing, and integration traffic. The billable units layered on those include organisations, environments, teams, workflow executions, agents, support, customisations, and integrations. There are no artificial billing layers and no rent-like mechanisms such as a mandatory monthly charge disconnected from outcomes. Customers pay because they are using capabilities that create operational, compliance, security, or governance value, at the point where that value is created.

## The Three Operating Modes, Orthogonal to the Products

The former tiers are not tiers at all; they are operating modes that cut across every product. Self-hosted open source means the customer runs the primitives themselves. Managed consumption means Risk Mandate hosts and meters. Sovereign enterprise means a dedicated deployment in the customer's region and keys, with support. The crucial property is orthogonality: every mode runs the same products on the same primitives, so the customer is choosing how they operate, not which features they receive. Sovereignty is the floor in all three, and the mode changes only maintainability, scalability, and security posture, never the feature set.

## Legible Billing: Turning the Product on Itself

The obvious danger of the hyperscaler model is the hyperscaler bill: consumption pricing is powerful but famously unpredictable, and unpredictability contradicts the show-use-value-pay simplicity the product depends on. The answer is to apply the product's own discipline to itself. The same visibility and risk-based approach Risk Mandate brings to a customer's risks is turned on its own billing, so consumption is shown before it is incurred and accepted like any other risk, for an interval, by an owner. The principle that you cannot decide to accept a cost you were never shown applies to spend exactly as it applies to risk. A platform whose billing is itself a legible, owned, accept-for-an-interval decision turns the model's greatest weakness into a differentiator.

## The Strategic Payoff: Scope and Certification Control by Design

The most important property of this model is the one that is easiest to miss. Because the platform is open source and can run in the customer's own environment, the endless enterprise demands that bloat a SaaS tech stack and consume a security team are deflected by design. When a large customer asks for an integration with a particular single-sign-on provider, or for a specific security certification, the answer is not a roadmap commitment and a quarter of engineering; it is run the platform in your own environment and do the integration yourself. That single answer controls two things that sink most enterprise software companies. It controls the tech stack, because bespoke integrations live in the customer's deployment rather than in the core, so the core stays small and general. And it controls the certification burden, because a customer running the platform inside their own already-certified environment inherits their own certifications, rather than requiring Risk Mandate to chase every certification every customer names. Risk Mandate owns the primitives and the products; the customer owns their environment, their integrations, and their certifications. This is the same where-you-touch-it-you-own-it boundary the black-box model draws, now turned into a commercial and scope-control advantage, and it is only possible because the platform is open and runs anywhere.

## Each Product Is Its Own P&L

The catalogue is disciplined by making every product accountable on its own terms. For each one, the business model, architecture, service offering, customer value, ROI, profitability, primitive dependencies, and driven consumption are all defined, so a product earns its place by creating customer value and by driving meaningful consumption of the underlying primitives. A product that does neither does not ship. This keeps the catalogue from sprawling into features that cost more to maintain than they return.

## The Positioning, Stated

Risk Mandate is the platform. The Risk Acceptance primitives are the foundation. The hyperscaler-like products are the monetizable surface area. Token and infrastructure consumption are the economic engine. The three operating modes are how customers choose to run it, and every product runs in every mode.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not tiered SaaS.** Features are products, not bands, and modes are how you operate, not what you get.
- **Not rent.** No mandatory monthly charge disconnected from value; consumption follows outcomes.
- **Not a bespoke-integration shop.** Enterprise integrations and certifications are the customer's, in their environment.
- **Not illegible billing.** Cost is shown, owned, and accepted like any other risk.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Consumption versus predictability | Value-based billing is fair but must be made legible, or it becomes the AWS bill shock |
| Deflecting integrations versus adoption | Telling a customer to self-integrate controls scope but can raise the barrier to entry |
| Many products versus focus | A composable catalogue must not become sprawl; each product needs its own P&L |
| Primitive stability versus evolution | Products depend on stable primitives, which constrains how freely the core can change |
