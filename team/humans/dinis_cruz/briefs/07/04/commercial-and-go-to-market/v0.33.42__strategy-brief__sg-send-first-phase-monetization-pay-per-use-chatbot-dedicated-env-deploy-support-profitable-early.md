# First-Phase Monetization: Three Revenue Streams, Profitable From the Start

**version** v0.33.42
**date** 4 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Product, Architect, @Dev
**type** Strategy brief

## What This Is

The monetization strategy for the first phase, now that the website ships two things users can already use, the scenarios and the Risk Acceptance Maturity Model: **this is the discovery phase, but it should be profitable and take revenue from the start, because revenue is the best signal of value, because charging early forces the hard plumbing to be built while volume is low, onboarding, payment, provisioning, visualisation, and customer service, and because it is the best feedback loop; the product vision it serves is show, use, get value, pay; and there are three revenue streams forming a ladder of increasing isolation and price, first pay-per-use chatbot and agent interactions on top of the site's features, a pound to five pounds each, bring-your-own-key optional but charged on top, profitable because the cost to render is tiny and volume is what matters, second a dedicated secure environment spun up per customer and charged monthly for confidential data, isolated VPCs, dedicated accounts, or a deployment in the cloud and region they need, and third support to deploy on the customer's own environment, a maintained per-customer repo and a slimmed-down platform with exactly what they need.** It is the first brief of 4 July (cross-ref: the product-roadmap Phase 4, the v0.33.38 consulting-firms brief, and the v0.33.38 demo-workflow brief). New contributions: **the three revenue streams, profitable-from-the-start, and the isolation-and-price ladder.**

## The Baseline: Two Things Users Can Already Use

The strategy has something real to sell because the site now carries usable product. The project lead: **"the first website has been released, we added the Risk Acceptance Maturity Model to the site, and the first version of the scenarios, so users have two things they can sink their teeth into."** The scenarios and the RAMM are both live, both interactive, and both are the surfaces the monetization attaches to.

## Why Profitable From the Start

This is the discovery phase, but it should still take money. The project lead: **"this is the first phase, we are discovering the product, but we should make it profitable and have revenue from there on."** There are three reasons. The first is signal. The project lead: **"revenue is the best indicator that what we are doing adds value."** The second is that charging early forces the hard, unglamorous work to be built while it is still small. The project lead: **"the second is to sort out all those little workflows that are hard to do, at a time when you do not have a crazy amount of requests; onboarding, payment, account provisioning, capturing and visualising, customer service, all the little plumbing that sounds simpler than it is."** At low volume some of that plumbing can even be manual or lightly wired, and an agentic team can help, but it still has to be built and learned. The third is learning. The project lead: **"it is the best feedback loop you can get."**

## The Product Vision It Serves

Monetization is downstream of a simple product test, covered more fully in the next memo. The project lead: **"you should be able to just show the website to somebody, and they should be able to use it, get value, and pay for it."** Show, use, value, pay, in that order, with no sales call in between.

## Stream One: Pay-Per-Use Chatbot and Agent Interactions

The first stream monetises the moment a user wants to do something bespoke on top of what the site already shows. The project lead: **"the first stream is anything the user wants to do that requires an integration with the chatbot, and we already have two good examples, the scenarios and the maturity model, which are painful done purely programmatically but become far more customisable once you put a chatbot on top."** A chatbot turns a fixed feature into a bespoke one, tailored to the user's own scenario, and the same surface does translation and generation. The project lead: **"translate this to X, translate this to Y, or create an infographic for my scenario."** Keys are optional but not a way out of paying. The project lead: **"the user can bring their own key if they want, but we still charge on top of that."** And the principle is that compute is the customer's cost, not ours. The project lead: **"the customer should be paying for that, we should not be funding it."** The prices are small and the economics come from volume. The project lead: **"the customer will pay one, two, three, five quid max, it is not a lot, but it is the amount of those that matter, and the whole thing should be profitable from a pure cost point of view because the cost to render is really low."**

## Stream Two: Dedicated Secure Environment

The second stream begins when trust and confidentiality raise the stakes. The project lead: **"the next one is when someone says this is great but I want to put confidential data here, set up an environment for us, so we spin up an environment and charge per month; isolated, separate VPCs, dedicated AWS accounts, a GCP deployment, whatever account, region, and data sovereignty they need."** Here the customer pays monthly for an isolated place to ask the same questions of sensitive data, and the isolation can be as strong as a separate VPC, a dedicated cloud account, or a deployment in the provider and region their compliance requires.

## Stream Three: Deploy on Their Environment

The third stream is for customers who want to run it themselves. The project lead: **"the third is support to deploy it on their environment, where we have a separate Git repo with all the materials relevant to them and maintain it for them, so they get new versions and customisation, and a slimmed-down version of the platform with exactly what they need."** This is a maintained relationship rather than a hosted service: a per-customer repo that carries their materials and customisation, kept current by us, delivering a trimmed platform with only the features they use. It is the managed-service angle the consulting-firms brief describes, priced accordingly.

## The Ladder: Isolation and Price

The three streams are one ladder. Stream one is the shared, hosted product, cheapest and per-use. Stream two is a dedicated, hosted environment, monthly, for confidential data. Stream three is the customer's own environment, maintained by us. Each rung buys more isolation and more sovereignty, and each costs more, and the progression maps directly onto the sovereignty-from-keys-not-data thesis: the further up the ladder, the more the customer holds, and the higher the price for the assurance that they do.

## The Metric That Matters

Success in this phase is counted in paying users, not pounds per transaction. The project lead: **"the question is how many users can we get to actually use this and pay for it."** With render costs tiny and prices small, the whole thing is profitable per unit, so the growth question is simply how many users reach the point of paying at all.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not scale-first.** Low volume is a feature now; it is when the plumbing gets built.
- **Not subscription-only.** Stream one is pay-per-use; only the dedicated environment is monthly.
- **Not lock-in.** Bring-your-own-key and the deploy-on-their-environment option keep it open.
- **Not the product-vision memo.** Show-use-value-pay is developed separately next.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Profit now versus discovery | Charging early is a discipline, but it must not distort what the product becomes |
| Small prices versus real costs | Per-use must stay profitable after payment fees and support overhead |
| Manual plumbing versus scale | Manual workflows are fine at low volume but must be designed to be automated later |
| BYO-key versus charging | Charging on top of a customer's own key must feel fair, not like double-billing |
