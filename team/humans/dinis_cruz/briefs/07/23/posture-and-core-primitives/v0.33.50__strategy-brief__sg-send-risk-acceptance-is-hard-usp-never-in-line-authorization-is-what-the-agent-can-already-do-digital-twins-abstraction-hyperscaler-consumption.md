## What This Is

The core primitives of the RiskMandate solution, stated for readers who have not yet absorbed them: **risk acceptance is genuinely hard, and that difficulty is precisely the market gap, because asking a stakeholder to accept a risk makes that stakeholder accountable for what is written down, which triggers immediate and legitimate pushback on evidence, on accuracy, and on whether the right person is being asked, and most GRC platforms cannot meet that demand because they cannot follow the rabbit hole, which is fractal, and cannot connect risks across multiple altitudes, which is also why those tools are widely used and little loved, functioning as overhead rather than as instruments of decision; getting the business to actually accept risks is therefore the unique selling point, and it should be measured as one, with two KPIs, how many risks are accepted and what risk reduction the business achieves; the second principle is that we are never in line, because being in line demands tight integration, is highly customer specific, means making authorization decisions on the fly, and is already an industry others are pursuing with partial success, and the reason we do not need to be there follows from the definition of authorization we adopt, that authorization is the moment the capability is given to the agent, and that the union of everything an agent can actually do, through the privilege it was granted, the file system it discovered, the adjacent system it reached or exploited, and the account it recovered through a password reset, is the authorization, so that if an agent is able to do something it was authorised to do it, which means an agent that deleted a database was by our definition already authorised, and our contribution is helping the business see the exposure those capabilities create, lock it down, and reduce exploitation, collateral damage, and extended damage; because the model is predictive rather than reactive, the mapping should already have anticipated the damage, so what we consume from observability, near misses, and alerts is not traffic to police but confirmation that there is no blind spot, a check for error in the mappings, since an agent modelled with read access that is evidenced modifying a database changes the risk, and capability can be reverse-engineered from what agents actually do and from how they move between dev, QA, and production; the digital twin is the abstraction layer that makes all of this possible without integration, because no vendor, however dominant, will integrate with every system in every organisation, so the twin models only the primitives we care about, the capabilities that map to assets and to flows, rather than the entire capability surface of the system underneath; a great deal of risk will persist because the platforms below lack granularity and their databases and APIs are missing, hard to integrate, or poorly connected, which positions us to drive improvement in those platforms and, from sitting at the intersections, to accumulate the data that shows the art of the possible, which countermeasures work and which do not, what is exploited in reality and why, so that the business can prioritise; the moat is the combination rather than any single piece, the fractal risk register, the approach to risk, the connecting of the dots, the graph database, the open source, the speed of iteration, the breadth of integration, and an on-demand, scalable, worldwide practice; the commercial form is on-demand consumption, which de-risks adoption while still hooking the business to delivered value, cappable at the customer's discretion and value-driven by design, making this fundamentally a hyperscaler play billed on the same dimensions they use, compute, tokens, bandwidth, storage, access, and uptime; and the last primitive to capture is the case where people delegate to agents an authority they never held themselves.** It is the first brief of 23 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.46 ai-in-defence brief, the v0.33.48 governance-layer brief, the v0.33.48 grc-airgapped brief, the v0.33.49 risk-mandate brief, and the v0.33.47 authorization brief). New contributions: **risk acceptance named as the hard problem and therefore the USP, with accepted-risk count and business risk reduction as the two KPIs, the never-in-line property stated as a standing product commitment, authorization defined as the union of what an agent can already do including what it discovered, reached, exploited, or recovered, observability consumed to detect mapping error rather than to police traffic, capability reverse-engineered from behaviour and from dev-to-prod movement, the digital twin as an abstraction layer modelling only the capabilities that map assets and flows, the platform granularity gap and the intersection data position it creates, the moat as a combination rather than a component, on-demand consumption as a hyperscaler play, and delegated authority the delegator never held.**

## Risk Acceptance Is Hard, And That Is The Market

The brief opens by naming the difficulty rather than minimising it. The project lead: **"risk acceptance is hard; there is a reason why most GRC tools do not go there, there is a reason why there is a gap in the market."** The reason is that acceptance is not a data-entry act, it is the transfer of accountability onto a named human being. The project lead: **"when you are getting a stakeholder to accept the risk, you fundamentally are making that stakeholder accountable for what is in there."** Everything difficult about the product follows from that one fact, because a person about to become accountable will interrogate what they are being asked to sign. The project lead: **"there is a massive pushback in terms of requirements for evidence, requirements for accuracy, and requirements for making sure that it is the correct person that is actually going to be held accountable."** Those three demands, evidence, accuracy, and correct attribution, are not obstacles to work around; they are the specification. Legal points are factual and not legal advice.

## Why The Existing Tools Cannot Go There

The incumbents avoid acceptance because their data model cannot support the interrogation it provokes. The project lead: **"they do not have a way to follow the rabbit hole, which is fractal, and they do not have a way to connect the risks at the multiple altitudes, and that is why they do not go there."** A stakeholder challenged on why a risk is theirs needs the chain shown, downward into the technical specifics and upward to the board, and a flat register cannot produce it. The project lead on the tooling: **"if you have a spreadsheet, or a glorified spreadsheet, you cannot do this."** The consequence is a category that is used without being valued. The project lead: **"there is not a lot of love for GRC tools out there, because although they are used, they tend to be an overhead and not really a decision-making tool, which is kind of what we are doing here."** That last clause is the positioning in a sentence: overhead records what was decided elsewhere, while a decision-making tool is where the deciding happens.

## The USP, And The Two KPIs

If acceptance is the hard part, then doing it is the product. The project lead: **"one of our unique selling points is our ability to get the business to accept risks."** And because it is the USP, it is also the measure. The project lead: **"that should be one of our KPIs: how many risks are accepted, and what is the risk reduction of the business."** Two metrics, one counting decisions made and one counting exposure removed, which together resist the usual failure of governance metrics, since acceptance volume alone could be satisfied by trivia and reduction alone could be claimed without anybody owning anything.

## We Are Never In Line

The second primitive is a standing architectural commitment with commercial consequences. The project lead: **"we cannot be in line in the way this works, because being in line is very difficult, requires tight integration, and is very customer specific."** That territory is already contested. The project lead: **"there is already a whole industry that is trying to do that, and they are going to succeed to some levels, but that is not one tool to rule them all."** The specific thing being refused is runtime adjudication. The project lead: **"we cannot be the one that makes authorization decisions on the fly."** And the project lead returns to it as a property to hold onto. The project lead: **"we are never in line, and that is very important."**

## Authorization Is Whatever The Agent Can Already Do

The definition that makes never-in-line coherent is the sharpest idea in the memo. Authorization is not a decision made at request time; it is the moment capability was conferred. The project lead: **"we treat the moment of authorization as the moment that the privilege, the ability, the capability is given to an agent."** Crucially, capability means what is actually possible, not what was intended. The project lead: **"the agent is able to do it with the privilege that he got, with the file system that he was able to discover, with another system that it was able to access and exploit, and an account that he was able to recover maybe via email password reset."** The sum of those paths is the real grant. The project lead: **"if you take the union of all of the capabilities that the agents can do, we start there; we start by saying that is the authorization."** From which the definition falls out. The project lead: **"from our point of view, if the agent is able to do something, the agent was authorised to do that; that is our definition."** Applied to the worst case, it is deliberately unflinching. The project lead: **"when the agent deleted the database, from my point of view the agent has been authorised to delete the database."** The value is not in disputing that but in making it visible and shrinking it. The project lead: **"we can help business to lock things down, to start understanding the risks of having these agents with these capabilities, and then measuring and helping the business to make decisions, or to reduce the risks of exploitation, or the risks of collateral damage or extended damage caused by that particular access."**

```
   WHAT THE GRANT SAYS            WHAT THE AGENT CAN ACTUALLY DO
   the privilege issued           the privilege issued
                             +    the file system it discovered
                             +    the adjacent system it reached or exploited
                             +    the account it recovered by password reset
                             =    THE UNION  ->  this is the authorization

   if the agent is able to do it, it was authorised to do it
```

## The Feedback Loop: Checking The Mappings, Not Policing The Traffic

Being out of line does not mean being blind, and this is where consumed telemetry earns its place. The posture is predictive first. The project lead: **"we also operate proactively; we have an ability to predict what is going to happen, because unless there is an error in the mappings, we will already have predicted whatever damage has occurred."** So an incident is not new information about the world, it is a test of the model. The project lead: **"where we can get a feed, and where we can actually get some data from the systems, is to confirm that we do not have blind spots."** The question asked of every alert, near miss, and observation is therefore a question about our own map. The project lead: **"was there an original error in the mappings that we received?"** The worked example is exact. The project lead: **"if we had an agent that had a risk model where he only had read access to a database, but suddenly there is evidence that the agent modified a database, then the risk changes."** And the same logic runs on impact as well as access. The project lead: **"if we had an expectation that the database could not be deleted, and that then is proven to be possible, then the risk changes."** Which opens a genuinely powerful inference: capability can be recovered from behaviour. The project lead: **"we can also reverse engineer what the agents are able to do via the actions that the agents are doing, and via how they move from dev to QA to prod."**

```
   PREDICT            the mapping already says what this agent can do
      |
      v
   OBSERVE            alerts, near misses, behaviour, dev -> QA -> prod movement
      |               (consumed, never intercepted)
      v
   ASK               "was there an error in the mappings?"
      |
      +-- no  ->  the prediction held, no blind spot
      +-- yes ->  the capability was wider than modelled, RE-RATE the risk
```

## Digital Twins As The Abstraction Layer

The twin is what lets the model reach every system without connecting to any of them. The project lead: **"the digital twins is a way for us to interact with the system, a way to model the system, a way for us to consume what is out there."** The premise underneath is a refusal of the integration treadmill. The project lead: **"the beauty of this system is that we are not going to be integrated with the systems."** Integrations may exist without being the strategy. The project lead: **"we might be able to provide, and most likely in some cases we will provide, some open source integrations for this, but I do not think that should be our core element."** The reason is arithmetic rather than preference. The project lead: **"most organisations are going to have something a bit different; even if a company is a massive winner, we need to integrate with everything, every company, every system that exists."** So the twin becomes the boundary. The project lead: **"our digital twins is our abstraction layer to what is on the other side."** And the twin is deliberately partial, which is the move that makes it tractable. The project lead: **"we do not need our digital twins to reflect the entire capabilities of a system; we just need to reflect the type of capabilities that map the assets, that map the flows."**

## The Granularity Gap, And The Data Position It Creates

Some exposure will not be closable, and the memo is honest about why. The project lead: **"there is a lot of risks that will continue to exist because the underlying platforms today do not have enough granularity, and the underlying platforms do not have the databases, and even the APIs either do not exist, or they are hard to integrate with, or they are not easily connected."** That gap turns into influence over time. The project lead: **"we should be driving a lot of improvements on the capabilities."** And the position generates an asset that is hard to assemble any other way. The project lead: **"because we will sit on a lot of these intersections, we can also represent the art of the possible, and we will have data to support a lot of decisions from the point of view of what countermeasures work, what countermeasures do not work, what is being exploited in the real world, why certain things get exploited versus other things do not get exploited, and all that this is doing is helping the business to prioritise."**

## Delegated Authority Nobody Had

The final primitive is short in the memo and large in implication. The project lead: **"we also capture the cases where people are delegating authority that they do not have to agents."** This is a different failure from over-permissioning, because the defect is not that the grant was too wide but that the grantor never held what they handed over. The result is a break in the acceptance chain: an agent operating on an authority no accountable person ever owned, which means no one upstream accepted it and, by the fractal register logic, it has nowhere legitimate to roll up to. Detecting it requires modelling both the agent's capability and the delegator's own mandate, and comparing them.

## The Moat Is The Combination, Not Any One Piece

The memo asks the right sceptical question about itself. The project lead: **"it is important to review why, if it was so easy, why have the existing GRC tools, the existing issue tracking tools, not done it?"** The answer offered is explicitly compound. The project lead: **"our approach to the fractal risk register, our approach to the risks, our approach to connect the dots, our graph database, our open source, when you put them all together, and also the ability to quickly iterate and the ability to integrate with just about everything, and have an on-demand, scalable, and also worldwide practice, that ultimately is our moat."** Each element on its own is replicable, and the claim rests on the assembly plus the pace, which is a position that must be defended continuously rather than a barrier that holds by itself.

## On-Demand Consumption: The Hyperscaler Play

The commercial model is presented as load-bearing rather than incidental, because it removes the reason buyers stall. The project lead: **"the on-demand consumption is super critical to make this work, because that is what de-risks the adoption of the platform."** The hook is meant to come from delivered value, not from contract lock. The project lead: **"the platform should have a level of hook to the business, but it should be based on the value that it is providing."** Control stays with the customer. The project lead: **"companies can cap it, but it should be on-demand consumption, which basically means it is value-driven."** And the incentive alignment is the point. The project lead: **"it rewards the businesses and us for finding ways to use more and more the platform, just like the hyperscalers."** The conclusion is stated plainly. The project lead: **"fundamentally, this is a hyperscaler play, and we can charge for a lot of the same things they do: we charge for tokens, for bandwidth, for storage, for access, for uptime, for compute."**

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not in line.** No runtime authorization decisions, no enforcement point in the request path.
- **Not an integration play.** Twins abstract the systems; open-source integrations may exist but are not the core element.
- **Not a diagnostic or a detection tool.** Telemetry is consumed to test our mappings, not to police traffic or catch attacks.
- **Not a seat licence.** On-demand consumption, cappable by the customer, billed on hyperscaler dimensions.
- **Not an endorsement of what agents can do.** Calling a capability authorised is descriptive of what is possible, not a judgement that it should be.
- **Not a complete model of any system.** The twin reflects only the capabilities that map assets and flows.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Our definition of authorization versus everyone else's | Ours is descriptive, naming what is possible; boards, auditors, and IAM teams hear authorization as a decision someone made, so the redefinition is powerful and will need translating in every conversation |
| Never in line versus the demand to intervene | A customer who watches a predicted risk materialise will ask why we did not stop it; the answer is principled but commercially uncomfortable in the moment |
| Predicted-it-already versus mapping error | The claim that we would already have predicted the damage holds only if the mappings are right, and the same memo concedes mappings will be wrong; the defensible posture is predicted-or-learned, not predicted |
| Partial twins versus completeness | Modelling only the capabilities that map assets and flows is what makes this tractable, and is also precisely where an unmodelled capability would hide |
| Consumption billing versus buyer predictability | On-demand de-risks adoption but makes budgets unpredictable; caps answer the fear and cap the revenue at the same time |
| Acceptance count as a KPI versus gaming | Counting accepted risks rewards volume, and a register can be padded with trivial acceptances unless the metric is weighted by exposure |
| The intersection data position versus consent | Cross-customer data on what actually gets exploited is a genuine asset and a genuine governance question about what may be pooled and on whose terms |
| Moat as combination versus moat as barrier | Every element is individually replicable, so the moat is a race to stay ahead rather than a wall, which is a weaker claim than it first sounds |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| How is the union of capabilities actually computed? | Discovered file systems, reachable systems, recoverable accounts, and exploit paths appear in no grant table; this is the hardest part of the definition |
| What is the mapping-error feedback schema? | Which observability, near-miss, and alert signals are consumed, in what form, and what triggers a re-rate |
| How is business risk reduction measured? | The second KPI needs a baseline and a defensible unit before it can be reported to anyone |
| Which twin primitives are canonical? | The minimum set of capability types that map assets and flows, and how new system classes are onboarded to it |
| What exactly is metered? | Which of compute, tokens, bandwidth, storage, access, and uptime are billed, at what granularity, and how caps interact with value delivered |
| How is delegated-authority-nobody-had detected? | Comparing an agent's capability against the delegator's own mandate requires both to be modelled and current |
| What may be pooled across customers? | The contractual and ethical boundary around the exploitation and countermeasure dataset |
| Where does the acceptance USP meet the airgapped MVP? | The 17 Jul demo assumes no integration; the mapping-error loop assumes consumed telemetry, so the sequencing between them needs stating |
