# Not A Runtime Authority Control Plane: A Response To The Investor Analysis, Why We Consume And Integrate Rather Than Enforce, The Meta-Risk That Funds Discovery, And Decision-Making As The Real Market

**version** v0.33.50
**date** 23 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Strategy, Product, Commercial, Board, Investors

**type** Strategy brief

*Third of 23 July, a point-by-point response to an external investor analysis of RiskMandate, recorded while reading it. External vendor claims are grounded and cited. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

---

## What This Is

A direct response to an outside investor analysis of RiskMandate, which was built almost entirely from public material and reaches one central recommendation that cannot be accepted: **the analysis argues that RiskMandate must become the runtime authority control plane governing consequential agent actions, and that framing fails on two words, because runtime authority implies that we make the decisions, which we do not, and governing consequential actions implies acting on what agents are doing right now, whereas our whole logic is that the decision already happened at the moment of authorization, when the agent was given the access, so containment and blast-radius limitation are another tool's responsibility, and that tool should itself appear in our model as a risk reducer whose value is conditional on it doing what it claims to do; the ServiceNow-feature dismissal is superficial, because if all we sold were risk acceptance it would be correct, but even a ServiceNow that added acceptance would not have the graph or the follow-through on the evidence chain, and these platforms are not designed for hundreds, thousands, or tens of thousands of hyperlinked risks, nor for the fractal element, and in practice they are glorified registers that lack the speed, the agility, and the stakeholder-specific translation we can build, so the answer is not to compete with them but to integrate, since the morphability of the platform means we use whatever already exists inside the company, and the same applies to the identity, discovery, and enforcement vendors, all of which large organisations already own, all of which we consume, and on top of which we add our own layer; the enforcement layer the analyst wants us to own is a genuinely different tool, already attempted by several funded specialists, and usually attempted with only a subset of the information, which is why they struggle and why we can make them considerably more realistic by supplying the authority and purpose mapping they lack, without ever being in line ourselves; on the three specific questions raised, who authorised this particular action is one we should already be able to answer because we mapped it and it was already authorised, should this action proceed right now is explicitly not our role, and under what business mandate may it act is precisely what we build the maps, graphs, and operating models for; the no-deny principle survives the analyst's reframing, because silent acceptance is already happening everywhere and the more common failure is in fact silent non-acceptance, and no-deny exists precisely to force the conversation that neither of those produces; the discovery objection is answered not by building another scanner but by the meta-risk, since telling someone that they do not know how many agents they have is itself a risk that triggers and funds the discovery project; and the TAM analysis is the wrong shape, because it prices a workflow rather than the thing we actually do, which is enabling effective and cost-effective decision-making, in strategic, cost, and risk terms, across organisations of every size, which is a far wider market than agent governance and deserves its own mapping.** It is the third brief of 23 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.50 core-primitives brief, the v0.33.50 no-judgement brief, the v0.33.48 governance-layer brief, the v0.33.48 vendor-sprawl brief, and the v0.33.46 not-in-line brief). New contributions: **the refusal of the runtime-authority framing on the grounds that it implies we decide, the containment tool modelled as a conditional risk reducer inside our own register, morphability as the integration doctrine of consuming whatever the company already runs, the three-question split between what we can answer and what is not our role, silent non-acceptance named as the more common failure than silent acceptance, the meta-risk that triggers and funds agent discovery, a gap between mapping and reality treated as an incident in its own right, and decision-making rather than risk acceptance or runtime authority proposed as the actual market.**

## The Core Correction: Runtime Authority Implies We Decide

The analysis lands close to the truth and then attaches the wrong noun. The project lead: **"this is kind of there, but the problem here is runtime authority, because what this is implying is that the risk mandate is making decisions, and it is not."** The second half of the phrase carries the same error. The project lead: **"it is not that it governs the actions; it actually allows the business to evolve into a point where those actions match the business risk profile."** And the reason the distinction holds is the definition established this morning. The project lead: **"our whole logic is that that happened in the moment of authorization; the moment of authorization is the moment that the agent was given access to it."** What happens after that moment belongs to someone else. The project lead: **"it is another tool's responsibility to try to limit the blast radius and try to contain it."** The move that makes this constructive rather than merely a refusal is that the other tool enters our model as a rated object. The project lead: **"we should capture that and have that as a risk to say, hey, if you use this tool, you can reduce the risk as long as the tool does what it is supposed to do."** That is the `conditional_on` edge already in the grammar: a control whose value depends on a rateable assumption, which is exactly how an enforcement gateway should appear in a register we own.

```
   THE ANALYST'S ASK              WHAT WE ACTUALLY DO
   decide at request time    ->   the decision already happened at grant time
   enforce the decision      ->   another tool enforces; we rate that tool
   govern the action         ->   the business evolves so actions match appetite
   be in the path            ->   never in line
```

## Not A ServiceNow Feature, And The Reason Is Scale And Shape

The dismissal is treated as shallow rather than wrong-headed. The project lead: **"I think that is a very superficial view of what it is, and there is a reason why ServiceNow has not done this before."** The conditional is conceded honestly. The project lead: **"if what we are talking about is risk acceptance, it will be a ServiceNow feature."** What survives the concession is everything else. The project lead: **"even if ServiceNow adds the risk acceptance, it probably will not have the graph and the follow-up of the evidence chain the way we have."** The structural objection is about volume and shape rather than features. The project lead: **"they are not designed for hundreds, thousands, tens of thousands of risks, where they are all hyperlinked; they are not designed for this sort of fractal element of risk registers."** And the assessment of the category is blunt. The project lead: **"a lot of these companies, they are glorified registers, and they tend not to have the speed, the agility, the UIs that we can create, especially when you talk about translating the whole thing to specific stakeholders."**

## Morphability: We Consume What The Company Already Runs

The answer to every named competitor is the same, and it is a doctrine rather than a dodge. The project lead: **"the morphability of the platform is that we can use as much as exists in the company, so if you have a successful risk acceptance workflow, then great, we will use it."** Applied to the vendor list, the posture is uniform. The project lead: **"we connect to that, CyberArk discovering agents we will grab information from it, and a lot of organisations, the big organisations, will have some of these, so we will integrate with all of them, and we leverage all of them, and then we add our stuff on top, and that is the power of what we do."** This is the governance-layer position from 12 July extended one step: there, the argument was that we consume findings from the diagnostic stack rather than collecting our own; here, consumption widens to include identity, discovery, and even an existing acceptance workflow if the customer already has one that works.

## The Enforcement Layer Is A Different Tool, And It Is Already Contested

On the analyst's explicit definition of the winning category, the response is that he has described a product that exists and is not ours. The project lead: **"this is a different tool; in fact, there are a lot of tools that are already trying to do this."** The reason they struggle is informational rather than technical. The project lead: **"usually those tools try to do this with a subset of information, which is why also they are going to struggle."** Which is where the relationship becomes complementary rather than competitive. The project lead: **"we can actually provide a lot of the pieces of the puzzle for an agent that wants to do this to actually be able to do it."** And then the boundary is restated. The project lead: **"but that is not what we do, because again, we are not in line."** The same applies to the analyst's proposed runtime mandate checks. The project lead: **"the idea is no; we work with companies who do that, we work with teams who do that, but we should not be in line on this."** And to the list of enforcement verbs, allow, limit, escalate, revoke, quarantine, stop, the answer concedes the requirement and rejects the assignment. The project lead: **"I agree, but that is why we are not a runtime security platform; in fact, we allow the runtime security platforms to be a lot more effective, and we highlight those gaps."**

## What We Can Answer, And What Is Not Our Role

The analyst's own three-layer table is answered line by line, and the split is clean.

| The question | Our position |
|---|---|
| Who authorised this particular action? | We can answer it, and should already know, because we mapped it and it was already authorised. The project lead: **"we already mapped it, the thing is already authorised."** |
| Should this action proceed right now? | Not our role. The project lead: **"that is not our role, it is another tool; we can make those tools much more realistic, but not do it."** |
| Under what business mandate may it act? | Ours, and the thing we build. The project lead: **"we can help to create those maps, those graphs, those operating models."** |

Two of the three are already the product. The one that is refused is the one that requires being in the request path.

## Silent Non-Acceptance Is The More Common Failure

The analyst proposes replacing no deny with no silent acceptance, and the response is that the reframing is close but diagnoses the wrong failure. The project lead: **"that is kind of it, but the silent acceptance is already happening; in fact, what usually happens is silent non-acceptance."** That is a sharper observation than the one it corrects. Silent acceptance means a risk is carried without anybody saying so; silent non-acceptance means nobody has even been asked, so there is no decision at all, merely an exposure with no name attached to it. And the no-deny principle exists precisely to prevent that outcome. The project lead: **"no deny is important because you have to force that conversation, you have to force the thing."** The analyst read no-deny as a missing capability. It is not a statement about what the platform can do; it is a statement about what is true of a risk already running in production, where the only real variable is how long it is carried and by whom.

## Discovery: The Meta-Risk That Funds The Project

On automatic discovery the concession is immediate and the answer is unexpected. The project lead: **"yes, we should feed the data that exists from Microsoft Copilot, AWS, MCP, but again, there are tools who already do this; we can have our own set of tools to do this, but we will consume what already exists."** Then the mechanism that makes the objection dissolve. The project lead: **"we have a way to gamify this, because we can give somebody a risk that says you do not know how many agents you have in your portfolio, and immediately that will trigger the discovery."** The position this creates is a level above the tooling. The project lead: **"we are the meta risk; we allow the creation of the project that is going to discover this and that funds this."** This is the discovery-project-as-a-node primitive from 12 July arriving at its commercial form: not knowing your agent population is itself a rateable, ownable, accountable risk, and the moment someone accepts it, they have to fund the inventory work that resolves it.

## Business Authority, Not Technical Access

Where the analyst says the graph should map business authority rather than technical access, the response is agreement plus a description of the existing mechanism. The project lead: **"absolutely, and that is what we do; we distinguish between a technical stakeholder, which is the one that confirms what it is and the reality and the multiple situations, and then we connect that to the business owner and the owners and the org chart."** The technical stakeholder confirms what is true; the business owner accepts what it means. That separation is the acceptance chain, and it is already the corpus position rather than a change of direction.

## The Graph Already Holds The History, And A Mapping Gap Is An Incident

The analyst argues that the valuable data is not the semantic graph but the accumulated history of what agents were permitted to do, who delegated the authority, what justified it, what controls applied, and which mandates proved safe. The response is that this is a distinction without a difference. The project lead: **"well, I agree, it is part of the graph; all this information is also included in the graph."** And on controls specifically, the model is forward-looking rather than merely archival. The project lead: **"when you say what controls were applied, we should be able to say what controls exist or could be applied."** Then the observation that generalises this morning's mapping-error loop into something stronger. The project lead: **"when you map reality to what we know about, any gap there is almost an incident in itself, because it means there is a gap in the mapping, so there is a gap in the workflow."** With the accountability mechanism that makes it work. The project lead: **"once you make people accountable for capturing that, people take that very seriously."**

## Where The Analysis Is Right

The response concedes several points without hedging. On making the architecture commercially legible, replacing semantic graph of graphs, digital twins, no database, and token-based deployment with outcome language such as customer-controlled encrypted authority graph, immutable decision provenance, data sovereignty, and portable evidence, the project lead: **"yeah, there is some good stuff here; I agree with this."** On the wedge, the agreement is specific. The project lead: **"production AI agents with write privileges, yes, that is exactly the ones."** With one correction to the verb. The project lead: **"we could do this, but govern means different things; the govern is understand the blast radius, trigger the things, it is not govern in terms of being in line."** So the offer stands with the meaning fixed: discover and provide governance over every production agent capable of changing critical systems. The analyst's points on team gaps, on separating RiskMandate from SGraph, and on the need for design-partner evidence are not disputed anywhere in the memo, and should be read as accepted.

## Evidence The Analysis Did Not Have

The vendor landscape moved between the analysis and this response, and it moves in both directions.

It sharpens the ServiceNow warning beyond what the analyst wrote. At its Knowledge 2026 event ServiceNow expanded AI Control Tower across five dimensions, discover, observe, govern, secure and measure, adding thirty enterprise integrations spanning AWS, Google Cloud and Azure alongside SAP, Oracle and Workday, extending discovery to non-human identities and connected devices. It acquired Traceloop to add runtime observability into agent behaviour, and integrated Veza to bring patented access-graph technology, scoped permissions and least-privilege enforcement into the tower, with agent kill switches in the same release. These are the company's own announcements rather than audited fact, but they matter here: an incumbent now has an access graph and a runtime kill switch, which means the claim that they will not have the graph is narrowing, and the differentiation has to rest on the acceptance chain, the fractal registers and the stakeholder translation rather than on possessing a graph at all.

It also validates the consume-and-integrate doctrine. Microsoft Entra Agent ID applies conditional access to agent identities, evaluating agent context and risk before a token is issued, and can block high-risk agents or revoke a permission grant across every agent built from a blueprint. CyberArk positions an AI Agent Gateway and Identity Broker as an enforcement point between agents and the tools they use, granting per-task privilege with zero standing privileges and routing agent access to registered MCP servers through a proxy that records session and tool activity. Arcade operates an MCP runtime performing per-action authorization at execution time, scoped to the intersection of what the user may do and what the agent is permitted to do, and raised a sixty million dollar Series A in June 2026, taking total funding to seventy-two million. Discovery, identity and enforcement are therefore each already commodity or well-capitalised specialist territory, which is a strong argument for consuming all three rather than rebuilding any of them.

And one data point cuts directly against the analyst's own thesis. He cites Vanta as the comparison for scaling from a narrow wedge into a platform, and the figure checks out: Vanta announced crossing three hundred million dollars of ARR in April 2026, roughly tripling in two years, with more than sixteen thousand customers, and it was named a leader in Forrester's GRC platforms wave in the second quarter of 2026. But Vanta reached that scale as a trust, compliance and GRC platform, not by becoming a runtime enforcement point. That does not prove a decacorn is reachable without runtime, since the analyst's own arithmetic needs five hundred million or more. It does undercut the segmentation that caps a governance-layer business at three hundred to eight hundred million, because a governance-layer business has already passed the top of that band and is still accelerating.

## The TAM Is The Wrong Shape: Decision-Making Is The Market

The memo does not accept the market sizing and asks for the exercise to be redone. The project lead: **"I want to do a separate mapping on this, because I feel this is the AWS math, the on-demand billing, and I think we need to add a whole bunch of other angles here, including the multilingual element, including the distributed, including the decision making."** The reach is wider than the enterprise segments the analyst modelled. The project lead: **"every organisation, from big organisations to small organisations at all levels, so we actually have quite a wide market that we can go after."** And the reason is a reframing of the category itself, which is the most consequential idea in the memo. The project lead: **"we are basically about decision making; decision making happens across the whole organisation."** Stated as the core proposition. The project lead: **"what we do is we enable effective and cost-effective, cost-effective from a strategic cost and enormous risk, decision making, and I think that is very important."** The analyst segmented into risk-acceptance workflow, agent governance, and runtime authority, and priced the first as narrow. If the category is decision-making rather than agent governance, all three segments are the wrong axis, because decisions are made at every altitude of every organisation regardless of whether agents are involved.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a runtime authority control plane.** We do not decide at request time, and we do not enforce.
- **Not a competitor to the identity, discovery, or enforcement vendors.** We consume all of them and add a layer above.
- **Not a replacement for an existing GRC workflow.** If a customer already has one that works, we use it.
- **Not a rejection of the whole analysis.** Architecture legibility, the write-privileges wedge, business-authority mapping, and the team gaps are accepted.
- **Not a claim that the graph alone is the moat.** An incumbent now has an access graph; the differentiation is the acceptance chain and the translation.
- **Not the final word on market size.** The TAM mapping is explicitly deferred to its own exercise.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Never in line versus where the budget sits | Enforcement vendors sell into a security budget with an urgent production blocker; a layer that explicitly refuses to enforce must find a different urgency, and the analyst's GTM readiness score of four is partly about exactly this |
| Consume-and-integrate versus dependency | If discovery, identity, and enforcement are all consumed, our value sits entirely in the layer above, and every supplier below has a shorter path up into it than we have down into them |
| The graph as differentiator versus incumbents acquiring graphs | An access graph inside the incumbent control tower narrows the gap this response relies on; the differentiation has to move to the acceptance chain and stakeholder translation, and to say so plainly |
| The meta-risk is elegant versus whether it converts | Telling a customer they cannot count their agents is a strong trigger; it is not yet evidence they will fund the resulting project through us rather than through a discovery vendor |
| Decision-making as the category versus buyer legibility | It is the widest and probably truest framing, and also the least legible, since no buyer has a decision-making budget line to spend from |
| The scores versus what is public | A meaningful part of the low scoring measures how little of this thinking has been published, which argues for publishing rather than for rebutting; an investor can only underwrite what they can see |
| Combination as moat versus each part being replicable | The same tension flagged this morning: the combination holds only while the pace holds, and the analyst is not wrong that no single element is a barrier |
