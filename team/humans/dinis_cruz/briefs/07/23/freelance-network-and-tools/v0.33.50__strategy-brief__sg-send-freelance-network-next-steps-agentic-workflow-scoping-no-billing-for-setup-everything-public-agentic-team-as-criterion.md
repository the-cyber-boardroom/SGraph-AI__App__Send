# Next Steps With The Freelance Network: An Hour To Scope If You Have The Agentic Workflow, No Billing For Your Own Setup, Everything Public And Creative Commons, And The Agentic Team As A Criterion

**version** v0.33.50
**date** 23 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Commercial, Operations, Partners, Strategy

**type** Strategy brief

*Fourth of 23 July, the operating procedure that follows the partnership decision. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

---

## What This Is

The operating procedure for engaging a freelancer or partner company, now that the commercial forms have been set out: **whether the arrangement is direct payment or revenue share matters far less than what happens next, so this defines the next step; the first thing is defining project scope, deliverables, measurables, OKRs, and objectives, and that should take about half an hour to an hour on their side, because the expectation is that they already run an agentic workflow, and if they do not yet have one they are welcome to build it but they cannot bill for it, since the principle is that I pay only for actual work done on my material and never for the environment they need in order to work this way, which is fair because that capability then serves every other customer they will ever have, so the arrangement effectively gets them to invest in the next generation of services they can sell to everyone else; everything created in the engagement is released immediately as open source and Creative Commons, with the communication itself happening through open repositories, which means their own materials come back to them for reuse elsewhere and the exchange runs in both directions; the working flow is a short conversation about which tool they want to focus on and which services they want to provide, after which they hand that to their own agent, run a couple of rounds, and publish into the repository both the plan they propose and an infographic, where the infographic carries the top-level analysis and the documents carry the detail, with as much documentation, mapping, and referencing as they can supply, generated by the agents but peer reviewed by the humans, especially the infographic and the top-level analysis; the same then happens on my side, where their materials are consumed into an existing session that already runs its own agents; a further expectation is that they arrive with an agentic team already set up for the project, since an agentic team is fundamentally a set of role definitions in markdown that each hold a different opinion, and the brief itself should already show those agents, which is one of the explicit criteria; the criteria run in order, the first being self-fulfilling because anyone unhappy with the workflow will simply not engage, so engagement itself demonstrates agreement with the terms and the rules, followed by the quality of the proposal, which will be reviewed and classified with an agent's help and then commented on publicly, since every one of these exchanges is public and Creative Commons released, and followed by the depth and the relevance of the agentic team they assembled, which has to fit the project because every agent adds value and every agent also adds overhead; they also need payment rails already in place, a Stripe-like card or bank transfer arrangement, so that once the hours are done they can be entered and paid; and the proposal should map the next batch of investment in commercial terms, so many hours to achieve this, against this much revenue over this period, because what is being signed off is a set of specific projects that are time-bound, budget-defined, and clear about their objectives, inputs, and outputs, which is deliberately the same model that will run at much larger scale once some of these attract investment, with only a couple of extra zeros on the budget.** It is the fourth brief of 23 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.49 commercialising-the-primitives brief, the v0.33.49 consulting-model brief, the v0.33.49 open-source brief, the v0.33.48 fractal-semantic-graphs brief, and the v0.33.44 evidence-packs brief). New contributions: **the engagement procedure that follows the partnership decision, the one-hour scoping expectation as a proxy for agentic maturity, the principle that a partner's own tooling setup is never billable because it accrues to their whole business, open repositories and Creative Commons release as the default medium of communication in both directions, the infographic-for-analysis and documents-for-detail deliverable pair, the agentic team as a visible part of the proposal and an explicit selection criterion, the value-versus-overhead trade in sizing an agentic team, public review and public feedback as part of the model, payment rails as a precondition, and time-bound budget-defined projects as the unit that scales unchanged with extra zeros.**

## The Model Matters Less Than The Next Step

The three partnership forms set out on 17 July are treated here as settled enough to move past. The project lead: **"this could either be me paying them or having a revenue share, so the model does not really matter as much as the next step."** That is a useful simplification at this stage, because the choice between paying cash and sharing revenue changes the economics without changing any of the operating mechanics below it. The scoping, the deliverable, the review, and the sign-off are identical either way.

## Step One: Scope, Deliverables, OKRs, In About An Hour

The engagement opens on definition rather than on work. The project lead: **"the first thing that needs to happen is that we need to define what is the project scope, what are they going to focus on, what are the deliverables, what are the measurables, what are the OKRs, what are the objectives of what we are trying to do here."** And the time budget for that definition is deliberately tight, because it is a test. The project lead: **"I would not expect this to take more than half an hour or an hour on their side if they have an agentic workflow."** The clause at the end is the whole point: an hour is achievable only by someone whose own tooling is already working, so the timebox is a proxy for maturity rather than an arbitrary constraint.

## Do Not Bill Me For Building Your Own Capability

The billing boundary is the sharpest principle in the memo, and it is argued rather than asserted. The project lead: **"I should only be billed for actual work done on my stuff, not for the work that they might need to do at their end to set up an environment to work like this."** Missing the workflow is not a disqualification, it is simply not chargeable. The project lead: **"the other angle here is they can create it, but they cannot bill for it."** The justification is that the capability is an asset that outlives this engagement entirely. The project lead: **"the good news is that for those guys, that value added, they can work with any customer."** Which reframes what is happening commercially. The project lead: **"I am almost getting them to invest in the development of the next phase and the next set of services that they can provide, and that is why they should not be charging it."**

```
   BILLABLE                          NOT BILLABLE
   work done on my material     |    building your own agentic workflow
   the plan, the analysis       |    setting up your environment
   the documentation and maps   |    learning to work this way
                                |
                                +--> accrues to YOUR business,
                                     serves every future customer
```

## Everything Public, Everything Creative Commons

The default is openness, and it applies to the communication itself and not only to the output. The project lead: **"every interaction, every material, everything that gets created is immediately released, Creative Commons and open source, so we communicate through open repos, and we communicate through Creative Commons materials."** The reciprocity is explicit and is part of the offer. The project lead: **"which actually means that they could also use it for other stuff, so in a way, it goes both ways; they also get to use this at their end."** This is the open-source thesis of 17 July applied to an engagement rather than to a codebase: the partner leaves with reusable material, which is a real part of the compensation and helps justify the unbillable setup above.

## The Proposal: An Infographic For The Top, Documents For The Detail

The flow from conversation to proposal is short and specified. The project lead: **"it should be a very simple conversation, which is what tool particularly they want to focus on, and I will write more about the different tools and different maturity levels that they are, so they can pick the one they want and what services they want."** From there the work is handed to their own tooling. The project lead: **"they should give that to their agent, for example Claude or ChatGPT or whatever they have created, have just a couple of rounds of that, and then publish in the repo the plan that they propose and the infographic of it."** The two artifacts have distinct jobs. The project lead: **"the infographic provides the top level analysis, and the document provides the details."** Depth is expected rather than optional. The project lead: **"you should include as much documentation, as much mappings, as much references as possible."** And generation does not remove the human obligation. The project lead: **"this documentation should be generated, of course peer reviewed by them, especially the infographics and the top level analysis."** The receiving side mirrors it. The project lead: **"what I will do on my side is the same thing; I will consume their materials, I feed that to a closed session that I have already set up that has a bunch of agents."**

```
   pick a tool + services   ->   their agent, a couple of rounds
                                          |
                                          v
                            publish to the open repo:
                              INFOGRAPHIC  = top-level analysis
                              DOCUMENTS    = detail, mappings, references
                                          |
                            peer reviewed by them (not just generated)
                                          |
                                          v
                            consumed into my own agent session
                                          |
                            reviewed, commented publicly, assigned
                                          |
                            hours done -> entered -> paid
```

## The Agentic Team Is Part Of The Proposal

The expectation goes beyond having tooling to having a structured team of roles. The project lead: **"I would expect for them to already have an agentic team set up for this project, because agentic teams are fundamentally just a bunch of role markdown definitions, but each one has a different opinion."** That last clause is the reason a team beats a single agent: differing opinions produce the friction that catches what a single perspective misses. And the team is not a private implementation detail, it is visible in the deliverable. The project lead: **"if you look at the brief, the brief should already have the different agents; in fact, that is one of the criteria I have."**

The roles vary by project, and the examples given span both generic and project-specific sets. The project lead: **"these days, for these projects, you should have a designer, you should have a developer, and depending on the project you could have a go-to-market product specialist, you can have a manager, I usually call the architect or the conductor."** From the SGraph work the set is richer. The project lead: **"you have the alchemist, you have the sherpa, you have the advocate, you have the cartographer, you have the journalist, you have the historian, the librarian, one of the best ones."** Plus the delivery roles. The project lead: **"in the dev team you have the individual types of resources, from developer to QA to security, AppSec, to DPO."**

## Every Agent Adds Value, And Every Agent Adds Overhead

The sizing principle is stated in one line and deserves to be a standing rule. The project lead: **"every agent adds value, but then every agent adds overhead, so we need to connect to that."** This is why the team is a judgement rather than a checklist: an oversized team burns tokens, time, and attention producing opinions nobody reads, while an undersized one misses the perspectives that would have caught the flaw. Assessing whether the team fits the project is therefore assessing whether the partner understands the trade, which is a better signal of competence than the length of the role list.

## Payment Rails As A Precondition

The last operational requirement is unglamorous and blocking. The project lead: **"they need to have a Stripe-like credit card or bank transfer solution in place so that I can use to pay it."** With the mechanics stated plainly. The project lead: **"once we got the hours done, I should be able to go there, put the hours in, and then pay them, and have that managed like that."** Given that the whole model is on-demand hours drawn against a ceiling, a partner without a way to take payment per block cannot participate in it at all.

## The Criteria

The selection criteria run in order, and the first one is unusual because it evaluates itself. The project lead: **"the first criteria is, are they even happy, are they happy to engage with this?"** And it resolves without any assessment being made. The project lead: **"the first criteria is self-fulfilling, because if they are not happy with the workflow, they will not be engaged; the fact that they are engaged means they passed the first one, and they are happy with the terms, and we are happy with the rules of engagement."**

The second is the proposal itself, and the assessment is both agent-assisted and public. The project lead: **"I am going to look at the quality of the proposals; I will ask Claude to review and classify it based on this, which again I will share publicly, so my feedback is also public."** The publicity is treated as structural rather than incidental. The project lead: **"all of these communications, all of these exchanges are public, and that is quite an important piece of the puzzle, and released in the Creative Commons."**

The third is the team. The project lead: **"one of the things I look for is the quality and the depth and the detail and how relevant is the agentic team that they set up."**

## Time-Bound, Budget-Defined, And Built To Take Extra Zeros

The proposal has to close the commercial loop rather than stopping at a plan. The project lead: **"what they should be doing in their proposal is they should be mapping the next batch of investment; they should say, we need X hours to achieve this, are we going to do this and get X revenue for X period of time, that is kind of the learning curve."** Which turns the review into an investment decision. The project lead: **"then you look at it from a commercial point of view, what is the cost, what is the return, what are they doing."** And what gets signed is tightly bounded. The project lead: **"what I am looking for is to sign off specific projects that are time-based, time-bound, budget-defined, very clear objectives, very clear inputs and outputs."** The reason to be this disciplined at small scale is that the mechanism is intended to survive growth without redesign. The project lead: **"this is basically in preparation for when some of these get investment, then we can do this at bigger scale, but the model is the same; it just has a couple of extra zeros on the budget."**

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a decision between the partnership models.** Cash or revenue share, the operating procedure is identical.
- **Not a paid setup engagement.** Building their own agentic workflow accrues to them and is never billable to us.
- **Not a private arrangement.** The repositories, the exchanges, and the review feedback are all public and Creative Commons.
- **Not a single-agent expectation.** A team of role definitions with differing opinions, sized to the project.
- **Not an open-ended contract.** Time-bound, budget-defined, with stated objectives, inputs, and outputs.
- **Not a throwaway process.** It is deliberately the same model intended to run at investment scale.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| The entry bar versus the learning opportunity | 17 July framed this as a brilliant opportunity for somebody who wants to learn; requiring a working agentic workflow, an agentic team, open repos, and payment rails selects for people who have already learned, so the two need reconciling with an on-ramp or an honest restatement |
| Public feedback versus candour and reach | Publishing a critique of a named partner's proposal creates a real reputational stake for them, which may deter strong candidates or quietly soften the feedback below what is useful |
| The self-fulfilling first criterion versus learning why people decline | If engagement itself is the test, the people who bounce are never assessed, and their reasons are exactly the signal a new engagement model most needs |
| Not billing for setup versus verifying it | The boundary is principled and hard to police, since the only real evidence of where the hours went is the deliverable itself |
| One hour as a maturity proxy versus what it measures | It measures the speed of their tooling as much as their fit for the work, and a slower, more considered partner could be screened out by it |
| Everything public versus commercially sensitive scoping | Some scoping conversations will touch pricing, positioning, or customer specifics that cannot sit in an open repo, and the boundary is currently undefined |
| Agent count versus overhead | The trade is named but not quantified, so there is no rule yet for how many agents is right for a given project size |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| What is the tool list and the maturity ladder? | The project lead undertakes to write this; partners cannot pick until it exists |
| Where do the repositories live, and who owns them? | Open by default, but the hosting, licensing boundary, and ownership on exit need stating |
| What is the review rubric? | Reviewing and classifying proposals with an agent requires the criteria to be written down before they can be applied consistently and published |
| What is public and what is not? | The line between open exchange and commercially sensitive material, given everything defaults to public |
| What are the standard agent roles by project type? | A starting team per project class would make the criterion concrete rather than judged case by case |
| How are hours verified and paid? | Rates, blocks, the entry mechanism, and what evidence accompanies an hour submitted |
| How does a strong candidate without an agentic workflow enter? | Whether there is an on-ramp, or whether the workflow is a hard precondition |
