# The Tools Mature Enough To Productise: Nine Candidates, One Identical Missing Piece (Token Purchase, A Page, Users), And Two Billing Shapes

**version** v0.33.50
**date** 23 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Commercial, Product, Partners, Engineering

**type** Strategy brief

*Fifth of 23 July, the tool list promised to partners in the preceding brief, so that a freelancer can pick one. Offered to be built on and challenged.*

---

## What This Is

The inventory of tools judged mature enough to convert into products, each one a candidate a freelancer or partner company could take to market under the engagement model defined earlier today: **the infographics generator is the furthest along, published on the Tools SGraph site and already used by a number of people, with a simple prompt, a defined input and output, a chat interface, an OpenRouter integration that requires a key, and a built-in mechanism for mapping the work out; the chat tool is strong, including a variant built inside a vault, and carries a concept worth exploring further in the single-token-execution prompt and the curation of the prompt; audio transcription works well against the models and pairs naturally with the video splitter that already exists to separate audio out of video; the video editing tools are powerful and include upload to YouTube, and they are the one set that suits a different billing shape, because a lot of the traffic happens on a website, which makes a small simple charge per use more appropriate than metering tokens; the list also holds OpenRouter key management and visualisation, which works nicely, a YouTube uploader, and then the two most mature of all, the SGraph encrypted file upload and download with its gallery and folder views, and the SG Vaults themselves; the striking thing across nearly the whole list is that the missing piece is identical in every case, and it is not the tool, it is the commercial wrapper, specifically the purchase of tokens, the page to sell from, and the users to sell to, which means the work to be done is trimming a workflow rather than building a product; and the billing intent is consistent throughout, keep the billable unit simple and cheap to use, whether that unit is a token passed through or a single use of a page.** It is the fifth brief of 23 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.50 freelance-next-steps brief, the v0.33.49 commercialising-the-primitives brief, the v0.33.50 core-primitives brief, the v0.33.44 evidence-packs brief, and the v0.33.49 open-source brief). New contributions: **the named inventory of tools judged ready to productise, the observation that nearly all of them share one identical missing piece in the token purchase flow, the page, and the users, the split between per-token and per-use billing shapes with website traffic as the reason for the second, the single-token-execution prompt and prompt curation named as a concept to explore further, the pairing of the video splitter with audio transcription, and the two vault tools named as the most mature of the set.**

## The Missing Piece Is The Same In Almost Every Case

The most useful finding in the memo is that the portfolio does not have nine different problems, it has one problem nine times. On the infographics generator, which is the most advanced of them, the project lead: **"the thing that you need, just a bit of trimming, is just the workflow, which actually is one that most of these tools need, which is the purchase of the tokens, and what we need to do is to create the page for it, and to get users."** The tool works. What is absent is the ability to charge for it, somewhere to sell it from, and anybody using it. This matches the position taken on 17 July, that the bottleneck is not invention but partners, and it sharpens it considerably: the missing work is nearly identical across the set, which is what makes the freelance model efficient rather than merely convenient, because a partner who solves it once has solved most of it for the rest.

```
   WHAT EXISTS  (the hard part, already done)
   the tool itself, working, with real features
        |
        +--- WHAT IS MISSING, and it is the same nearly every time:
               1. purchase of the tokens
               2. a page
               3. users
```

## Two Billing Shapes

Most of the set meters tokens, because that is what the work consumes. The video tools are called out as the exception, and the reason given is about traffic rather than cost. The project lead: **"the other ones are charged per token use; this is actually one that we could also just charge per use, because a lot of traffic is done on a website."** The design intent is the same in both cases. The project lead: **"again, it is the idea of that billable unit, but make it simple and cheap to use, which I think is part of the point here."** Simple and cheap is a deliberate position rather than an absence of ambition: these are tools people will try on impulse, and any pricing that requires a decision will lose more revenue than it captures.

```
   PER TOKEN                    PER USE
   infographics                 video editing
   chat                         YouTube upload
   transcription                (high website traffic, so a small
                                 flat charge beats metering)

   both: keep the billable unit SIMPLE and CHEAP
```

## The Furthest Along: Infographics

The infographics generator is treated as the lead candidate because it has the one thing none of the others are credited with, which is real usage. The project lead: **"a really nice one that was already actually used by a number of people is the infographics generator."** Its completeness is described in terms of the pieces already in place. The project lead: **"it has a simple prompt, it has an input and output, a nice chat message, and already has integration with OpenRouter, you need a key."** And it carries something the others will need. The project lead: **"this one has already a nice built-in mechanism for mapping this out."** If one tool is to prove the commercialisation kit works, this is the one with the shortest distance to travel.

## The Chat Tool And The Prompt-Curation Concept

The chat tool is noted less for the interface than for a workflow idea inside it. The project lead: **"we also have a really good chat one that we created, including one that was created inside a vault, which also has a really powerful workflow, especially on those workflows of the single token execution prompt or the curation of the prompt."** And it is flagged for further work rather than simply listed. The project lead: **"that is a really powerful concept that we should explore."** This is the only item on the list marked as needing conceptual development rather than only commercial packaging, which suggests it should not be first out of the door but may be the most interesting in the medium term.

## Audio, Video, And The Natural Pairing

Transcription is described as working, and its value increases when combined with something already built. The project lead: **"the audio transcript, that is working quite well in terms of using the models there, and actually we can combine that with the video splitter, which we already have, which basically then allows us to extract and split the audio from the videos."** That pairing is worth naming as a single offer rather than two tools, because upload a video and get a transcript is a complete user-facing job, whereas split audio from video is a step inside somebody else's workflow. The video editing set is credited with genuine capability. The project lead: **"we also have a set of video tools, video editing tools that are actually quite powerful, including upload to YouTube."** And with existing depth. The project lead: **"this actually already has some really powerful features."**

## The Two Most Mature

The list closes on the pieces closest to the platform itself. The project lead: **"then the two most mature ones, which is the SGraph encrypted file upload and download, the gallery view, the folder view, and again the SG Vaults."** These are the most developed and also the most entangled with everything else being built, since the vault is the substrate the RiskMandate architecture rests on. Productising them is therefore a different kind of decision from productising a transcription tool, and the brief does not resolve which way it should go.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a build plan.** These tools exist; the work is the commercial wrapper around them.
- **Not a launch sequence.** Nine candidates are named, and the order is not decided here.
- **Not a single billing model.** Per token for most, per use where website traffic makes that simpler.
- **Not a complex pricing exercise.** Simple and cheap is stated as the design goal for the billable unit.
- **Not a decision on the vault tools.** The two most mature are also the closest to the core product, and that tension is left open.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Nine candidates versus one project at a time | The engagement model signs off time-bound, budget-defined projects individually; nine parallel launches would break exactly the discipline it just established |
| Mature enough versus market ready | Mature here means the tool works, not that anyone will pay for it; the shared gap is precisely the part that has never been built, and it is the harder half |
| Commodity risk on several items | Transcription, infographics, and video splitting are widely available and cheap; the 17 July position was that isolation, privacy, and deployment are the differentiators, and several of these tools do not obviously have one |
| Two billing shapes versus building the rails once | Per-token and per-use need different plumbing, which partly undoes the efficiency of solving the shared gap a single time |
| Token pass-through versus margin | Reselling tokens through a provider that already takes its own cut is a thin, stacked margin, which puts the weight on volume or on the per-use tools |
| Open source versus a paid product | Everything is released open, so a capable user can self-host and skip the purchase entirely; what is actually being sold is convenience, hosting, and key management, which is real but thin |
| The vault tools versus the core product | The two most mature items are the foundation RiskMandate sits on; productising them separately could build credibility or fragment attention at the moment the MVP needs it |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| Which one ships first, and on what criteria? | Infographics has real users and the shortest distance, which is an argument for leading with it and proving the kit |
| What is the shared commercialisation kit? | Purchase flow, page, key management, metering, and auth built once and reused across the set is the highest-leverage piece of work available |
| What is the markup on pass-through tokens? | The 17 July pricing question applied concretely to tools that resell provider tokens |
| What is the per-use unit and price for the video tools? | Simple and cheap needs an actual number before a partner can model revenue |
| Is the YouTube uploader distinct from the video editing set? | It appears twice in the memo and may be one capability or two |
| What differentiates the commodity tools? | For transcription and infographics specifically, what makes ours the one someone pays for |
| How do the vault tools relate to RiskMandate? | Whether the encrypted upload and the vaults are products, infrastructure, or both |
| Who owns a tool after a revenue-share or venture engagement? | The ownership question the partnership models leave open, made concrete by a named tool |
