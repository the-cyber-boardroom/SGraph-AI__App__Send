## The Read Is Right: A Governance Layer, Not A Diagnostic Tool

The characterisation to affirm first is the correct one. RiskMandate inventories every autonomous system, sizes its blast radius, assigns a business owner, and forces a decision by a date, and those are risk-register mechanics expressed in the language a risk officer, a general counsel, and a board actually use, not a scanner's output. That is deliberate, and it is the same position the recent briefs reached from other directions: a governance and meta-layer that sits above the stack rather than another tool inside it, deepening what an organisation already runs rather than adding a parallel system. Being recognised as governance mechanics in board language, rather than as a diagnostic, is the product working as intended.

## The Core Question: Assess, Or Consume?

The question that shapes everything downstream is how the blast radius and impact view get populated, and specifically whether RiskMandate assesses each agent itself or consumes findings from the diagnostic tools that probe agent behaviour. The answer is that it consumes, and it does not probe. RiskMandate does not red-team an agent, does not run prompt-injection tests against it, and does not monitor its runtime behaviour. What it does is take the findings those tools produce and turn them into an owned, rated, dated risk decision. The one important refinement is that not every input is a consumed finding; one of them is a computation, which the next section makes precise.

## How The Blast Radius Is Populated

The blast radius and impact view are assembled from three kinds of input, and it is worth being exact about which is computed and which is consumed.

| Input | Where it comes from | Computed or consumed |
|-------|--------------------|----------------------|
| Reach, the union of what the agent can touch | The agent's grants, tokens, scopes, roles, and identity | Computed by us, deterministically, from access data |
| Behaviour, whether it is injectable or misbehaves | Red-teaming, prompt-injection testing, runtime monitoring | Consumed as findings from those tools |
| Population, which agents exist at all | Discovery and inventory tooling | Consumed as findings from those tools |

To these three inputs RiskMandate adds what the diagnostic layer does not do at all: it attaches a business owner, rates the residual, and forces a dated, owned decision that rolls up to the board. So the impact view is not something we probe out of the agent; it is something we assemble from access data we can compute over and findings we ingest, and then govern.

## Reach Is Computed, Behaviour Is Consumed

The distinction that answers the question cleanly is between reach and behaviour. Reach, the blast radius in its structural sense, is the union of everything an agent's credentials can touch, and that is a calculation over grants and access, not a behavioural probe, so RiskMandate computes it directly from configuration and identity data it can consume from the identity and access tooling an organisation already has. Behaviour, by contrast, is empirical, whether the agent is actually prompt-injectable, whether it actually exceeds its intent, what it actually did, and that can only come from tools built to probe and watch it, which RiskMandate consumes rather than reproduces. This maps exactly onto the agent-authorization model: the union of the possible is the reach we compute, and the expected-versus-unexpected behaviour is the delta the diagnostic tools measure. We own the reach computation and the governance; we consume the behaviour. We assess the risk, not the agent.

## Where We Sit In The Stack

That answer places RiskMandate above the diagnostic and discovery layer, not within it. Beneath us sit the tools that discover agents, probe their behaviour, test them for injection, and monitor them at runtime, along with the identity and access systems that hold the grants we compute reach from. Above that layer, RiskMandate is where those signals become a decision: inventory becomes an owned register, reach and behaviour become a rated blast radius and impact, and the whole thing becomes a dated acceptance with a name against it. The security team keeps its diagnostic stack; we are the governance surface that consumes it and turns it into something a board can own.

## Partner Versus Compete

The place in the stack settles the partner-versus-compete question directly. The diagnostic, testing, discovery, and monitoring tools are partners and evidence sources, not competitors, because their findings are exactly what populates our views, and the better they get, the better our impact view is, so we are complementary to that whole ecosystem by design. Where we sit closer to competition is the governance layer itself, the workflow-based registers and spreadsheets that assign owners and track decisions today without a grounding graph, without the agentic reach computation, and without the evidence trail, and even there the posture is deepen rather than displace, sitting beneath a mature program and enriching its register rather than ripping it out. In short, we compete with how governance is done in a workflow, and we partner with everything that produces the evidence governance needs.

## Why Consume-Not-Probe Is The Right Design

Designing it this way is a choice, and it is the right one for three reasons. It keeps us evidence-source-agnostic, so as the probing and testing tools evolve, and they are evolving fast, we ingest their improving findings rather than competing with them or falling behind. It keeps us out of the detection arms race and deterministic where it counts, because computing reach from access and governing the result does not require us to chase agent behaviour we would never fully capture anyway. And it lets us sit on top of a stack the security team already owns, which is the only position a buyer exhausted by tool sprawl will accept, the layer that makes their existing investments add up to an owned decision rather than the twenty-sixth console. Consume-not-probe is not a limitation; it is the architecture that makes the governance layer credible and adoptable.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a diagnostic or testing tool.** We do not probe, red-team, or monitor agents; we consume those findings.
- **Not blind to reach, though.** We compute the blast radius from access data directly, which is not a behavioural probe.
- **Not a competitor to the diagnostic ecosystem.** Those tools are our evidence sources and partners.
- **Not a rip-and-replace for a mature register.** Even in the governance layer, we deepen rather than displace.

| Tension | Note |
