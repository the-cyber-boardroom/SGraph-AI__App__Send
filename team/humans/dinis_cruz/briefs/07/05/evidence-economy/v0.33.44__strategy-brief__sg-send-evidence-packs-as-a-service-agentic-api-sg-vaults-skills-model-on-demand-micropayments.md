# Evidence Packs As A Service: How A Journalist Sells Facts, Trust, And Evidence On Demand

## What This Is

The supplier's playbook for the evidence economy, from the journalist's side: **a journalist or news organisation looking to monetize should stop selling stories and start selling facts, trust, and evidence packs through an agentic-friendly API that agents, automation, and humans can all consume; the pack is the legwork rather than the prose, the evidence, the trails, the assurance, and the graph of the article, the connect-the-dots work that is the real value, packaged so it can be delivered as multiple versions, in many languages and cultures and personalised to a particular company at scale; the enabling inversion is that the journalist does not use the LLM to write the material, they use it to parse information, build tools and visualisations, and maintain the semantic knowledge graphs they still own, so the human keeps the ground and the model does the scaling, which is what makes something previously too hard now work; the delivery vehicle is the SG vault, sold the same way skills are sold, a versioned vault carrying the evidence, the semantic graphs, and the structure, with provenance, explainability, and repeatability, which matters most when a story is still developing and a curated, trustworthy view of the world is scarce; and the money is on-demand and pay-for-what-you-use, lots and lots of small payments rather than rent or lock-in, priced up by service and SLA where the value warrants, because inside any organisation tens of departments need accurate information, especially when events are unfolding and they urgently need to know what is going on.** It is the ninth brief of 5 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.44 news-backed evidence vault and evidence-economy briefs, the v0.33.42 commercial-model brief, and the v0.33.36 grounding-ladder brief). New contributions: **evidence-packs-as-a-service over an agentic API, the legwork-not-prose pack, the LLM-maintains-the-graph inversion with the journalist owning the ground, the SG-vault-sold-like-a-skill delivery, and the on-demand, no-lock-in payment model.**

## The Product: Sell Facts, Trust, And Evidence Packs, By API

The reframe is to sell the substance under the story, not the story. The project lead: **"if I was a journalist with a news website looking for ways to monetize the research and the information, I would create a service where you sell facts, you sell trust, and you sell evidence packs."** And the channel is a machine-first interface. The project lead: **"you are providing the service by API, agentic friendly, consumed by agents or other automation, or even humans."** Building the service API-first and agent-consumable is the right call, because the growing buyer of structured, trustworthy facts is an agent doing work on someone's behalf, and a human reader is then just one more client of the same API rather than the only audience.

## What Is In The Pack: The Legwork, Not The Prose

The value is the work of connecting the dots, which the prose usually hides. The project lead: **"not just the news story, but the evidence, the trails, the assurance, you have done the legwork to connect the dots, and then you have the graph of your article."** A finished article is the thin visible layer over a large body of research, and what a company or an agent will pay for is that body: the sources, the trails followed, the assurance that the dots connect, exposed as the graph behind the article rather than compressed into a paragraph. Selling the graph, not the paragraph, is the core of the model.

## Personalised At Scale

Once the article is a graph, one piece of research becomes many deliverables. The project lead: **"the multiple versions of the article, in multiple languages, multiple cultures, customised to particular companies, to personalise the feed for a particular company at scale, is very valuable."** The same grounded graph can be rendered per language, per culture, and per company without redoing the research, so personalisation stops being bespoke labour and becomes a projection of the underlying graph. Doing that at scale is the feat, and it is what turns a single investigation into a catalogue of tailored, payable products.

## The Inversion: LLMs Maintain The Graph, The Journalist Owns It

The role of the model here is the opposite of the usual one, and this is the load-bearing point. The project lead: **"they are not using the LLMs to produce the materials, they are using LLMs to parse information, create tools and visualisations, and maintain semantic knowledge graphs that they still own."** The journalist does not hand authorship to the model; they keep the ground and use the model to parse, to build the mini tools and UIs, and to maintain the semantic graph. That is also why it is newly possible. The project lead: **"you can only do that if you get the LLMs to help with the semantic graphs and the maintenance, so you can scale, the LLMs make something that before was really hard into something that works quite effectively."** The graph maintenance that made this uneconomic before is exactly what the model now absorbs, so the human keeps ownership and credibility while the model supplies the scale.

## The SG Vault Is The Pack, Sold Like A Skill

The pack needs a container, and the vault is it. The project lead: **"the SG vault is a perfect partner here, they should be selling SG vaults, the vault that has the evidence, the semantic graphs, and the structure."** The vault carries the evidence, the graphs, and the structure as one versioned, sovereign unit, which is precisely what a pack is. And the selling model already exists. The project lead: **"if you look back at all the work we did with mapping skills and selling skills, the same thing applies to news stories."** The skill-vault and marketplace work established how to sell expertise as a versioned vault with provenance, applicability, and on-demand billing; an evidence pack is the same shape with reporting in place of a skill. The provenance discipline transfers directly, and it matters most under pressure. The project lead: **"especially when stories are developing, it is important to have that factual data, that curated view of the world, with evidence, with provenance, with explainability, with repeatability."** A developing story is when trustworthy, provenanced, repeatable facts are scarcest and most valuable, which is exactly when the pack earns its price.

## The Money: On Demand, Pay For What You Use, No Lock-In

The pricing model is deliberately the opposite of a subscription. The project lead: **"it is about micro payments, small payments but lots of them, on demand consumption, not about rent, not about locking in the user, but lots of mini payments."** The unit is a consumption event, not a seat or a contract, and the design goal is volume of small on-demand payments rather than captured tenants. The size is not fixed. The project lead: **"depending on the organisation, the service, and the SLA, you can rank up the prices, so it does not even need to be micro payments, but what matters is on demand payments, you pay for what you use."** So micro is the default, not the constraint; a higher SLA or a premium service can lift the price per call, and the invariant is pay-for-what-you-use with no lock-in. This is the platform commercial model's consumption posture applied to evidence, and it is the same billing substrate the skills work already treats as the moat.

## The Buyer: Tens Of Departments, Especially When Events Unfold

Demand inside an organisation is broad and spikes on events. The project lead: **"in an organisation there are tens of departments that need access to accurate information, especially when events are unfolding, to really know what is going on."** The buyer is not one subscription for the company; it is many departments each pulling the packs they need, and the pull intensifies exactly when a story is breaking and the cost of not knowing is highest. On-demand pricing fits that shape precisely, because it lets a department pay for the surge of information it needs during an unfolding event without carrying a standing subscription the rest of the time.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not selling the prose.** The product is the evidence graph and the legwork, not the finished article text.
- **Not LLM-authored news.** The model maintains the graph and builds the tools; the journalist owns the ground.
- **Not a subscription or a lock-in.** The model is on-demand, pay-for-what-you-use, many small payments.
- **Not micro-only.** Micro is the default; SLA and service can rank the price up while staying on-demand.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Owning the graph versus model dependence | The journalist keeps ownership, but the scaling now depends on the model doing the maintenance |
| On-demand volume versus revenue predictability | Many small payments avoid lock-in but make revenue less predictable than a subscription |
| Personalisation versus a single source of truth | Many tailored versions must all trace to one grounded graph, or trust fragments |
| Surge demand versus SLA | Value peaks during unfolding events, which is exactly when the SLA is hardest to hold |
