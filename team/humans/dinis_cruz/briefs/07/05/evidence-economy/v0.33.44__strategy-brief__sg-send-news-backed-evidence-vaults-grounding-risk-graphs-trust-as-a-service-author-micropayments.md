## What This Is

A plan to ground the risk graphs in cited news, and the product thesis that comes with it: **build a set of vaults of news stories that back up the evidence for the risks shown on the Risk Mandate site, so the graphs on the site can be walked down to a real, cited source rather than asserted, which is grounding made literal, because a news story is Evidence on the grounding ladder and it is exactly what turns an alarm into a grounded alarm; the vault is a separate SG/Send vault run as a small news organisation, holding semantic graphs of news ontologies with full provenance, and the move that makes it a product rather than a clippings file is connecting those news graphs to the risk graphs we are already capturing, source node to risk node, so the citation becomes the flow; this is the future-of-news work, MyFeeds, Trust-as-a-Service, semantic knowledge graphs, and micro-payments to authors, pointed at risk, and it demonstrates the same thesis those projects carry, a product that sells trust, that sells access to good and reliable data that can be consumed effectively, with the provenance that lets each cited author be paid per use; and the first deliverable is a set of good examples, a demonstrator vault that shows a risk on the site resolving to the news graph that grounds it, with the incidents already cited in the catastrophic-failure thesis as its first entries.** It is the seventh brief of 5 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.44 grounded-alarm brief, the v0.33.36 grounding-ladder brief, the v0.33.44 catastrophic-failure thesis, and the v0.33.42 commercial-model brief). New contributions: **the news-backed evidence vault as the grounding supply for the risk graphs, the news-graph-to-risk-graph connection as the flow, and the future-of-news trust-and-author-micropayment model applied to risk.**

## The Idea: News That Backs the Evidence for the Risks

The starting point is concrete. The project lead: **"to create a series of vaults that has news stories that back up the evidence for the risk, for the Risk Mandate.ai website, like the graphs that we have."** The risk graphs on the site currently assert that a class of risk is real; this makes that assertion walkable, so a claim on the site links to the reporting that substantiates it. The point is not to decorate the graphs with headlines but to give every scary claim a cited origin, which is the difference between a risk register that is believed and one that is merely presented.

## Grounding, Made Literal

This is the grounding ladder stopped being a metaphor. A news story is Evidence, and Evidence is the rung that backs a Fact, so a well-sourced incident report is the literal grounding underneath a risk node. It is also the missing supply side of the grounded-alarm strategy from earlier today: grounded alarm requires that every frightening claim ship with its evidence attached, and a news-backed evidence vault is where that evidence comes from. The incidents already cited in the catastrophic-failure thesis, the Replit production-database deletion and the EchoLeak prompt-injection exfiltration, are the first two entries in exactly this corpus; the vault generalises that from two hand-picked citations into a maintained evidence base.

## Built as a Separate Vault: a Mini News Organisation

The delivery vehicle is a dedicated vault. The project lead: **"create a set of good examples of what that will look like, where we put in a separate vault and create a kind of mini news organisation."** Running it as a small news organisation is the right framing, because the work is editorial as much as technical: selecting credible sources, capturing them with provenance, and maintaining them over time. Keeping it in its own SG/Send vault keeps the evidence base sovereign and versioned, separable from any single customer's risk data, and reusable across every risk graph that needs grounding.

## Connect the News Graph to the Risk Graph

The step that turns a news archive into part of the product is the connection. The project lead: **"we create a set of semantic graphs of ontologies and then we connect them, in our case, to the risks that we are capturing, and that becomes the flow."** The news is captured as its own semantic graph, a news ontology of stories, claims, sources, and authors, and then bridged into the risk graph the way the ontology brief bridges any external framework, source node to risk node, evidence backing a fact. The citation is not a footnote bolted on at the end; the edge from a risk to the news that grounds it is the flow, the path a user walks from a claim on the site down to the reporting underneath it.

## This Is the Future-of-News Work, Pointed at Risk

None of this machinery is new; it is the future-of-news research aimed at a new target. That body of work already built the parts: MyFeeds for personalised, provenance-aware feeds, Trust-as-a-Service for verification and credibility scoring, semantic knowledge graphs for fact-level traceability, and micro or nano payments that compensate authors per read across publishers. The project lead: **"if you go back to the news and my feeds and the work we have done on creating connectivity and meaning in the news, and even the micro payments to the news authors."** Risk Mandate is a natural first customer of that machinery, because a risk register lives or dies on the credibility of its evidence, and the future-of-news stack is precisely a credibility and provenance engine.

## Selling Trust: Access to Good and Reliable Data

The thesis underneath the feature is a business model. The project lead: **"how do you create this website that fundamentally sells trust, that sells access to good and reliable data, that can be used effectively and consumed effectively."** What the vault sells is not the news itself but the trust in it, the assurance that every claim is grounded, cited, and traceable, packaged so it can be consumed by a human reading the site and by a machine querying the graph. That is the same product Trust-as-a-Service describes, editorial integrity turned into something purchasable, and it slots directly into the platform commercial model as reliable-evidence-as-a-product alongside the risk primitives.

## Micro-Payments to the Authors

The provenance that grounds a risk is also what makes the authors payable. Because every cited claim traces to a specific source and author, the same graph that proves a risk is grounded can meter which author's work was consumed, which is the condition micro-payments need. The project lead's inclusion of author payments is not a side note; it is what keeps the evidence base sustainable and ethical, since a product that sells trust built on other people's reporting should pay the reporters. In commercial-model terms, an author micro-payment is a consumption event, a billing primitive that fires when a cited source is read or queried, so paying authors and metering usage are the same mechanism seen from two sides.

## The Deliverable: A Set of Good Examples

The ask is a demonstrator, not a finished newsroom. The project lead: **"let's capture this."** The first build is a small, real example: a separate vault holding a handful of well-sourced agent-risk incidents as semantic news graphs, connected to the corresponding risk nodes on the site, so a viewer can click a risk and land on the grounded reporting behind it. That single walk, from an asserted risk to its cited evidence, is the whole thesis in miniature, and it is enough to show a customer what selling trust actually looks like.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not decoration.** The news is grounding for the risk graphs, not headlines pasted onto them.
- **Not a general newsroom.** It is a focused evidence base for the risks the site captures, run editorially.
- **Not unattributed scraping.** Provenance is mandatory, and the authors whose work grounds a risk get paid.
- **Not new machinery.** It reuses the future-of-news stack; the novelty is pointing it at risk.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Editorial cost versus automation | A credible evidence base needs human editorial judgment, which does not fully automate away |
| Coverage versus credibility | A larger corpus grounds more risks but is harder to keep uniformly trustworthy |
| Author payments versus friction | Paying authors per read is right but adds settlement and licensing complexity |
| Grounding depth versus site speed | Walking every claim to a source is the point but must stay fast enough to browse |
