# The Risk Mandate Experience Loop: Map, Accept, Two Streams, Recalibrate

**version** v0.33.44
**date** 5 July 2026
**from** Human (project lead)
**to** Product, Design, Strategy, Architect

**type** Arch brief (workflow)

## What This Is

A first map of the Risk Mandate demo and product experience, as one loop: **the flow starts by mapping what the customer already has, so for each agent we take its connected assets and produce a first pass of the risks and the actions to take, carrying the ambiguity rather than pretending it away; that map then hits the risk-acceptance decision, which resolves into exactly two actions, a risk accepted short-term because more information is needed, and a risk accepted with enough understanding to choose what to do, where acting is now one of several options on the table; those two actions open two streams, an investigate stream that gathers the missing information inside the time limit the acceptance set and secures the funding and the people to do it, and an execute stream that allocates, funds, and runs the remediation projects; both streams return to the beginning, the investigate stream when its answer arrives and the risk is re-mapped, the execute stream when the risk needs recalibration or has finally been accepted for a long interval, so the experience is a cycle and not a report; our role in it is to make the loop happen by integrating with the tools, systems, spreadsheets, and capabilities the customer already runs, as low touch as possible, helping build what is missing only where it is missing, and hosting where the agents run; and the single measure the whole loop is judged by is the reduction of risk for the business.** It is the sixth brief of 5 July (cross-ref: the v0.33.35 no-deny risk-register brief, the v0.33.38 confidence-margin brief, the v0.33.44 AWS ontology brief, and the v0.33.42 commercial-model brief). New contributions: **the two-phase map-then-decide experience, the two acceptance actions, the investigate and execute streams, and the return-to-start recalibration loop, framed by our integrate-low-touch role and the risk-reduction measure.**

## Two Things The Experience Has To Convey

The experience carries a payload and a process at once. The project lead: **"to map the experience, especially in the beginning, of the Risk Mandate demos and workflows we want to provide to the users."** Part of it is teaching the ideas. The project lead: **"explaining the core principles like the risk acceptance, the objectives, and what we are going to do."** The rest is running the actual loop. The project lead: **"there are a number of concepts we want to explain, and a number of objectives and workflows we have."** A good demo does both: it explains risk acceptance while the customer watches their own risks move through it, so the principle is learned by using it rather than by being lectured on it.

## Phase One: Map Agents and Assets to a First Pass of Risks

The loop opens by turning what exists into a mapped set of risks. The project lead: **"the first phase is, take what agents or assets you have and map that to the first pass of the risks and the actions to be taken."** The mapping is anchored on the agent. The project lead: **"for every agent we identify, there are the assets connected to it, and a series of risks connected to that."** This is the ontology from earlier today read as an experience: an agent is a principal, its connected assets are what its authorization closure reaches, and the risks are what that reach exposes. The first pass is honest about not being the last pass; it produces the risks at whatever ambiguity the current evidence supports, and that ambiguity is data the next phase acts on, not a defect to hide.

## The Two Acceptance Actions

Everything the customer does with a mapped risk collapses into two moves, and naming them is the core of the workflow. The project lead: **"there are two main actions for the risks that people accept, there is that we need more information, risks accepted short term because you need more information, and there are the risks accepted with already an understanding of what needs to happen."** The first action is an acceptance whose direction is investigate, taken because the ambiguity is still too high to decide well. The second is an acceptance whose direction is act, taken because the picture is clear enough. The project lead: **"we already have enough information to make a decision, it is just now one of the multiple options available."** Both are genuine acceptances in the no-deny sense, a decision made and owned for an interval, not a refusal; they differ only in what the interval is for.

## Phase Two: Decide What To Do With Each Risk

Phase two is the decision layer over the mapped risks. The project lead: **"phase two is the decision on what you want to do with those risks, accepted to find more information or accepted to actually do something about it."** This is where the first-pass map becomes a set of owned commitments, each risk routed by its acceptance direction into one of the two streams below. The decision is per risk, so a single map fans out into a mix of investigate and act commitments rather than one blanket verdict.

## The Two Streams

The two acceptance actions open two streams of work. The project lead: **"then you go into two streams, the stream of finding more information within the time limit provided, and finding who is going to fund and who can do those projects."** The investigate stream is time-boxed by the acceptance that created it and has to answer two questions, who funds the investigation and who can carry it out. The execute stream does the same for remediation, allocating and funding the projects that actually reduce the risk. Both streams therefore surface the same two constraints the corpus keeps returning to, money and ownership, at the moment the risk is accepted rather than months later.

## The Loop: Everything Returns To The Beginning

Neither stream terminates; both feed back to the start. The project lead: **"from the top you go back to the beginning once you have the answer, and on the bottom you allocate the projects and they get executed, and you go back to the beginning once the risk needs to be recalibrated or reexamined, or we finally accept it for a long period."** The investigate stream returns when its answer lands, so the risk is re-mapped with the ambiguity now reduced. The execute stream returns when the remediation changes the risk enough to need recalibration, or when the risk has been driven low enough to accept for a long interval. The experience is a cycle whose job is to keep pulling each risk back through the map at a lower ambiguity and a lower level, which is what makes it a living register rather than a one-off assessment.

## Our Role: Make The Loop Happen, Low Touch

Risk Mandate is not the register of record for everything; it is the thing that makes the loop turn over what the customer already has. The project lead: **"our role is to make this happen, to integrate as much as we can with the existing tools, systems, spreadsheets, and capabilities, so that we make this happen."** Integration is the default and building is the exception. The project lead: **"ideally it is as low touch as possible, and if they do not have the tools and capabilities, then we can help with that."** So the product meets a customer at whatever maturity they have, driving the loop through their existing systems where those exist and supplying the missing pieces where they do not, including hosting where the agents run. This is the commercial model's integrate-first, low-touch posture stated as an experience.

## The Measure: Reduction of Risk For The Business

The loop needs one number to justify itself, and the memo is unambiguous about which. The project lead: **"the value here is the reduction of the risk for the business."** Everything in the cycle, the mapping, the acceptances, the two streams, the recalibration, is instrumental to moving that one measure, so the feedback loop worth building is the one that shows risk going down over successive passes. A demo that maps risk beautifully but cannot show it falling has not yet shown the value.

## What This Does Not Try To Be

- **Not a one-off assessment.** The experience is a cycle that re-maps each risk at lower ambiguity and level.
- **Not a rip-and-replace.** The default is integrating with existing tools and spreadsheets, low touch.
- **Not a deny switch.** Both actions are acceptances in a direction; investigate and act, never refuse-and-forget.
- **Not judged by coverage.** The single measure is the reduction of risk for the business, not the size of the map.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| First-pass ambiguity versus trust | Showing risks at high ambiguity is honest but can unsettle a customer expecting crisp answers |
| Low touch versus depth | Integrating with existing spreadsheets is easy to adopt but bounds how deep the mapping can go |
| Two clean actions versus reality | Real risks sometimes need both investigate and act at once, which the two-stream model must accommodate |
| Risk-reduction measure versus attribution | Proving the loop reduced the risk, rather than something else, is harder than showing the loop ran |
