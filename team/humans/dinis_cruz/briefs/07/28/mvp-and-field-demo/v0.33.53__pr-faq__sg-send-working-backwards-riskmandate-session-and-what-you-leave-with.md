# PRESS RELEASE

## RiskMandate turns twenty minutes of your own answers into a risk register your board can actually act on

### For the person responsible for AI agents already running in production, who needs to know who can stop them, what stopping them would cost, and which obligations apply

**London, [launch date]** RiskMandate today made available a guided session that takes what you already know about the agents running in your organisation and turns it into an owned risk register, translated for every level from the engineer who holds the credential to the board that carries the liability. The session runs in a browser or on a tablet, takes about twenty minutes, requires no integration with your systems, and ends with a document you can send to the people who need to answer for it. You can see what the analysis cost you while you run it.

**The problem.** Most organisations running AI agents are not short of information about them. They know the agents exist, they often know what those agents can reach, and many have already bought tooling that discovers and monitors them. What they do not have is the connection. The findings sit in one system, the risk register sits in a spreadsheet somewhere else, and nobody has carried either up to the person with the authority to accept the exposure. Three specific gaps recur. First, nobody can say who would stop a given agent, how quickly, and what stopping it would break. Second, the technical fact and the board's exposure are the same thing described in two languages, and no one translates between them. Third, obligations that already apply are met or not met by accident, because nothing connects a live system to the provision that governs it.

**The solution.** RiskMandate asks a short series of questions about one scenario that matches what you actually run. It asks whether you can pull the plug, whether you know what else goes down when you do, how fast you could do it, and what would still be unrecoverable afterwards. From those answers it builds a register, connects each entry to the regulatory provisions that bear on it, and then shows you the same register as each person in the chain would see it, in their own language, from the operator through to the chief executive. Nothing you enter leaves your device unless you choose to take it with you.

**"We built this because the hardest part of risk was never finding it,"** said the founder of RiskMandate. **"Almost every organisation we speak to already knows more than they think. What they lack is the line between what an engineer knows on Tuesday and what a board has to sign in November. That line is the product."**

**How it works.** You choose a scenario from a small library that matches the kind of agent you run. You answer between six and ten questions from memory, standing up, without preparing anything. As you answer, a register assembles beside you, and each risk shows its owner, its blast radius, how fast it could be stopped, and how much of the damage could be undone. Where a provision of a regulation applies to your answer, it is cited with a link back to the source text. When you have finished you flick between the views for each role in the chain and watch the same facts change language. A running total shows what the analysis has cost in model usage, typically a few pounds. You then take the result away as a document, a link, or a vault of your own.

**"I have been trying to get this conversation onto the board agenda for eight months,"** said a head of platform engineering at a financial services firm who used the session in preview. **"I answered nine questions on someone's tablet and walked away with the exact paragraph our general counsel needed to see. It cost about three pounds."**

**Getting started.** Visit riskmandate.ai, choose a scenario, and begin. No account is required to run a session. You can bring your own model key, or pay for the run itself, which is priced at what the analysis actually consumes.

# EXTERNAL FAQ

**What do I actually leave with?**
A register of the risks your answers surfaced, each with a named accepting role, the plug profile, and any regulatory provisions that apply, plus a list of the questions worth asking internally and the actions worth taking. It is available as a document, a shareable link that resolves to content rather than a login, or a vault you own outright.

**Do you connect to my systems?**
No. The first session is deliberately airgapped. Everything is based on what you tell it, which is also why it takes twenty minutes rather than a procurement cycle. Integration is available later and is not required to get value.

**Where does my data go?**
Nowhere, unless you ask for a takeaway. The session holds its state in your own browser. This is a property of the architecture rather than a policy promise, and the technical explanation is published.

**Is this a compliance assessment?**
No, and it would be dishonest to present it as one. It shows which provisions bear on a situation like the one you described, so that you know what you would need to be able to demonstrate. Whether you comply is a judgement for your own compliance function, and the tool marks clearly which statements are yours, which are ours, and which are the instrument's.

**How accurate can it be if I answered from memory?**
It is as accurate as your answers, and it says so. The value is not in a precise measurement of your estate. It is in discovering which questions you could not answer, because those are the findings.

**What does it cost?**
The session shows you as it runs. The typical analysis consumes a few pounds of model usage. You can bring your own key and pay nothing to us for the run.

**Which regulations are covered?**
The European AI Act at launch, with the graph structured so further instruments attach without rework. The same system can sit inside several regimes at once, which is precisely why a checklist does not work here.

**I already have tooling that discovers my agents. Why do I need this?**
You probably do not need it to find things. This starts where that stops: it takes findings, attaches an accountable owner, translates the exposure up the chain, and produces a dated decision somebody signed. If your discovery tooling already does that, you do not need us.

# INTERNAL FAQ

*The dragons. Per the discipline, this section carries what is unresolved, and the product should be understood as not yet viable to the extent that these remain open.*

**Is it viable today?**
Not fully. Three things are unfinished: the scenario library is small, the regulatory graph covers one instrument, and the takeaway path has not been tested with anyone who was not already sympathetic. The honest position is that the session is demonstrable and the takeaway is not yet proven to be acted on.

**What is the single riskiest assumption?**
That a person will act on a document produced from twenty minutes of their own self-reported answers. Everything else is engineering. This one is behavioural and is the thing the field test exists to check.

**Would anyone pay, and how much?**
Unknown, and deliberately set low to find out. The stated bar is a token payment, a few pounds for the run, a brought key, or a connection to their own environment. The purpose of pricing at cost plus a small margin is to convert interest into a transaction cheaply enough that the answer is not confounded by budget approval.

**What is the metric that tells us this worked?**
Two candidates. Whether the person asks what it costs to keep, which is the only reliable indication that the value registered. And whether the takeaway is opened by a second person inside their organisation, which is the only indication it travelled.

**What could make this look bad?**
Presenting a compliance conclusion from thin input. Naming a provision from six answers edges toward advice, and if a customer acts on it and is wrong, the damage is to credibility rather than to a contract. The mitigation is in the wording, and the wording is not yet finalised.

**Why now rather than after more development?**
Because the three components required for it, the plug profile, the register altitudes and the regulation graph, only intersected this month, and because a conference next week supplies a concentrated population of exactly the right people. The alternative to shipping something thin is learning nothing for another quarter.

**What does it cost us to run?**
Model usage per session is small and is the number shown to the customer. The material costs are the scenario library and the regulatory graph, both of which are fixed rather than per-session, which is what makes a few pounds per run a real price rather than a loss leader.

**What happens if a large incumbent copies it?**
The session is copyable. The regulatory graph, the acceptance chain and the vault substrate are the parts that took months. The honest answer is that the demonstration is not defensible and the thing underneath it is the moat, which means the session should be given away freely rather than protected.

**What are we deliberately not building?**
Discovery, enforcement, and anything in the request path. The corpus position is settled: we consume findings, we do not probe, and we are never in line.

## Honest Tensions

| Tension | Note |
|---------|------|
| Writing as though launched versus what exists | The discipline requires the past tense and the corpus requires honesty, which is why the header and the internal FAQ state plainly that this has not shipped |
| Twenty minutes of self-report versus a register anyone should act on | The strongest objection to the whole premise, and the field test is designed to answer it rather than argue it |
| Naming provisions versus giving advice | The most valuable output and the closest to a line the product should not cross |
| Pricing to learn versus pricing to earn | A few pounds tests willingness cheaply and anchors the product low, and the anchor may be hard to move later |
| A copyable demonstration versus a slow moat | Giving the session away is probably right and makes the visible thing the least defensible part |
| The customer quote | It is invented, as the format requires, and in a corpus that grounds its claims that needs saying out loud |

## Open Questions

| Question | Notes |
|----------|-------|
| What exactly is in the takeaway? | The single most important unresolved question, since the takeaway is the product |

| Bring your own key, or pay per run, or both? | Both are named; which is the default changes the funnel |
