# Product Partnership Agreement: Voice Note Transcription Tool

**version** v0.33.52
**date** 27 July 2026
**status** DRAFT for legal review. Not executed. Not legal advice.
**type** Contract draft

*A short agreement covering the collaboration on the voice note transcription tool. It is deliberately brief, because almost everything produced is open source and published, so there is little to allocate and little to keep secret. The commercial terms are those settled on 27 July 2026: a ninety day term, a twenty five percent take on net receipts, and an even split of what remains.*

**Before use.** This draft is written by a non-lawyer to make the commercial intent explicit and reviewable. It has not been checked against the law of any jurisdiction, and it makes no attempt at tax treatment. Both parties should have it reviewed by a qualified solicitor before signing, and the open points listed at the end should be settled first.

## 2. The Product

**2.1** The Parties will collaborate to build, publish, and operate a tool that transcribes voice memos and returns a transcript, an analysis, a short debrief, and optionally an infographic, referred to as **the Product**.

**2.2** The Product will be published as a web application and as applications for iOS and Android, from a shared repository to which both Parties have access. Further distribution channels may be added by agreement.

**2.3** Inference is purchased through a third party model routing service, currently OpenRouter, referred to as **the Model Provider**. Payments from users are collected through a payment processor, currently Stripe, referred to as **the Processor**.

## 3. Term

**3.1** This Agreement begins on **1 August 2026** and runs for **ninety days**, ending on **30 October 2026**, unless extended or ended earlier under this clause. For the avoidance of doubt, 1 August 2026 is counted as the first day.

**3.2** At the end of the term this Agreement **lapses automatically** unless the Parties agree in writing to extend or replace it. Neither Party is obliged to renew, and no reason need be given.

**3.3** Either Party may end this Agreement early on [fourteen] days written notice, for any reason.

**3.4** Either Party may end this Agreement immediately on written notice if the other commits a material breach that is not remedied within [seven] days of being asked to remedy it in writing.

## 4. Openness

**4.1** All code, documentation, designs, prompts, and written material produced under this Agreement are **open source and published**. Code is released under [licence to be inserted, for example MIT or Apache 2.0] and written material under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0.

**4.2** Material is published as it is produced, in the shared repository, rather than held back for release later.

**4.3** Neither Party holds exclusive rights in the material. **Each Party may use, adapt, publish, and commercialise anything produced under this Agreement, during the term and after it ends, for any purpose, without owing the other anything.** This is intended and is not a concession by either side.

**4.4** Clause 4.3 does not extend to the items listed in clause 5, and does not permit either Party to represent that the other endorses a later derivative work.

## 5. Confidential Material

**5.1** The only confidential material under this Agreement is:

  (a) credentials, including API keys, passwords, signing keys, and access tokens;
  (b) financial data, including Processor and banking records, and revenue figures not yet published; and
  (c) personal data of users, to the extent any is held.

**5.2** Each Party will keep that material secure, use it only for operating the Product, and not disclose it to anyone else without written agreement.

**5.3** This obligation continues for [three] years after the Agreement ends.

**5.4** Everything not listed in clause 5.1 is public by default. There is no general confidentiality obligation in this Agreement, because there is almost nothing to keep confidential.

## 6. Money

**6.1 Definitions.** For each period:

  **Gross Receipts** means all amounts paid by users for credits or usage of the Product.

  **Processor Fees** means the fees actually charged by the Processor on those payments.

  **Net Receipts** means Gross Receipts less Processor Fees.

  **Service Allocation** means **seventy five percent of Net Receipts**, which is applied to purchasing inference for users and is not revenue of either Party.

  **Gross Margin** means **twenty five percent of Net Receipts**.

  **Direct Costs** means the costs listed in Schedule A, actually incurred in the period and evidenced.

  **Distributable Profit** means Gross Margin less Direct Costs.

**6.2 The take.** The Parties' combined take on the Product is **twenty five percent of Net Receipts**. The take is calculated **after** Processor Fees, so that those fees are borne by both Parties in proportion rather than by either alone.

**6.3 The split.** Distributable Profit is divided **fifty percent to the Operator and fifty percent to the Partner**.

**6.4 How the Service Allocation is delivered.** When a user purchases credits, the Operator provisions a key with the Model Provider carrying a credit limit equal to the Service Allocation for that purchase. The Model Provider meters and deducts the user's consumption against that limit. Neither Party invoices the user for inference separately.

**6.5 Worked example**, using the Processor's published domestic schedule of one and a half percent plus twenty pence, for illustration only:

```
   User pays                                    GBP 10.00
   less Processor Fees (1.5% + 0.20)                 0.35
   ------------------------------------------------------
   Net Receipts                                      9.65
        Service Allocation (75%)   7.24   -> issued as user key credit
        Gross Margin       (25%)   2.41
             less Direct Costs in period             0.00
   ------------------------------------------------------
   Distributable Profit                              2.41
        Operator  50%                                1.21
        Partner   50%                                1.21
```

**6.6 Minimum purchase.** Because Processor Fees include a fixed component, small purchases deliver poor value to the user and thin margin to the Parties. The minimum credit purchase is set at **GBP 5.00**, and the Parties will review whether it should be GBP 10.00 before launch.

**6.7 Working capital.** Keys issued to users draw on a single balance held with the Model Provider. **The Operator will keep that balance funded** so that issued keys can be consumed. Amounts placed on that balance are working capital, not a cost, and are not deducted from Gross Margin.

**6.8 Accounting and payment.** The Operator will prepare a statement for each calendar month within [fifteen] days of month end, showing Gross Receipts, Processor Fees, Net Receipts, Service Allocation, Direct Costs, and Distributable Profit. Payment of the Partner's share follows within [fifteen] days of the statement. Either Party may inspect the underlying Processor and Model Provider records on reasonable notice.

**6.9 Currency.** All amounts are in pounds sterling unless agreed otherwise.

**6.10 Tax.** Each Party is responsible for its own tax. Where value added tax applies to a payment under this Agreement it will be charged in addition and evidenced by a valid invoice.

## 7. Costs

**7.1** Direct Costs are limited to the items in Schedule A. Any other cost is a Direct Cost only if agreed in writing in advance.

**7.2 Neither Party may charge the other for building or maintaining its own working environment, tooling, agentic workflows, or general capability.** Those remain the property and the expense of the Party that builds them, and each Party is free to reuse them elsewhere.

**7.3** Neither Party charges the other for its time. The consideration for each Party's effort is its share of Distributable Profit.

**7.4** Processor Fees are deducted before Gross Margin is calculated under clause 6.1 and are not also Direct Costs.

## 8. Accounts and Custody

**8.1** Schedule B records, for each account and credential used by the Product, which Party holds it and who has access.

**8.2** Each Party will give the other the access it reasonably needs to operate the Product, and will not lock the other out of the shared repository during the term.

**8.3** On termination the Parties will follow Schedule B in transferring or closing accounts. Where an account cannot reasonably be transferred, the holding Party will keep it operating for as long as clause 10.3 requires.

## 9. Operating Commitments

**9.1** The Parties will agree scope, deliverables, and objectives at the start, and record them in the shared repository.

**9.2** Work is coordinated in the open, in the shared repository, rather than privately.

**9.3** Each Party will act in good faith and will tell the other promptly about anything that materially affects the Product, its users, or its revenue.

**9.4** The Parties will publish a privacy notice telling users how their audio is handled, including that in the default mode the request is routed to a third party model provider that is not guaranteed in advance.

## 10. Termination and What Follows

**10.1** Termination does not affect either Party's rights under clause 4.3. The material stays open, and each Party keeps everything it has.

**10.2** Distributable Profit earned up to the termination date is calculated and paid under clause 6.8.

**10.3 User credits survive termination.** Where users hold credit that has not been consumed, the Operator will keep the Model Provider balance funded until that credit is consumed or refunded. The cost of honouring outstanding user credit is a Direct Cost and comes out of Gross Margin before any final distribution. If Gross Margin is insufficient, the Parties will share the shortfall equally.

**10.4** The Parties will agree in writing whether the Product continues to operate after termination and, if so, under whose name and on whose accounts. Absent agreement, the Operator may continue to operate it and the Partner may fork and operate its own version, and neither owes the other a share of what follows.

**10.5** Clauses 4, 5, 6.8, 10, and 11 survive termination.

## 11. General

**11.1 Liability.** Neither Party is liable to the other for indirect or consequential loss, or for loss of profit, beyond amounts payable under clause 6. Nothing limits liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, for fraud, or for anything else that cannot lawfully be limited.

**11.2 Warranties.** The Product is provided to users as it is. Neither Party warrants to the other that it will be free of defects or that any particular revenue will be earned.

**11.3 Publicity.** Each Party may state publicly that it worked with the other on the Product, consistent with clause 4.

**11.4 Assignment.** Neither Party may assign this Agreement without the other's written consent.

**11.5 Entire agreement.** This document and its Schedules are the whole agreement between the Parties on this subject and replace any earlier discussion.

**11.6 Variation.** Any change must be in writing and signed by both Parties.

**11.7 Governing law.** This Agreement is governed by the law of [jurisdiction to be inserted], and the courts of [jurisdiction] have exclusive jurisdiction.

## Open Points To Settle Before Signing

These are deliberately left blank in the draft because they are decisions rather than drafting.

| # | Point | Note |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | Registered names, numbers, and addresses of both entities | Clause 1 |
| 2 | Governing law and jurisdiction | Clause 11.7 |
| 3 | The open source licence for code | Clause 4.1; written material is already settled as Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 |
| 4 | Notice periods in clauses 3.3 and 3.4 | Bracketed in the draft |
| 5 | Whether the minimum purchase is GBP 5.00 or GBP 10.00 | Clause 6.6 |
| 6 | Schedule A, the final list of Direct Costs | The most common source of later disagreement |
| 7 | Schedule B, custody of every account and key | Also the practical exit plan |
| 8 | How credits are sold on mobile | If sold inside the apps, store commission may apply and would exceed the take; selling on the web and consuming in the apps is the likely answer, subject to each store's rules |
| 9 | How the take applies to a browser local mode | That mode consumes no Model Provider credit, so twenty five percent of Net Receipts has no Service Allocation to sit against and needs a different basis or price |
| 10 | Whether the entities are value added tax registered | Affects whether Processor fees and inter party payments carry recoverable tax |
| 11 | Data protection roles and the privacy notice | Clause 9.4; who is controller, and what is told to users about routing |
