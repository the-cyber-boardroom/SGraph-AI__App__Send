## What I learned from their verification

A few things worth marking down so the contract draft + future interop work benefit:

- **The branch-index byte-vector test "for free."** They confirmed our §4 claim explicitly: their `Vault__Crypto.encrypt`/`decrypt` is the same function that handles refs. So an index byte-vector test is structurally `their_ref_vector + plaintext = §2_payload`. No new crypto test plumbing needed on either side. The contract should cite this as a property, not require a separate test suite.
- **The `idx-pid-muw-` prefix is a caller-side concern.** Their `derive_file_id` returns the bare 12-hex; the `idx-pid-muw-` / `ref-pid-muw-` prefixes are added at the call site (`Vault__Transfer.py:77`). Same on our side. The contract should be explicit that the prefix is part of the **stored file id**, not the derivation, so the shared 12-hex tail across the index file id and `branch_id` is invariant.
- **`created_at` accepts ISO-8601 *and* int-ms.** They re-confirmed both; our omission stays correct, but if we ever do emit one, either format is fine. Worth pinning in the contract because most "accepts both" handlers eventually grow a "but prefer X" wrinkle.
- **Their `Schema__Branch_Meta` tolerates unknown fields.** So we could ship `display_name` from our side the moment it lands in their schema, with no transitional dance. That's a nice property for the round-out.

## What I'm NOT doing now (and why)

I considered three things and explicitly chose not to:

1. **Pre-emptively shipping `display_name` from our side before it's in their schema.** Their parser tolerates unknown fields, so the wire would already accept it. But adding a field web-only before the contract pins its semantics is exactly how round 2 happened — somebody adds something with a reasonable-looking convention and the other side picks an incompatible one. Wait for the contract.
2. **Front-running the graceful-degrade by adding *our own* defensive validation around `writeBranchIndex` callers.** Their hardening absorbs *any* drift (CLI bump, partial write, foreign index); duplicating it on the write side just adds maintenance and doesn't cover the cases that matter (CLI-written vaults with future schema changes). Their fix is the right layer.
3. **Drafting the contract from our side.** They asked to draft v0 from CLI source with citations; that's the right starting point (they have the receivers + schemas; we have the producer). I'll review/mark up.

If you'd rather I do any of these now, say the word — I just don't want to repeat the round-2 pattern by adding things without a pinned shared contract.
